Full ENT Examination & OSCE

Safety first: Stridor, respiratory distress, sudden hearing loss, focal neurological signs and suspected mastoiditis require urgent assessment.

OSCE structure

Introduce yourself, confirm identity, explain examination, obtain consent, wash hands, position the patient and ask about pain before starting.

PLAB application: Adapt the examination to the presenting complaint and patient comfort.

Focused ENT history

Ask about onset, duration, laterality, pain, discharge, hearing loss, tinnitus, vertigo, nasal obstruction, epistaxis, sore throat, dysphagia, voice change and neck lumps.

PLAB application: Ask smoking, alcohol, occupational exposure, allergies, medication and previous surgery.

Initial safety assessment

Check observations and ABCDE if acutely unwell. Stridor, drooling, severe respiratory distress, sepsis or altered consciousness need immediate senior help.

PLAB application: Do not delay escalation to finish a routine examination.

External ear inspection

Inspect both pinnae, preauricular and postauricular regions for scars, swelling, erythema, lesions and discharge.

PLAB application: Compare both sides.

Pinna, tragus and mastoid

Gently palpate tragus, move pinna and assess mastoid tenderness if appropriate.

PLAB application: Tragal pain suggests otitis externa; postauricular swelling suggests mastoiditis.

Otoscopy technique

Choose a suitable speculum, hold otoscope like a pen and brace against the face. Pull adult pinna up and back; in young children down and back. Examine canal then tympanic membrane.

PLAB application: Do not force otoscopy when painful or obstructed.

Otoscopy findings

Normal tympanic membrane is pearly grey with visible landmarks. Look for wax, canal oedema, perforation, retraction, effusion, bulging and cholesteatoma debris.

PLAB application: A bulging tympanic membrane supports acute otitis media.

Bedside hearing screen

Ask about hearing aid use and test whispered voice or finger rub in each ear with the other ear masked appropriately.

PLAB application: Formal audiometry is needed for diagnosis and quantification.

Rinne test

Use a 512 Hz tuning fork: compare air conduction near ear canal with bone conduction over mastoid. Normal or sensorineural loss: air > bone (Rinne positive); conductive loss: bone ≥ air (Rinne negative).

PLAB application: Interpret with Weber and clinical context; bedside tests are imperfect.

Weber test

Place a vibrating 512 Hz fork in midline on forehead. Conductive loss tends to lateralise to affected ear; sensorineural loss to better ear.

PLAB application: Sudden hearing loss warrants urgent assessment regardless of bedside tests.

Nasal inspection

Inspect external nose and assess airflow through each nostril. Use a light and appropriate instrument to examine septum, mucosa, turbinates, discharge, polyps and bleeding sites.

PLAB application: Avoid instrumentation during severe active bleeding unless trained.

Oral cavity and oropharynx

Use a torch and tongue depressor when safe. Inspect lips, teeth, gums, tongue, floor of mouth, palate, tonsils and posterior pharynx. Ask patient to say ah.

PLAB application: Do not examine throat forcibly if epiglottitis or unstable airway is suspected.

Neck examination

Inspect from front and side, then systematically palpate cervical lymph node chains, submandibular region, thyroid and salivary glands as appropriate.

PLAB application: Persistent hard or fixed lumps need prompt assessment.

Cranial nerves and balance

Assess facial movements (CN VII); consider palate and voice (IX/X), tongue (XII), eye movements and nystagmus when indicated. Check gait only when safe.

PLAB application: New focal neurology or severe truncal ataxia with acute vertigo raises stroke concern.

Presenting and completing

Summarise positive and negative findings, thank patient, ensure comfort and wash hands. State further tests: audiometry, tympanometry, flexible nasendoscopy or imaging when indicated.

PLAB application: Never claim tests you have not performed.

PLAB 2 counselling and safety-netting

Explain findings without jargon, acknowledge concerns, discuss treatment/referral and confirm understanding. Urgent red flags: stridor, facial weakness, sudden hearing loss, mastoid swelling, severe headache or meningism.

PLAB application: Use shared decisions, clear follow-up and documentation.

PLAB 2: example OSCE sequence

Opening: “Hello, I am one of the doctors. I would like to examine your ears, nose, mouth and neck. Is that okay?”

Focused examination: Inspect and palpate external ears, perform safe otoscopy, assess hearing and tuning forks; inspect nose, mouth and throat; palpate neck; assess relevant cranial nerves.

Closing: Thank the patient, summarise findings, suggest audiometry or ENT review as indicated, explain red flags and document.

Deep Dive

Why Rinne and Weber work

Air conduction travels through the outer and middle ear. Bone conduction bypasses them. Conductive deficits alter the balance; sensorineural deficits impair cochlear or neural perception.

False reassurance risk

A normal-looking ear canal and tympanic membrane do not exclude sudden sensorineural hearing loss, vestibular pathology or referred otalgia.

Exam technique versus diagnosis

OSCE scoring rewards a safe, systematic and patient-centred method. State the clinical meaning of findings and when you would refer.

When not to complete the exam

In threatened airway or severe systemic illness, prioritise ABCDE and urgent specialist support over a full routine ENT examination.
