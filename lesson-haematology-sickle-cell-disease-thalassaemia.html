Sickle Cell Disease & Thalassaemia

Emergency red flags: Fever, chest symptoms, hypoxia, neurological deficits, splenic sequestration or shock in SCD need immediate assessment.

Overview

Sickle cell disease (SCD) is an inherited haemoglobin disorder caused by HbS. Thalassaemia involves reduced production of alpha or beta globin chains.

PLAB application: Differentiate haemoglobin structure from globin-chain synthesis.

SCD genetics

HbSS is a common severe genotype; HbSC and HbS/beta-thalassaemia are other forms of SCD. Sickle cell trait (HbAS) is generally not SCD.

PLAB application: Ask about family history and newborn screening.

Sickling mechanism

Deoxygenated HbS polymerises, deforming red cells. Haemolysis, endothelial activation and vaso-occlusion lead to anaemia and organ injury.

PLAB application: Pain crises are not simply low haemoglobin.

SCD presentation

Chronic haemolytic anaemia, jaundice, painful crises, dactylitis in children, recurrent infection and progressive organ complications can occur.

PLAB application: Assess pain, fever, breathing, neurology and hydration.

Vaso-occlusive crisis

Severe bone or soft-tissue pain may occur without another obvious cause. Provide prompt analgesia, assess observations and search for complications or triggers.

PLAB application: NICE CG143 recommends prompt analgesia and reassessment.

Acute chest syndrome

New pulmonary infiltrate with fever and/or respiratory symptoms in SCD may indicate acute chest syndrome, a life-threatening emergency.

PLAB application: Urgent hospital assessment, oxygen if hypoxaemic, antibiotics and specialist care.

Sepsis and splenic dysfunction

Functional asplenia increases risk of invasive encapsulated bacterial infection. Fever in SCD warrants urgent assessment.

PLAB application: Check immunisation, antibiotic prophylaxis and local fever pathway.

Splenic sequestration

Acute pooling of blood in the spleen may cause rapidly enlarging spleen, falling haemoglobin and shock, particularly in young children.

PLAB application: Immediate emergency resuscitation and haematology involvement.

Stroke and neurological disease

SCD can cause ischaemic or haemorrhagic stroke. New focal weakness, speech changes or seizures require emergency stroke assessment.

PLAB application: Urgent neuroimaging and specialist transfusion planning.

Other SCD complications

Aplastic crisis (often parvovirus B19), priapism, avascular necrosis, gallstones, kidney injury and retinopathy are recognised complications.

PLAB application: Check reticulocyte count in unexpectedly severe anaemia.

SCD long-term care

Specialist care includes vaccination, infection prevention, folate when indicated, screening for organ complications and discussion of hydroxycarbamide.

PLAB application: Hydroxycarbamide can reduce crises but needs monitoring and counselling.

SCD transfusion

Simple or exchange transfusion may be needed for selected serious complications, guided by haematology; routine transfusion is not indicated for uncomplicated pain alone.

PLAB application: Avoid reflex transfusion without indication.

Thalassaemia classification

Alpha thalassaemia ranges from silent carrier to HbH disease and Hb Bart’s hydrops fetalis. Beta thalassaemia ranges from trait to transfusion-dependent disease.

PLAB application: Family and reproductive counselling are important.

Thalassaemia blood tests

Thalassaemia trait often causes marked microcytosis with a relatively preserved/high RBC count. Ferritin helps distinguish coexisting iron deficiency.

PLAB application: Do not automatically prescribe iron for microcytosis.

Haemoglobin analysis

Haemoglobin electrophoresis or HPLC identifies many variants; beta-thalassaemia trait often has raised HbA2. Alpha-thalassaemia trait may need genetic testing.

PLAB application: Interpret results with recent transfusions and iron status in mind.

Thalassaemia treatment and PLAB 2

Severe disease may require regular transfusions, iron chelation and specialist surveillance. Discuss inheritance, pregnancy screening, adherence and complications.

PLAB application: Ask about transfusion history, iron overload and family planning.

PLAB 2 — Counselling and emergency assessment

Introduce yourself, confirm consent and explore pain, fever, chest symptoms, past crises, transfusions, prophylaxis and family history. Explain inheritance, acute warning signs, vaccinations, specialist follow-up and reproductive screening. For acute symptoms, prioritise ABCDE and timely escalation.

Deep Dive

HbS polymerisation

Deoxygenated HbS forms polymers that damage red cells and promote vaso-occlusion and haemolysis.

Acute chest syndrome

Infection, infarction and fat embolism may contribute. Hypoxia can worsen sickling, creating a dangerous cycle.

Hydroxycarbamide

Increases fetal haemoglobin and can reduce vaso-occlusive episodes, but requires specialist monitoring.

Thalassaemia microcytosis

Reduced globin synthesis produces small red cells; iron deficiency must be assessed rather than assumed.

Iron overload

Repeated red-cell transfusions deliver iron that the body cannot readily excrete; chelation and organ monitoring are essential.

Inheritance

Both conditions are inherited; carrier screening and genetic counselling help families understand reproductive risks.
