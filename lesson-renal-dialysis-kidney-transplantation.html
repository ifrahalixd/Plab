Renal Replacement Therapy: Dialysis & Kidney Transplantation

UKMLA / PLAB-focused clinical decisions, dialysis modalities, transplant care and emergencies.

01. When kidney replacement is needed

Kidney replacement therapy (KRT) is considered for advanced kidney failure or life-threatening complications not controlled medically. Discuss options early in progressive CKD; decisions are based on symptoms, biochemical abnormalities, fluid status and patient preference, not eGFR alone.

PLAB pearl: Do not use a single creatinine or eGFR threshold as the only trigger.

02. Urgent dialysis indications

Refractory hyperkalaemia, severe acidosis, pulmonary oedema or fluid overload despite treatment, and uraemic complications such as pericarditis or encephalopathy require urgent renal assessment. Certain dialysable poisonings are additional indications.

PLAB pearl: Think AEIOU as a memory aid, but apply clinical context.

03. Planning in advanced CKD

Offer shared decision-making about haemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, transplantation and comprehensive conservative care. Explain likely benefits, burdens, access needs, home support and patient priorities.

PLAB pearl: Early planning can avoid emergency catheter-dependent dialysis.

04. How haemodialysis works

Blood circulates through an extracorporeal dialyser; diffusion removes small solutes across a semipermeable membrane and ultrafiltration removes excess water. Anticoagulation may be used depending on bleeding risk.

PLAB pearl: Dialysis replaces some kidney functions but not all endocrine functions.

05. Haemodialysis vascular access

An arteriovenous fistula is often preferred for long-term access when suitable; grafts and central venous catheters are alternatives. Fistulas require maturation and protection.

PLAB pearl: Check for a thrill and bruit; avoid routine venepuncture or blood pressure on a fistula arm.

06. Haemodialysis complications

Intradialytic hypotension, cramps, access infection or thrombosis, bleeding, and electrolyte changes can occur. Dialysis disequilibrium is uncommon but can cause neurological symptoms, especially with rapid initial solute removal.

PLAB pearl: Fever in a patient with a dialysis catheter may indicate bloodstream infection.

07. Peritoneal dialysis mechanism

Sterile dialysate enters the peritoneal cavity through a catheter; the peritoneal membrane allows solute diffusion and fluid removal. Continuous ambulatory PD uses manual exchanges; automated PD uses a cycler.

PLAB pearl: PD can be home-based and offers flexibility but requires infection-prevention training.

08. Peritoneal dialysis complications

Peritonitis presents with cloudy effluent, abdominal pain and sometimes fever. Exit-site infection, hernia, leaks, inadequate clearance and glucose absorption can occur.

PLAB pearl: Cloudy PD effluent is peritonitis until proven otherwise: contact the renal team urgently.

09. Haemodialysis versus peritoneal dialysis

Haemodialysis is commonly performed in-centre several times weekly or at home; PD is usually performed daily at home. Selection depends on clinical suitability, lifestyle, capacity and informed choice.

PLAB pearl: Neither modality is universally best for every patient.

10. Kidney transplantation overview

Transplantation can improve survival and quality of life for suitable patients with kidney failure. Donors may be living or deceased. Assessment covers cardiovascular health, infection, malignancy, adherence support and surgical suitability.

PLAB pearl: Refer early for transplant assessment when appropriate, including before dialysis.

11. Transplant immunosuppression

Common regimens combine induction therapy with maintenance agents such as tacrolimus, mycophenolate and corticosteroids, adjusted by the transplant team. Immunosuppression increases infection and malignancy risk.

PLAB pearl: Never stop transplant immunosuppression without specialist advice.

12. Acute transplant rejection

A rising creatinine may be the first sign; other causes include dehydration, obstruction, infection and drug toxicity. Urgent transplant-team assessment and often biopsy are needed to distinguish causes.

PLAB pearl: New graft dysfunction is time-sensitive even without pain or fever.

13. Post-transplant infection

Opportunistic infections (including CMV and BK virus) are important after transplantation. Fever or systemic illness needs prompt assessment; drug interactions can alter calcineurin inhibitor levels.

PLAB pearl: A transplant recipient with fever needs early escalation.

14. CKD complications despite dialysis

Patients may still need management of anaemia, mineral and bone disorder, blood pressure, nutrition, cardiovascular risk and vaccination. Dialysis does not completely replace renal hormone production.

PLAB pearl: Coordinate erythropoiesis-stimulating agents, iron and phosphate management with renal specialists.

15. Conservative kidney management

Comprehensive conservative care is an active approach focused on symptom control, advance care planning and quality of life without dialysis. Discuss it respectfully alongside other options.

PLAB pearl: Shared decision-making must account for patient goals and treatment burden.

16. PLAB 2 consultation and safety-netting

Explore symptoms, understanding, values, home circumstances and support; explain options in plain language. Urgent red flags include severe breathlessness, chest pain, collapse, fever with access problems, cloudy PD fluid and transplant graft dysfunction.

PLAB pearl: Use teach-back and ensure clear renal-team contact arrangements.

Deep Dive

Dialysis transport physics

Diffusion clears small solutes; ultrafiltration removes water. Dialysate composition and treatment intensity influence electrolyte changes.

Access and infection

Fistulas have fewer catheter-related bloodstream infections but require maturation; PD catheters create a peritonitis risk requiring careful aseptic technique.

Dialysis timing

Symptoms and complications guide initiation; eGFR is part of assessment but does not alone determine when dialysis starts.

Transplant immune response

Allorecognition can lead to cellular or antibody-mediated rejection. Immunosuppressive regimens reduce this risk but increase susceptibility to infections.

Medication safety

Renal clearance changes drug dosing; dialysis can remove some medications. Transplant drugs have interactions and require therapeutic monitoring.

PLAB 2 counselling framework

Ask what matters most; explain options, expected routines, complications, fertility and travel considerations; check understanding and document shared decisions.
