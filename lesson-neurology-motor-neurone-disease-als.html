Motor Neurone Disease (MND) & Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)

PLAB priority: Recognise progressive upper and lower motor neurone signs, screen for bulbar and respiratory danger, and refer for specialist assessment.

01. Overview and terminology

Motor neurone disease (MND) is a progressive neurodegenerative group of disorders affecting motor neurons. Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) is the most common adult phenotype, involving upper and lower motor neurons. Sensation is usually relatively preserved.

PLAB application: Mixed upper and lower motor neuron signs with progressive weakness should trigger neurology referral.

02. Epidemiology and causes

Most ALS is sporadic; a minority is familial. Pathogenic variants can involve C9orf72, SOD1 and other genes. It usually presents in later adulthood, but age alone does not determine diagnosis.

PLAB application: Ask about family history and functional change; do not assume all cases are inherited.

03. Upper motor neurone signs

Corticospinal tract dysfunction produces spasticity, brisk reflexes, extensor plantar responses and slowed movements. Weakness may coexist with muscle wasting.

PLAB application: An extensor plantar response alongside fasciculations is a useful clue.

04. Lower motor neurone signs

Anterior horn cell or motor axon loss produces muscle wasting, fasciculations, weakness and reduced reflexes in affected segments. Fasciculations alone are common and do not diagnose MND.

PLAB application: Look for progressive objective weakness and wasting, not twitching alone.

05. Limb-onset disease

Often begins as asymmetric hand clumsiness, foot drop, tripping or loss of fine motor tasks. Weakness gradually spreads to other regions.

PLAB application: Ask whether buttons, handwriting, walking or stairs have changed.

06. Bulbar-onset disease

Dysarthria, dysphagia, choking, weak cough and tongue wasting or fasciculations may appear early. Bulbar dysfunction increases aspiration and nutritional risk.

PLAB application: New progressive dysphagia or dysarthria needs prompt assessment; exclude stroke when onset is sudden.

07. Respiratory involvement

Diaphragmatic weakness can cause orthopnoea, morning headaches, poor sleep, daytime somnolence and weak cough. Respiratory failure may occur even when limb weakness is modest.

PLAB application: Ask specifically about lying flat and morning headaches; escalate new breathlessness.

08. Cognition and behaviour

Some people have executive or behavioural change, and a subset develops frontotemporal dementia. Decision-making capacity is assessed for the specific decision, not presumed absent.

PLAB application: Include carers appropriately with patient consent and assess communication needs.

09. Important differential diagnoses

Cervical myelopathy, multifocal motor neuropathy, myasthenia gravis, inflammatory neuropathies, myopathies and structural CNS lesions may mimic MND. Some are treatable.

PLAB application: Do not label progressive weakness as MND without specialist evaluation.

10. Clinical examination

Assess gait, speech, swallowing safety, tongue movements, muscle bulk, tone, power, reflexes, plantar responses and respiratory function. Check sensation and cranial nerves.

PLAB application: Document upper and lower motor neuron signs across anatomical regions.

11. Investigations and diagnosis

Diagnosis is primarily clinical by a neurologist, supported by electromyography/nerve conduction studies and targeted MRI and blood tests to exclude mimics. No single blood test confirms ALS.

PLAB application: EMG supports denervation but is interpreted in clinical context.

12. Multidisciplinary management

NICE NG42 recommends coordinated multidisciplinary care including neurology, specialist nursing, physiotherapy, occupational therapy, speech and language therapy, dietetics, respiratory services and palliative care.

PLAB application: Early coordinated care supports function, communication and quality of life.

13. Disease-modifying treatment

Riluzole may be offered for ALS according to UK guidance and specialist assessment; it offers a modest survival benefit and needs monitoring. Availability and eligibility of newer treatments depend on current local commissioning and specialist guidance.

PLAB application: Do not promise a cure or describe riluzole as reversing weakness.

14. Nutrition, swallowing and communication

Arrange speech and language assessment for dysphagia and dysarthria; consider texture modification, communication aids and gastrostomy discussion before severe nutritional or respiratory decline.

PLAB application: Ask about weight loss, choking and communication preferences.

15. Respiratory support and emergencies

Regular respiratory assessment helps identify need for non-invasive ventilation. Acute choking, aspiration, respiratory distress or inability to clear secretions requires urgent assessment. Oxygen alone may not address hypoventilation.

PLAB application: New respiratory symptoms are red flags; involve specialist respiratory teams promptly.

16. Prognosis, planning and counselling

Course and survival vary substantially. Offer sensitive, honest discussions about advance care planning, symptom control, psychological support, genetic counselling when appropriate and patient goals.

PLAB application: In PLAB 2, avoid fixed survival predictions and check understanding and preferences.

Deep Dive

Motor neuron pathways

Corticospinal tract degeneration causes UMN signs; anterior horn cell degeneration causes LMN weakness, wasting and fasciculations.

Why symptoms spread

ALS usually progresses between motor regions over time, but speed and distribution vary greatly between individuals.

Bulbar dysfunction

Weakness of muscles controlling speech and swallowing causes dysarthria, dysphagia and aspiration risk.

Nocturnal hypoventilation

Diaphragm weakness can first manifest during sleep, leading to morning headache, daytime somnolence and orthopnoea.

Mimics and diagnostic caution

Cervical cord compression and multifocal motor neuropathy are important alternatives; EMG and MRI are used to exclude other causes.

Shared care and prognosis

Early MDT review, respiratory support, nutrition and communication planning improve care; progression and prognosis are variable.
