Inflammatory Bowel Disease

Emergency: Acute severe ulcerative colitis, toxic megacolon, perforation and sepsis require urgent hospital assessment.

IBD overview

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) includes Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis (UC). Both are chronic relapsing inflammatory disorders with intestinal and extraintestinal effects.

PLAB application: Symptoms, severity, distribution and complications determine investigation and treatment.

Crohn’s versus ulcerative colitis

Crohn’s can affect anywhere from mouth to anus, typically with skip lesions and transmural inflammation. UC affects the colon continuously from the rectum, primarily the mucosa.

PLAB application: Crohn’s may cause fistulas and strictures; UC commonly causes bloody diarrhoea and urgency.

Presenting symptoms

Ask about persistent diarrhoea, blood or mucus, abdominal pain, urgency, tenesmus, nocturnal stools, weight loss, fever and fatigue.

PLAB application: Record stool frequency, blood, systemic upset, dehydration and functional impact.

Risk factors and mimics

Family history, smoking status and prior disease course are relevant. Infective colitis, IBS, coeliac disease, colorectal cancer and medication-related diarrhoea may mimic IBD.

PLAB application: Always consider infection, particularly Clostridioides difficile during an apparent flare.

Extraintestinal manifestations

IBD may involve peripheral or axial arthritis, uveitis, episcleritis, erythema nodosum, pyoderma gangrenosum and hepatobiliary disease including primary sclerosing cholangitis.

PLAB application: Painful red eye or visual change warrants urgent ophthalmic assessment.

Initial investigations

Check FBC, CRP/ESR, renal function, electrolytes, liver tests, albumin and stool tests for infection. Faecal calprotectin helps distinguish intestinal inflammation from functional symptoms in appropriate settings.

PLAB application: Normal inflammatory markers do not exclude IBD.

Confirming diagnosis

Ileocolonoscopy with biopsies is central; imaging such as MR enterography or intestinal ultrasound can evaluate small bowel disease and complications.

PLAB application: Exclude infection and use specialist assessment; do not diagnose solely from calprotectin.

Ulcerative colitis severity

Assess stool frequency, rectal bleeding, pulse, temperature, haemoglobin and CRP. Acute severe UC is a medical emergency.

PLAB application: Truelove and Witts criteria: six or more bloody stools/day plus at least one systemic feature such as fever, tachycardia, anaemia or elevated ESR.

Acute severe ulcerative colitis

Admit urgently for fluid/electrolyte correction, infection testing, VTE prophylaxis unless contraindicated, specialist review and IV corticosteroids.

PLAB application: Monitor stool chart, observations and bloods daily; reassess response early, usually by day 3, for rescue therapy or surgery.

Mild to moderate UC treatment

5-aminosalicylates (mesalazine) are often first-line, with topical rectal treatment important for distal disease. Steroids may induce remission if needed.

PLAB application: Choose oral and/or topical regimens based on disease extent and response under current NICE guidance.

Crohn’s treatment

Corticosteroids can induce remission for active Crohn’s; biologic or other advanced therapies and immunomodulators may be used by specialists for ongoing control.

PLAB application: Mesalazine is not routinely used to induce remission in Crohn’s disease; avoid long-term steroids as maintenance.

Maintenance and biologic safety

Maintenance is individualised by subtype and severity. Therapies include mesalazine for UC, immunomodulators and targeted/biologic medicines where indicated.

PLAB application: Before immunosuppression consider TB/hepatitis screening, vaccination status, infection risk and medicine-specific monitoring.

Complications of Crohn’s

Transmural inflammation can cause strictures, obstruction, abscesses, fistulas and perianal disease; malabsorption and nutritional deficiencies may occur.

PLAB application: Fever with focal abdominal pain or perianal sepsis warrants urgent assessment and imaging.

Complications of UC

Severe colitis may lead to toxic megacolon, perforation, haemorrhage and venous thromboembolism. Long-standing colonic inflammation increases colorectal cancer risk.

PLAB application: Severe pain, distension, fever and systemic toxicity require emergency escalation.

Monitoring and long-term care

Review disease activity, treatment adherence, nutrition, anaemia, bone health, psychological wellbeing and surveillance colonoscopy eligibility.

PLAB application: Smoking cessation is especially important in Crohn’s; offer shared decision-making and specialist follow-up.

PLAB 2 counselling

Explain that IBD is chronic but often controllable. Explore symptoms, concerns, fertility/pregnancy plans, medication adherence and the impact on work and life.

PLAB application: Safety-net severe bloody diarrhoea, dehydration, fever, abdominal distension, severe pain or eye symptoms.

PLAB 2: IBD counselling OSCE

  1. Introduce yourself, confirm identity and explore ideas, concerns and expectations.
  2. Clarify stool frequency, bleeding, weight loss, pain, medications and extraintestinal symptoms.
  3. Explain Crohn’s and UC differences in plain language.
  4. Discuss tests, induction versus maintenance therapy, monitoring and shared decisions.
  5. Safety-net fever, severe bloody diarrhoea, distension, dehydration and severe abdominal pain.

Deep Dive

Why Crohn’s causes fistulas

Transmural inflammation may penetrate bowel wall and connect to adjacent organs or skin.

Why UC causes bloody diarrhoea

Inflamed colonic mucosa becomes friable and ulcerated, producing bleeding, urgency and tenesmus.

Calprotectin limitations

Faecal calprotectin can rise with infection and other inflammatory conditions; it is not specific for IBD.

Steroids versus maintenance

Corticosteroids rapidly suppress inflammation but cumulative toxicity makes them unsuitable for long-term maintenance.

ASUC rescue decisions

Non-response to IV steroids prompts urgent multidisciplinary consideration of rescue treatments such as infliximab or ciclosporin and timely surgery.

Cancer surveillance

Long-standing extensive colonic IBD increases colorectal cancer risk; surveillance colonoscopy timing is risk-based under specialist guidance.
