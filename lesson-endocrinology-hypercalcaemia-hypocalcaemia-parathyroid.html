Hypercalcaemia, Hypocalcaemia & Parathyroid Disorders

Emergency: Severe symptomatic calcium abnormalities require urgent assessment, ECG and protocol-based treatment.

Calcium homeostasis

Ionised calcium supports neuromuscular function, coagulation and cardiac activity. PTH raises serum calcium; vitamin D supports intestinal absorption.

PLAB application: Interpret adjusted calcium alongside albumin, renal function and symptoms; ionised calcium can help in critical illness.

Recognising hypercalcaemia

Symptoms include thirst, polyuria, constipation, abdominal pain, fatigue, confusion and dehydration; severe cases may cause arrhythmia or reduced consciousness.

PLAB application: Assess hydration, ECG, renal function and severity; symptomatic marked hypercalcaemia requires urgent care.

Hypercalcaemia differential

Primary hyperparathyroidism and malignancy are major causes. Also consider vitamin D excess, granulomatous disease, thyrotoxicosis, thiazides and immobilisation.

PLAB application: PTH is the key branching test: elevated or inappropriately normal versus suppressed.

Primary hyperparathyroidism

Autonomous PTH secretion is usually due to a parathyroid adenoma. Calcium is elevated with PTH not appropriately suppressed; phosphate may be low.

PLAB application: Assess stones, fractures, bone density and renal function; consider surgery according to specialist criteria.

Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcaemia

FHH causes lifelong mild hypercalcaemia with relatively low urinary calcium; it may mimic primary hyperparathyroidism.

PLAB application: Urinary calcium assessment and family history help distinguish FHH; avoid unnecessary parathyroidectomy.

Malignancy-related hypercalcaemia

Cancer may cause hypercalcaemia via PTHrP, osteolysis or calcitriol; PTH is usually suppressed.

PLAB application: Look for red flags including weight loss and known malignancy; investigate underlying cause.

Acute hypercalcaemia management

Urgent evaluation includes ABCDE, ECG, U&Es, fluid status and cause. IV isotonic saline may be needed if dehydrated, tailored to cardiac/renal status; specialist antiresorptive treatment may follow.

PLAB application: Avoid reflexively giving large fluid volumes in heart or kidney failure; discuss severe cases urgently.

Hypocalcaemia presentation

Perioral tingling, paraesthesia, muscle cramps, tetany, seizures, laryngospasm and prolonged QT may occur.

PLAB application: Symptomatic severe hypocalcaemia is an emergency requiring monitored IV calcium under protocol.

Hypocalcaemia causes

Consider postoperative hypoparathyroidism, vitamin D deficiency, chronic kidney disease, hypomagnesaemia, pancreatitis and medications.

PLAB application: Check magnesium, phosphate, renal function, vitamin D and PTH.

Hypoparathyroidism

Low PTH leads to hypocalcaemia and often hyperphosphataemia; commonly follows thyroid or neck surgery.

PLAB application: Post-thyroidectomy tingling or spasms warrants urgent calcium assessment.

Vitamin D deficiency and osteomalacia

Low vitamin D reduces calcium absorption and may cause secondary hyperparathyroidism, bone pain and proximal weakness.

PLAB application: Replace vitamin D per local guidance; consider nutritional, malabsorptive and renal causes.

CKD mineral-bone disorder

Reduced phosphate excretion and impaired vitamin D activation contribute to secondary hyperparathyroidism and bone disease.

PLAB application: Interpret PTH, phosphate and calcium together with renal function.

Investigations and interpretation

Repeat calcium if appropriate; assess albumin or ionised calcium, PTH, phosphate, magnesium, creatinine/eGFR, ALP and vitamin D as indicated.

PLAB application: An elevated calcium with suppressed PTH points away from primary hyperparathyroidism.

ECG and complications

Hypercalcaemia can shorten QT; hypocalcaemia can prolong QT. Both can affect cardiac rhythm and neurological status.

PLAB application: Do an ECG when clinically significant or symptomatic abnormalities are suspected.

Long-term primary hyperparathyroidism care

Specialist assessment includes renal imaging where indicated, bone density assessment, surgical eligibility and follow-up if nonoperative.

PLAB application: Parathyroidectomy can be definitive for suitable patients.

PLAB 2 communication and safety-netting

Explain calcium abnormalities, symptom monitoring, medication review and why repeat blood tests or referral may be needed.

PLAB application: Advise urgent review for confusion, severe weakness, seizures, palpitations or spasms.

PLAB 2: Calcium disorder consultation OSCE

  1. Introduce yourself, confirm identity, gain consent and assess immediate symptoms.
  2. Ask about thirst, urination, constipation, stones, fractures, tingling, spasms, surgery, medications and cancer symptoms.
  3. Review hydration, neurological state and cardiac red flags; examine appropriately.
  4. Explain calcium, PTH, vitamin D and kidney tests in accessible language.
  5. Discuss treatment, follow-up and urgent safety-netting for confusion, seizures, spasms or palpitations.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Calcium-sensing feedback

Calcium-sensing receptors regulate PTH: falling calcium stimulates PTH, while rising calcium suppresses it.

Why PTH is central

PTH should be suppressed in hypercalcaemia; an inappropriately normal value is diagnostically important.

Albumin and calcium

Total calcium depends on albumin; adjusted calcium has limitations, especially in critical illness, where ionised calcium may be useful.

FHH versus adenoma

Low urinary calcium and lifelong mild elevation suggest FHH; specialist interpretation is needed in CKD or vitamin D deficiency.

Magnesium and PTH

Severe magnesium depletion can inhibit PTH release and cause resistance, complicating calcium correction.

Emergency physiology

Severe hypercalcaemia can cause volume depletion and renal dysfunction; hypocalcaemia increases neuromuscular excitability and may cause laryngospasm.
