Endocrine Examination & Hormone Investigations

PLAB safety: Recognise endocrine emergencies early: DKA, adrenal crisis, severe hyponatraemia, pituitary apoplexy and thyroid storm.

Clinical approach to endocrine disease

Endocrine conditions can present with fatigue, weight change, polyuria, menstrual disturbance, altered blood pressure or electrolyte abnormalities. Identify acute instability before detailed endocrine testing.

PLAB application: Use an ABCDE approach for critically ill patients; check bedside glucose promptly.

Focused endocrine history

Ask about symptom onset, weight and appetite, heat or cold intolerance, thirst, urine output, bowel habit, skin and hair changes, sexual and reproductive health, headaches and visual symptoms.

PLAB application: Review prescribed and over-the-counter medicines, especially glucocorticoids, lithium, amiodarone and diabetes medicines.

General endocrine examination

Assess appearance, BMI, pulse, blood pressure including postural changes where indicated, hydration, temperature, pigmentation, striae, bruising, hair distribution and peripheral oedema.

PLAB application: Describe positive findings objectively and preserve dignity.

Thyroid examination OSCE

Inspect from the front for swelling and scars; observe swallowing; examine hands, pulse, eyes and neck; palpate thyroid from behind after consent and assess cervical nodes.

PLAB application: Ask about pain before palpation; stridor or compressive symptoms require urgent assessment.

Diabetes examination

Assess feet for skin breaks, pulses, deformity, sensation with a 10-g monofilament where trained, and footwear; review retinal screening, kidney health and cardiovascular risk.

PLAB application: Never rely on one normal foot finding to exclude risk; arrange appropriate structured assessment.

Cushing and adrenal signs

Cushing features include proximal myopathy, wide violaceous striae, easy bruising and facial rounding; primary adrenal insufficiency may produce hyperpigmentation, weight loss and postural hypotension.

PLAB application: Acute hypotension with vomiting or collapse may indicate adrenal crisis: treat urgently.

Pituitary examination

Look for acromegalic facial and hand changes, galactorrhoea when clinically relevant, and visual field deficits; ask about headaches and sexual dysfunction.

PLAB application: Sudden severe headache with visual loss suggests pituitary apoplexy and is an emergency.

Choosing initial investigations

Use targeted testing: glucose or HbA1c, thyroid function, U&E, calcium, liver tests and relevant pituitary or adrenal hormones based on the presentation.

PLAB application: Avoid indiscriminate hormone panels and interpret results against clinical context.

Interpreting thyroid function

High TSH with low free T4 suggests primary hypothyroidism; suppressed TSH with high free T4 or T3 suggests thyrotoxicosis. Low free T4 with low or inappropriately normal TSH suggests central hypothyroidism.

PLAB application: Non-thyroidal illness, pregnancy, biotin and medicines may affect interpretation.

Glucose and HbA1c interpretation

HbA1c ≥48 mmol/mol (6.5%) can support diabetes diagnosis in suitable non-pregnant adults; symptomatic hyperglycaemia and suspected type 1 diabetes require timely plasma glucose-based assessment.

PLAB application: Do not use HbA1c alone to diagnose rapidly developing type 1 diabetes or acute hyperglycaemic crises.

Sodium and osmolality

Interpret sodium with volume status, serum osmolality, urine osmolality and urine sodium where appropriate. Hyponatraemia has many causes; SIADH is a diagnosis of exclusion.

PLAB application: Seizures or severe neurological symptoms with hyponatraemia need emergency management.

Calcium and PTH patterns

High calcium with non-suppressed PTH suggests PTH-dependent hypercalcaemia; high calcium with suppressed PTH suggests non-PTH causes. Low calcium with low PTH suggests hypoparathyroidism.

PLAB application: Use albumin-adjusted or ionised calcium as appropriate; check magnesium, renal function and vitamin D.

Adrenal testing principles

Morning cortisol may help assess adrenal insufficiency; interpretation depends on timing, illness and steroid exposure. Suspected Cushing syndrome requires validated screening tests selected by specialists.

PLAB application: Do not delay emergency hydrocortisone in suspected adrenal crisis for diagnostic sampling.

Pituitary hormone testing

Raised prolactin needs assessment of pregnancy, medicines, thyroid dysfunction and other causes. IGF-1 screens for acromegaly; pituitary MRI and dynamic tests are specialist-led.

PLAB application: Check for hypopituitarism when pituitary disease is suspected; cortisol deficiency takes priority over thyroid replacement.

Dynamic tests and interpretation pitfalls

ACTH stimulation, dexamethasone suppression, water deprivation and other dynamic tests require protocol-based specialist supervision. Hormones have diurnal rhythms and assay interference.

PLAB application: Never attempt water deprivation testing outside an appropriate monitored specialist setting.

OSCE synthesis and safety-netting

Summarise findings, suggest a focused differential, select relevant investigations and explain next steps in plain language. Ask about patient concerns and arrange follow-up.

PLAB application: Escalate suspected DKA, adrenal crisis, thyroid storm, pituitary apoplexy, severe calcium disturbance or symptomatic hyponatraemia.

PLAB 2: Endocrine examination OSCE

  1. Wash hands, introduce yourself, confirm identity, obtain consent and ask about pain.
  2. Assess general appearance, observations and hydration.
  3. Perform focused thyroid, diabetes, adrenal or pituitary examination as indicated.
  4. Thank patient, summarise positive and negative findings, propose targeted tests.
  5. Escalate emergencies and explain safety-netting clearly.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Feedback loops

Pituitary tropic hormones are regulated by hypothalamic input and peripheral hormone feedback; primary gland failure often raises the stimulating hormone.

Timing and circadian rhythm

Cortisol and some other hormones vary by time of day. Sampling time and acute illness materially affect interpretation.

Assay interference

Biotin supplements, heterophile antibodies and some medicines can distort immunoassays. Unexpected results should be discussed with the laboratory.

Volume status in sodium disorders

Clinical volume assessment plus serum and urine osmolality helps separate hypovolaemic, euvolaemic and hypervolaemic hyponatraemia.

Calcium-PTH feedback

High calcium normally suppresses PTH; an inappropriately normal PTH in hypercalcaemia is therefore abnormal.

Endocrine emergencies

Adrenal crisis, DKA, pituitary apoplexy, thyroid storm and severe electrolyte disturbances require immediate escalation, not routine outpatient investigation.
