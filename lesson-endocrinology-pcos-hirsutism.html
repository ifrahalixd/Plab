Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) & Hirsutism

PLAB safety: Rapidly progressive hirsutism or virilisation needs prompt investigation for an androgen-secreting tumour. Ultrasound alone does not diagnose PCOS.

Overview

PCOS is a common endocrine-metabolic syndrome characterised by ovulatory dysfunction, hyperandrogenism and sometimes polycystic ovarian morphology. It is a diagnosis of exclusion.

PLAB application: Do not diagnose PCOS from ultrasound alone.

Presentation

Irregular or absent periods, subfertility, hirsutism, acne, androgenic alopecia and weight or metabolic concerns may occur. Some people have few outward signs.

PLAB application: Ask about cycle pattern and patient priorities.

Diagnostic criteria in adults

Modified Rotterdam criteria: two of clinical/biochemical hyperandrogenism, ovulatory dysfunction, and polycystic ovarian morphology on ultrasound or elevated AMH in adults, after exclusion of other causes. AMH is not a standalone test.

PLAB application: If irregular cycles and hyperandrogenism are present, ultrasound is not necessary for diagnosis.

Adolescents

Diagnosis in adolescents requires both persistent irregular menstrual cycles appropriate to time since menarche and hyperandrogenism, after excluding mimics; ultrasound and AMH should not be used diagnostically.

PLAB application: Avoid overdiagnosis in teenagers.

Hirsutism assessment

Assess distribution, onset and progression of coarse terminal hair, acne, hair loss, virilisation, menstrual history, drugs and family history; consider a modified Ferriman–Gallwey score.

PLAB application: Rapid progression or virilisation is a red flag.

Important differential diagnoses

Exclude pregnancy when relevant, thyroid dysfunction, hyperprolactinaemia, non-classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia, Cushing syndrome and androgen-secreting tumours where indicated.

PLAB application: Severe rapid-onset androgen excess needs urgent specialist assessment.

Investigations

Consider total/free testosterone or calculated free androgen index with reliable assays; TSH, prolactin and 17-hydroxyprogesterone when indicated. Assess BP, lipids and glycaemic status.

PLAB application: Do not rely on LH:FSH ratio to diagnose PCOS.

Metabolic health

PCOS is associated with insulin resistance, dysglycaemia, dyslipidaemia, sleep apnoea and increased cardiometabolic risk. A 75 g OGTT is the most accurate glycaemic test in PCOS when feasible.

PLAB application: Offer appropriate metabolic risk assessment irrespective of BMI.

Lifestyle and wellbeing

Support sustainable physical activity, nutritious eating, sleep and patient-centred weight management if relevant, without stigma. Screen for anxiety, depression and body-image concerns.

PLAB application: Use shared decision-making and avoid assuming weight loss is every patient’s goal.

Irregular periods and endometrial protection

Chronic anovulation can increase endometrial hyperplasia risk. Combined hormonal contraception or appropriate progestogen regimens can regulate bleeding and protect the endometrium; tailor to contraindications.

PLAB application: Prolonged amenorrhoea or abnormal bleeding warrants assessment.

Treating hirsutism and acne

Cosmetic hair removal and combined oral contraceptives are common first options. If inadequate after a trial, specialist-guided anti-androgens may be considered with effective contraception because of fetal risk.

PLAB application: Discuss realistic response times and contraceptive safety.

Metformin

Metformin may be considered particularly for metabolic indications, especially in adults with BMI ≥25 kg/m²; decisions are individualised and gastrointestinal adverse effects are common.

PLAB application: Metformin is not a universal first-line treatment for cosmetic hirsutism.

Fertility

If pregnancy is desired, discuss preconception health and ovulation assessment. Letrozole is first-line pharmacological ovulation induction for anovulatory infertility in PCOS without other infertility factors, under specialist care.

PLAB application: Avoid prescribing ovulation induction without appropriate fertility assessment.

Pregnancy considerations

PCOS is associated with increased risk of gestational diabetes and hypertensive pregnancy disorders. Offer preconception counselling and pregnancy-related screening as clinically indicated.

PLAB application: Check medicines for pregnancy safety.

Red flags

Rapid-onset severe hirsutism, deepening voice, clitoromegaly, marked testosterone elevation, pelvic/adrenal mass symptoms, severe abnormal uterine bleeding or pregnancy-related concerns require prompt assessment.

PLAB application: Consider androgen-secreting tumour rather than routine PCOS.

Long-term follow-up

Review menstrual pattern, metabolic risks, blood pressure, mental health, sleep, fertility plans and treatment effects. Tailor follow-up to patient needs and evolving guidance.

PLAB application: PCOS care is longitudinal and multidisciplinary.

PLAB 2: Irregular periods and excess hair growth OSCE

  1. Introduce yourself, confirm identity, consent and confidentiality.
  2. Ask about menstrual history, onset of hair growth, acne, weight/metabolic symptoms, fertility wishes and pregnancy possibility.
  3. Explore red flags: rapid progression, virilisation, abnormal bleeding and severe distress.
  4. Discuss appropriate examination and targeted blood tests, explaining that ultrasound is not always needed.
  5. Explain PCOS in non-stigmatising language and agree priorities: periods, hair, metabolic health or fertility.
  6. Safety-net severe or rapid changes and arrange follow-up for metabolic and menstrual health.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Ovarian androgen excess

Altered ovarian theca-cell steroidogenesis and insulin signalling can increase androgen production.

Insulin resistance

Hyperinsulinaemia can amplify androgen excess and reduce sex hormone-binding globulin.

Why ultrasound alone misleads

Polycystic ovarian morphology is not synonymous with PCOS and can occur in otherwise healthy individuals.

Endometrial risk

Chronic anovulation can lead to prolonged unopposed oestrogen exposure and endometrial proliferation.

Hirsutism treatment timeline

Hair follicles cycle slowly, so medical improvement may take several months; cosmetic methods can bridge the interval.

Patient-centred care

PCOS may affect reproductive, metabolic and psychological health; goals vary across life stages.
