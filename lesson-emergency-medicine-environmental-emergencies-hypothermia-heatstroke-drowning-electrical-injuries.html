Environmental Emergencies

Emergency red flags: Altered consciousness after heat exposure, severe hypothermia, respiratory impairment after submersion, cardiac symptoms after electrical injury and suspected inhalation injury require immediate ABCDE assessment and escalation.

Environmental emergencies: first approach

Ensure scene safety before contact. Use ABCDE, summon emergency and critical-care support, identify exposure history and duration, remove the patient from ongoing danger and assess for associated trauma.

PLAB application: Protect rescuers first; avoid becoming a second casualty.

Accidental hypothermia: definition and staging

Hypothermia is core temperature below 35°C. Mild cases may shiver and remain alert; moderate or severe cases develop impaired consciousness, bradycardia, hypotension and arrhythmias. Use a reliable low-reading core thermometer when available.

PLAB application: A normal standard thermometer may not detect severe hypothermia.

Hypothermia: safe handling and rewarming

Handle gently, remove wet clothes in a warm environment, insulate and warm the trunk. Conscious stable patients may receive warm sweet drinks if safe to swallow. Moderate or severe hypothermia needs monitored active rewarming, warmed oxygen/fluids where appropriate and specialist input.

PLAB application: Rough handling of profoundly hypothermic patients can provoke arrhythmias.

Hypothermic cardiac arrest

Assess breathing and signs of life carefully; profound hypothermia may make pulses difficult to detect. Start CPR and follow current Resuscitation Council UK hypothermia-specific ALS guidance; defibrillation and medicine timing differ at very low core temperatures. Consider extracorporeal rewarming in selected patients.

PLAB application: Do not apply routine normothermic drug/defibrillation sequences without checking temperature-specific guidance.

Frostbite and cold injury

Frostbite causes cold, numb, pale or waxy tissue and may blister after thawing. Protect from refreezing, remove constricting jewellery, avoid rubbing and seek specialist advice. Rewarm in controlled warm water only when refreezing can be prevented.

PLAB application: Never massage frostbitten tissue or use direct intense heat.

Heat exhaustion versus heatstroke

Heat exhaustion causes weakness, dizziness, nausea and heavy sweating without severe CNS dysfunction. Heatstroke is life-threatening hyperthermia with central nervous system dysfunction; sweating may still be present. Core temperature often exceeds 40°C, but clinical judgement matters.

PLAB application: Altered mental state after heat exposure is heatstroke until assessed otherwise.

Heatstroke: immediate treatment

Move to a cool area, call for help, assess ABCDE and begin rapid active cooling without waiting for investigations. Cold-water immersion is effective when safe and feasible; evaporative cooling with water and fans is an alternative. Monitor core temperature and organ injury.

PLAB application: Cooling is time-critical; antipyretics do not treat heatstroke.

Exertional heatstroke and complications

Exertional heatstroke may occur in athletes or workers and can cause rhabdomyolysis, AKI, coagulopathy, hepatic injury and electrolyte derangement. Check ECG, glucose, blood gas, renal function, CK, liver tests and coagulation while cooling continues.

PLAB application: A young fit person can have life-threatening heatstroke.

Drowning: initial priorities

Drowning causes respiratory impairment from submersion or immersion. Rescue only when safe; initiate rescue breaths and CPR according to current resuscitation guidance, give oxygen and ventilatory support, and assess for hypoxic brain injury.

PLAB application: Hypoxia is the central problem; ventilation is especially important.

Drowning: hospital assessment

Assess respiratory effort, oxygen saturation, mental status and temperature. Persistent cough, hypoxia, increased work of breathing or reduced consciousness needs monitored care. Imaging and antibiotics are not routine for every asymptomatic patient; investigate based on findings.

PLAB application: Do not use terms such as dry drowning to imply a distinct delayed syndrome.

Electrical injury: scene safety

Do not touch the patient until the electricity source is isolated. High-voltage injuries and lightning may cause cardiac arrest, deep tissue injury, compartment syndrome and rhabdomyolysis even with small skin wounds. Follow ABCDE and trauma assessment.

PLAB application: Visible burns underestimate deep electrical damage.

Electrical injury: cardiac and tissue assessment

Obtain an ECG, examine entry/exit wounds and neurovascular status, and assess for associated falls or fractures. High-voltage exposure, loss of consciousness, abnormal ECG, chest symptoms or major burns warrant specialist monitoring and assessment.

PLAB application: Electrical injury plus syncope or chest pain is not a routine minor burn.

Lightning strike

Lightning can cause cardiac or respiratory arrest, transient neurological deficits, burns and tympanic membrane injury. Victims do not retain an electrical charge; once the scene is safe, immediate resuscitation is appropriate.

PLAB application: Prioritise patients in arrest for prompt resuscitation when multiple victims are present.

Burns and inhalation injury

Assess total body surface area, depth, circumferential burns, airway exposure and carbon monoxide risk. Facial burns, soot, hoarseness or stridor raise concern for inhalation injury. Follow local burns referral criteria and avoid delayed airway escalation.

PLAB application: A normal pulse oximeter reading does not exclude carbon monoxide exposure.

Special groups and prevention

Older adults, infants, people with impaired mobility, outdoor workers and people taking thermoregulation-impairing drugs are vulnerable. Consider neglect or safeguarding where circumstances suggest risk. Counsel on hydration, heat avoidance, cold-weather protection and water/electrical safety.

PLAB application: Prevention advice should be tailored to age and circumstances.

PLAB 2 emergency communication and handover

Explain the immediate threat in clear language, seek consent where possible, communicate uncertainty, contact senior/critical-care services and hand over with SBAR including exposure, core temperature, vital signs, treatment and response. Reassess ABCDE frequently.

PLAB application: Describe what you have done, what remains concerning and what help is needed.

PLAB 2 — Environmental emergency station

Check scene safety, introduce yourself if feasible, call for help, begin ABCDE, treat immediate threats, identify exposure and core temperature, request appropriate senior assistance and provide an SBAR handover. Reassess and explain the plan to the patient or family.

Clinical deep dive

Environmental Emergencies — Advanced PLAB Reasoning

Hypothermia and resuscitation

At low core temperatures, metabolism and drug clearance change. Use current temperature-specific ALS algorithms and consider extracorporeal life support for selected arrested patients. Avoid declaring futility prematurely without specialist advice.

Heatstroke: cooling before tests

Rapid active cooling is the definitive immediate treatment. Continue assessment and monitoring for rhabdomyolysis, renal injury, liver injury, coagulopathy and arrhythmia; do not use paracetamol to lower environmental hyperthermia.

Drowning and delayed respiratory symptoms

Drowning is a hypoxic insult. Patients with ongoing respiratory symptoms or abnormal observations require assessment and observation. Routine prophylactic antibiotics or corticosteroids are not recommended without a specific indication.

Electrical exposure: internal injury

Deep muscle damage may occur despite limited skin burns. Assess ECG, renal function, CK and urine when clinically indicated, and consider trauma and compartment syndrome.

Carbon monoxide and inhalation

CO can impair oxygen carriage without lowering conventional SpO₂. Obtain appropriate co-oximetry when exposure is suspected and provide oxygen while arranging specialist assessment.

OSCE decision-making

Lead with safety and ABCDE, identify reversible threats, give the immediate treatment, communicate clearly, document response and hand over concisely.
