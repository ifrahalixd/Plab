Diabetes Insipidus (AVP Deficiency/Resistance) & SIADH

Emergency: Severe symptomatic hyponatraemia requires urgent monitored treatment; missing desmopressin in known central DI can cause dangerous hypernatraemia.

Water balance and AVP

Arginine vasopressin (AVP, formerly ADH) is produced in the hypothalamus and released by the posterior pituitary. It acts at kidney V2 receptors to retain free water.

PLAB application: Connect AVP to urine concentration and serum sodium.

Terminology

Central diabetes insipidus is increasingly called AVP deficiency (AVP-D); nephrogenic diabetes insipidus is AVP resistance (AVP-R). SIADH is syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis.

PLAB application: Recognise both traditional and updated names.

Central DI causes

Pituitary surgery, head injury, hypothalamic or pituitary disease and idiopathic cases can reduce AVP release.

PLAB application: Ask about neurosurgery, trauma and pituitary symptoms.

Nephrogenic DI causes

Lithium, hypercalcaemia, hypokalaemia, kidney disease and inherited renal receptor defects can impair AVP response.

PLAB application: Review medicines and calcium and potassium.

DI presentation

Large volumes of dilute urine, persistent thirst and nocturia are characteristic. Inability to drink or impaired thirst risks severe hypernatraemic dehydration.

PLAB application: Polyuria means increased urine volume, not simply frequency.

Initial DI assessment

Measure fluid intake/output, weight, serum sodium, glucose, calcium, potassium, kidney function and paired serum/urine osmolality. Exclude osmotic diuresis from hyperglycaemia.

PLAB application: Hypernatraemia with inappropriately dilute urine raises suspicion.

Confirming DI

Specialist-supervised dynamic testing, sometimes including copeptin-based protocols, distinguishes AVP deficiency, resistance and primary polydipsia. Water deprivation is potentially hazardous.

PLAB application: Never recommend unsupervised water deprivation.

Central DI management

Desmopressin replaces AVP action. Ensure access to water, correct dosing, monitor sodium and prevent missed doses in hospital. Excess desmopressin plus water can cause hyponatraemia.

PLAB application: Desmopressin is a critical medicine in established AVP deficiency.

Nephrogenic DI management

Treat the cause, consider stopping lithium with specialist input, ensure water access and use specialist-directed dietary or pharmacological measures. Desmopressin is usually less effective.

PLAB application: Differentiate renal resistance from hormone deficiency.

SIADH pathophysiology

Excess antidiuretic effect retains water, dilutes plasma sodium and produces urine that is not maximally dilute despite low serum osmolality.

PLAB application: SIADH is typically hypotonic hyponatraemia in a clinically euvolaemic patient.

SIADH causes

Small-cell lung cancer, pulmonary or CNS disease and medicines such as SSRIs, carbamazepine and some antipsychotics can contribute.

PLAB application: Medication review and cause-finding are essential.

SIADH diagnostic criteria

Check low serum osmolality, urine osmolality usually above 100 mOsm/kg, urine sodium often above 30 mmol/L with normal intake, apparent euvolaemia and adequate adrenal, thyroid and kidney function. Interpret in context.

PLAB application: Exclude adrenal insufficiency and relevant hypothyroidism before labelling SIADH.

Hyponatraemia symptoms

Nausea, headache, gait instability and confusion may progress to seizures, coma and respiratory compromise. Severity and speed of onset both matter.

PLAB application: Seizures or severe neurological symptoms demand emergency care.

SIADH management

Treat the cause. For stable chronic SIADH, fluid restriction is often initial treatment with close monitoring; specialist therapies depend on response and context.

PLAB application: Do not apply routine fluid restriction to hypovolaemic hyponatraemia.

Severe symptomatic hyponatraemia

Emergency senior/critical care assessment, monitored hypertonic saline under local protocol and frequent sodium checks are needed. Overly rapid correction can cause osmotic demyelination.

PLAB application: Treat symptoms urgently while preventing overcorrection.

Comparing DI and SIADH

DI causes excessive free-water loss and often high sodium; SIADH causes water retention and low sodium. Serum and urine osmolality help separate them.

PLAB application: Always assess volume status, glucose, medication and urine output.

PLAB 2: Polyuria / hyponatraemia consultation OSCE

  1. Introduce yourself, confirm identity and consent; ask about the patient’s main concern.
  2. Explore thirst, urine volume, nocturia, fluid intake, headache, nausea, confusion, seizures, medicines, pituitary history and lung disease.
  3. Assess observations, hydration, volume status and neurological red flags.
  4. Explain paired blood and urine tests and when specialist assessment is needed.
  5. For central DI, discuss desmopressin adherence, water access and sodium monitoring.
  6. Safety-net severe headache, confusion, seizures, collapse, inability to drink and worsening dehydration.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

AVP and collecting ducts

AVP activates V2 receptors and aquaporin-2 insertion, increasing free-water reabsorption.

Copeptin testing

Copeptin is a stable precursor marker of AVP secretion; specialist stimulation protocols may help distinguish AVP deficiency from primary polydipsia.

Why sodium rises in DI

Loss of water disproportionate to solute raises plasma tonicity unless thirst and access to water compensate.

Why SIADH causes low sodium

Persistent antidiuresis reduces electrolyte-free water excretion and dilutes extracellular sodium.

Cerebral adaptation

In chronic hyponatraemia brain cells lose osmolytes; rapid correction can lead to osmotic demyelination.

Desmopressin safety

Missed doses can cause severe hypernatraemia, while excessive dosing with water intake can cause hyponatraemia; monitoring matters.
