Tonsillitis, Pharyngitis & Quinsy

Emergency: Stridor, drooling, respiratory distress or inability to swallow saliva needs urgent airway assessment.

Definitions

Pharyngitis is inflammation of the pharynx; tonsillitis affects the palatine tonsils. Most acute sore throats are viral. Quinsy is a peritonsillar abscess.

PLAB application: Recognise uncomplicated sore throat versus deep infection or threatened airway.

History

Ask onset, fever, cough, rhinorrhoea, painful swallowing, oral intake, sick contacts, recurrent episodes and antibiotic allergies.

PLAB application: Ask about unilateral pain, voice change, trismus, drooling and breathing difficulty.

Examination

Assess observations, hydration, neck nodes, mouth opening, tonsillar swelling or exudate and uvula position.

PLAB application: Do not force examination in suspected airway compromise; summon senior help.

Viral pharyngitis

Cough, coryza, hoarseness and conjunctivitis favour viral infection, though no single feature is definitive.

PLAB application: Provide symptom relief rather than routine antibiotics.

Group A streptococcal infection

Streptococcus pyogenes may cause fever, tender anterior cervical nodes, tonsillar exudate and absence of cough.

PLAB application: Use clinical scoring and overall assessment; exudate alone does not prove bacterial infection.

FeverPAIN score

One point each: fever in previous 24 hours; purulence; attend rapidly within 3 days; severely inflamed tonsils; no cough or coryza.

PLAB application: NICE NG84: 0–1 generally no antibiotic; 2–3 no or back-up; 4–5 consider immediate or back-up.

Centor score

One point each: tonsillar exudate, tender anterior cervical lymphadenopathy, fever history and absence of cough.

PLAB application: NICE NG84: 0–2 usually no antibiotic; 3–4 consider immediate or back-up.

Supportive care

Encourage fluids, rest and suitable paracetamol or ibuprofen. Discuss likely course: symptoms often last around a week.

PLAB application: Give safety-netting for deterioration, dehydration or new focal symptoms.

Antibiotic prescribing

When an antibiotic is indicated, NICE NG84 usually recommends phenoxymethylpenicillin as first choice, subject to age, allergy and local guidance.

PLAB application: Use current NICE/BNF for dose, duration, pregnancy and alternatives; avoid unnecessary antibiotics.

Quinsy presentation

Peritonsillar abscess often causes severe unilateral throat pain, fever, muffled hot-potato voice, trismus, drooling and uvula deviation away from affected side.

PLAB application: Not all cases show every sign; suspected quinsy warrants same-day urgent ENT assessment.

Quinsy management

Assess airway and hydration; obtain urgent ENT input. Management may require drainage, antibiotics, analgesia and fluids.

PLAB application: Do not attempt drainage without appropriate training and setting.

Airway emergencies

Stridor, inability to swallow saliva, respiratory distress or rapidly progressing neck swelling requires emergency airway assessment.

PLAB application: Consider epiglottitis and deep neck space infection; do not delay escalation for routine throat swabs.

Infectious mononucleosis

EBV can cause prolonged sore throat, marked fatigue, posterior cervical lymphadenopathy and splenomegaly.

PLAB application: Consider EBV testing in the right context; counsel on avoiding contact sports if splenomegaly is present.

Complications

Tonsillitis may be complicated by peritonsillar abscess, dehydration or rare deep neck infections. Scarlet fever can accompany group A streptococcal infection.

PLAB application: Recognise neck swelling, systemic toxicity and signs of sepsis.

Recurrent tonsillitis

Document frequency, severity and functional impact. Tonsillectomy decisions follow specialist criteria and shared decision-making.

PLAB application: Discuss surgical bleeding risk and expected postoperative pain when relevant.

PLAB 2 consultation

Explain most sore throats improve without antibiotics; explore expectations, provide pain relief and a clear return plan.

PLAB application: Safety-net for drooling, breathing difficulty, inability to drink, severe unilateral pain, trismus or worsening illness.

PLAB 2: sore throat counselling

  1. Check identity, consent, symptoms and expectations.
  2. Ask about fever, cough, oral intake, voice change, trismus and breathing difficulty.
  3. Explain viral versus bacterial causes and scoring-guided antibiotic choices.
  4. Offer analgesia, fluids and shared decision-making.
  5. Safety-net for stridor, drooling, inability to swallow or rapidly worsening illness.

Deep Dive

FeverPAIN

Five features help estimate streptococcal likelihood and inform NICE NG84 antibiotic decisions; scores do not replace clinical judgement.

Why quinsy causes trismus

Inflammation near the medial pterygoid muscle may limit jaw opening.

Quinsy anatomy

A peritonsillar abscess forms between the tonsillar capsule and adjacent pharyngeal muscle, often producing asymmetric swelling.

Antibiotic stewardship

Antibiotics have modest average symptom benefit in uncomplicated sore throat and can cause adverse effects and resistance.

EBV differential

Mononucleosis may present with prominent fatigue, posterior cervical lymphadenopathy and splenic enlargement.

Airway safety

Drooling, stridor and rapidly worsening swelling suggest threatened airway and require immediate escalation.
