Urinary Tract Infections & Pyelonephritis

PLAB focus: Differentiate cystitis from pyelonephritis, recognise sepsis and infected obstruction, and use antibiotics responsibly.

01. Clinical overview

UTIs include lower urinary tract infection (cystitis), upper UTI (pyelonephritis) and infection associated with catheters or urinary tract abnormalities. Asymptomatic bacteriuria is not the same as symptomatic infection.

PLAB application: Differentiate localised lower symptoms from systemic infection.

02. Lower UTI symptoms

Dysuria, new frequency, urgency, suprapubic pain and sometimes haematuria suggest cystitis. Vaginal discharge or irritation suggests alternative diagnoses such as vaginitis or STI.

PLAB application: Use symptoms and patient context, not dipstick alone.

03. Pyelonephritis presentation

Fever, chills, flank or loin pain, costovertebral angle tenderness, nausea or vomiting with urinary symptoms suggest upper UTI. Urinary symptoms may be absent.

PLAB application: Systemic illness needs urgent assessment.

04. Red flags and sepsis

Hypotension, confusion, tachypnoea, reduced urine output, rigors or severe illness suggest sepsis. Obstruction plus infection is a urological emergency.

PLAB application: ABCDE assessment and urgent escalation when unstable.

05. History and examination

Ask about pregnancy, sex, previous cultures, recent antibiotics, catheter, stones, diabetes, immunosuppression, renal impairment and urinary retention. Check observations, hydration, abdomen and flanks.

PLAB application: Ask about pregnancy where relevant before choosing treatment.

06. Diagnosis in women under 65

NICE guidance uses combinations of key urinary symptoms and exclusion of vaginal symptoms to support diagnosis in non-pregnant women under 65. Dipstick testing is selective and interpretation depends on context.

PLAB application: Avoid diagnosing UTI from cloudy or smelly urine alone.

07. Urine culture indications

Send culture for suspected pyelonephritis, pregnancy, men with UTI, recurrent or complicated infection, treatment failure and when indicated by local guidance. Obtain samples before antibiotics where feasible without delaying urgent treatment.

PLAB application: Use previous microbiology and susceptibility results.

08. Lower UTI antibiotics

Follow current NICE/local antimicrobial guidance, allergies, pregnancy status and renal function. Nitrofurantoin is a common first choice for uncomplicated lower UTI when renal function permits; it does not treat pyelonephritis.

PLAB application: Do not use nitrofurantoin for renal parenchymal infection.

09. Pyelonephritis treatment

Assess need for admission, hydration and ability to take oral medication. Use guideline-based oral or IV antibiotics depending on severity, resistance risk, pregnancy and renal function; review cultures and step down when appropriate.

PLAB application: Severely ill patients need prompt IV treatment and monitoring.

10. UTI in pregnancy

Pregnant patients with symptomatic UTI require urine culture and appropriate antibiotic therapy. Suspected pyelonephritis in pregnancy generally warrants hospital assessment. Asymptomatic bacteriuria is screened for and treated in pregnancy.

PLAB application: Check pregnancy-specific drug safety and follow-up.

11. UTI in men

Consider prostate involvement, retention, stones or other structural causes. Send urine culture before antibiotics. Fever, perineal pain or obstructive symptoms raise concern for acute prostatitis.

PLAB application: Nitrofurantoin is unsuitable if prostate involvement is suspected.

12. Catheter-associated UTI

Catheters frequently cause bacteriuria without infection. Treat symptomatic infection rather than positive culture alone; review catheter need and replace or remove when indicated, without delaying antibiotics in severe illness.

PLAB application: Never equate catheter bacteriuria with CAUTI automatically.

13. Recurrent UTI

Repeated symptomatic episodes require review of diagnosis, cultures, triggers and risk factors. Consider behavioural measures, vaginal oestrogen in eligible peri/postmenopausal patients and preventive options through shared decision-making.

PLAB application: Avoid repeated empirical antibiotics without reassessment.

14. Obstruction, stones and imaging

Fever with an obstructed urinary system can progress rapidly to sepsis and requires urgent urology input for drainage. Consider imaging if obstruction, stones, poor response or atypical presentation is suspected.

PLAB application: Infected obstructed kidney is a source-control emergency.

15. Complications and differential

Complications include bacteraemia, renal or perinephric abscess, AKI and sepsis. Differentials include STI, vaginitis, interstitial cystitis, renal colic, appendicitis and prostatitis.

PLAB application: Persistent symptoms despite treatment require reassessment.

16. PLAB 2 counselling and safety-netting

Explain diagnosis, antibiotic use and adherence, hydration, analgesia when safe, culture follow-up and return precautions. Seek urgent help for fever, flank pain, vomiting, confusion, worsening symptoms or inability to pass urine.

PLAB application: Use empathetic, non-stigmatising explanations and clear safety-netting.

Deep Dive

Ascending infection

Most UTIs arise from ascending uropathogens, often E. coli; host factors and urinary stasis increase risk.

Pyelonephritis physiology

Infection reaches renal pelvis and parenchyma, producing fever, inflammation and potentially bacteraemia.

Why nitrofurantoin differs

High urine concentrations make nitrofurantoin useful for lower UTI, but it does not achieve adequate tissue levels for pyelonephritis.

Asymptomatic bacteriuria

Bacteria in urine without urinary symptoms should not automatically trigger antibiotics; exceptions include pregnancy and certain invasive urological procedures.

Antimicrobial stewardship

Narrow therapy where possible, review culture results, check renal dosing and avoid unnecessary treatment.

PLAB 2 OSCE framework

Ask about urinary and systemic symptoms, pregnancy, sex-related concerns, risk factors, hydration and red flags; explain tests, medicines and safety-netting.
