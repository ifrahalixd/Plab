Sinusitis & Allergic Rhinitis

Emergency: Proptosis, diplopia, reduced vision, meningism or altered consciousness with sinusitis needs urgent hospital assessment.

Overview and definitions

Acute rhinosinusitis is inflammation of the nose and paranasal sinuses, often after a viral upper respiratory infection. Allergic rhinitis is an IgE-mediated inflammatory response to allergens.

PLAB application: Differentiate acute infection from chronic allergic symptoms.

Sinus anatomy

The maxillary, frontal, ethmoid and sphenoid sinuses drain into the nasal cavity. Obstruction of drainage contributes to facial pressure and discharge.

PLAB application: Orbital and intracranial complications are anatomically possible, particularly with ethmoid/frontal disease.

Acute sinusitis symptoms

Nasal blockage, discharge, facial pain or pressure and reduced smell are typical. Symptoms commonly follow a cold.

PLAB application: Pain alone is not diagnostic; ask about nasal symptoms and duration.

Viral versus bacterial features

Most acute sinusitis is viral. Bacterial disease is more likely with prolonged symptoms, purulent discharge, severe unilateral pain, fever or marked deterioration after initial improvement.

PLAB application: No single feature proves bacterial infection.

Sinusitis examination

Assess observations and general appearance; inspect the nose, face, eyes and oral cavity. Look for unilateral swelling, orbital signs and neurological abnormalities.

PLAB application: Routine sinus imaging is not needed for uncomplicated acute sinusitis.

NICE NG79: symptoms 10 days or less

For symptoms lasting 10 days or less, NICE recommends not offering antibiotics in uncomplicated cases. Explain the usual course of 2–3 weeks.

PLAB application: Offer symptom relief and clear safety-net advice.

NICE NG79: symptoms over 10 days

If symptoms persist without improvement for over 10 days, consider a high-dose nasal corticosteroid for 14 days in eligible patients aged 12 or over, and consider no or back-up antibiotic prescribing depending on clinical findings.

PLAB application: Check current NICE guidance for exact eligibility and prescribing.

When immediate antibiotics or referral matter

Systemically very unwell people, signs of serious illness or high risk of complications may require immediate antibiotics and/or urgent assessment.

PLAB application: Do not delay emergency referral for orbital or intracranial red flags.

Orbital and intracranial red flags

Periorbital swelling or redness, proptosis, double vision, reduced visual acuity, severe frontal headache, meningism, focal neurological signs or altered consciousness are emergencies.

PLAB application: Suspected orbital cellulitis or intracranial extension needs hospital assessment.

Allergic rhinitis presentation

Sneezing, nasal itch, clear watery rhinorrhoea, congestion and itchy/watery eyes suggest allergic rhinitis. Symptoms may be seasonal or perennial.

PLAB application: Ask about pollen, dust mites, pets, mould and occupational triggers.

Allergic rhinitis assessment

Explore timing, sleep, work/school impact, asthma, eczema, medications and trigger exposure. Examination may reveal pale oedematous turbinates.

PLAB application: A unilateral persistent obstruction or blood-stained discharge warrants evaluation for other causes.

Allergic rhinitis treatment

Allergen avoidance where practical, saline rinses, non-sedating oral antihistamines and intranasal corticosteroids are common options. Intranasal corticosteroids are particularly useful for persistent or moderate/severe symptoms.

PLAB application: Demonstrate correct nasal spray technique and explain that regular use may take days for full benefit.

Nasal spray technique

Gently blow the nose, keep head slightly forward and direct the nozzle away from the nasal septum toward the outer wall; sniff gently.

PLAB application: Correct technique improves effectiveness and reduces irritation or epistaxis.

Rhinitis differential diagnoses

Consider non-allergic rhinitis, rhinitis medicamentosa from prolonged topical decongestant use, nasal polyps, structural obstruction, foreign body and chronic rhinosinusitis.

PLAB application: Persistent unilateral symptoms, recurrent bleeding or facial mass need specialist consideration.

Chronic rhinosinusitis and nasal polyps

Symptoms lasting 12 weeks or more suggest chronic rhinosinusitis. Nasal polyps may cause obstruction and smell loss. Management often includes saline and intranasal corticosteroids with ENT review when indicated.

PLAB application: Do not repeatedly prescribe antibiotics for chronic symptoms without evidence of acute bacterial infection.

PLAB 2 counselling and safety-netting

Clarify duration, fever, discharge, facial pain, allergy triggers, asthma, visual symptoms and prior treatments. Explain why antibiotics are often unnecessary, discuss spray technique and advise when to seek help.

PLAB application: Urgent review for eye swelling, visual changes, severe headache, neurological symptoms or marked deterioration.

PLAB 2 OSCE: nasal symptoms

  1. Introduce yourself and confirm consent.
  2. Explore duration, discharge, blockage, facial pain, itch, sneezing, triggers, fever and impact.
  3. Ask about visual changes, swelling, severe headache and neurological symptoms.
  4. Examine observations, nose, face and eyes appropriately.
  5. Explain likely cause, antibiotic stewardship, nasal spray technique and safety-netting.

Deep Dive

Viral sinus inflammation

Inflammatory oedema obstructs sinus drainage after URTI. Symptoms may persist despite no bacterial infection.

Allergic immune response

Allergen-triggered IgE and mast-cell mediators cause itch, sneezing and watery secretions; later inflammation contributes to congestion.

Antibiotic stewardship

NICE NG79 avoids antibiotics in most uncomplicated acute sinusitis. Severe illness or complications change management.

Orbital complications

Ethmoid sinus infection can spread toward the orbit. Visual changes, ophthalmoplegia or proptosis require emergency assessment.

Nasal corticosteroids

Regular topical anti-inflammatory treatment is important for persistent allergic rhinitis. Aim the spray away from the septum.

Chronic symptoms

Symptoms for 12 weeks or more warrant consideration of chronic rhinosinusitis, polyps and other causes of obstruction.
