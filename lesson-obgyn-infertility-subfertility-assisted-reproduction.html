Infertility, Subfertility & Assisted Reproduction

Urgent safety-net: Severe abdominal pain, breathlessness, rapid abdominal swelling or reduced urine output after fertility stimulation may indicate severe ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome. Severe pain or collapse after a positive pregnancy test requires urgent assessment for ectopic pregnancy.

Definitions and scope

Infertility is failure to conceive after regular unprotected intercourse; assessment is generally offered after 1 year, earlier for known causes or when the woman is 36 or older. Subfertility describes reduced fertility rather than absolute inability to conceive.

PLAB application: Avoid blame; investigate both partners together.

Initial consultation

Ask duration of trying, frequency of intercourse, previous pregnancies and outcomes, menstrual regularity, sexual function, contraception, medical and surgical history, medications, lifestyle and psychosocial impact.

PLAB application: Ask about both partners and preferred language sensitively.

When to refer early

Offer earlier specialist assessment for age 36 or over, amenorrhoea, known tubal disease, endometriosis, previous pelvic infection, previous chemotherapy, or known male-factor infertility.

PLAB application: Do not insist on waiting 12 months where a known risk factor exists.

Ovulation and cycles

Regular cycles usually suggest ovulation but do not prove it. Irregular or absent cycles suggest ovulatory dysfunction such as PCOS, hypothalamic causes, thyroid dysfunction or hyperprolactinaemia.

PLAB application: Assess pregnancy possibility and hormonal causes when periods are absent.

Confirming ovulation

Mid-luteal serum progesterone can support ovulation assessment; for a 28-day cycle it is often measured around day 21, but in longer cycles take it approximately 7 days before the expected period.

PLAB application: Do not order day-21 progesterone for every cycle length.

Male-factor assessment

Semen analysis assesses concentration, motility and morphology. Repeat an abnormal test according to guidance, usually after about 3 months unless a severe abnormality warrants earlier review.

PLAB application: A normal male sexual history does not exclude abnormal semen parameters.

Tubal and uterine factors

Previous pelvic inflammatory disease, chlamydia, ectopic pregnancy or pelvic surgery may damage tubes. Hysterosalpingography or HyCoSy may assess patency in appropriate patients; laparoscopy is selected for suspected pelvic pathology.

PLAB application: Screen for relevant infection before uterine instrumentation per local policy.

Ovarian reserve

AMH and antral follicle count can inform likely ovarian response to stimulation, not reliably predict spontaneous conception. Age remains central to counselling.

PLAB application: Never present AMH as a definitive fertility or pregnancy test.

Preconception optimisation

Advise folic acid, smoking cessation, avoidance of recreational drugs, medication review, healthy weight, appropriate vaccinations and management of diabetes or thyroid disease. Discuss alcohol guidance.

PLAB application: Offer supportive, non-judgemental advice to both partners.

Timing intercourse

Intercourse every 2–3 days optimises the chance of conception without requiring precise ovulation tracking. Discuss timing and sexual difficulties without creating pressure.

PLAB application: Provide practical, reassuring counselling.

Ovulation induction

In anovulatory PCOS, letrozole is commonly recommended first-line for ovulation induction in current international guidance, with specialist oversight and local licensing considerations. Other options depend on cause.

PLAB application: Exclude pregnancy and discuss multiple pregnancy risk with stimulation.

Intrauterine insemination

IUI places prepared sperm in the uterus around ovulation. It may be appropriate in selected situations, including some donor-sperm pathways; routine IUI is not generally offered for unexplained infertility in NICE guidance.

PLAB application: Explain that treatment selection depends on the cause and local eligibility.

IVF and ICSI

IVF stimulates ovaries, retrieves oocytes and fertilises them in the laboratory; embryos are transferred into the uterus. ICSI injects a sperm into an egg and may be used for significant male-factor problems.

PLAB application: Explain stages, consent, success variability and emotional burden.

Assisted reproduction risks

Discuss ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS), multiple pregnancy, ectopic pregnancy, procedure-related complications and psychological stress. Single embryo transfer can reduce multiple pregnancy risk.

PLAB application: Severe abdominal pain, rapid swelling, breathlessness or low urine output after stimulation requires urgent review.

NHS access and consent

NICE recommendations for IVF access are not identical to local NHS commissioning criteria, which vary by area. Consent, storage, donor implications and parenthood issues require specialist discussion under HFEA regulation.

PLAB application: Do not promise NHS-funded IVF based solely on NICE recommendations.

PLAB 2 consultation and follow-up

Acknowledge distress; use open questions, ask about both partners, explore ICE, explain tests and likely referral timing, agree a plan, address mental wellbeing and offer safety-netting.

PLAB application: Use inclusive language and avoid implying that infertility is the woman’s responsibility.

PLAB 2 — Infertility consultation

Introduce yourself, confirm consent and privacy, explore both partners’ concerns and duration of trying, menstrual and sexual history, previous pregnancies, medical conditions, medications and lifestyle. Ask about psychological impact and expectations. Explain shared investigations and possible early referral, discuss fertility treatment options without promising success or funding, and agree follow-up.

Deep Dive

Why fertility declines with age

Oocyte number and quality decline with age, affecting chance of conception and aneuploidy risk. Age-based referral is important, but individual prognosis varies.

Interpreting semen analysis

Semen values vary with illness, abstinence and collection technique. Interpret against current laboratory reference ranges; a single abnormal result does not always establish permanent male infertility.

Ovarian reserve versus fertility

AMH and antral follicle count can help estimate response to ovarian stimulation; they should not be used as stand-alone tests of ability to conceive naturally.

Ovulation induction and multiple gestation

Monitoring aims to improve ovulation while limiting multiple pregnancy and OHSS. Letrozole use should follow specialist protocols and current guidance.

IVF, ICSI and OHSS

IVF includes controlled stimulation, egg retrieval, fertilisation, embryo culture and transfer. ICSI is a laboratory fertilisation technique, often for male-factor problems. Severe OHSS may cause ascites, thrombosis and renal injury.

UK access, consent and psychosocial care

NICE fertility guidance informs clinical recommendations, but actual NHS-funded access is subject to local commissioning rules. HFEA regulates licensed fertility treatment. Counselling should acknowledge uncertainty, costs and emotional effects.
