Strabismus, Diplopia & Cranial Nerve Palsies

Emergency: New third nerve palsy, acute diplopia with neurological signs, or painful ophthalmoplegia needs urgent assessment.

Definitions

Strabismus is ocular misalignment. Diplopia is seeing two images of one object. Binocular diplopia resolves when either eye is covered; monocular diplopia persists when the affected eye alone views the target.

First question in diplopia

Establish monocular versus binocular diplopia, onset, direction, variability, pain, headache, trauma, neurological symptoms and vascular risk factors.

Emergency red flags

Acute diplopia with headache, pupil-involving third nerve palsy, other neurological deficits, painful ophthalmoplegia, proptosis or reduced consciousness needs emergency assessment.

Ocular alignment examination

Check visual acuity, pupils, ptosis, ocular position, corneal light reflex, cover-uncover test and eye movements in an H pattern. Assess for nystagmus and proptosis.

Cover-uncover test

Cover one eye while observing the uncovered eye for refixation. A refixation movement suggests manifest deviation (tropia). Alternate cover testing can reveal latent deviation (phoria).

Cranial nerve III anatomy

The oculomotor nerve supplies most extraocular muscles, levator palpebrae and parasympathetic pupillary fibres. A palsy may cause ptosis, a down-and-out eye and diplopia.

Third nerve palsy red flag

A new third nerve palsy, especially with pupil dilation or severe headache, may indicate a posterior communicating artery aneurysm. Urgent emergency neurovascular assessment is required; pupil sparing does not reliably exclude aneurysm.

Cranial nerve IV palsy

Trochlear nerve palsy weakens superior oblique. Vertical or torsional diplopia is often worse when looking down, such as on stairs; head tilt may compensate.

Cranial nerve VI palsy

Abducens nerve palsy weakens lateral rectus, causing impaired abduction and horizontal binocular diplopia, often worse looking towards the affected side or at distance.

Raised intracranial pressure

A sixth nerve palsy can be a false-localising sign of raised intracranial pressure. Ask about headache, vomiting and transient visual obscurations; inspect optic discs.

Internuclear ophthalmoplegia

A lesion of the medial longitudinal fasciculus causes impaired adduction on horizontal gaze, often with abducting nystagmus of the opposite eye. Causes include multiple sclerosis and stroke.

Myasthenia gravis

Fluctuating ptosis and variable diplopia, often worse with fatigue, suggest ocular myasthenia. Screen for dysphagia, dysarthria and respiratory weakness.

Thyroid eye disease

Restrictive extraocular muscle disease can cause diplopia with proptosis, lid retraction and exposure symptoms. Reduced vision or colour vision may indicate compressive optic neuropathy.

Childhood strabismus

Children may suppress one image rather than complain of diplopia. Persistent squint can cause amblyopia and impaired binocular vision; early orthoptic/ophthalmic assessment matters.

Differential diagnoses

Monocular diplopia may arise from refractive error, dry eye, cataract or corneal irregularity. Binocular diplopia suggests misalignment from neurological, muscular or orbital disease.

PLAB 2 communication

Explain examination and findings, ask about driving and falls, advise against driving with uncontrolled diplopia, and arrange referral based on urgency. Do not offer an eye patch as a substitute for emergency assessment.

PLAB 2 — Diplopia consultation

Confirm identity and consent. Ask onset, monocular/binocular symptoms, headache, ptosis, trauma, neurological signs and functional impact. Examine acuity, pupils, cover test and extraocular movements. Explain emergency referral when red flags are present; discuss driving safety and follow-up.

Deep Dive

Extraocular muscle actions

CN III supplies medial, superior and inferior recti plus inferior oblique; CN IV supplies superior oblique; CN VI supplies lateral rectus.

Why diplopia disappears on covering an eye

Binocular diplopia requires discordant images from two eyes; removing one image eliminates double vision.

Aneurysmal CN III palsy

Compression of the third nerve by an aneurysm can threaten life. Urgent imaging decisions belong to emergency specialist teams.

Internuclear ophthalmoplegia

The MLF connects abducens and oculomotor nuclei to coordinate conjugate gaze.

Paediatric visual development

Suppression of a deviated eye can produce amblyopia if misalignment is not addressed early.

OSCE examination

Describe each test, check comfort, and document pupil sizes, eye movements and diplopia direction.
