Thalassaemia & Haemoglobinopathies

PLAB safety: Do not assume all microcytosis is iron deficiency; severe anaemia or acute deterioration needs urgent assessment.

01. Overview and definitions

Thalassaemias are inherited disorders with reduced alpha- or beta-globin synthesis. Haemoglobinopathies also include structural variants such as HbS, HbC and HbE.

PLAB focus: Distinguish impaired globin production from abnormal globin structure.

02. Haemoglobin structure and genetics

Adult HbA is alpha2 beta2, HbA2 is alpha2 delta2, and HbF is alpha2 gamma2. Alpha genes are on chromosome 16 and beta-globin genes on chromosome 11.

PLAB focus: Interpret electrophoresis in context of age and recent transfusion.

03. Alpha-thalassaemia spectrum

One deleted alpha gene is usually silent; two cause trait, three cause HbH disease, and four may cause Hb Bart’s hydrops fetalis.

PLAB focus: Severity relates broadly to the number and type of affected alpha genes.

04. Beta-thalassaemia spectrum

Beta-thalassaemia trait is usually mild; intermedia/non-transfusion-dependent disease is variable; severe transfusion-dependent disease commonly presents in infancy.

PLAB focus: Beta-thalassaemia major can present after HbF declines in infancy.

05. Clinical manifestations

Symptoms range from no symptoms to pallor, jaundice, poor growth, splenomegaly, skeletal changes, gallstones and complications of chronic haemolysis.

PLAB focus: Assess growth, transfusion history and iron overload complications.

06. Microcytosis differential

Thalassaemia trait commonly causes low MCV with relatively preserved red-cell count; iron deficiency also causes microcytosis. Check ferritin and iron studies rather than assuming.

PLAB focus: Do not prescribe long-term iron unless iron deficiency is demonstrated.

07. Blood film and first investigations

Request FBC, reticulocytes, film, ferritin and haemoglobin analysis. Target cells and microcytosis may be present; severe disease may show nucleated red cells.

PLAB focus: A blood film suggests a diagnosis but does not establish genotype.

08. Haemoglobin analysis

Beta-thalassaemia trait often has raised HbA2; HbF may rise. Alpha-thalassaemia trait can have normal electrophoresis and need molecular testing.

PLAB focus: Iron deficiency may alter HbA2 interpretation; specialist interpretation matters.

09. Carrier testing and reproductive counselling

Discuss autosomal recessive inheritance, partner screening, reproductive choices and referral for genetic counselling. UK antenatal and newborn screening pathways are important.

PLAB focus: If both parents carry relevant variants, explain pregnancy-specific risk without assuming a single genotype.

10. Transfusion-dependent thalassaemia

Regular red-cell transfusions may support growth and suppress ineffective erythropoiesis. Care plans include phenotype matching, surveillance and specialist coordination.

PLAB focus: Monitor for transfusion reactions, alloimmunisation and iron overload.

11. Iron overload and chelation

Repeated transfusions and increased intestinal absorption may cause iron loading, damaging heart, liver and endocrine organs. Chelation is specialist-directed and monitored.

PLAB focus: Ferritin trends and MRI-based organ iron assessments guide care.

12. Non-transfusion-dependent thalassaemia

Patients may have anaemia, splenomegaly, thrombosis, pulmonary hypertension or iron overload despite infrequent transfusions.

PLAB focus: Avoid equating lack of transfusion with absence of complications.

13. HbE, HbC and compound states

HbE can cause mild microcytosis alone but HbE/beta-thalassaemia ranges from mild to severe. HbSC disease is a form of sickle cell disease with vaso-occlusive risks.

PLAB focus: Interpret the exact genotype and clinical phenotype, not only the variant name.

14. Complications and emergency signs

Severe symptomatic anaemia, infection, acute haemolysis, cardiac decompensation or new chest and neurological symptoms need prompt assessment.

PLAB focus: Escalate acute deterioration and contact the patient’s specialist team.

15. Long-term multidisciplinary care

Follow growth, bone health, endocrine function, liver and cardiac status, infection risk, vaccination and psychosocial needs. Some patients may be eligible for curative therapies.

PLAB focus: Management and treatment eligibility are specialist and patient specific.

16. PLAB 2 counselling and safety-netting

Explore symptoms, family origins, pregnancy plans, previous tests and transfusions; explain inherited disease non-judgementally, arrange appropriate testing and shared decisions.

PLAB focus: Explain carrier status separately from disease and offer partner screening.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Ineffective erythropoiesis

Globin-chain imbalance damages developing erythroblasts and contributes to anaemia and marrow expansion.

Why microcytosis occurs

Reduced haemoglobin synthesis produces smaller erythrocytes, sometimes despite a relatively high RBC count.

HbA2 interpretation

Raised HbA2 supports beta-thalassaemia trait, but coexisting iron deficiency and variant haemoglobins may complicate results.

Iron without transfusions

Suppressed hepcidin in ineffective erythropoiesis can increase intestinal iron absorption.

Genetic complexity

Deletional and non-deletional alpha variants and beta mutations influence severity; genotype-phenotype correlation is imperfect.

Organ monitoring

Cardiac and liver MRI, endocrine screening and chelation monitoring help prevent cumulative complications.
