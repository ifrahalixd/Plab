Obstetric & Gynaecological Emergencies: Ectopic Pregnancy, Eclampsia, Postpartum Haemorrhage & Ovarian Torsion

Emergency red flags: collapse with possible pregnancy, severe pelvic pain, seizures in pregnancy or postpartum, heavy postpartum bleeding, severe headache with hypertension, or suspected ovarian torsion need immediate escalation.

Initial approach to obstetric emergencies

Use ABCDE, summon senior obstetric/anaesthetic support, check vital signs and establish IV access. Consider pregnancy in reproductive-age patients with abdominal pain, bleeding or collapse.

PLAB application: Resuscitation and diagnosis happen simultaneously.

Pregnancy testing and localisation

Offer a pregnancy test when pregnancy is possible, including with contraception or reported bleeding. A positive test with pain or bleeding requires assessment of pregnancy location and stability.

PLAB application: A negative urine test does not override high clinical concern; seek specialist advice.

Ectopic pregnancy presentation

Ectopic pregnancy may cause unilateral pelvic pain, vaginal bleeding, shoulder-tip pain, syncope or shock; rupture can occur without heavy vaginal bleeding.

PLAB application: Unstable suspected ectopic pregnancy is a surgical emergency.

Ectopic investigations

Stable patients generally need transvaginal ultrasound and serial serum hCG where location remains uncertain. A pregnancy of unknown location requires follow-up until resolved or localised.

PLAB application: Do not diagnose location from one hCG value alone.

Ectopic management

Choose expectant, methotrexate or surgical management under NICE NG126 based on stability, scan findings, hCG, pain and follow-up reliability. Rupture or instability needs immediate operative review.

PLAB application: Do not give methotrexate without specialist assessment and appropriate eligibility.

Miscarriage and heavy early-pregnancy bleeding

Assess bleeding volume, pain, tissue passage, infection and haemodynamic status. Exclude ectopic pregnancy; ultrasound helps classify miscarriage. Provide sensitive explanations and follow-up.

PLAB application: Heavy bleeding, collapse or fever warrants emergency assessment.

Pre-eclampsia recognition

New hypertension after 20 weeks with proteinuria or maternal organ dysfunction, sometimes with severe headache, visual symptoms, epigastric pain or abnormal liver/renal tests.

PLAB application: Proteinuria is not essential when other organ dysfunction is present.

Eclampsia emergency

Eclampsia is seizure activity associated with pre-eclampsia. Protect from injury, call for help, ABCDE, give magnesium sulfate per protocol and manage severe hypertension; plan delivery after stabilisation.

PLAB application: Magnesium sulfate is first-line for eclamptic seizures.

HELLP syndrome

Haemolysis, elevated liver enzymes and low platelets may cause right upper quadrant pain, nausea, malaise and serious bleeding risk. Seek urgent obstetric assessment and blood tests.

PLAB application: Consider HELLP even without dramatic hypertension.

Postpartum haemorrhage recognition

Primary PPH is blood loss of 500 mL or more within 24 hours of birth; major PPH is 1000 mL or more. Clinical instability matters more than estimated volume alone.

PLAB application: Activate the major obstetric haemorrhage pathway early.

PPH: the four Ts

Consider Tone (uterine atony), Trauma (genital tract injury), Tissue (retained placenta/products) and Thrombin (coagulopathy). Examine and treat the cause with specialist support.

PLAB application: Uterine atony is a common cause of primary PPH.

PPH immediate management

Call obstetric, anaesthetic and blood bank teams; ABCDE, uterine massage if atony, uterotonics, tranexamic acid promptly as indicated, IV access, blood products and definitive haemorrhage control.

PLAB application: Reassess continuously; follow local obstetric massive-haemorrhage protocol.

Ovarian torsion

Sudden severe unilateral pelvic pain, often with vomiting, can result from adnexal torsion. Ultrasound can help but normal Doppler flow does not exclude torsion.

PLAB application: Urgent gynaecological surgical assessment; avoid delay for inconclusive imaging.

Ruptured ovarian cyst and pelvic infection

Ruptured cyst can cause sudden pelvic pain and sometimes haemoperitoneum. Pelvic inflammatory disease can cause pain, discharge, fever and cervical motion tenderness; consider tubo-ovarian abscess and sepsis.

PLAB application: Always exclude pregnancy-related emergencies first.

Other obstetric emergencies

Placental abruption may cause pain, uterine tenderness and concealed bleeding; placenta praevia often presents with painless bleeding. Maternal collapse can reflect PE, haemorrhage or sepsis.

PLAB application: Avoid digital vaginal examination with unexplained later-pregnancy bleeding until placenta location is assessed.

PLAB 2 counselling and handover

Use compassionate, nonjudgmental language, explain the need for urgent assessment, obtain consent and relevant obstetric history, address RhD prophylaxis according to guidance, and hand over with SBAR.

PLAB application: Explain uncertainty and give explicit return precautions for pain, bleeding, fainting or seizures.

PLAB 2 — collapsing patient with abdominal pain

Call for help, ABCDE, IV access and bloods, establish whether pregnancy is possible, arrange urgent ultrasound/surgical review without delaying resuscitation, explain concerns empathetically, and give an SBAR handover.

Deep Dive — Obstetric & Gynaecological Emergencies

Ectopic pregnancy and pregnancy of unknown location

Symptoms, ultrasound and serial hCG are interpreted together. A pregnancy of unknown location remains potentially ectopic until confirmed otherwise; clear safety-netting and reliable follow-up are essential.

Magnesium sulfate and seizure safety

Eclampsia management prioritises airway protection, maternal stabilisation, magnesium sulfate under local protocol, monitoring for toxicity and specialist obstetric delivery planning. Treat severe hypertension concurrently.

Haemorrhage physiology in pregnancy

Physiological pregnancy changes can mask shock. Assess clinical perfusion, mental state, heart rate and bleeding trajectory rather than waiting for profound hypotension.

PPH causes and escalation

The four Ts guide rapid targeted treatment. Uterotonics, early tranexamic acid, blood products, balloon tamponade or operative control may be required under the obstetric emergency team.

Why torsion cannot be ruled out by Doppler

Ovarian perfusion can persist with partial/intermittent torsion. Severe typical symptoms need urgent surgical discussion despite reassuring imaging.

Communicating pregnancy loss

Avoid assumptions about feelings or pregnancy intentions. Explain findings plainly, allow questions, offer written follow-up and support resources, and address safeguarding and privacy.
