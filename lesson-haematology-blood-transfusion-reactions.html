Blood Transfusion, Transfusion Reactions & Patient Blood Management

Emergency: If a transfusion reaction is suspected, stop transfusion immediately, assess ABCDE and call for help.

01. Overview

Transfusion can be lifesaving but has risks. Use blood components only when clinically indicated and consider alternatives.

PLAB application: Check the indication and urgency.

02. Blood components

Red cells improve oxygen-carrying capacity; platelets support haemostasis; plasma replaces multiple coagulation factors; cryoprecipitate supplies fibrinogen.

PLAB application: Choose the component for the deficit.

03. Anaemia assessment

Assess symptoms, bleeding, haemodynamics, Hb trend and cause. Treat iron, B12 or folate deficiency where appropriate rather than automatically transfusing.

PLAB application: A number alone is not the whole decision.

04. Red-cell thresholds

NICE NG24 generally advises a restrictive red-cell threshold of 70 g/L in patients who do not have major haemorrhage, acute coronary syndrome or need regular transfusions; target 70–90 g/L after transfusion.

PLAB application: Use individualised thresholds in special situations.

05. Acute coronary syndrome

NICE NG24 recommends considering an 80 g/L threshold and 80–100 g/L target for patients with acute coronary syndrome; check current specialist/local guidance.

PLAB application: Do not apply a universal 70 g/L threshold.

06. Single-unit policy

For non-bleeding adults, give one red-cell unit then reassess clinically and recheck haemoglobin before considering another.

PLAB application: Document response and symptoms.

07. Compatibility

Confirm identity, blood group, antibody screen and compatibility testing. ABO-incompatible transfusion can cause catastrophic haemolysis.

PLAB application: Correct patient identification is essential.

08. Consent and bedside checks

Explain indication, expected benefits, alternatives and significant risks. Obtain and document valid consent where possible. Follow local bedside identity and product checks.

PLAB application: Never bypass checks for convenience.

09. Monitoring

Record baseline observations and monitor during transfusion per local policy, particularly early after starting. Watch for fever, rigors, rash, dyspnoea, hypotension and back pain.

PLAB application: React immediately to new symptoms.

10. Acute haemolytic reaction

ABO incompatibility may cause fever, chills, flank/back pain, hypotension, haemoglobinuria and DIC. Stop transfusion and urgently escalate.

PLAB application: Maintain IV access with normal saline using new tubing.

11. Febrile and allergic reactions

Fever/rigors can be febrile non-haemolytic reaction or infection; urticaria may be allergic. Anaphylaxis causes airway/breathing/circulatory compromise.

PLAB application: Never assume fever is benign before assessment.

12. TACO and TRALI

Transfusion-associated circulatory overload (TACO) causes fluid overload; TRALI is acute non-cardiogenic lung injury. Both may present with hypoxaemia and breathlessness.

PLAB application: Stop transfusion and assess ABCDE.

13. Sepsis and delayed reactions

Bacterial contamination can cause fever, shock and sepsis. Delayed haemolytic reactions may occur days later with falling Hb and jaundice.

PLAB application: Notify blood bank and investigate.

14. Platelets, plasma and cryoprecipitate

Use according to bleeding, procedure and laboratory context. Do not use fresh frozen plasma simply to correct mildly abnormal clotting tests without bleeding or a procedure.

PLAB application: Follow NICE NG24 and local major haemorrhage protocol.

15. Massive haemorrhage

Control bleeding, activate major haemorrhage protocol, resuscitate and use component therapy guided by clinical and laboratory findings. Prevent hypothermia, hypocalcaemia and acidosis.

PLAB application: Escalate immediately; do not delay life-saving treatment.

16. PLAB 2 communication

Explain why transfusion is proposed, benefits, alternatives, risks and monitoring; invite questions. If a reaction is suspected, stop transfusion, call for help and communicate clearly.

PLAB application: Use shared decision-making and document consent.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Oxygen delivery

Red cells contain haemoglobin, which transports oxygen; transfusion is not a substitute for treating the cause of anaemia.

ABO incompatibility

Recipient antibodies can rapidly destroy incompatible donor red cells, producing haemolysis, shock and renal injury.

TACO versus TRALI

TACO reflects hydrostatic pulmonary oedema from volume overload; TRALI involves acute lung injury not primarily due to circulatory overload.

Why single-unit transfusion

Reassessment after each unit limits unnecessary exposure and permits symptom-guided decisions.

Patient blood management

Optimise haemoglobin and haemostasis, minimise blood loss and avoid unnecessary transfusion.

PLAB 2 emergency script

Stop transfusion, call for help, assess ABCDE, keep IV access with saline/new set, check identity, inform blood bank and document.
