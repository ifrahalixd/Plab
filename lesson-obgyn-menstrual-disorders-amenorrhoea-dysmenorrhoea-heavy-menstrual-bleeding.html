Menstrual Disorders: Amenorrhoea, Dysmenorrhoea & Heavy Menstrual Bleeding

Red flags: Haemodynamic compromise, severe acute pelvic pain, suspected ectopic pregnancy, or significant bleeding with syncope require urgent assessment.

Menstrual physiology

The hypothalamic–pituitary–ovarian axis regulates follicular growth, ovulation and endometrial shedding. Cycle patterns vary with age and life stage.

PLAB application: Ask about cycle length, regularity, bleeding duration, pain and impact on daily life.

Focused menstrual history

Record last menstrual period, onset, frequency, duration, volume, flooding, clots, intermenstrual/postcoital bleeding, dysmenorrhoea, pregnancy possibility, contraception, medication, sexual history and symptoms of anaemia.

PLAB application: Clarify the patient’s priorities, fertility plans and preferences.

Initial assessment and red flags

Assess haemodynamic stability, pregnancy possibility and symptoms of significant blood loss. Severe pain, syncope, positive pregnancy test with bleeding, or haemodynamic compromise needs urgent evaluation.

PLAB application: Always exclude pregnancy in a reproductive-age patient with amenorrhoea or abnormal bleeding when relevant.

Amenorrhoea definitions

Primary amenorrhoea is absence of menarche by age 15, or earlier evaluation if secondary sexual characteristics are absent by 13. Secondary amenorrhoea is cessation of established menstruation for at least 3 months if previously regular or 6 months if irregular. Definitions vary by guidance.

PLAB application: Pregnancy is a common and important first diagnosis to exclude.

Causes of amenorrhoea

Physiological causes include pregnancy, lactation and menopause. Other causes include PCOS, hypothalamic suppression from low energy availability or stress, hyperprolactinaemia, thyroid disease, premature ovarian insufficiency and outflow abnormalities.

PLAB application: Review weight changes, exercise, eating, galactorrhoea, headaches, visual changes and hot flushes.

Amenorrhoea investigations

Start with urine/serum pregnancy test. Depending on history examine TSH, prolactin, FSH/LH, oestradiol, androgen profile and pelvic ultrasound. Interpret hormone results with timing and contraceptive use.

PLAB application: Marked prolactin elevation with headache or visual symptoms warrants specialist assessment.

Polycystic ovary syndrome

PCOS can cause oligomenorrhoea or amenorrhoea, hyperandrogenism and metabolic risk. Diagnosis requires appropriate criteria and exclusion of mimics; ultrasound is not always necessary.

PLAB application: Discuss endometrial protection when periods are infrequent and assess cardiometabolic risk.

Premature ovarian insufficiency

POI is ovarian insufficiency before age 40, usually with irregular/absent periods and elevated FSH on appropriately timed repeat testing; specialist assessment is important.

PLAB application: Discuss fertility, bone health, symptoms and hormone replacement options with specialists.

Dysmenorrhoea

Primary dysmenorrhoea is menstrual pain without pelvic pathology, typically starting around menstruation. Secondary dysmenorrhoea may result from endometriosis, adenomyosis, fibroids or pelvic infection.

PLAB application: Progressive pain, dyspareunia, infertility or pain outside menstruation suggests a secondary cause.

Dysmenorrhoea management

Offer appropriate NSAIDs if safe, heat, and hormonal contraception when suitable. Persistent or atypical pain warrants assessment for endometriosis or other pathology.

PLAB application: Do not dismiss severe menstrual pain as normal; assess its effect on school, work and wellbeing.

Heavy menstrual bleeding definition

HMB is excessive menstrual blood loss that interferes with physical, social, emotional or material quality of life; measured volume is not required for diagnosis.

PLAB application: Ask about flooding, double protection, night changes and iron-deficiency symptoms.

Heavy menstrual bleeding causes

Consider fibroids, adenomyosis, polyps, ovulatory dysfunction, endometrial disease, coagulopathy and medication-related bleeding. Structural pathology may coexist with anaemia.

PLAB application: Bleeding since menarche or a family history of bleeding may suggest a coagulation disorder.

HMB investigations

NICE NG88 recommends full blood count for all with HMB. Do not routinely order serum ferritin or thyroid tests without indication. Choose hysteroscopy or ultrasound based on symptoms, examination and suspected pathology.

PLAB application: Persistent intermenstrual bleeding or risk factors for endometrial pathology require appropriate evaluation.

HMB treatment

For HMB with no identified pathology, fibroids under 3 cm without cavity distortion or suspected/diagnosed adenomyosis, NICE generally recommends LNG-IUS first if acceptable. Alternatives include tranexamic acid, NSAIDs, combined hormonal contraception or cyclical oral progestogens.

PLAB application: Consider contraindications, pregnancy intentions and shared decision-making.

Fibroids and adenomyosis

Fibroids may cause HMB, pressure symptoms and fertility issues. Adenomyosis often causes HMB with painful periods. Ultrasound is useful when examination or symptoms suggest pathology.

PLAB application: Large fibroids, persistent symptoms or failed medical treatment may need gynaecology referral.

PLAB 2 communication and follow-up

Explore ICE, impact on life, safeguarding where relevant, contraception and fertility goals. Explain tests, options, side effects, review timing and emergency signs.

PLAB application: Safety-net for severe bleeding, fainting, new severe pain and pregnancy-related symptoms.

PLAB 2 — Menstrual concerns consultation

Introduce yourself, confirm identity and consent, use open questions, ask about bleeding and pain, pregnancy risk, sexual health, fertility wishes and impact on life. Explain targeted investigations, shared treatment choices and red flags; arrange follow-up.

Deep Dive

Why NSAIDs work

Prostaglandin inhibition reduces uterine contractions and menstrual pain; NSAIDs can also reduce blood loss in suitable patients.

Endometrial safety in PCOS

Prolonged anovulation can increase endometrial hyperplasia risk. Discuss cycle regulation and endometrial protection.

NICE NG88 investigation strategy

Full blood count is recommended for all HMB; ultrasound and outpatient hysteroscopy are selected according to suspected pathology, symptoms and examination.

Distinguishing POI from functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea

POI typically has raised FSH and low oestradiol; hypothalamic suppression usually has low or inappropriately normal gonadotrophins with low oestradiol.

When to escalate

Pregnancy-related pain or bleeding, shock, severe anaemia, suspected malignancy or acute pelvic pathology need timely escalation.
