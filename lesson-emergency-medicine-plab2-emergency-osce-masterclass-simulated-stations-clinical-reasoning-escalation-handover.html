PLAB 2 Emergency OSCE Masterclass: Simulated Stations, Clinical Reasoning, Escalation & Handover

Masterclass rule: Recognise immediate threats, call for help, treat within competence, reassess and hand over. Simulation material is educational and not a substitute for local emergency protocols.

OSCE priorities and station structure

Read the task carefully. Introduce yourself, confirm identity, obtain consent where feasible, check immediate safety and begin ABCDE when the patient is acutely unwell. Verbalise critical actions and call for help early.

PLAB application: Prioritise safety and clinical action over reciting a checklist.

Recognising a deteriorating patient

Use respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, pulse, blood pressure, temperature, mental state and NEWS2 as adjuncts. A normal blood pressure does not exclude shock. Repeat observations after interventions.

PLAB application: Escalate on clinical concern even if an early-warning score is low.

ABCDE demonstration

Airway: patency and obstruction. Breathing: rate, effort, saturation and auscultation. Circulation: pulse, BP, capillary refill and bleeding. Disability: AVPU/GCS, pupils and glucose. Exposure: temperature, rash and injury.

PLAB application: Treat life threats as they are identified; then reassess.

Acute chest pain station

Assess stability, obtain a 12-lead ECG promptly, monitor, establish IV access if indicated and seek senior/cardiology advice. Consider ACS, PE, dissection and pneumothorax; a normal first ECG does not exclude ACS.

PLAB application: Do not delay reperfusion assessment in clear STEMI.

Acute breathlessness station

Assess airway, respiratory effort, SpO2, auscultation and haemodynamic status. Consider asthma, pulmonary oedema, PE, pneumonia and pneumothorax. Give titrated oxygen and escalate respiratory exhaustion.

PLAB application: Suspected unstable tension pneumothorax requires immediate treatment, not imaging delay.

Sepsis and shock station

Identify infection and organ dysfunction; use ABCDE, cultures when feasible without delaying urgent antibiotics, lactate and source control. Give fluids thoughtfully with reassessment and escalate to critical care.

PLAB application: Never use fever as the sole screening requirement for sepsis.

Cardiac arrest station

Recognise unresponsiveness and absent normal breathing; call the resuscitation team, begin CPR and attach a defibrillator. Shock VF/pulseless VT, not PEA/asystole; minimise interruptions and consider reversible causes.

PLAB application: Resume compressions promptly after a shock.

Stroke station

Record exact last-known-well time, perform rapid neurological assessment and check glucose. Activate the stroke pathway for urgent brain imaging and specialist evaluation for reperfusion where appropriate.

PLAB application: Do not give antiplatelets before haemorrhage is excluded.

Seizure and reduced consciousness station

Protect the patient from injury, time the seizure, assess ABCDE and glucose. Convulsive seizures lasting five minutes or more need emergency treatment. Consider poisoning, metabolic problems, infection and intracranial disease.

PLAB application: Do not put objects in the mouth or restrain convulsive movements.

Anaphylaxis station

Recognise rapid airway, breathing or circulation compromise after possible allergen exposure, even without rash. Call for help and administer IM adrenaline promptly per current protocol; position and reassess.

PLAB application: Adrenaline is first-line; antihistamines are not substitutes.

Acute abdomen and GI bleeding station

Assess shock, peritonism, pregnancy possibility and haemorrhage. Provide analgesia, IV access and resuscitation as indicated; arrange urgent surgical or endoscopic input and appropriate imaging after stabilisation.

PLAB application: Always consider ectopic pregnancy when pregnancy is possible.

Paediatric emergency station

Use a child-appropriate ABCDE approach, weight-based prescribing, glucose assessment and early senior help. Communicate with caregivers while prioritising the child’s safety; use paediatric-specific resuscitation guidance.

PLAB application: Hypotension is often a late sign of paediatric shock.

Procedural and prescribing safety

Confirm identity, allergies, indication, contraindications, current weight when relevant, drug, dose, route, frequency and monitoring. Obtain consent, use aseptic technique and recognise limits of competence.

PLAB application: Never invent a dose; consult BNFC/BNF and local protocol.

Communication under pressure

Introduce yourself, explain immediate priorities in plain language, acknowledge concerns, avoid false reassurance and use short updates. If the patient lacks capacity, act according to applicable law and emergency best interests.

PLAB application: Be calm, clear and honest about uncertainty.

Escalation and SBAR handover

State Situation, Background, Assessment and Recommendation with key observations, treatments, response and explicit request. Confirm who is taking responsibility and document timing and decisions.

PLAB application: Close the loop: confirm the plan was heard and accepted.

Practice, feedback and safety-netting

Rehearse timed stations with a peer, use an observer checklist, practise prioritisation and communication, then review missed red flags. Finish stable scenarios with follow-up, return precautions and documentation.

PLAB application: An OSCE pass requires safe clinical reasoning, not memorised speeches.

PLAB 2 — timed emergency simulation

Use an eight-minute practice station: first identify instability and call for help, then verbalise ABCDE, immediate treatment and investigations, repeat observations, communicate with the patient and provide a concise SBAR handover. Time limits and station formats can change; check current GMC guidance.

Deep Dive — Emergency OSCE Masterclass

ABCDE under time pressure

Speak and act simultaneously: ask for monitoring, oxygen if indicated, IV access and senior support while assessing. Do not continue a routine examination when a life-threatening problem is identified.

Clinical reasoning and prioritisation

State your working diagnosis and immediately dangerous alternatives. Choose investigations that change urgent decisions; do not delay life-saving management for tests.

Six mini-simulation prompts

Practice anaphylaxis after antibiotics; hypotensive febrile adult; crushing chest pain with ST elevation; confused patient with glucose 2 mmol/L; child with respiratory exhaustion; post-partum heavy bleeding. For each, rehearse ABCDE, treatment, reassessment and SBAR.

Common OSCE pitfalls

Failing to call for help, omitting glucose, ignoring allergies, failing to recheck observations, confusing defibrillation with cardioversion, and giving vague handovers are preventable errors.

Human factors and speaking to relatives

Use closed-loop communication, delegate specific tasks, respect consent and confidentiality, communicate uncertainty honestly, and seek interpreters when needed.

Post-station deliberate practice

Score yourself on recognition, action, communication, escalation and safety. Repeat weak stations with feedback; consult up-to-date NICE, GMC and Resuscitation Council UK guidance.
