HIV Infection & AIDS

PLAB priority: Recognise acute HIV and opportunistic infections, offer confidential testing, understand ART, PEP, PrEP and U=U, and refer promptly to specialist services.

01. Definitions and natural history

HIV infects CD4 T lymphocytes and causes progressive immune dysfunction if untreated. AIDS describes advanced HIV disease with specified opportunistic illnesses or severe immunosuppression. Modern antiretroviral therapy (ART) permits near-normal life expectancy when started and maintained.

PLAB application: Avoid using AIDS as a synonym for every HIV diagnosis.

02. Transmission and prevention

HIV can be transmitted sexually, through blood exposure, shared injecting equipment, and vertically during pregnancy, birth or breastfeeding. It is not spread through casual contact, hugging or shared utensils. Condoms, PrEP, PEP, testing and effective ART prevent transmission.

PLAB application: Offer non-judgemental, confidential sexual health assessment.

03. Acute HIV infection

Acute retroviral syndrome may occur weeks after exposure: fever, rash, sore throat, lymphadenopathy, myalgia and sometimes aseptic meningitis. Symptoms are nonspecific and may be absent.

PLAB application: Consider HIV testing in compatible illness after a relevant exposure.

04. Clinical stages and examination

Chronic HIV may be asymptomatic or cause persistent lymphadenopathy, weight loss, recurrent infections, oral candidiasis or constitutional symptoms. Examine skin, mouth, lymph nodes, chest and neurological status as indicated.

PLAB application: Recognise red flags for opportunistic disease.

05. Consent, testing and window periods

Use a laboratory fourth-generation HIV antigen/antibody test for routine diagnosis; HIV RNA may help when acute infection is suspected. A negative test during the window period may need repeating. Follow local laboratory algorithms for confirmation.

PLAB application: Explain testing, confidentiality and the need for repeat testing when appropriate.

06. Initial investigations

After a confirmed diagnosis, assess HIV viral load, CD4 count, resistance testing, renal and liver function, FBC, hepatitis B/C, TB risk, STI screening and vaccination history. Further testing depends on symptoms and background.

PLAB application: Refer promptly to a specialist HIV service.

07. CD4 count and viral load

CD4 count reflects immune status and guides opportunistic infection risk and prophylaxis decisions; viral load measures circulating HIV RNA and response to treatment. Effective ART generally suppresses viral load to undetectable levels.

PLAB application: Distinguish immune recovery from virological suppression.

08. Antiretroviral therapy

Offer ART to everyone diagnosed with HIV, usually as soon as practicable with specialist support. Common modern regimens use an integrase inhibitor plus nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors, selected for interactions, comorbidities, resistance and pregnancy considerations.

PLAB application: Do not stop or change ART without specialist advice.

09. U=U and counselling

People with HIV who maintain an undetectable viral load on ART do not sexually transmit HIV (Undetectable = Untransmittable). This does not protect against other STIs or eliminate the need for individualised pregnancy or blood-exposure advice.

PLAB application: Use accurate, stigma-free language and support adherence.

10. Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP)

Following significant potential HIV exposure, urgent risk assessment is needed. Start PEP as soon as possible, ideally within 24 hours and no later than 72 hours where indicated; standard courses are 28 days under current UK guidance.

PLAB application: Do not delay urgent assessment while awaiting source testing.

11. Pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP)

PrEP is HIV medication used before potential exposure by HIV-negative people at increased risk. Confirm HIV-negative status and assess renal function, hepatitis B and STI screening before and during use according to service protocol.

PLAB application: Explain PrEP prevents HIV but not other STIs.

12. Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia

PJP often presents with subacute dry cough, exertional dyspnoea, fever and hypoxia, especially with low CD4 counts. Chest X-ray may show bilateral interstitial changes or be initially subtle. Treatment is specialist-directed co-trimoxazole; adjunctive steroids may be indicated for significant hypoxaemia.

PLAB application: Assess oxygenation and escalate urgently.

13. Tuberculosis and other opportunistic infections

TB can occur at any CD4 count and may be pulmonary or disseminated. Advanced HIV can be associated with cryptococcal meningitis, toxoplasma encephalitis, CMV retinitis and oesophageal candidiasis. Test based on symptoms and exposure risk.

PLAB application: New headache, focal deficits or visual loss warrant urgent investigation.

14. Pregnancy and perinatal care

Early HIV testing, specialist ART, viral load monitoring and coordinated obstetric planning greatly reduce vertical transmission. Infant prophylaxis, delivery planning and feeding advice are individualised according to UK specialist guidance.

PLAB application: Never assume HIV prevents a healthy pregnancy.

15. Complications and long-term health

Monitor cardiovascular, renal, liver, bone and mental health; review drug interactions, vaccinations and cancer screening. Consider immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome after ART starts in selected patients with opportunistic infection.

PLAB application: Manage long-term health proactively alongside viral suppression.

16. PLAB 2 approach and emergencies

Take a confidential, non-judgemental history; explain the test and results sensitively, assess safety and support, discuss ART and partner notification via specialist services. Emergency signs include severe breathlessness, confusion, meningism, focal neurology or acute visual changes.

PLAB application: Escalate emergencies and arrange specialist follow-up.

Deep Dive

HIV lifecycle

HIV enters CD4 cells, reverse transcribes RNA to DNA, integrates into the host genome and produces new virions. ART targets different stages of this cycle.

CD4 versus viral load

CD4 count estimates immune competence; viral load quantifies HIV RNA and is the main treatment-response marker. Both matter clinically.

Fourth-generation testing

Combination antigen/antibody testing detects p24 antigen and antibodies, reducing but not eliminating the early window period.

Why combination ART works

Combining active agents from different drug classes suppresses replication and reduces resistance. Check interactions and hepatitis B status.

Opportunistic infection pattern

Risk generally increases as CD4 count falls, but symptoms, exposures and prophylaxis influence presentation. TB may occur at any CD4 level.

U=U evidence and scope

Sustained undetectable viral load on ART prevents sexual HIV transmission; this message supports stigma reduction while other infection prevention remains relevant.
