Skin Infections: Impetigo, Scabies & Fungal Disease

PLAB priority: Differentiate common contagious skin infections, select appropriate treatment and identify emergencies.

01. Initial approach

Assess lesion morphology, itch or pain, onset, contacts, travel, immune status, systemic symptoms and prior treatments.

PLAB application: Check for fever, rapidly spreading disease and severe pain.

02. Impetigo

Non-bullous impetigo commonly causes honey-coloured crusts; bullous impetigo produces flaccid blisters.

PLAB application: Consider impetigo in children with crusted facial lesions.

03. Localised impetigo treatment

For localised non-bullous impetigo, NICE supports topical hydrogen peroxide 1% when appropriate; alternatives depend on suitability and local resistance.

PLAB application: Avoid routine oral antibiotics for limited uncomplicated disease.

04. Extensive impetigo

Widespread disease, bullous impetigo or systemic illness may require oral antibiotics guided by NICE and clinical assessment.

PLAB application: Review if deteriorating or not responding.

05. Impetigo transmission

Hand hygiene, avoiding shared towels and appropriate school/work exclusion reduce spread.

PLAB application: Give clear infection-control advice.

06. Scabies presentation

Scabies causes intense nocturnal itch, papules and burrows, often between fingers, wrists, waistline and genital area.

PLAB application: Ask if household members also itch.

07. Scabies treatment

Use a recommended scabicide such as permethrin 5% correctly, usually repeated after 7 days; treat close contacts simultaneously.

PLAB application: Explain full-body application per age-specific instructions.

08. Post-scabetic itch

Itching may persist for several weeks after successful scabies treatment due to residual inflammation.

PLAB application: Persistent itch alone does not prove treatment failure.

09. Crusted scabies

Crusted scabies in immunocompromised or vulnerable patients is highly contagious and requires urgent specialist treatment and infection control.

PLAB application: Recognise thick crusted plaques with variable itch.

10. Tinea corporis

Dermatophyte infection often forms annular scaly plaques with a raised active border and central clearing.

PLAB application: Consider fungal microscopy when uncertain.

11. Tinea pedis

Interdigital scaling, maceration or plantar hyperkeratosis suggests athlete’s foot.

PLAB application: Advise foot drying and appropriate topical antifungals.

12. Tinea capitis

Scalp dermatophyte infection can cause scaling, broken hairs and alopecia; systemic antifungal treatment is usually needed.

PLAB application: Do not treat tinea capitis with topical antifungal alone.

13. Onychomycosis

Fungal nail infection causes thickened discoloured nails but mimics are common.

PLAB application: Confirm fungal infection before prolonged oral therapy.

14. Candida intertrigo

Moist flexural erythema with satellite pustules may indicate candidal intertrigo.

PLAB application: Address moisture and predisposing factors.

15. Danger signs

Rapidly progressive pain out of proportion, bullae, skin necrosis or systemic toxicity suggests necrotising infection.

PLAB application: Immediate emergency surgical assessment is needed.

16. PLAB 2 counselling

Explain correct application, household contact management, hygiene, expected recovery and return precautions.

PLAB application: Check patient understanding of repeat scabies treatment.
