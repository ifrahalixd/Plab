Uveitis, Iritis & Scleritis

Red flag: A painful red eye with photophobia, reduced vision or severe deep pain requires urgent ophthalmic assessment.

Overview

Uveitis is inflammation of the uveal tract. Anterior uveitis (iritis/iridocyclitis) involves the iris and ciliary body. Scleritis is deeper inflammation of the sclera; episcleritis is more superficial.

PLAB application: Painful red eye with photophobia or reduced vision warrants urgent assessment.

Anatomy

The uvea comprises iris, ciliary body and choroid. Uveitis is classified as anterior, intermediate, posterior or panuveitis.

PLAB application: Anatomical classification guides investigation and treatment.

Anterior uveitis symptoms

Typical symptoms include ocular pain, photophobia, redness, blurred vision and sometimes a small irregular pupil.

PLAB application: Consensual photophobia supports intraocular inflammation.

Anterior uveitis signs

Ciliary flush, anterior chamber cells and flare, keratic precipitates and posterior synechiae may be seen on slit lamp.

PLAB application: A normal torch exam cannot rule out uveitis.

Causes of uveitis

Many cases are idiopathic. Associations include HLA-B27 spondyloarthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, psoriasis, sarcoidosis and infections such as herpes viruses or tuberculosis.

PLAB application: Ask about inflammatory back pain, bowel symptoms, skin lesions and prior episodes.

Uveitis investigations

Ophthalmology assessment includes visual acuity, slit-lamp examination and intraocular pressure. Further tests are tailored to recurrent, bilateral, granulomatous or posterior disease.

PLAB application: Avoid indiscriminate testing in every first uncomplicated episode.

Anterior uveitis management

Urgent ophthalmology review is needed. Specialist-directed topical corticosteroids and cycloplegics are often used after infection is excluded.

PLAB application: Do not start steroid eye drops empirically in an undifferentiated painful red eye.

Uveitis complications

Posterior synechiae, cataract, glaucoma, cystoid macular oedema and visual loss can occur.

PLAB application: Follow-up and pressure monitoring are essential.

Episcleritis

Episcleritis usually causes sectoral redness with mild discomfort and little or no photophobia or visual loss; it often resolves spontaneously.

PLAB application: Still assess visual acuity and exclude deeper inflammation.

Scleritis presentation

Scleritis causes severe deep boring pain, often radiating to face or jaw and worse with eye movement or at night, with violaceous redness.

PLAB application: Scleritis is potentially sight-threatening and requires same-day specialist assessment.

Scleritis associations

Scleritis can accompany rheumatoid arthritis, ANCA-associated vasculitis and other systemic inflammatory disease. Infection is an important alternative cause.

PLAB application: Ask about joint pain, systemic symptoms and immunosuppression.

Scleritis treatment

Specialist management may require systemic NSAIDs, corticosteroids or immunosuppressive treatment, with cause-directed therapy if infectious.

PLAB application: Do not manage suspected scleritis as routine conjunctivitis.

Keratitis differential

Corneal ulcer/keratitis causes pain, photophobia and reduced vision, especially in contact lens wearers. Fluorescein staining may reveal epithelial defects.

PLAB application: Steroid drops can worsen some corneal infections.

Acute glaucoma differential

Acute angle closure may cause severe pain, blurred vision, haloes, headache, nausea and a mid-dilated pupil.

PLAB application: Emergency ophthalmology treatment is required.

Referral and safety-netting

Urgent ophthalmology review for suspected uveitis; same-day emergency assessment for scleritis, significant visual loss, severe pain or corneal infection.

PLAB application: New visual deterioration or worsening pain should trigger immediate reassessment.

PLAB 2 OSCE

Ask onset, unilateral/bilateral symptoms, pain, photophobia, vision, contact lenses, trauma, systemic inflammatory symptoms and prior episodes. Check acuity, pupils and external eye safely.

PLAB application: Explain uncertainty, avoid inappropriate drops and arrange timely referral.

PLAB 2 OSCE

Obtain consent. Ask about pain, photophobia, vision, contact lenses, trauma, previous episodes and systemic disease. Assess visual acuity and pupils; explain why urgent ophthalmology review may be needed. Avoid empirical steroid drops in undiagnosed red eye and safety-net deterioration.

Deep Dive

Anterior chamber inflammation

Inflammatory cells and protein cause cells and flare on slit-lamp examination.

Photophobia mechanism

Iris and ciliary muscle movement can provoke pain in anterior uveitis.

HLA-B27 link

Recurrent acute anterior uveitis can occur with axial spondyloarthritis and related conditions.

Scleral inflammation

The deep scleral vascular network is involved, explaining intense pain and risk of tissue damage.

Why the differential matters

Keratitis, acute angle closure and uveitis can all present with red painful eyes but need different urgent treatment.

Follow-up

Monitor visual acuity, inflammation and intraocular pressure to prevent complications.
