Final Obstetrics & Gynaecology PLAB Revision & Clinical Emergencies

Emergency principle: ABCDE, call for help, initiate treatment and reassess. Clinical protocols and senior judgement take priority.

Emergency priorities

Obstetric and gynaecological emergencies need rapid recognition, ABCDE, simultaneous resuscitation and early senior obstetric, anaesthetic, midwifery or surgical help. Assess mother first while coordinating fetal assessment when appropriate.

PLAB application: State the immediate action before requesting confirmatory tests.

Early pregnancy pain and bleeding

Exclude ectopic pregnancy in reproductive-age patients with abdominal pain, bleeding or collapse. Perform pregnancy test, assess haemodynamic status and arrange urgent ultrasound and specialist review. A pregnancy of unknown location needs structured follow-up.

PLAB application: Shoulder-tip pain, syncope or shock may signal ruptured ectopic pregnancy.

Pre-eclampsia and eclampsia

New hypertension after 20 weeks with proteinuria or maternal organ/uteroplacental dysfunction suggests pre-eclampsia. Severe hypertension is 160/110 mmHg or higher. Eclampsia is a seizure associated with pre-eclampsia and requires emergency management.

PLAB application: Magnesium sulfate for eclampsia; monitor airway, respiration and reflexes with protocol-led care.

Antepartum haemorrhage

Placenta praevia classically causes painless bleeding; abruption often causes pain, uterine tenderness and fetal compromise, although bleeding may be concealed. Stabilise, assess fetus and request urgent obstetric review.

PLAB application: Do not perform digital vaginal examination before placenta praevia is excluded.

Preterm labour and PPROM

Before 37 weeks, contractions or suspected membrane rupture need assessment for labour, infection and fetal wellbeing. Use sterile speculum examination for suspected PPROM. Steroids, magnesium sulfate, antibiotics and tocolysis depend on gestation and clinical context.

PLAB application: Avoid routine digital examination in suspected PPROM.

Intrapartum fetal compromise

Interpret CTG alongside maternal condition, gestation, contractions and risk factors. Persistent fetal bradycardia, maternal shock or uterine rupture concern requires immediate escalation and possible expedited birth.

PLAB application: A concerning CTG is a clinical decision, not a standalone diagnosis.

Shoulder dystocia

After delivery of the head, the shoulders fail to deliver with usual gentle traction. Call for help, note time, use McRoberts position and suprapubic pressure; proceed through trained manoeuvres.

PLAB application: Never use fundal pressure or excessive traction on the fetal head.

Umbilical cord prolapse

Cord below the presenting part can cause sudden fetal bradycardia after rupture of membranes. Call for help, elevate the presenting part to relieve compression and arrange urgent birth; minimise cord handling.

PLAB application: Do not delay definitive delivery while trying to reposition the cord.

Uterine rupture

Risk increases with a uterine scar, particularly during labour. Fetal heart abnormalities may be earliest clue; pain, bleeding, loss of station or maternal collapse may occur. Stop uterotonics, resuscitate and organise emergency laparotomy.

PLAB application: A prior caesarean scar plus sudden fetal bradycardia is an emergency.

Postpartum haemorrhage

Consider four Ts: tone, trauma, tissue, thrombin. Call for help, activate major haemorrhage pathway as indicated, use uterine massage/uterotonics for atony, give early tranexamic acid according to protocol, and control source.

PLAB application: A boggy uterus suggests atony; persistent bleeding with firm uterus suggests trauma or coagulopathy.

Maternal sepsis

Postpartum or antenatal infection may cause fever, tachycardia, hypotension, altered mental state or organ dysfunction. Take cultures if this does not delay treatment, give prompt antibiotics and fluids with close reassessment.

PLAB application: Do not wait for fever or laboratory confirmation if clinical sepsis is suspected.

Venous thromboembolism

Pregnancy and puerperium increase VTE risk. Unilateral swollen leg suggests DVT; acute dyspnoea, pleuritic pain or collapse may indicate PE. Escalate and use pregnancy-specific imaging and anticoagulation pathways.

PLAB application: A normal oxygen saturation does not exclude pulmonary embolism.

Gynaecological acute abdomen

Ovarian torsion causes sudden unilateral pain with nausea/vomiting; ultrasound cannot reliably exclude it. PID causes pelvic pain and cervical motion tenderness; treat clinically while considering pregnancy and other causes.

PLAB application: Urgent surgical assessment for suspected torsion preserves ovarian function.

Cancer red flags

Postcoital bleeding and suspicious cervix need examination and referral; postmenopausal bleeding warrants investigation; persistent bloating/early satiety may suggest ovarian cancer. Explain investigations without prematurely diagnosing cancer.

PLAB application: A normal cervical screening result does not rule out symptomatic cervical cancer.

Contraception and reproductive counselling

Consider pregnancy intention, medical eligibility, STI protection and informed choice. Copper IUD is the most effective emergency contraception; ulipristal and levonorgestrel have different windows and restarting-hormone advice.

PLAB application: Assess safeguarding, consent and confidentiality sensitively.

PLAB 2 integrated approach

Introduce yourself, confirm identity, assess pain and stability, obtain focused obstetric/gynaecological history, offer chaperone, explain differentials, shared plan, red flags and follow-up. Use SBAR when escalating.

PLAB application: In emergency OSCE stations, prioritise safe actions and clear communication over exhaustive history.

PLAB 2 — Emergency handover practice

Scenario: A 28-year-old, 20 minutes after vaginal delivery, has heavy bleeding, pulse 130 and BP 85/50. Call for emergency help, use ABCDE, activate the PPH protocol, request bloods and blood products, identify the four Ts and hand over using SBAR. Explain each action calmly to the patient and partner where possible.

Deep Dive

ABCDE and escalation

Recognise shock even before profound hypotension: tachycardia, pallor, altered mentation, low urine output and rapid bleeding may precede collapse. Communicate early and use structured handover.

Emergency medication safety

Magnesium sulfate is used for eclampsia; uterotonics and tranexamic acid for PPH; antibiotics for maternal sepsis. Check current local protocols, contraindications, dosing, monitoring and senior support.

Differentiating antepartum haemorrhage

Praevia may be painless and abruption may be concealed. Neither bleeding colour nor pain alone establishes diagnosis. Maternal stabilisation comes first.

Fetal assessment

Interpret fetal monitoring in the whole clinical context. Severe maternal compromise can impair fetal oxygenation; coordinate maternal resuscitation with obstetric decision-making.

Consent and communication

Explain urgency simply, ask permission when possible, use chaperones for intimate examinations, preserve privacy, and document capacity and best-interest decisions if immediate lifesaving care is necessary.

UK exam approach

Use NICE guidance and local NHS obstetric protocols. Practice emergency calls, SBAR, differential diagnoses, consent, discharge advice and safety-netting.
