Growth, Developmental Milestones & Developmental Delay

Red flags: Loss of skills, persistent asymmetry, seizures, raised intracranial pressure signs or failure to walk by 18 months require assessment.

Why growth and development matter

Growth describes changes in body size; development describes acquisition of skills in gross motor, fine motor, language, social and adaptive domains. Interpret in the context of the child’s health, family and environment.

PLAB application: Ask what the child can do now, what they used to do, and whether progress has stalled.

Growth measurement

Measure weight, length or height and head circumference when age-appropriate. Use calibrated equipment and serial measurements; plot on the correct UK-WHO growth chart with age and sex.

PLAB application: A single centile is less informative than trajectory.

Corrected age in prematurity

For a preterm child, corrected age is chronological age minus the number of weeks born before 40 weeks. UK growth chart guidance uses gestational age and correction rules; commonly correct developmental age up to around 2 years.

PLAB application: Check gestation before interpreting an apparent delay.

Growth faltering

Consider inadequate intake, feeding difficulties, malabsorption, chronic illness, neglect or social stressors. Take a feeding history, assess hydration and plot serial weights; assess length/height as well.

PLAB application: Do not diagnose failure to thrive from one low weight alone.

Head circumference

Rapidly increasing head circumference, bulging fontanelle, vomiting or developmental concerns may indicate hydrocephalus or other pathology. Small head circumference may reflect microcephaly; verify measurement and trend.

PLAB application: Urgently assess signs of raised intracranial pressure.

Gross motor milestones

Approximate anchors: good head control around 3–4 months; sitting without support around 6–9 months; pulling to stand and cruising around 9–12 months; independent walking commonly by 12–18 months.

PLAB application: Not walking independently by 18 months warrants assessment.

Fine motor and vision

Reaching and grasping develop during the first months; transferring objects around 6–9 months; immature pincer grasp around 9 months and more refined pincer grasp by about 12 months.

PLAB application: Ask about hand preference before 12 months; early persistent asymmetry may be a red flag.

Language milestones

Babbling commonly emerges around 6 months; first meaningful words around 12 months; a growing vocabulary and two-word phrases typically by 2 years. Assess both understanding and expression.

PLAB application: No babbling, poor response to sound, or no two-word phrases by 2 years needs evaluation.

Social and play development

Social smile appears around 6–8 weeks; interactive play develops in infancy; pointing to share interest commonly by 12–18 months; pretend play develops in toddler years.

PLAB application: Absence of joint attention, reduced social reciprocity or regression needs assessment.

Developmental surveillance versus screening

Surveillance is longitudinal observation and parental discussion; structured screening uses validated tools where indicated. UK Healthy Child Programme contacts support early identification; tools do not replace clinical judgement.

PLAB application: Always listen to parental concern even when a screening score is reassuring.

Global developmental delay

Significant delay across two or more developmental domains in a young child requires holistic assessment. Differential diagnoses include genetic conditions, cerebral palsy, hearing/vision impairment, endocrine or metabolic illness and environmental factors.

PLAB application: Document which domains are affected and arrange paediatric assessment.

Regression and red flags

Loss of previously acquired skills is particularly concerning. Other red flags include persistent asymmetry, abnormal tone, seizures, no response to sound, swallowing difficulty or progressive head enlargement.

PLAB application: Developmental regression warrants prompt specialist referral.

Autism and neurodevelopment

Autism can present with differences in social communication, restricted/repetitive behaviours and sensory processing. Avoid making a diagnosis from one milestone; assess across settings and refer through local neurodevelopment pathways.

PLAB application: Consider hearing assessment in children with language delay.

Cerebral palsy and motor delay

Look for abnormal tone, persistent primitive reflexes, asymmetrical movement, delayed sitting or walking and feeding problems. Cerebral palsy is a clinical diagnosis related to non-progressive disturbance of the developing brain, although manifestations change with age.

PLAB application: Early intervention and multidisciplinary referral should not wait for a definitive label.

Initial assessment and investigations

Take antenatal, birth, neonatal, family, medical and psychosocial histories; examine growth, dysmorphism, tone, reflexes, hearing and vision. Investigations are targeted to findings and may include hearing/vision testing, blood tests, genetics or neuroimaging under specialist guidance.

PLAB application: Do not order broad investigations without a clinical question.

Management, safeguarding and family support

Use shared decision-making, physiotherapy, occupational and speech-language therapy, early years support, health visiting and paediatric services. Consider safeguarding sensitively when concerns exist; explain plans clearly and provide follow-up.

PLAB application: Safety-net for regression, seizures, feeding problems and loss of mobility.

PLAB 2 — Parent concerned about delayed speech

Introduce yourself, confirm child’s age and gestation, explore the concern, milestones in every domain, hearing, vision, family history and regression. Examine growth and neurology, explain uncertainty empathetically, arrange audiology and community paediatric assessment, and provide safety-netting for regression or seizures.

Deep Dive

Developmental milestone interpretation

Milestones represent ranges, not rigid deadlines. Assess quality and symmetry of movement, functional communication and progression; a single isolated difference may be less concerning than regression or multiple-domain delay.

UK growth charts and prematurity

Use UK-WHO growth charts and follow the chart’s specific gestational-age plotting instructions. Corrected age is particularly important for premature infants, but correction practices differ by chart and age.

Language delay differential

Consider hearing impairment, developmental language disorder, autism, global developmental delay, limited language exposure opportunities and psychosocial factors. Multilingualism itself is not a disorder.

Cerebral palsy clues

Early abnormal tone, persistent primitive reflexes, asymmetrical movement and delayed motor skills merit referral. Early therapy can improve function while diagnostic evaluation continues.

Developmental regression

Regression may indicate neurological, metabolic, epileptic or neurodegenerative disorders and should not be managed by routine watchful waiting.

PLAB 2 parent-centred communication

Use concrete examples rather than labels: “I would like to check hearing and arrange a developmental review.” Ask about strengths, routines and family priorities. Document the plan and red flags.
