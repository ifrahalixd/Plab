Sepsis, Meningitis & Life-Threatening Infections

Emergency red flags: altered consciousness, shock, rapidly progressive rash, suspected meningococcal disease, severe respiratory compromise or necrotising infection require immediate ABCDE assessment and urgent escalation.

Recognising sepsis

Sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated response to infection. Look for altered mentation, tachypnoea, hypotension, poor perfusion, oliguria and new oxygen requirement.

PLAB application: Normal temperature or blood pressure does not rule out sepsis.

ABCDE and immediate escalation

Assess and treat airway, breathing, circulation, disability and exposure. Record observations, calculate NEWS2 in adults where appropriate, request senior review and repeat assessment.

PLAB application: A deteriorating patient needs immediate help, not just a score.

Risk stratification

Use current NICE NG51 age-appropriate risk criteria and local sepsis pathways. Consider frailty, immunosuppression, recent surgery, pregnancy and recent antibiotic exposure.

PLAB application: Clinical judgement overrides a reassuring single observation.

Sepsis investigations

Take blood cultures before antibiotics if this does not cause significant delay. Check lactate, blood gas, FBC, U&E, CRP, glucose, coagulation, and tests directed by the suspected source.

PLAB application: Cultures must not delay lifesaving treatment.

Antibiotics and source control

Give prompt IV antibiotics for high-risk suspected sepsis according to NICE/local protocol, allergies and likely source. Identify drainable abscesses, infected devices or obstructed systems requiring source control.

PLAB application: Antibiotics alone may not resolve an uncontrolled source.

Fluid resuscitation and shock

Assess fluid responsiveness and risks of overload. Give protocol-guided crystalloid boluses with frequent reassessment; involve critical care early for persistent shock or vasopressor need.

PLAB application: Avoid automatic repeated large boluses in heart or renal failure.

Septic shock and lactate

Persistent circulatory failure despite appropriate resuscitation requires urgent senior/critical-care management. Elevated lactate can signal tissue hypoperfusion but has other causes.

PLAB application: Trend lactate alongside clinical perfusion, not in isolation.

Bacterial meningitis recognition

Fever, headache, neck stiffness and altered cognition are concerning, but not all are present, especially in infants, older adults or immunocompromised patients.

PLAB application: Do not exclude meningitis because there is no neck stiffness.

Meningococcal disease

Meningococcal infection may cause rapid sepsis, limb pain, cold extremities and a non-blanching rash; the rash can be absent early. Initiate urgent emergency treatment.

PLAB application: Do not wait for purpura before acting.

Meningitis investigations

Obtain blood cultures and urgent senior review. Perform lumbar puncture only when safe and without delaying antibiotics; brain imaging is not routine before every LP.

PLAB application: Instability or signs of raised ICP require specialist assessment before LP.

Meningitis treatment

For suspected bacterial meningitis, start empiric IV antibiotics urgently according to NICE NG240/local guidance, with dexamethasone when indicated and without delaying antibiotics.

PLAB application: Antibiotics take priority over CT or LP when delay is likely.

Encephalitis

Consider encephalitis in fever with altered behaviour, seizures, focal neurological signs or reduced consciousness. Investigate with CSF, MRI and EEG as appropriate and give empiric IV aciclovir when HSV encephalitis is suspected.

PLAB application: HSV encephalitis is time-critical and treatable.

Necrotising soft-tissue infection

Disproportionate pain, rapid spread, systemic toxicity, bullae or skin necrosis suggest necrotising infection. Call surgery immediately and start broad-spectrum antibiotics/resuscitation.

PLAB application: Do not delay surgery for imaging in an unstable patient.

Severe pneumonia and urinary sepsis

Assess oxygenation and respiratory failure in pneumonia; consider obstructed infected kidney in urinary sepsis. Obtain targeted imaging and urgent source control when indicated.

PLAB application: An infected obstructed kidney needs urgent decompression.

Special populations

Neutropenic sepsis, neonatal sepsis and pregnancy-associated sepsis use dedicated pathways. Immunocompromised patients may have subtle signs and need immediate specialist assessment.

PLAB application: Treat suspected neutropenic sepsis as an emergency.

PLAB 2 communication and handover

Explain the urgency without unnecessary alarm, obtain allergy/medication history, discuss blood tests and antibiotics, document observations and response, and hand over using SBAR.

PLAB application: Always reassess and clearly state the escalation plan.

PLAB 2 — fever, confusion and hypotension

Call for urgent senior support, perform ABCDE, obtain cultures and lactate without delaying treatment, start protocol-guided antibiotics and reassessed fluids, identify the source, consider meningitis and communicate an SBAR handover.

Deep Dive — Sepsis and CNS Infections

Why sepsis is difficult to recognise

Fever may be absent; tachycardia and confusion can be early clues. NEWS2 supports but does not replace clinical judgement. Reassess trends.

Antibiotic timing and stewardship

NICE NG51 recommends urgency based on risk. In high-risk suspected sepsis, prompt antibiotics are critical; review cultures and narrow or stop therapy as appropriate once information returns.

Lumbar puncture safety

Do not perform LP in an unstable patient or when contraindications are present. Imaging is reserved for appropriate clinical indications, and should not postpone urgent antibiotics.

Meningococcal deterioration

Meningococcal disease may initially resemble viral illness. Severe limb pain, cold hands/feet, mottling, altered consciousness or non-blanching lesions warrant urgent response.

HSV encephalitis

Temporal-lobe involvement, seizures, personality change and altered cognition are classic clues; urgent aciclovir is considered while definitive investigations are arranged.

Source control and critical care

Drainage, device removal, surgery or urinary decompression may be required. Persistent hypotension or respiratory failure warrants early critical care input.
