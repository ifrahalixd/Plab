Systemic Sclerosis (Scleroderma) & Raynaud's Phenomenon

PLAB priority: Recognise secondary Raynaud, screen systemic sclerosis organ involvement and act immediately on digital ischaemia or renal crisis.

01. Overview

Systemic sclerosis (SSc) is an autoimmune connective tissue disease characterised by vasculopathy, immune activation and fibrosis of skin and internal organs. Raynaud phenomenon may precede other manifestations by years.

PLAB application: Distinguish primary Raynaud from secondary Raynaud and screen for organ involvement.

02. Raynaud phenomenon

Episodic digital vasospasm provoked by cold or stress produces colour change (often white, blue, then red), numbness and pain. Not all three colours must occur.

PLAB application: Ask about triggers, symmetry, ulceration and functional impact.

03. Primary versus secondary Raynaud

Primary Raynaud commonly begins young, is symmetrical and has no tissue injury. Secondary Raynaud may begin later, be asymmetric or severe, and cause digital ulcers; look for connective tissue disease.

PLAB application: Digital ulceration, necrosis or critical ischaemia needs urgent assessment.

04. SSc subtypes

Limited cutaneous SSc predominantly affects distal extremities and face; diffuse cutaneous SSc may involve proximal limbs and trunk with earlier major organ disease. Sine scleroderma has visceral features without obvious skin thickening.

PLAB application: Extent of skin involvement informs risk but does not replace organ screening.

05. Skin and hand findings

Puffy fingers, sclerodactyly, skin tightening, reduced finger flexion, fingertip pits, telangiectasia, calcinosis and microstomia are clues. Examine nailfold capillaries when possible.

PLAB application: Describe the skin distribution and check for ulcers or infection.

06. CREST mnemonic

Calcinosis, Raynaud, Esophageal dysmotility, Sclerodactyly and Telangiectasia describe a classic limited SSc phenotype, but not every patient has all features.

PLAB application: Do not assume CREST excludes lung or pulmonary vascular disease.

07. Gastrointestinal involvement

Reflux, dysphagia and oesophageal dysmotility are common; patients may also develop bowel dysmotility, malabsorption or weight loss.

PLAB application: Ask about swallowing, reflux, aspiration and nutritional status.

08. Interstitial lung disease

SSc-associated interstitial lung disease may cause exertional dyspnoea, dry cough and basal fine inspiratory crackles. Assessment includes pulmonary function tests with DLCO and high-resolution CT when indicated.

PLAB application: New breathlessness merits assessment for ILD, pulmonary hypertension and other causes.

09. Pulmonary arterial hypertension

Pulmonary arterial hypertension can occur particularly in limited SSc; symptoms include exertional breathlessness, fatigue, syncope and oedema. Regular screening includes echocardiography and lung function measures under specialist care.

PLAB application: Syncope or significant breathlessness requires prompt evaluation.

10. Scleroderma renal crisis

Abrupt severe hypertension and acute kidney injury, sometimes with microangiopathic haemolysis, constitute a medical emergency. It can occur without marked hypertension. High-dose glucocorticoids increase risk.

PLAB application: Urgently check BP, creatinine and urinalysis; initiate emergency specialist management.

11. Autoantibodies and investigations

ANA is often positive. Anti-centromere is associated with limited SSc and PAH; anti-topoisomerase I (Scl-70) with ILD; anti-RNA polymerase III with diffuse skin disease and renal crisis risk. Associations are not absolute.

PLAB application: Interpret antibodies alongside clinical findings; normal tests do not replace assessment.

12. Diagnosis and referral

Diagnosis is clinical supported by serology, nailfold capillaroscopy and organ evaluation. Suspected secondary Raynaud or SSc warrants rheumatology referral, especially with puffy fingers, ulcers or abnormal nailfolds.

PLAB application: Ask specifically about lung, kidney and swallowing symptoms at first review.

13. Raynaud management

Keep the whole body warm, avoid cold exposure and smoking, manage stress and review vasoconstricting drugs. A dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker such as nifedipine may be used for troublesome Raynaud after clinical review.

PLAB application: Check blood pressure, pregnancy status and contraindications before prescribing.

14. Organ-specific treatment

Specialists may use immunomodulatory therapy for progressive skin or ILD disease, targeted PAH therapy, acid suppression for reflux and vasodilators for digital ulcers. Treatment is individualised.

PLAB application: Avoid treating all SSc manifestations with the same drug.

15. Renal crisis treatment and steroid caution

Scleroderma renal crisis requires immediate hospital care and ACE-inhibitor therapy under specialist supervision, often with close renal monitoring. Avoid unnecessary high-dose systemic corticosteroids.

PLAB application: Do not delay emergency referral for renal crisis while arranging outpatient tests.

16. PLAB 2 consultation and safety-netting

Explain the condition in plain language, assess impact on work and daily life, examine hands and cardiovascular/respiratory systems, and discuss follow-up. Warn about painful discoloured digits, new breathlessness, syncope, severe headache or reduced urine output.

PLAB application: Close with a clear differential, investigations, urgent red flags and shared management plan.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Vascular dysfunction

Endothelial injury and impaired vasodilatory signalling promote vasospasm, vascular remodelling and digital ischaemia.

Fibrosis and autoimmunity

Activated fibroblasts deposit excess extracellular matrix; immune mediators contribute to skin and visceral fibrosis.

Antibody patterns

Anti-centromere, anti-topoisomerase I and anti-RNA polymerase III support phenotyping but cannot independently diagnose or exclude SSc.

Pulmonary complications

ILD causes parenchymal restriction while PAH affects pulmonary vessels; both may cause dyspnoea and require different investigations and treatment.

Renal crisis mechanism

Renal vascular injury activates the renin–angiotensin system, driving hypertension and kidney damage; ACE inhibition is crucial.

OSCE reasoning

Differentiate primary from secondary Raynaud, inspect nailfolds and skin, screen for organ involvement and communicate urgent warning signs.
