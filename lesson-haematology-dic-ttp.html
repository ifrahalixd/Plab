DIC & TTP

Emergency: Suspected DIC or TTP requires urgent hospital assessment and senior/haematology escalation. Never delay treatment for confirmatory testing when TTP is strongly suspected.

Overview

DIC is systemic activation of coagulation with consumption of platelets and clotting factors. TTP is a thrombotic microangiopathy due to severe ADAMTS13 deficiency. Both can be life-threatening.

PLAB application: Recognise these as distinct emergencies; treatment is not interchangeable.

DIC triggers

Common causes include sepsis, major trauma, obstetric complications, malignancy (especially acute promyelocytic leukaemia) and severe pancreatitis.

PLAB application: Treat the underlying trigger immediately.

DIC clinical presentation

Bleeding from cannula sites, mucosal bleeding, bruising, organ dysfunction and sometimes thrombosis or digital ischaemia.

PLAB application: Do not assume DIC presents only with bleeding.

DIC blood results

Typically low platelets, prolonged PT and often APTT, raised D-dimer and falling fibrinogen; trends matter. Fibrinogen can be initially normal in inflammation.

PLAB application: Repeat serial FBC, coagulation profile and fibrinogen.

DIC diagnosis

Diagnosis integrates clinical context and serial laboratory findings; the ISTH overt DIC score can support assessment.

PLAB application: No single blood test confirms or excludes DIC.

DIC management

Resuscitate and treat the cause, seek senior/haematology advice, and give platelets, plasma or fibrinogen replacement when clinically indicated, especially with bleeding or procedures.

PLAB application: Do not transfuse blood components solely to normalise mildly abnormal numbers.

TTP mechanism

Severe ADAMTS13 deficiency permits unusually large von Willebrand factor multimers to form platelet-rich microthrombi, causing microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia and thrombocytopenia.

PLAB application: Most adult cases are immune-mediated; inherited TTP also exists.

TTP presentation

Fatigue, petechiae, headache, confusion, focal neurological signs, abdominal pain or renal injury may occur. Fever is not always present.

PLAB application: Do not wait for the classic five-feature pentad.

TTP blood film and haemolysis

Thrombocytopenia, schistocytes, raised LDH, raised unconjugated bilirubin and low haptoglobin suggest microangiopathic haemolysis. Coagulation tests are often normal.

PLAB application: Thrombocytopenia plus MAHA is a red flag for urgent TTP assessment.

TTP diagnosis

Send ADAMTS13 activity and inhibitor/antibody testing before plasma therapy if this does not delay treatment. Severe activity deficiency (often <10%) supports TTP.

PLAB application: A PLASMIC score can support risk stratification but does not replace urgent specialist assessment.

TTP emergency treatment

Urgent therapeutic plasma exchange and immunosuppression are central to immune TTP treatment; caplacizumab may be used according to specialist UK pathways.

PLAB application: Start emergency treatment on strong suspicion without waiting for ADAMTS13 results.

Platelet transfusion caution

Routine platelet transfusion is generally avoided in TTP because of potential thrombotic harm; life-threatening bleeding or urgent procedures require specialist decisions.

PLAB application: Differentiate platelet support in bleeding DIC from routine avoidance in TTP.

Other thrombotic microangiopathies

Haemolytic uraemic syndrome (including Shiga toxin-associated HUS), complement-mediated TMA, pregnancy-related HELLP, malignant hypertension and drug-induced TMA can mimic TTP.

PLAB application: Take diarrhoeal, pregnancy, drug and blood-pressure history.

DIC versus TTP laboratory pattern

DIC often has prolonged clotting times and low fibrinogen; TTP usually has near-normal PT/APTT with prominent MAHA and thrombocytopenia. Exceptions exist.

PLAB application: Use the full clinical picture rather than a single cut-off.

Initial emergency assessment

Use ABCDE, check observations and neurological status, obtain IV access and urgent FBC/film, PT/APTT, fibrinogen, D-dimer, U&Es, LFTs, LDH and haemolysis studies.

PLAB application: Escalate immediately to senior clinicians and haematology.

Monitoring and follow-up

DIC requires repeated assessment of organ function and clotting results. TTP needs specialist monitoring for platelet recovery, haemolysis, ADAMTS13 activity and relapse risk.

PLAB application: Explain urgent return precautions for new bleeding, bruising or neurological symptoms.

PLAB 2: Bleeding and suspected TMA OSCE

  1. Introduce yourself, confirm identity, obtain consent and assess immediate stability.
  2. Ask about bruising, bleeding, fever, recent infection, pregnancy, medicines, neurological symptoms and urine output.
  3. Assess ABCDE, vital signs, skin, neurological status and signs of organ dysfunction.
  4. Explain that urgent blood tests and specialist assessment are needed; avoid falsely reassuring before results.
  5. Escalate immediately and communicate the emergency management plan and safety-netting clearly.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Consumption versus microthrombi

DIC activates widespread thrombin formation, consuming factors and platelets. TTP causes platelet-rich microvascular thrombosis due to severe ADAMTS13 deficiency.

Why coagulation tests differ

PT/APTT measure plasma clotting pathways, which are frequently depleted in DIC but generally preserved in TTP.

Why schistocytes form

Red cells fragment as they pass through small vessels containing microthrombi, producing MAHA.

Why fibrinogen can mislead

Fibrinogen is an acute-phase reactant and may remain normal early in septic DIC; serial changes matter.

Why plasma exchange saves lives

In immune TTP, exchange removes anti-ADAMTS13 antibodies and replaces functional enzyme.

Differential diagnosis of TMA

Consider HUS, complement-mediated TMA, HELLP, malignant hypertension and medication-associated TMA; treatment varies substantially.
