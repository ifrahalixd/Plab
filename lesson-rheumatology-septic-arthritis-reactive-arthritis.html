Septic Arthritis & Reactive Arthritis

PLAB priority: An acutely hot, swollen joint is septic arthritis until adequately excluded. Escalate urgently.

01. Why this matters

Septic arthritis is an infection inside a native joint and can destroy cartilage rapidly or cause sepsis. Reactive arthritis is sterile inflammatory arthritis following a distant infection. Distinguishing them is essential because septic arthritis requires emergency treatment.

PLAB application: A hot swollen joint is septic until adequately evaluated.

02. Septic arthritis presentation

Usually acute painful swollen warm joint with restricted active and passive movement; the knee is common. Fever may be absent, especially in older or immunosuppressed patients. Hip infection may cause inability to bear weight.

PLAB application: Never rely on fever or blood results to exclude joint infection.

03. Risk factors

Pre-existing joint disease, diabetes, immunosuppression, prosthetic joint, recent surgery or injection, skin infection, bacteraemia and intravenous drug use raise risk. Infection can occur without any risk factors.

PLAB application: Ask about recent procedures, immunosuppressants and skin wounds.

04. Likely organisms

Staphylococcus aureus is a major cause; streptococci also occur. Consider gonococcal infection in sexually active patients, Gram-negative organisms in selected risk groups, and unusual pathogens with immunosuppression or exposure.

PLAB application: Culture-directed antibiotics are important.

05. Emergency assessment

Assess ABCDE, observations, sepsis features and analgesia. Examine the joint and nearby skin, neurovascular status and other joints. Arrange immediate emergency orthopaedic or relevant specialist discussion.

PLAB application: Do not discharge a patient with unexplained severe acute monoarthritis.

06. Joint aspiration

Urgent synovial fluid aspiration for Gram stain, culture, white cell count and crystals is central. Obtain blood cultures before antibiotics if feasible without delaying care. A high synovial white cell count supports infection but no single threshold excludes it.

PLAB application: Aspirate promptly, but do not delay antibiotics in sepsis or critical illness.

07. Blood tests and imaging

FBC, CRP, ESR, renal function and blood cultures help assessment but normal markers do not exclude septic arthritis. Ultrasound can locate an effusion and guide aspiration; X-ray helps assess baseline joint disease. MRI may identify deep infection or osteomyelitis.

PLAB application: Imaging must not delay specialist care.

08. Acute septic arthritis treatment

Urgent hospital admission, IV antibiotics after sampling where safely possible, and joint drainage or washout directed by specialists. Adjust antimicrobials to cultures, allergy, renal function and local microbiology advice.

PLAB application: Early drainage and antibiotics preserve joint function.

09. Prosthetic joint infection

A painful warm prosthetic joint, wound discharge or systemic illness requires urgent orthopaedic assessment. Avoid unplanned antibiotic initiation in stable patients before specialist sampling when possible, but treat sepsis immediately.

PLAB application: Prosthetic infections need specialist surgical and microbiology planning.

10. Gonococcal arthritis

Disseminated gonococcal infection may present with migratory polyarthralgia, tenosynovitis, skin lesions or purulent arthritis. Take a sexual history sensitively, obtain appropriate NAAT and cultures, and involve sexual health services.

PLAB application: Consider STI testing when presentation and risk fit.

11. Reactive arthritis overview

Reactive arthritis is an inflammatory arthritis developing typically 1–4 weeks after gastrointestinal or genitourinary infection. The joint is inflamed but usually not directly infected. It often affects knees, ankles or feet asymmetrically.

PLAB application: Ask about recent diarrhoea and urinary or genital symptoms.

12. Reactive arthritis clinical features

Asymmetric oligoarthritis of lower limbs, enthesitis, heel pain, dactylitis and inflammatory back pain may occur. Conjunctivitis or uveitis and urethritis can coexist but the classic triad is often incomplete.

PLAB application: A painful photophobic red eye needs same-day eye assessment.

13. Triggers and investigation

Possible triggers include Chlamydia trachomatis, Campylobacter, Salmonella, Shigella and Yersinia. Investigate suspected ongoing infection appropriately; HLA-B27 is associated with more persistent disease but is not diagnostic. Aspirate an acute hot joint to exclude sepsis or crystals.

PLAB application: Do not label a hot joint reactive arthritis without excluding septic arthritis.

14. Reactive arthritis treatment

Treat active infection according to its own indication; antibiotics do not routinely resolve sterile arthritis once infection has cleared. NSAIDs when safe, activity modification and physiotherapy are usual first steps. Persistent disease may need local steroids or rheumatology-directed DMARDs.

PLAB application: Screen for NSAID contraindications and arrange follow-up.

15. Differential diagnosis

Gout and CPPD cause acute crystal arthritis; rheumatoid and psoriatic arthritis, trauma, haemarthrosis and Lyme arthritis can mimic infection. Crystals in aspirate do not exclude concurrent septic arthritis.

PLAB application: Joint fluid microscopy and culture matter even if gout seems likely.

16. PLAB 2 communication and safety-net

Explain the emergency need to exclude infection, consent for aspiration, check allergies, and communicate uncertainty clearly. Advise urgent reassessment for fever, increasing pain, inability to bear weight, confusion or worsening swelling.

PLAB application: State explicitly that septic arthritis is time-critical.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Why septic joints deteriorate

Bacterial proliferation recruits neutrophils and inflammatory mediators that rapidly injure cartilage. Joint drainage reduces bacterial and inflammatory burden.

Synovial fluid pitfalls

A high synovial white-cell count is supportive but cannot alone diagnose infection; a low count does not always exclude it. Culture and clinical context are vital.

Reactive arthritis immunology

An infection at another site triggers inflammatory immune responses in susceptible hosts; the joint itself is usually sterile.

HLA-B27 and spondyloarthritis

HLA-B27 increases susceptibility and may correlate with more prolonged symptoms, but neither a positive nor negative test establishes or excludes disease.

Septic versus crystal arthritis

Both may cause intense monoarthritis and elevated CRP. Crystal detection cannot exclude simultaneous bacterial infection; send fluid for culture.

Gonococcal disease and sexual health

Disseminated gonococcal infection can cause arthritis-dermatitis syndrome or purulent arthritis; appropriate site-specific NAAT and specialist treatment are needed.
