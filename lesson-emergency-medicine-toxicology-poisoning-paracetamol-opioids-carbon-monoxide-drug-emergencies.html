Toxicology & Poisoning: Paracetamol Overdose, Opioids, Carbon Monoxide & Drug Emergencies

Immediate priorities: ABCDE, ventilation and glucose; check ECG, contact UK toxicology/TOXBASE, and treat time-critical poisoning. Do not induce vomiting.

Initial approach to poisoning

Use ABCDE, PPE and early senior/toxicology advice. Identify substance, formulation, dose, route, timing, co-ingestants and intent; check glucose, ECG and observations.

PLAB application: Treat the patient before waiting for a toxicology screen.

Poisoning history and risk

Ask about immediate versus modified release, body weight, alcohol, prescription medicines, access to other drugs, pregnancy and safeguarding. Obtain packaging without delaying care.

PLAB application: An unreliable ingestion history requires cautious risk assessment.

Paracetamol toxicity mechanism

Toxic metabolite NAPQI depletes glutathione and can cause delayed liver injury. Patients may initially be asymptomatic; symptoms do not predict safety.

PLAB application: A well-looking patient can still have serious paracetamol poisoning.

Paracetamol assessment

Obtain timed plasma paracetamol level for eligible single acute ingestions and interpret using the current UK treatment nomogram and local protocol. Check liver function, INR, renal function and acid–base status as indicated.

PLAB application: The nomogram is not valid for staggered or uncertain-time ingestions.

N-acetylcysteine treatment

N-acetylcysteine replenishes glutathione and is most effective when started promptly. Initiate according to current UK guidance and specialist advice; do not delay when significant risk is suspected.

PLAB application: Use TOXBASE/local protocol for indications and infusion regimen.

Staggered and delayed paracetamol ingestion

Repeated supratherapeutic, staggered or unknown-time ingestion needs specialist-guided assessment, blood tests and often prompt NAC; do not plot a single concentration on the acute nomogram.

PLAB application: Clarify ingestion pattern, not just total dose.

Opioid toxicity

Look for reduced consciousness, respiratory depression and often pinpoint pupils; mixed overdoses may alter signs. Prioritise airway support, ventilation and naloxone when indicated.

PLAB application: Restoring adequate breathing is the primary goal.

Naloxone and observation

Naloxone reverses opioid effects but may wear off before long-acting opioids. Titrate to adequate ventilation, monitor recurrence and consider infusion/specialist care for persistent toxicity.

PLAB application: Do not discharge solely because the patient wakes after naloxone.

Carbon monoxide poisoning

CO exposure causes headache, nausea, dizziness, confusion or collapse, sometimes in multiple people. Standard pulse oximetry can appear normal; use blood co-oximetry and administer high-concentration oxygen.

PLAB application: Normal SpO₂ does not rule out carbon monoxide poisoning.

Carbon monoxide escalation

Remove from exposure, give high-concentration oxygen and assess ECG, neurological and cardiac findings. Seek specialist advice on hyperbaric oxygen for severe poisoning, pregnancy or significant neurological/cardiac features.

PLAB application: Pregnancy lowers the threshold for specialist discussion.

Sedatives and benzodiazepines

Benzodiazepine overdose often causes drowsiness and ataxia; combined ingestion with alcohol or opioids increases respiratory risk. Support airway and breathing; flumazenil is generally avoided in mixed/unknown overdoses due to seizure risk.

PLAB application: Supportive care is the usual approach.

Stimulants and serotonin toxicity

Sympathomimetic toxicity can cause agitation, tachycardia, hypertension, hyperthermia and seizures. Serotonin syndrome features clonus, hyperreflexia and autonomic instability; stop serotonergic agents and provide urgent supportive care.

PLAB application: Clonus and hyperreflexia help distinguish serotonin toxicity.

Tricyclic antidepressant poisoning

TCA toxicity may cause anticholinergic features, seizures, hypotension and QRS widening. Obtain serial ECGs, seek toxicology help and use IV sodium bicarbonate for significant cardiotoxicity under protocol.

PLAB application: A widened QRS after TCA ingestion is a major red flag.

Salicylate and toxic alcohol emergencies

Salicylate poisoning can cause tinnitus, vomiting and mixed respiratory alkalosis/metabolic acidosis; toxic alcohols may cause severe acidosis and organ injury. Seek urgent specialist advice regarding alkalinisation, antidotes or dialysis.

PLAB application: Do not overlook anion gap and acid–base abnormalities.

Decontamination and antidotes

Activated charcoal has a limited, time-sensitive role for selected ingestions if the airway is protected; it is not routine. Never induce vomiting. Antidotes depend on the toxin and should follow current TOXBASE guidance.

PLAB application: Decontamination must not compromise airway safety.

Intentional overdose and PLAB 2

After medical stabilisation, assess self-harm risk, capacity, safeguarding and psychosocial needs; involve mental health liaison. Explain uncertainty, monitoring, antidotes and follow-up without judgement.

PLAB application: Every intentional self-poisoning needs compassionate psychosocial assessment.

PLAB 2 — suspected overdose

Stabilise airway and breathing, establish the substance and timing, check glucose and ECG, involve a senior and toxicology service, and explain investigations, antidotes, observation and psychosocial assessment with empathy.

Deep Dive — Toxicology

Why paracetamol can be deceptive

Initial absence of symptoms does not exclude serious toxicity. Use a reliable ingestion timeline, appropriate laboratory tests and UK-specific guidance; nomogram rules have important exclusions.

Opioid reversal is not the endpoint

The goal of naloxone is effective ventilation. Re-sedation can occur, especially with long-acting formulations or co-ingestants, requiring observation and sometimes repeated dosing.

Carbon monoxide and misleading pulse oximetry

Conventional pulse oximetry cannot distinguish oxyhaemoglobin from carboxyhaemoglobin. Exposure history, co-oximetry and organ injury assessment matter.

ECG in poisoning

QRS widening, QT prolongation, dysrhythmias and conduction abnormalities guide risk assessment and treatment in several overdoses. Repeat ECG when toxicity may evolve.

Antidotes and toxicology consultation

NAC, naloxone and sodium bicarbonate are examples of toxin-specific interventions; exact indications and dosing require up-to-date TOXBASE or specialist guidance.

Self-harm and safe discharge

Medical clearance alone is insufficient after intentional self-poisoning. Conduct appropriate psychosocial assessment, safeguarding and safety planning before discharge.
