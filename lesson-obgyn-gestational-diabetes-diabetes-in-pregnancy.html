Gestational Diabetes Mellitus & Diabetes in Pregnancy

Emergency: A pregnant patient with diabetes who is vomiting, unwell or has ketones needs urgent assessment for DKA, even if glucose is not very high.

Definitions

Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is hyperglycaemia first detected during pregnancy that does not meet criteria for overt pre-existing diabetes. Pre-existing type 1 and type 2 diabetes require different planning, especially before conception.

PLAB application: Distinguish GDM from known diabetes in a vignette.

Why pregnancy causes insulin resistance

Placental hormones increase maternal insulin resistance, particularly in later pregnancy. GDM develops when pancreatic insulin secretion cannot compensate.

PLAB application: Explain physiology without blaming the patient.

Risk factors for GDM

NICE risk factors include BMI 30 kg/m² or above, previous baby weighing 4.5 kg or more, previous GDM, first-degree relative with diabetes, and family origin with high diabetes prevalence.

PLAB application: Offer screening based on risk factors; do not use urine glucose as the diagnostic test.

Screening timing

At booking assess GDM risk. Offer a 75-g 2-hour oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) at 24–28 weeks for most women with risk factors. After previous GDM, offer early self-monitoring or OGTT as soon as possible after booking, and repeat OGTT at 24–28 weeks if the early result is normal.

PLAB application: Know the previous-GDM exception.

NICE diagnostic OGTT thresholds

NICE NG3 diagnoses GDM if fasting plasma glucose is 5.6 mmol/L or above OR 2-hour plasma glucose is 7.8 mmol/L or above after a 75-g OGTT. These are UK NICE thresholds, not interchangeable with other international criteria.

PLAB application: A single abnormal value meets the NICE diagnosis.

Initial assessment after diagnosis

Arrange joint diabetes and antenatal care promptly; review symptoms, gestational age, fetal growth, diet, activity, glucose results, and medicines. Provide education and equipment for capillary glucose monitoring.

PLAB application: Use a non-judgemental, collaborative consultation.

Blood glucose targets

NICE advises individualised targets, commonly fasting below 5.3 mmol/L; 1-hour after meals below 7.8 mmol/L; or 2-hour after meals below 6.4 mmol/L if 2-hour testing is used, provided hypoglycaemia is avoided.

PLAB application: Distinguish diagnostic OGTT cut-offs from treatment targets.

Lifestyle management

Offer dietary advice from an appropriately trained professional, regular physical activity where safe, and glucose self-monitoring. A short trial of diet and exercise may be appropriate for GDM with fasting OGTT glucose below 7.0 mmol/L.

PLAB application: Avoid restrictive diets that compromise maternal nutrition.

Metformin and insulin

For GDM with fasting OGTT glucose below 7.0 mmol/L, NICE recommends metformin if targets are not met after 1–2 weeks of diet and exercise; offer insulin if metformin is contraindicated or unacceptable. Insulin may be needed sooner with marked hyperglycaemia or complications.

PLAB application: Follow individualised specialist plans; discuss metformin crossing the placenta.

When insulin is offered immediately

NICE recommends immediate insulin, with or without metformin, if fasting OGTT glucose is 7.0 mmol/L or above. Consider immediate insulin if fasting is 6.0–6.9 mmol/L with complications such as macrosomia or hydramnios.

PLAB application: Do not delay escalation for severe hyperglycaemia.

Maternal and fetal risks

Risks include pre-eclampsia, polyhydramnios, large-for-gestational-age baby, birth trauma, caesarean birth, neonatal hypoglycaemia and later type 2 diabetes. Good control reduces several risks.

PLAB application: Counsel on risks accurately without implying inevitable harm.

Antenatal surveillance and delivery

Care is multidisciplinary. Growth and amniotic fluid ultrasound may be arranged according to local and NICE pathways. For uncomplicated GDM, NICE advises birth no later than 40+6 weeks; earlier birth may be considered for complications.

PLAB application: Do not give every woman the same delivery date.

Pre-existing diabetes and preconception care

For type 1 or type 2 diabetes, optimise glucose before conception, review medicines, prescribe high-dose folic acid 5 mg daily until 12 weeks under UK guidance, and assess retinal and renal health. Certain medicines require review before pregnancy.

PLAB application: Preconception HbA1c target is below 48 mmol/mol (6.5%) if achievable without problematic hypoglycaemia.

Type 1 diabetes and DKA

Pregnancy increases risk of diabetic ketoacidosis, sometimes at lower glucose levels. Vomiting, abdominal pain, ketones or illness in someone with type 1 diabetes requires urgent assessment. Never stop basal insulin during illness without specialist direction.

PLAB application: Recognise euglycaemic DKA risk in pregnancy.

Intrapartum and neonatal care

Monitor maternal capillary glucose hourly during labour and birth, aiming 4–7 mmol/L according to NICE, with insulin/dextrose infusion if indicated. Babies may require early feeding and blood glucose monitoring because of neonatal hypoglycaemia risk.

PLAB application: Coordinate delivery and newborn care with obstetric and neonatal teams.

After birth and long-term follow-up

For GDM, stop glucose-lowering therapy immediately after birth unless there is concern for persistent diabetes. Offer fasting plasma glucose at 6–13 weeks postpartum; if not done, HbA1c after 13 weeks may be used. Offer annual HbA1c testing and lifestyle support.

PLAB application: Explain future GDM and type 2 diabetes risk, and plan testing before the next pregnancy.

PLAB 2 — Explaining a new GDM diagnosis

Introduce yourself, confirm identity and gestation, explore concerns and baseline knowledge, explain placental insulin resistance, discuss monitoring and achievable diet/activity changes, outline metformin/insulin when indicated, and explain fetal surveillance and follow-up. Ask about health literacy and access to equipment. Safety-net for reduced fetal movements, severe illness, vomiting or ketones; arrange the joint diabetes–antenatal team.

Deep Dive

Understanding different glucose numbers

OGTT values diagnose GDM; home fasting and post-meal targets guide treatment. Do not confuse fasting diagnostic 5.6 mmol/L with fasting treatment target below 5.3 mmol/L.

Why neonatal hypoglycaemia occurs

Maternal hyperglycaemia drives fetal insulin secretion. After delivery maternal glucose supply stops abruptly while neonatal insulin can remain elevated, producing hypoglycaemia.

Why previous GDM changes screening

Recurrence risk is high; early testing helps detect pre-existing or early hyperglycaemia, but a normal early result does not eliminate the need for 24–28-week testing.

Metformin in pregnancy

NICE includes metformin as a treatment option in GDM and selected pre-existing type 2 diabetes contexts. Discuss benefits, uncertainty and alternatives in shared decisions; insulin is appropriate where targets are unmet or metformin is unsuitable.

Pre-existing type 1 diabetes

Requires intensive specialist planning: insulin, glucose technology where appropriate, retinal/renal assessment, preconception counselling and ketone sick-day advice.

Long-term health after GDM

GDM often resolves after delivery, but future type 2 diabetes risk remains elevated. Postpartum fasting glucose and ongoing annual HbA1c enable early detection.
