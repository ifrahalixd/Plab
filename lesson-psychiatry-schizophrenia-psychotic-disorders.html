Schizophrenia & Psychotic Disorders

PLAB priority: First-episode psychosis needs urgent specialist assessment. Always check medical causes, risk and safeguarding.

01. What is psychosis?

Psychosis is a syndrome involving impaired reality testing, often with hallucinations, delusions or disorganised thought. It may arise from psychiatric, substance-related or medical causes. Schizophrenia is one cause, not a synonym for psychosis.

PLAB application: In a first presentation, assess safety and exclude physical and substance-related causes.

02. Positive symptoms

Delusions are fixed beliefs not explained by cultural context and held despite contrary evidence. Hallucinations are perceptions without external stimuli; auditory voices are common. Thought interference and disorganised speech may occur.

PLAB application: Ask what the experience means to the person without arguing about its truth.

03. Negative symptoms

Avolition, reduced emotional expression, alogia, anhedonia and social withdrawal can be disabling and may be mistaken for depression or medication effects.

PLAB application: Assess functioning, self-care, education, work and relationships.

04. Disorganisation and catatonia

Disorganised thought may present as derailment or incoherent speech. Catatonia can include immobility, mutism, posturing or excitement and requires urgent specialist evaluation.

PLAB application: Do not assume a mute or immobile patient is simply refusing to cooperate.

05. Schizophrenia and diagnostic context

Diagnosis is made through longitudinal psychiatric assessment, considering characteristic symptoms, duration, functional impact and exclusions. UK practice uses ICD-based clinical diagnosis; no blood test confirms schizophrenia.

PLAB application: Avoid diagnosing schizophrenia after one brief episode without a full history.

06. Differential diagnosis

Consider bipolar mania, psychotic depression, brief psychotic disorder, delusional disorder, PTSD, autism-related phenomena, delirium, dementia, epilepsy, encephalitis, endocrine/metabolic disease and substance-induced psychosis.

PLAB application: New confusion, fluctuating attention, fever, seizures or abnormal observations suggest a medical cause.

07. First-episode psychosis

A suspected first episode needs prompt assessment by an early intervention in psychosis service; NICE recommends urgent referral. Obtain collateral history with consent when possible.

PLAB application: Ask about onset, sleep, mood, drugs, functioning and risk.

08. Risk assessment

Ask about suicide, self-harm, self-neglect, aggression, exploitation, safeguarding, command hallucinations and access to means. Explore intent and ability to resist commands, not just whether voices are present.

PLAB application: A command to harm someone plus intent or inability to resist requires urgent escalation.

09. Mental state examination

Document appearance, behaviour, speech, mood and affect, thought form/content, perception, cognition, insight and judgement. Use the patient’s own words when recording unusual beliefs or voices.

PLAB application: Check orientation and attention to help identify delirium.

10. Physical assessment and investigations

Perform observations, physical and neurological examination, medication and substance review, and targeted tests guided by history and examination. Baseline monitoring before antipsychotics includes weight/BMI, pulse, blood pressure, metabolic measures and movement assessment; ECG where indicated.

PLAB application: Do not delay emergency treatment of an unstable patient for routine investigations.

11. Antipsychotic treatment

Offer oral antipsychotic medication together with psychological interventions as part of shared decision-making. Discuss expected benefit, adverse effects and monitoring; choice depends on patient preference, prior response and risk profile.

PLAB application: Never present antipsychotics as a cure or omit side-effect counselling.

12. Adverse effects and monitoring

Watch for extrapyramidal symptoms, akathisia, tardive dyskinesia, weight gain, diabetes, dyslipidaemia, hyperprolactinaemia, sedation and QT effects. Neuroleptic malignant syndrome is rare but life-threatening: fever, rigidity, autonomic instability and altered consciousness.

PLAB application: Fever and rigidity after an antipsychotic require emergency assessment.

13. Psychological and social care

NICE recommends CBT for psychosis and family intervention, alongside support for employment, education, physical health, smoking cessation and social needs. Early intervention services support recovery.

PLAB application: Ask what recovery means to the patient and support collaborative goals.

14. Relapse prevention and adherence

Explore early warning signs, substance use, sleep changes, medication concerns and support networks. Consider long-acting injectable antipsychotics when preferred or when avoiding covert non-adherence is a priority.

PLAB application: Ask about missed doses non-judgementally and address reasons.

15. Treatment resistance and clozapine

Clozapine is recommended for schizophrenia that has not responded adequately to at least two antipsychotics, one being a non-clozapine second-generation agent. It requires specialist blood monitoring because of agranulocytosis risk, plus vigilance for myocarditis and severe constipation.

PLAB application: Clozapine is specialist initiated; do not overlook severe constipation or fever.

16. PLAB 2 communication and capacity

Introduce yourself, ensure privacy, listen calmly, avoid validating delusions as fact or confronting them, assess decision-specific capacity, explain treatment options and arrange appropriate safety-netting. Mental Health Act assessment may be needed when risks are high and admission is refused.

PLAB application: Use empathetic language: “That sounds frightening” rather than “The voices are real.”

Deep Dive

Dopamine and salience

Dopamine signalling is implicated in psychosis, but schizophrenia is not explained by a single chemical imbalance. Psychosocial and neurodevelopmental factors also matter.

Why negative symptoms matter

Negative symptoms can be more disabling than dramatic positive symptoms and require differentiation from depression, sedation and extrapyramidal effects.

Psychosis versus delirium

Delirium typically has acute onset and disturbed attention/awareness; it is a medical emergency. Psychosis can coexist with medical illness.

Antipsychotic adverse-effect reasoning

Dopamine receptor blockade may cause movement disorders and hyperprolactinaemia. Different agents have different metabolic, sedative and cardiac profiles.

Clozapine safety

Blood dyscrasias, myocarditis, seizures and severe gastrointestinal hypomotility are important. New fever, chest pain, tachycardia or constipation warrant prompt assessment.

Therapeutic communication

A calm, curious, non-judgemental approach helps build trust. Acknowledge distress without endorsing the content of unusual beliefs; make a collaborative safety plan.
