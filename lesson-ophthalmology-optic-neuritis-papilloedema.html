Optic Neuritis & Papilloedema

Urgent: New unexplained visual loss or suspected papilloedema requires urgent specialist assessment. New neurological deficits or severe headache warrant emergency evaluation.

Overview

Optic neuritis is inflammation of the optic nerve, commonly presenting with subacute unilateral visual loss and pain on eye movement. Papilloedema is optic disc swelling due to raised intracranial pressure.

PLAB application: These are different diagnoses with different urgent pathways.

Optic nerve anatomy

The optic nerve carries retinal ganglion cell axons to the brain. Lesions can impair acuity, colour vision and visual fields.

PLAB application: Check each eye separately.

Optic neuritis presentation

Visual loss typically develops over hours to days, often with periocular pain worsened by movement. Red colour desaturation is common.

PLAB application: Pain with eye movement and impaired colour vision are useful clues.

Optic neuritis examination

Assess acuity, colour, pupils for relative afferent pupillary defect (RAPD), fields and fundus. The disc may appear normal in retrobulbar neuritis.

PLAB application: A normal fundus does not exclude optic neuritis.

Optic neuritis associations

Demyelination including multiple sclerosis is a common association. Other causes include MOG antibody disease, neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder, infection and inflammatory disease.

PLAB application: Avoid assuming every case is multiple sclerosis.

Optic neuritis investigations

Urgent ophthalmology/neuro-ophthalmology evaluation often includes MRI of brain and orbits with contrast, and targeted investigations for atypical cases.

PLAB application: MRI can inform diagnosis and future neurological risk.

Optic neuritis treatment

Specialists may use high-dose corticosteroids to speed recovery in selected cases, but the approach depends on cause and severity. Avoid unsupervised low-dose oral steroid regimens.

PLAB application: Refer rather than starting empirical steroids in primary care.

Optic neuritis prognosis

Many typical cases improve over weeks, but persistent visual or colour deficits can occur. Atypical, bilateral or severe presentations need urgent specialist assessment.

PLAB application: Discuss uncertainty and follow-up.

Papilloedema definition

Papilloedema means optic disc swelling from raised intracranial pressure, often bilateral. Other causes of swollen discs must be distinguished.

PLAB application: Disc swelling is a sign, not a complete diagnosis.

Papilloedema symptoms

Headache, transient visual obscurations, pulsatile tinnitus, nausea and diplopia from sixth nerve palsy may occur. Vision may initially be preserved.

PLAB application: Normal visual acuity does not make papilloedema safe.

Papilloedema examination

Look for bilateral blurred disc margins, vessel obscuration and sometimes haemorrhages. Assess pupils, fields, acuity and cranial nerves.

PLAB application: Arrange urgent assessment for suspected raised ICP.

Causes of raised ICP

Consider intracranial mass, venous sinus thrombosis, hydrocephalus, infection and idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH).

PLAB application: Exclude dangerous secondary causes before labelling IIH.

Papilloedema urgent pathway

Suspected papilloedema needs urgent same-day specialist assessment, neuroimaging and evaluation for raised ICP. Lumbar puncture is considered only after appropriate imaging and specialist decision.

PLAB application: Do not perform lumbar puncture before excluding contraindications such as mass effect.

Idiopathic intracranial hypertension

IIH is raised intracranial pressure without an identified structural cause, commonly in women of childbearing age with obesity but possible in others.

PLAB application: Treatment may include weight management, acetazolamide and sight-saving interventions under specialist care.

Important differentials

Differentiate optic neuritis, papilloedema, ischaemic optic neuropathy, optic disc drusen and compressive optic neuropathy. In patients over 50 with headache, jaw claudication or systemic symptoms, consider giant cell arteritis.

PLAB application: Suspected GCA with visual symptoms needs immediate emergency assessment and treatment.

PLAB 2 counselling

Ask about onset, pain, headache, diplopia, transient obscurations, neurological symptoms and medicines. Examine vision, pupils, colour, fields and fundus; explain urgent referral and safety-netting.

PLAB application: New visual loss, worsening headache, vomiting or neurological deficits need immediate escalation.

PLAB 2 — Visual loss and headache consultation

Introduce yourself, obtain consent, explore onset, pain on eye movement, colour change, headache, vomiting, transient visual obscurations, diplopia and neurological symptoms. Check visual acuity, pupils, colour, fields, ocular movements and fundus. Explain the urgency of referral and safety-net deterioration.

Deep Dive

Optic nerve conduction

Inflammation and demyelination disrupt retinal signal transmission and can impair colour perception.

RAPD physiology

The swinging flashlight test compares afferent light responses; an RAPD suggests asymmetric afferent dysfunction.

Disc appearance

Retrobulbar inflammation can leave the visible optic disc normal early.

Papilloedema mechanism

Raised CSF pressure around the optic nerve disrupts axoplasmic transport and produces disc swelling.

Why imaging precedes LP

Intracranial mass or obstructive pathology may make lumbar puncture unsafe; evaluate urgently.

IIH and vision

Chronic papilloedema can cause permanent field loss; specialist monitoring is essential.
