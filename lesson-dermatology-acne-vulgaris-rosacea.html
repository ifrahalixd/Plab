Acne Vulgaris & Rosacea

PLAB priority: Distinguish comedonal acne from rosacea and prescribe safe, resistance-conscious treatment.

01. Acne mechanism

Acne involves follicular plugging, sebum, Cutibacterium acnes and inflammation. It is not caused by poor hygiene.

PLAB application: Explain the condition without blame.

02. Acne morphology

Open and closed comedones, papules, pustules, nodules and scarring affect face, chest and back.

PLAB application: Comedones support acne rather than rosacea.

03. Severity

Assess inflammatory lesions, nodules, scarring, pigment changes and psychological distress.

PLAB application: Scarring or severe distress warrants early escalation.

04. Differentials

Consider rosacea, perioral dermatitis, folliculitis and drug-induced acneiform eruptions.

PLAB application: Monomorphic lesions without comedones suggest a mimic.

05. Skin care

Use gentle non-comedogenic products and avoid picking or abrasive scrubbing.

PLAB application: Set realistic expectations: improvement takes weeks.

06. Benzoyl peroxide

Benzoyl peroxide reduces bacterial burden and resistance pressure; irritation and fabric bleaching are common.

PLAB application: Start gradually if irritation occurs.

07. Topical retinoids

Adapalene and other topical retinoids help comedonal acne but can irritate and are contraindicated in pregnancy or pregnancy planning.

PLAB application: Ask about pregnancy before prescribing.

08. Antibiotic stewardship

Avoid topical or oral antibiotic monotherapy for acne; combine with non-antibiotic topical treatment where indicated.

PLAB application: Review treatment at around 12 weeks.

09. Moderate acne

Combination topical therapy is common; more extensive inflammatory acne may need a time-limited oral antibiotic plus topical therapy.

PLAB application: Do not combine topical and oral antibiotics routinely.

10. Isotretinoin

Severe nodulocystic, scarring or resistant acne may need specialist isotretinoin with strict pregnancy-prevention and mental-health monitoring requirements.

PLAB application: Refer rather than initiating in routine primary care.

11. Rosacea features

Rosacea causes central facial flushing, persistent erythema, papules or pustules and telangiectasia, usually without comedones.

PLAB application: Differentiate from acne by flushing and absence of comedones.

12. Rosacea triggers

Sunlight, heat, alcohol, spicy foods and certain skin products can aggravate rosacea, with individual variation.

PLAB application: Recommend sunscreen and trigger tracking.

13. Rosacea treatment

Topical metronidazole, azelaic acid or ivermectin may be used for papulopustular rosacea; oral doxycycline is used selectively.

PLAB application: Avoid prolonged facial topical corticosteroids.

14. Ocular rosacea

Gritty, burning or red eyes may occur; painful red eye, photophobia or visual change needs urgent ophthalmic review.

PLAB application: Always ask about eye symptoms.

15. Acne and mental health

Acne can cause significant anxiety, low mood and social withdrawal regardless of visible severity.

PLAB application: Assess mental wellbeing and safeguarding concerns.

16. PLAB 2 counselling

Explain a 12-week treatment review, irritation management, pregnancy precautions and when to seek specialist help.

PLAB application: Shared decisions improve adherence.
