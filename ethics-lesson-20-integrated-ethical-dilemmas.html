Integrated ethical reasoning: PLAB 1 + PLAB 2

Core principle: Identify immediate danger, assess capacity for the specific decision, clarify the patient's wishes, consider confidentiality and safeguarding, act proportionately, escalate appropriately, and document.

1 · The eight-step ethical approach

  1. Stabilise emergencies and identify immediate risks.
  2. Establish the patient's identity, understanding and preferences.
  3. Assess decision-specific capacity if there is reason for doubt; presume capacity unless shown otherwise.
  4. Identify the decision, reasonable options, benefits, harms and material risks.
  5. Check for valid consent or refusal; do not equate an unwise choice with incapacity.
  6. Respect confidentiality; identify lawful justification before sharing without consent.
  7. Consider safeguarding, professional duties, equality and relevant jurisdictional law.
  8. Seek senior/MDT/legal advice when needed, communicate openly, and record rationale.

2 · Common conflicts

Autonomy vs beneficence: a capable adult may decline beneficial treatment. Confidentiality vs prevention of serious harm: disclosure may sometimes be justified, but must be necessary and proportionate. Family wishes vs patient's wishes: relatives do not automatically decide for a capable adult. Safety vs hierarchy: escalate serious risks even when the colleague is senior.

3 · Key UK qualifications

The Mental Capacity Act 2005 framework discussed here applies to England and Wales; Scotland and Northern Ireland have different legislation. Mental Health Act detention does not itself mean a person lacks capacity for every decision. Decisions about minors, abortion, safeguarding and data sharing require attention to applicable UK jurisdiction and current law.

4 · GMC anchors

Good medical practice (2024): put patient care first, communicate, work collaboratively, be honest, raise safety concerns. Decision making and consent (updated 2026): meaningful dialogue, material risks, reasonable alternatives, capacity and supported decision-making. GMC MLA content map: professional knowledge and capabilities including ethics, safeguarding, consent and confidentiality.

Integrated decision pathways

A · Treatment refusal

  1. Assess immediate danger and treat urgent reversible problems within lawful limits.
  2. Check understanding and capacity for this decision at this time.
  3. Explore values, reasons and communication barriers; explain risks and alternatives.
  4. If capable and voluntary, respect refusal even if serious harm may follow.
  5. If lacking capacity, check for applicable advance decisions or authorised decision-makers and make a lawful best-interests decision.
  6. Document discussion, assessment, safety-netting and escalation.

B · Confidentiality and risk

  1. Clarify what information is requested, by whom and why.
  2. Seek patient consent when appropriate and safe.
  3. Check legal duties and potential public-interest justification.
  4. Share the minimum necessary information with the appropriate recipient.
  5. Explain to the patient where practicable, and document the rationale.

C · Clinical error or unsafe colleague

  1. Protect patients and address immediate harm.
  2. Inform the appropriate senior and follow local incident pathways.
  3. Be open and apologise when something has gone wrong; explain known facts without speculation.
  4. Escalate persistent safety concerns through appropriate channels.
  5. Record accurately, reflect and support prevention.

Four integrated PLAB clinical cases

Case 1 · Sepsis and refusal

A 46-year-old with severe infection refuses admission. Best approach: assess and support decision-specific capacity, explain likely consequences and alternatives; if they have capacity, respect refusal and provide safety-netting. Severe illness alone does not prove incapacity.

Case 2 · HIV and confidentiality

A patient with HIV refuses to tell a sexual partner. Best approach: explore risk, counsel and support voluntary disclosure, seek specialist/senior advice and consider whether a narrowly justified disclosure is lawful; do not automatically inform the partner or promise absolute secrecy.

Case 3 · Confused older patient

A delirious patient pulls out a cannula; a relative demands sedation. Best approach: manage reversible causes, assess capacity for the relevant decision, use least restrictive measures, consult those close to the patient about wishes, and apply the lawful best-interests framework. Family preference alone is not consent.

Case 4 · Wrong-dose medication

A colleague administers ten times the intended dose. Best approach: assess and treat the patient immediately, call for help, report the incident, communicate openly and apologise appropriately, document and review the error. Never conceal it to protect the team.

PLAB 2 OSCE · Integrated ethical consultation

Station: A 62-year-old with suspected bowel obstruction refuses hospital admission. Their adult daughter asks you to override the refusal and to discuss the patient's records privately.

Suggested approach

  1. Introduce yourself, verify identity, ensure privacy and assess clinical urgency.
  2. Ask what the patient understands about the illness and admission recommendation.
  3. Explore why they refuse; check for pain, delirium, communication barriers or coercion.
  4. Assess capacity only if there is reason to doubt it: understand, retain, use/weigh, communicate.
  5. Explain material risks, including deterioration, and reasonable options in plain language.
  6. If the patient has capacity, respect the decision and offer a clear safety-net plan.
  7. Ask permission before sharing clinical details with the daughter; explain confidentiality.
  8. Document assessment, advice, refusal, support and follow-up; seek senior help for uncertainty.

Examiner checklist

Empathy · immediate safety · patient autonomy · capacity · alternatives · confidentiality · safety-netting · documentation · appropriate escalation.

Exam trap: Family disagreement does not by itself invalidate a capable adult's decision.

Rapid revision slides

Slide 1 · First question

Is anyone at immediate risk? Protect safety first.

Slide 2 · Capacity

Presume capacity; assess the particular decision and time; support understanding; unwise ≠ incapable.

Slide 3 · Consent

Meaningful dialogue, material risks, alternatives including no treatment; voluntary choice.

Slide 4 · Confidentiality

Consent where possible; share only with justification, for an appropriate purpose and proportionately.

Slide 5 · Patient safety

Act on harm, report concerns, be candid, document facts and escalate.

Slide 6 · Ethics traps

Family ≠ automatic proxy. Detention ≠ universal incapacity. Signature ≠ informed consent. Poor outcome ≠ automatic negligence.
