Eyelid Disorders, Blepharitis, Stye & Chalazion

Emergency: Proptosis, painful or restricted eye movements, reduced vision or systemic illness with a swollen eyelid suggests orbital cellulitis. Urgent hospital assessment is required.

Overview

Blepharitis is chronic inflammation of the eyelid margins; a hordeolum (stye) is an acute painful infected or inflamed eyelid gland; a chalazion is a usually painless meibomian gland blockage.

PLAB application: Differentiate by pain, location and time course.

Eyelid anatomy

Eyelashes, Zeis and Moll glands and meibomian glands contribute to the tear film. Meibomian dysfunction often accompanies posterior blepharitis.

PLAB application: Examine lid margins and evert lids when appropriate.

Anterior blepharitis

Inflammation at lash bases causes crusting, irritation and gritty eyes. Staphylococcal and seborrhoeic processes may contribute.

PLAB application: Look for scales and crusts around eyelashes.

Posterior blepharitis

Meibomian gland dysfunction causes oily or blocked gland openings, evaporative dry eye and lid margin inflammation.

PLAB application: Assess associated rosacea and dry eye.

Blepharitis symptoms

Burning, itching, crusting on waking, red lid margins and fluctuating blurred vision are common. Symptoms may recur.

PLAB application: Ask about contact lenses, skin conditions and chronicity.

Blepharitis management

Regular warm compresses and gentle lid hygiene are the mainstays. Lubricants can relieve associated dry eye; selected cases need clinician-directed medicines.

PLAB application: Explain that daily care may be needed long term.

Stye (hordeolum)

An external stye affects lash-associated glands; an internal stye affects a meibomian gland. It is usually a tender red focal swelling.

PLAB application: Avoid squeezing or attempting to lance at home.

Stye treatment

Warm compresses several times daily and simple analgesia are often sufficient. Antibiotics are not routinely needed for an uncomplicated stye.

PLAB application: Escalate spreading redness, fever or visual symptoms.

Chalazion

A chalazion is a localised lipogranulomatous reaction to a blocked meibomian gland, typically a firm, relatively painless nodule.

PLAB application: Distinguish from an acutely tender stye.

Chalazion management

Warm compresses and gentle massage may help; persistent or visually significant lesions can be referred for ophthalmic treatment.

PLAB application: Do not assume every lump needs antibiotics.

Recurrent or atypical lump

A recurrent lesion in the same location, lash loss, ulceration or an atypical firm lid mass can indicate sebaceous carcinoma or another tumour.

PLAB application: Arrange ophthalmology assessment for suspicious lesions.

Preseptal cellulitis

Infection anterior to the orbital septum causes eyelid erythema, warmth and swelling without orbital signs.

PLAB application: Assess carefully for orbital involvement and systemic illness.

Orbital cellulitis

Painful or restricted eye movements, proptosis, diplopia, reduced vision or RAPD indicate possible postseptal infection.

PLAB application: Emergency hospital referral for imaging, IV antibiotics and specialist care.

Other eyelid disorders

Entropion turns the lid inward; ectropion turns it outward; trichiasis causes lashes to rub the cornea. Ptosis may have neurological causes.

PLAB application: New ptosis with diplopia, pupil changes or headache is urgent.

Examination

Check visual acuity if symptoms suggest eye involvement; inspect lid position, lashes, skin, conjunctiva, cornea and ocular motility. Assess fever and orbital red flags.

PLAB application: Do not miss keratitis or orbital cellulitis.

PLAB 2 counselling

Explain warm compresses, lid cleaning, hand hygiene and avoiding eye makeup during active inflammation. Safety-net worsening swelling, fever, painful eye movements or reduced vision.

PLAB application: Use clear, practical self-care instructions.

PLAB 2 — Eyelid lump and irritation

Introduce yourself, confirm identity, obtain consent and ask about pain, duration, recurrence, visual symptoms, fever and skin disease. Examine lids, lashes, acuity and eye movements as indicated. Explain stye versus chalazion, demonstrate warm compresses and lid hygiene, and safety-net orbital signs or suspicious lesions.

Deep Dive

Tear film and meibomian glands

Meibomian lipids limit tear evaporation; dysfunction promotes evaporative dry eye and inflammation.

Hordeolum versus chalazion

A stye is acute and painful; a chalazion is typically chronic and minimally tender. An internal stye can later form a chalazion.

Why compresses help

Gentle warmth softens meibum and supports drainage. Avoid excessive heat and forceful squeezing.

Orbital septum

The septum separates anterior eyelid infection from deeper orbital infection; postseptal spread can threaten sight and life.

Malignancy mimic

Persistent or recurrent unilateral lid nodules, lash loss or ulceration warrant assessment for sebaceous carcinoma or other eyelid tumours.

Clinical prioritisation

Visual decline, proptosis, painful eye movement, fever with systemic illness or acute neurological signs override routine self-care pathways.
