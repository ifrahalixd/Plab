Acute & Chronic Pancreatitis

Emergency pearl: Pancreatitis with shock, hypoxaemia, oliguria or confusion needs immediate escalation.

Overview

Acute pancreatitis is sudden pancreatic inflammation, commonly due to gallstones or alcohol. Chronic pancreatitis is persistent fibro-inflammatory damage with irreversible structural changes.

PLAB application: Acute disease can be life-threatening; chronic disease causes pain, malabsorption and diabetes.

Acute causes

Gallstones and alcohol are common causes. Also consider hypertriglyceridaemia, hypercalcaemia, ERCP, medications, trauma and less common autoimmune or genetic causes.

PLAB application: Take an alcohol and medication history; arrange biliary imaging and relevant blood tests.

Presentation

Acute pancreatitis causes severe persistent epigastric pain often radiating to the back, nausea and vomiting. Severe illness may cause tachycardia, hypotension, hypoxaemia and organ dysfunction.

PLAB application: Use ABCDE and assess for shock, sepsis and respiratory compromise.

Diagnosis

Acute pancreatitis is usually diagnosed when at least two are present: characteristic pain, amylase/lipase at least three times the upper limit of normal, or compatible imaging.

PLAB application: Enzyme levels do not reliably measure severity; normal or modest values do not always exclude pancreatitis in late presentation.

Initial investigations

Check FBC, U&Es, creatinine, liver tests, calcium, glucose, CRP and triglycerides when indicated. Ultrasound assesses gallstones and bile duct dilatation.

PLAB application: CT is not routinely needed immediately when diagnosis is clear; use imaging for uncertainty or suspected complications.

Severity and escalation

Monitor pulse, BP, urine output, oxygen saturation, renal function, inflammatory markers and organ dysfunction. Persistent organ failure defines severe acute pancreatitis in revised Atlanta classification.

PLAB application: Urgent senior, gastroenterology, surgical or critical care input for deterioration.

Initial treatment

Give prompt goal-directed IV crystalloid resuscitation when indicated, analgesia, antiemetics and monitoring. Avoid both under-resuscitation and fluid overload.

PLAB application: No routine prophylactic antibiotics in uncomplicated acute pancreatitis.

Nutrition

Offer oral feeding early as tolerated in mild acute pancreatitis. In severe disease use enteral nutrition when oral intake is inadequate, with specialist input.

PLAB application: Do not keep every patient nil by mouth for prolonged periods without a reason.

Gallstone pancreatitis

Ultrasound and liver tests assess biliary cause. ERCP is urgent when associated cholangitis or persistent biliary obstruction is present; not routine for all gallstone pancreatitis.

PLAB application: Offer cholecystectomy during the same admission for suitable mild gallstone pancreatitis after stabilisation.

Local complications

Peripancreatic fluid collections, pseudocysts, acute necrotic collections and walled-off necrosis can develop. Infection of necrosis requires specialist management.

PLAB application: Avoid assuming early collections are pseudocysts; classification depends on timing and contents.

Systemic complications

Acute kidney injury, ARDS, shock, sepsis and multi-organ failure may occur.

PLAB application: Escalate rapidly if oxygenation, urine output, blood pressure or consciousness deteriorate.

Chronic pancreatitis causes

Long-term alcohol exposure and smoking are major risk factors; also consider genetic, obstructive, autoimmune and idiopathic causes.

PLAB application: Smoking cessation and alcohol abstinence are central to risk reduction.

Chronic presentation and diagnosis

Recurrent or persistent upper abdominal pain, steatorrhoea, weight loss and diabetes suggest chronic pancreatitis. CT, MRI/MRCP or endoscopic ultrasound may identify calcification or ductal changes.

PLAB application: Assess nutritional status and fat-soluble vitamin deficiency.

Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency

Loss of digestive enzymes causes bulky oily stools, weight loss and malnutrition. Faecal elastase can support diagnosis.

PLAB application: Pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy is taken with meals and snacks; dietitian support is helpful.

Endocrine failure and long-term care

Chronic pancreatitis can cause pancreatogenic (type 3c) diabetes, brittle glycaemic control, osteoporosis and chronic pain.

PLAB application: Coordinate specialist diabetes, nutrition, pain and psychological care; assess for complications.

PLAB 2 counselling

Explain inflammation versus permanent damage, likely triggers, investigations, pain relief and follow-up. Discuss alcohol and smoking sensitively.

PLAB application: Safety-net severe persistent pain, fever, jaundice, vomiting, fainting or breathlessness.

PLAB 2: Pancreatitis counselling OSCE

  1. Introduce yourself and explore symptoms, alcohol use, gallstone history and concerns.
  2. Explain acute inflammation versus long-term pancreatic damage in plain language.
  3. Describe blood tests, ultrasound, supportive treatment and monitoring.
  4. Discuss alcohol abstinence, smoking cessation and nutritional support sensitively.
  5. Safety-net worsening pain, fever, jaundice, persistent vomiting, fainting or breathlessness.

Mechanisms and clinical reasoning

Deep Dive

Trypsin activation

Premature intrapancreatic enzyme activation contributes to acinar injury and inflammatory cascades.

Systemic inflammation

Inflammatory mediators can trigger capillary leak, hypovolaemia, ARDS, AKI and shock.

Revised Atlanta classification

Mild: no organ failure or complications; moderately severe: transient organ failure or complications; severe: persistent organ failure over 48 hours.

Collections and timing

Early collections differ from mature pseudocysts or walled-off necrosis, typically defined after around four weeks.

Chronic pancreatic failure

Progressive fibrosis destroys acinar and islet tissue, leading to steatorrhoea and type 3c diabetes.

PERT practicalities

Enzymes are taken with meals/snacks; dose titration and adherence are reviewed by specialists or dietitians.
