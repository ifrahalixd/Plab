Leukaemia: ALL, AML, CLL & CML

Emergency: Suspected acute promyelocytic leukaemia, neutropenic sepsis, severe bleeding or leukostasis needs immediate escalation.

Overview

Leukaemia is a clonal malignancy of blood-forming cells. Acute leukaemia progresses rapidly with immature blasts; chronic leukaemia often has a more indolent presentation.

PLAB application: Recognise unexplained cytopenias and systemic symptoms.

Four major types

ALL and AML are acute lymphoid and myeloid leukaemias; CLL and CML are chronic lymphoid and myeloid neoplasms.

PLAB application: Use lineage and tempo to classify.

Red flags

Fatigue, infections, bruising, bleeding, bone pain, weight loss, fever, night sweats and lymphadenopathy warrant assessment.

PLAB application: Urgently assess severe bleeding, sepsis or breathlessness.

Initial investigations

Request FBC with differential and blood film, renal and liver function, LDH, urate and coagulation studies as indicated.

PLAB application: Normal or low white cell count does not exclude acute leukaemia.

Blood film

Circulating blasts suggest acute leukaemia. Auer rods support myeloid lineage. Marked mature lymphocytosis can occur in CLL.

PLAB application: Urgent haematology discussion for suspected acute leukaemia.

Confirming diagnosis

Bone marrow morphology, flow cytometry, cytogenetics and molecular testing define subtype and guide treatment.

PLAB application: Do not rely on blood count alone for definitive classification.

ALL

Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia is common in children but occurs in adults. Bone pain, cytopenias and lymphadenopathy may occur; CNS involvement is important.

PLAB application: Treatment is specialist multi-agent therapy with CNS-directed prophylaxis.

AML

Acute myeloid leukaemia is more common in older adults and often presents with marrow failure, infection or bleeding.

PLAB application: Urgent specialist assessment is essential.

Acute promyelocytic leukaemia

APL is an AML subtype associated with PML::RARA and life-threatening coagulopathy/DIC.

PLAB application: Suspected APL is a haematological emergency: immediate specialist discussion and prompt ATRA under protocol.

CLL

Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia usually affects older adults and may be found incidentally as persistent lymphocytosis. It can cause lymphadenopathy, recurrent infections and autoimmune haemolysis.

PLAB application: Asymptomatic early CLL often needs monitoring rather than immediate treatment.

CML

Chronic myeloid leukaemia is driven by BCR::ABL1 (Philadelphia chromosome), often causing granulocytic leukocytosis and splenomegaly.

PLAB application: Tyrosine kinase inhibitors are the foundation of chronic-phase treatment.

CML monitoring

Quantitative BCR::ABL1 PCR monitors molecular response and treatment effectiveness.

PLAB application: Discuss adherence and regular blood monitoring.

Neutropenic sepsis

A patient receiving anti-cancer treatment with suspected neutropenic sepsis needs immediate emergency assessment and empiric IV antibiotics per NICE/local protocol.

PLAB application: Do not wait for neutrophil count before escalation.

Tumour lysis syndrome

Rapid cell breakdown can cause hyperkalaemia, hyperphosphataemia, hypocalcaemia, hyperuricaemia and acute kidney injury.

PLAB application: Monitor electrolytes and renal function; urgent specialist management.

Other emergencies

Leukostasis, hyperviscosity, DIC, severe thrombocytopenic bleeding and spinal cord compression are possible complications.

PLAB application: ABCDE, stabilise and contact haematology urgently.

PLAB 2 counselling

Explain the need for urgent tests, distinguish suspicion from confirmed diagnosis, ask about infection and bleeding, and discuss specialist-led treatment and support.

PLAB application: Safety-net fever, new bleeding, breathlessness and acute deterioration.

PLAB 2 — Explaining suspected leukaemia

Ask about fatigue, infections, bruising, bleeding, weight loss and night sweats. Explain that abnormal blood results require urgent specialist tests, not that cancer is already confirmed. Discuss immediate warning signs, support and the plan for haematology referral.

Deep Dive

Marrow failure

Leukaemic cells can suppress normal erythropoiesis, granulopoiesis and platelet production, leading to fatigue, infection and bleeding.

Acute leukaemia genetics

Cytogenetic and molecular findings define subtypes, risk and targeted treatment options.

APL coagulopathy

APL is strongly associated with life-threatening DIC and bleeding; rapid recognition is crucial.

CML mechanism

BCR::ABL1 encodes a constitutively active tyrosine kinase that drives myeloid proliferation.

CLL natural history

CLL is often slowly progressive; treating asymptomatic early disease does not routinely improve outcomes.

Treatment emergencies

Neutropenic sepsis, tumour lysis, leukostasis and severe bleeding demand rapid multidisciplinary action.
