Parkinson's Disease & Movement Disorders

PLAB priority: Recognise bradykinesia, distinguish tremor types, and never omit time-critical Parkinson medicines.

01. Recognising parkinsonism

Parkinsonism is bradykinesia with rest tremor and/or rigidity. Parkinson disease is the commonest neurodegenerative cause. Examine gait, arm swing, posture and facial expression.

PLAB pearl: Bradykinesia is essential for clinical parkinsonism; tremor is not obligatory.

02. Clinical presentation

Asymmetric rest tremor, slowness, stiffness, reduced arm swing, micrographia, hypomimia, soft voice and shuffling gait may occur.

PLAB pearl: Ask how symptoms affect dressing, walking and daily activities.

03. Non-motor symptoms

Constipation, anosmia, REM sleep behaviour disorder, depression, anxiety, orthostatic hypotension, urinary symptoms and cognitive change may precede motor symptoms.

PLAB pearl: A good Parkinson history includes non-motor symptoms.

04. Examination

Assess finger tapping and hand opening for decrement, wrist rigidity with activation, rest and postural tremor, rising from chair, walking and turning. Assess postural stability safely.

PLAB pearl: Do not perform a pull test without appropriate supervision and safeguards.

05. Diagnosis and referral

Diagnosis is primarily clinical. NICE NG71 advises referring suspected Parkinson disease quickly and untreated to a specialist with expertise in differential diagnosis.

PLAB pearl: Do not start treatment before specialist assessment when referral is feasible.

06. Differential diagnosis

Essential tremor is mainly action/postural; drug-induced parkinsonism follows dopamine-blocking medicines; atypical syndromes include PSP, MSA and corticobasal syndrome.

PLAB pearl: Early falls, severe autonomic failure or gaze palsy are atypical warning signs.

07. Levodopa

Offer levodopa when motor symptoms affect quality of life, following specialist assessment. It is converted to dopamine and given with a peripheral decarboxylase inhibitor.

PLAB pearl: Levodopa usually provides the strongest motor benefit.

08. Other drug options

Dopamine agonists and MAO-B inhibitors may be options for selected patients. Discuss impulse-control disorders, hallucinations, somnolence and other adverse effects.

PLAB pearl: Ask directly about gambling, shopping and hypersexuality with dopamine agonists.

09. Wearing off and dyskinesia

With progression, levodopa benefit may shorten and involuntary movements may emerge. Specialist review can adjust dose frequency or adjuncts.

PLAB pearl: Do not independently stop or abruptly alter dopaminergic medication.

10. Medication timing and hospital safety

Give Parkinson medicines on time. Avoid dopamine-blocking antiemetics and antipsychotics where possible; obtain specialist advice. Abrupt withdrawal can cause severe deterioration.

PLAB pearl: Medication omissions in hospital can be dangerous.

11. Rehabilitation and supportive care

Physiotherapy for gait and balance, occupational therapy for function, speech and language therapy for voice/swallowing and exercise programmes improve support.

PLAB pearl: Assess falls, swallowing, nutrition and carer needs.

12. Advanced Parkinson disease

Motor fluctuations, freezing, falls, hallucinations, dementia and dysphagia may develop. Specialist therapies include device-aided treatments and deep brain stimulation in selected patients.

PLAB pearl: Complex fluctuations require a multidisciplinary specialist plan.

13. Essential tremor

Usually bilateral action or postural tremor of hands, sometimes head or voice; no typical bradykinesia. Consider medication, thyroid disease and functional impairment.

PLAB pearl: Propranolol or primidone may be considered after assessing contraindications.

14. Other movement disorders

Dystonia causes sustained or intermittent patterned muscle contractions; chorea causes irregular flowing movements; tics are often suppressible; myoclonus is sudden brief jerking.

PLAB pearl: Describe the movement precisely before naming the diagnosis.

15. Urgent red flags

Sudden new movement abnormality, acute focal deficits, severe rigidity with fever, altered mental state, or rapidly progressive gait/cognitive change needs urgent assessment.

PLAB pearl: Consider stroke, drug toxicity, infection and neuroleptic malignant syndrome.

16. PLAB 2 Parkinson consultation

Introduce yourself, obtain consent, ask about onset, falls, function, medication timing, mood, sleep and cognition. Explain the diagnosis sensitively, arrange specialist review and safety-net.

PLAB pearl: Use shared decision-making and address driving and occupational safety.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Basal ganglia circuits

Loss of substantia nigra dopaminergic neurons disrupts motor circuit signalling and produces bradykinesia and rigidity.

Lewy pathology

Alpha-synuclein aggregation is associated with Parkinson disease, although diagnosis in life remains primarily clinical.

Motor fluctuations

Pulsatile dopaminergic stimulation and disease progression contribute to wearing off and dyskinesia.

Atypical parkinsonism

PSP, MSA and corticobasal syndromes may show early red flags and poorer levodopa response.

Autonomic and cognitive effects

Non-motor symptoms reflect involvement of pathways beyond nigrostriatal dopamine and need active assessment.

Safe prescribing

Dopaminergic medications are time critical; dopamine-blocking agents may worsen motor symptoms.
