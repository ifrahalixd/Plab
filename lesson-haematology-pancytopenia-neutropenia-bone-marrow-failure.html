Pancytopenia, Neutropenia & Bone Marrow Failure

Emergency: Suspected neutropenic sepsis, active bleeding or acute leukaemia requires urgent clinical assessment and escalation.

01. Overview and definitions

Pancytopenia is a reduction in red cells, white cells and platelets. Neutropenia is a reduced absolute neutrophil count (ANC); severe neutropenia is generally ANC below 0.5 × 10⁹/L. Bone marrow failure may result from aplastic anaemia, infiltration, dysplasia or treatment toxicity.

PLAB application: Start with clinical stability and a full blood count (FBC) with differential; verify abnormalities and review previous counts.

02. Immediate red flags

Fever or systemic illness with neutropenia, active bleeding, haemodynamic instability, severe symptomatic anaemia, blasts on blood film, or suspected acute promyelocytic leukaemia require urgent escalation.

PLAB application: Do not delay sepsis treatment while waiting for repeat blood counts or specialist investigations.

03. Why pancytopenia occurs

Reduced marrow production (aplastic anaemia, B12/folate deficiency, myelodysplastic syndromes, drugs, infection), marrow replacement (leukaemia, lymphoma, metastases), and peripheral sequestration (hypersplenism) are major mechanisms.

PLAB application: Think production, destruction/sequestration, and dilution; reticulocyte count helps assess marrow response.

04. History

Ask about infections and fever, bruising/bleeding, fatigue, weight loss, night sweats, recurrent mouth ulcers, medications (chemotherapy, antithyroid drugs, clozapine), alcohol, nutrition, travel, autoimmune disease and family history.

PLAB application: Ask explicitly about recent chemotherapy and time since the last cycle.

05. Examination

Use ABCDE if unwell. Record temperature, pulse, blood pressure, oxygen saturation. Look for pallor, petechiae, mucositis, lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly and signs of infection.

PLAB application: In suspected neutropenic sepsis, a normal or low temperature does not exclude serious infection.

06. First-line investigations

Repeat FBC and differential, blood film, reticulocyte count, renal/liver profile, B12/folate, ferritin/iron studies, haemolysis tests where indicated, CRP, and targeted viral testing. Blood cultures and lactate are urgent in suspected sepsis.

PLAB application: Blood film showing blasts, schistocytes or dysplasia changes the urgency and differential.

07. Interpreting the blood film

Macro-ovalocytes and hypersegmented neutrophils suggest megaloblastic deficiency; blasts suggest acute leukaemia; dysplastic cells may suggest myelodysplasia. A normal-looking film does not exclude serious marrow disease.

PLAB application: Avoid assuming pancytopenia is nutritional without checking the clinical context.

08. Reticulocyte count and marrow response

A low reticulocyte count suggests impaired production. A high count may reflect bleeding or haemolysis, though concurrent marrow pathology can blunt the response. Interpret with haemoglobin and clinical history.

PLAB application: Use absolute reticulocyte count rather than reticulocyte percentage alone where possible.

09. Aplastic anaemia

Acquired aplastic anaemia is failure of haematopoietic stem cells, usually with hypocellular marrow and cytopenias. It can be immune-mediated, drug-related or rarely inherited. Patients develop infection, bleeding and anaemia symptoms.

PLAB application: Urgent haematology referral; treatment may include immunosuppression or stem-cell transplantation depending on severity and suitability.

10. Myelodysplastic neoplasms

Clonal marrow disorders causing ineffective blood-cell production, often in older adults, with variable progression risk to AML. Diagnosis uses marrow morphology, cytogenetics and molecular testing.

PLAB application: Persistent unexplained cytopenia with dysplastic blood film warrants specialist assessment.

11. Marrow infiltration and malignancy

Acute leukaemia, lymphoma, myeloma and metastatic cancer can impair blood production. Constitutional symptoms, bone pain, lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly or blasts may point toward these causes.

PLAB application: Do not delay referral if acute leukaemia is suspected.

12. Neutropenia causes

Chemotherapy, viral infection, medication-induced agranulocytosis, autoimmune disease, nutritional deficiency, marrow disorders and benign ancestry-associated low neutrophil counts can contribute. Interpret ANC in context and over time.

PLAB application: A patient taking carbimazole with fever or sore throat needs urgent FBC and clinical assessment.

13. Neutropenic sepsis

Suspect in a patient receiving anticancer treatment who becomes unwell, especially with ANC ≤0.5 × 10⁹/L. NICE guidance defines neutropenic sepsis in anticancer-treatment patients using ANC ≤0.5 and temperature >38°C or other signs of clinically significant sepsis.

PLAB application: Treat as an emergency: immediate assessment, cultures when feasible without delaying antibiotics, and prompt empiric IV antibiotics per local protocol.

14. Managing febrile neutropenia

Assess ABCDE, obtain blood cultures, monitor lactate and organ function, start broad-spectrum IV antibiotics promptly, give fluids if indicated and involve oncology/haematology. Examine for infection sources without delaying treatment.

PLAB application: NICE CG151 recommends piperacillin–tazobactam as initial empiric monotherapy in appropriate patients, unless contraindicated or local guidance differs.

15. Bone marrow biopsy and specialist testing

Bone marrow aspirate/trephine is considered when cytopenias are unexplained or marrow disease suspected. Flow cytometry, cytogenetics and molecular tests are selected by haematology.

PLAB application: Explain discomfort, bleeding/infection risk, consent and why the test may clarify diagnosis.

16. Long-term care and PLAB 2

Treatment targets the cause: replace deficiencies, adjust causative drugs with specialist advice, treat infection, and provide transfusion support when indicated. Discuss infection precautions, urgent fever assessment, medication safety and follow-up.

PLAB application: Safety-net: fever, rigors, new bleeding, breathlessness or rapidly worsening fatigue require urgent review.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Marrow stem-cell biology

Haematopoietic stem cells generate erythroid, myeloid and megakaryocyte lineages. Damage to progenitors can cause simultaneous cytopenias.

Reticulocyte interpretation

A low absolute reticulocyte count despite anaemia suggests reduced erythropoiesis. High counts suggest compensatory production, unless other marrow disease coexists.

Aplastic anaemia versus MDS

Aplastic anaemia often has hypocellular marrow; MDS involves clonal dysplasia and ineffective haematopoiesis, with cellularity varying. Diagnosis requires specialist marrow assessment.

Neutropenic sepsis pathophysiology

Low neutrophils impair innate bacterial and fungal defence. Infection may present without marked local inflammation or typical signs.

Medication-related neutropenia

Chemotherapy predictably suppresses marrow; some medicines cause idiosyncratic agranulocytosis. Take a careful medication history and follow drug-specific safety advice.

PLAB 2 explanation

“Your blood test shows low levels of several types of blood cells. We need to look for the cause, assess your infection and bleeding risks, and sometimes ask the blood specialist to test the bone marrow.”
