Cushing Syndrome & Addison Disease

Emergency: Suspected adrenal crisis requires immediate parenteral hydrocortisone and resuscitation. Do not wait for test results.

Overview

Cushing syndrome is sustained glucocorticoid excess; adrenal insufficiency is inadequate cortisol production. Both may be life-threatening.

PLAB application: Check for exogenous steroid exposure before assuming endogenous Cushing syndrome.

Cushing causes

Exogenous prescribed glucocorticoids are the commonest overall cause. Endogenous causes include pituitary ACTH adenoma (Cushing disease), ectopic ACTH and adrenal cortisol-producing tumours.

PLAB application: Cushing disease specifically refers to pituitary ACTH excess.

Cushing clinical features

Central adiposity, facial rounding, proximal weakness, easy bruising, wide purple striae, thin skin, hypertension, diabetes, osteoporosis, mood changes and infection risk.

PLAB application: Progressive proximal myopathy, easy bruising and wide violaceous striae are discriminating features.

Cushing screening

In a patient with convincing progressive features, specialist-directed tests include late-night salivary cortisol, 24-hour urinary free cortisol or overnight dexamethasone suppression.

PLAB application: Random serum cortisol is not a screening test for Cushing syndrome.

Cushing investigation

After biochemical confirmation, ACTH helps distinguish ACTH-dependent from ACTH-independent disease; pituitary or adrenal imaging follows specialist interpretation.

PLAB application: Avoid imaging before biochemical confirmation because incidentalomas are common.

Cushing management

Treat the underlying cause with endocrine MDT input; pituitary or adrenal surgery may be indicated. Manage hypertension, diabetes, bone health, infection and thrombosis risk.

PLAB application: Do not abruptly stop long-term prescribed steroids.

Primary adrenal insufficiency

Addison disease is primary adrenal failure, often autoimmune in the UK; other causes include infection, haemorrhage and infiltration. Cortisol and often aldosterone are deficient.

PLAB application: Look for hyperpigmentation, postural hypotension, salt craving, weight loss and fatigue.

Secondary or tertiary adrenal insufficiency

Pituitary/hypothalamic disease or suppression of the HPA axis by glucocorticoid therapy can reduce ACTH and cortisol. Aldosterone is usually preserved.

PLAB application: Hyperpigmentation and marked hyperkalaemia are less typical than in primary adrenal insufficiency.

Symptoms and examination

Chronic adrenal insufficiency can cause fatigue, anorexia, weight loss, nausea, abdominal pain, dizziness and postural hypotension. Examine hydration, pigmentation and blood pressure.

PLAB application: Acute deterioration with vomiting, hypotension or altered consciousness is adrenal crisis until proven otherwise.

Adrenal insufficiency investigations

Check electrolytes, glucose and paired morning cortisol/ACTH as clinically appropriate; a short Synacthen test may be used under endocrine guidance. Consider renin and aldosterone for primary disease.

PLAB application: If crisis is suspected, take blood if feasible but never delay emergency hydrocortisone.

Typical laboratory clues

Primary adrenal insufficiency may show hyponatraemia, hyperkalaemia and hypoglycaemia. Secondary insufficiency can cause hyponatraemia without typical hyperkalaemia.

PLAB application: Normal electrolytes do not exclude adrenal insufficiency.

Adrenal crisis recognition

Triggers include infection, surgery, injury, missed steroid doses and vomiting preventing absorption. Signs include shock, dehydration, abdominal pain, fever, confusion and hypoglycaemia.

PLAB application: Treat immediately on clinical suspicion.

Adrenal crisis emergency treatment

Use ABCDE, call for urgent help, give adult hydrocortisone 100 mg IV or IM immediately, followed by 200 mg over 24 hours by infusion or 50 mg IV/IM every 6 hours per protocol; give IV 0.9% saline, correct glucose and treat triggers.

PLAB application: Do not wait for cortisol results; monitor fluid status and electrolytes.

Long-term Addison management

Hydrocortisone replacement is usually divided across the day; primary disease also often requires fludrocortisone. Monitor symptoms, postural BP, electrolytes and replacement adequacy.

PLAB application: Patients need an emergency steroid card, injectable hydrocortisone kit and training.

Sick-day rules and prevention

Increase oral glucocorticoid during significant intercurrent illness according to individual plan. Vomiting, inability to retain tablets, severe illness or injury requires emergency injection and hospital assessment.

PLAB application: Explain that abrupt steroid withdrawal or missed replacement can precipitate crisis.

PLAB 2 counselling

Explore medication history, adherence, illness plans, travel, emergency contacts and patient concerns. Explain the condition, treatment and urgent red flags in plain language.

PLAB application: Teach emergency injection and when to seek urgent care; check understanding.

PLAB 2: Addison disease counselling OSCE

  1. Introduce yourself, confirm identity, consent and explore concerns.
  2. Ask about fatigue, dizziness, pigmentation, medications and previous crises.
  3. Explain cortisol deficiency and lifelong replacement where indicated.
  4. Discuss sick-day rules, steroid emergency card and injection training.
  5. Safety-net severe vomiting, collapse, confusion or inability to retain steroids: urgent emergency care.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

HPA axis

Hypothalamic CRH stimulates pituitary ACTH, which drives adrenal cortisol. Cortisol normally suppresses CRH and ACTH by feedback.

Why ACTH matters in Cushing

Low ACTH suggests autonomous adrenal cortisol production; non-suppressed or high ACTH suggests pituitary or ectopic production.

Why Addison causes pigmentation

Low cortisol removes feedback inhibition, increasing POMC-derived ACTH and melanocyte-stimulating activity.

Why Addison causes hyperkalaemia

Primary adrenal destruction impairs aldosterone-mediated renal potassium excretion; secondary insufficiency generally spares aldosterone.

Why crisis causes shock

Cortisol deficiency reduces vascular responsiveness to catecholamines; volume depletion may coexist from mineralocorticoid loss.

Steroid safety

Chronic glucocorticoid therapy suppresses the HPA axis. Abrupt withdrawal or intercurrent illness without appropriate cover can precipitate adrenal crisis.
