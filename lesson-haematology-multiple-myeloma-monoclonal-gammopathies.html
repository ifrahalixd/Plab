Multiple Myeloma & Monoclonal Gammopathies

Emergency: New neurological deficits, severe hypercalcaemia, sepsis or acute kidney injury require urgent assessment.

Overview

Multiple myeloma is a clonal plasma-cell malignancy producing a monoclonal immunoglobulin or light chain. MGUS and smouldering myeloma are precursor states.

PLAB application: Differentiate symptomatic myeloma from asymptomatic monoclonal gammopathy.

Presentation

Bone pain, especially back or ribs, fatigue, recurrent infection, fractures, thirst, constipation and renal impairment are common. Some patients are found incidentally.

PLAB application: Ask about red flags for spinal cord compression and hypercalcaemia.

CRAB features

End-organ injury includes hyperCalcaemia, Renal impairment, Anaemia and Bone lesions. These findings must be attributable to the plasma-cell disorder.

PLAB application: CRAB is a core PLAB memory aid, not the entire diagnostic definition.

Myeloma-defining biomarkers

IMWG myeloma-defining events also include ≥60% clonal marrow plasma cells, involved/uninvolved free light-chain ratio ≥100 with involved chain ≥100 mg/L, or more than one MRI focal lesion ≥5 mm.

PLAB application: Specialist interpretation is required; not every monoclonal protein means myeloma.

MGUS

Monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance has a monoclonal protein without myeloma-defining events or attributable organ damage; monitoring depends on risk.

PLAB application: Do not label asymptomatic MGUS as cancer requiring immediate chemotherapy.

Smouldering myeloma

Smouldering multiple myeloma has a greater clonal burden than MGUS but lacks myeloma-defining events. Risk-based specialist surveillance is essential.

PLAB application: Know the distinction from active myeloma.

Initial blood investigations

Request FBC and blood film, U&Es/eGFR, adjusted calcium, albumin and other tests guided by symptoms. Anaemia, kidney dysfunction and hypercalcaemia need prompt assessment.

PLAB application: Normal haemoglobin does not exclude myeloma.

Monoclonal protein tests

Serum protein electrophoresis with immunofixation and serum free light chains are important; urine testing may be needed under specialist protocols. No single test reliably excludes myeloma.

PLAB application: Light-chain disease may have little or no obvious serum M-spike.

Imaging

Whole-body low-dose CT, MRI or other specialist imaging identifies lytic bone disease and focal lesions. Plain skeletal survey is less sensitive.

PLAB application: Urgent MRI is needed for suspected malignant spinal cord compression.

Bone marrow and diagnosis

Bone marrow aspirate/trephine assesses clonal plasma cells, cytogenetics and risk; diagnosis integrates marrow findings, imaging, blood tests and symptoms.

PLAB application: Refer to haematology for confirmation and staging.

Renal injury

Filtered monoclonal free light chains may cause cast nephropathy; dehydration, hypercalcaemia, infection and nephrotoxic drugs can worsen AKI.

PLAB application: Urgent fluids when appropriate and specialist treatment; avoid nephrotoxins.

Bone disease and hypercalcaemia

Osteoclast activation and impaired bone formation produce lytic lesions and fractures. Hypercalcaemia may cause dehydration, constipation, confusion and arrhythmias.

PLAB application: Symptomatic hypercalcaemia requires urgent hospital assessment.

Infection and immune dysfunction

Reduced functional immunoglobulins and treatment-related immunosuppression increase bacterial and viral infection risk.

PLAB application: Fever in a patient receiving anti-myeloma treatment may be an emergency.

Treatment principles

Specialist regimens often combine agents such as proteasome inhibitors, immunomodulatory drugs, monoclonal antibodies and dexamethasone; selected patients undergo autologous stem-cell transplantation.

PLAB application: Treatment depends on fitness, cytogenetics, renal function and current UK eligibility.

Supportive care

Manage pain, fractures, bone protection, thrombosis risk, infections, anaemia, renal impairment and psychological needs. Bisphosphonates or denosumab may be used when appropriate.

PLAB application: Review dental health and renal considerations before bone-modifying therapy.

Emergencies and follow-up

Urgent red flags include new limb weakness, bladder/bowel dysfunction, severe hypercalcaemia, AKI, sepsis and pathological fracture. Follow paraprotein/light-chain trends and symptoms.

PLAB application: Escalate possible spinal cord compression immediately; do not wait for routine follow-up.

PLAB 2: Myeloma consultation OSCE

  1. Introduce yourself, confirm identity, obtain consent and explore concerns.
  2. Ask about bone pain, fractures, infections, fatigue, weight loss, thirst and neurological symptoms.
  3. Explain that a paraprotein is not by itself a diagnosis of myeloma; further blood tests, imaging and sometimes marrow sampling are needed.
  4. Discuss urgent red flags, specialist referral, treatment options and support using accessible language.
  5. Safety-net new weakness, bladder or bowel changes, fever, confusion and worsening pain.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

How myeloma damages bone

Malignant plasma cells stimulate osteoclasts and suppress osteoblasts, causing lytic lesions, pain and fractures.

Why kidneys fail

Monoclonal free light chains can form obstructing casts and provoke tubular inflammation; dehydration and hypercalcaemia aggravate injury.

Why infections occur

Myeloma suppresses normal antibody production; therapies may further reduce immune defence.

Interpreting serum proteins

An intact immunoglobulin M-spike can be seen on electrophoresis, but light-chain-only disease needs serum free light-chain testing and specialist interpretation.

What separates precursor states

MGUS and smouldering myeloma lack myeloma-defining events; active disease is diagnosed using clonal plasma-cell criteria plus qualifying events.

Why emergency recognition matters

Cord compression threatens irreversible neurological injury; severe hypercalcaemia, sepsis and acute renal failure also need urgent treatment.
