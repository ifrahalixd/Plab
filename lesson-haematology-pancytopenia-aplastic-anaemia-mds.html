Pancytopenia, Aplastic Anaemia & Myelodysplastic Syndromes

Emergency rule: Fever in a neutropenic patient, major bleeding or suspected acute leukaemia warrants urgent assessment.

Definition and clinical significance

Pancytopenia is reduction of haemoglobin, white cells and platelets. It is a blood-count pattern, not a final diagnosis. Severity and symptoms determine urgency.

PLAB application: Assess for sepsis, bleeding and symptomatic anaemia immediately.

Mechanisms

Reduced marrow production, marrow infiltration, ineffective haematopoiesis and peripheral destruction or sequestration can all cause pancytopenia.

PLAB application: Separate marrow failure from peripheral causes.

Common differential diagnoses

Causes include aplastic anaemia, myelodysplastic neoplasms (MDS), acute leukaemia, marrow infiltration, B12/folate deficiency, medications, viral infections, autoimmune disease and hypersplenism.

PLAB application: Reversible deficiencies and drugs should not be missed.

Symptoms and signs

Anaemia causes fatigue, pallor and breathlessness; neutropenia predisposes to infection; thrombocytopenia causes bruising, petechiae and bleeding. Ask about fever, weight loss and night sweats.

PLAB application: Examine for lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly and mucosal bleeding.

Immediate red flags

Fever or clinical deterioration with neutropenia, active major bleeding, severe symptomatic anaemia and blasts on a film require urgent assessment.

PLAB application: Suspected neutropenic sepsis is a time-critical emergency.

Initial investigations

Repeat full blood count with differential, reticulocyte count and peripheral blood film. Check B12, folate, ferritin as indicated, renal and liver function, inflammatory markers, and haemolysis tests when suggested.

PLAB application: Interpret absolute neutrophil and platelet counts, not just total white count.

Reticulocytes and blood film

Low reticulocytes favour impaired production; high reticulocytes may suggest destruction or loss. Macro-ovalocytes and hypersegmented neutrophils suggest megaloblastic anaemia; blasts suggest acute leukaemia; dysplasia can suggest MDS.

PLAB application: A normal-looking film does not exclude marrow pathology.

Bone marrow examination

Bone marrow aspirate and trephine biopsy may establish cellularity, dysplasia, infiltration, blast percentage and fibrosis. Cytogenetics and molecular testing can refine classification.

PLAB application: Refer to haematology when unexplained or persistent.

Aplastic anaemia

Acquired aplastic anaemia is a marrow failure syndrome with hypocellular marrow and reduced production of blood cells, often immune mediated. Unlike many infiltrative conditions, substantial splenomegaly is not typical.

PLAB application: Pancytopenia plus hypocellular marrow supports diagnosis after excluding mimics.

Aplastic anaemia causes and associations

Most acquired cases are idiopathic or immune mediated; medications, toxins, viral infections and inherited marrow-failure syndromes may be relevant. Ask about drug exposures and family history.

PLAB application: Do not label drug causation without assessment.

Aplastic anaemia treatment

Specialist treatment may involve haematopoietic stem-cell transplantation or immunosuppression such as antithymocyte globulin and ciclosporin, sometimes with eltrombopag. Supportive transfusions and infection management are important.

PLAB application: Treatment selection depends on severity, age, donor availability and fitness.

Myelodysplastic syndromes

MDS are clonal myeloid neoplasms characterised by ineffective blood-cell production, cytopenias and variable risk of progression to AML. They are more common in older adults.

PLAB application: Consider MDS in persistent unexplained cytopenias, especially with dysplasia.

MDS investigations and risk

Diagnosis integrates marrow morphology, blasts, cytogenetic and molecular findings, and exclusion of alternative causes. Risk classification guides prognosis and management.

PLAB application: Do not diagnose MDS from macrocytosis alone.

MDS management

Management may include observation, red-cell transfusion support, erythropoiesis-stimulating agents in selected cases, other disease-modifying agents and stem-cell transplantation for eligible patients.

PLAB application: Management is individualised by risk, symptoms and patient goals.

Neutropenic sepsis and bleeding

Fever or suspected infection in neutropenia warrants emergency assessment and prompt empirical IV antibiotics according to local protocol. Severe bleeding with thrombocytopenia requires resuscitation and specialist blood-product support.

PLAB application: Do not wait for culture results before treating suspected neutropenic sepsis.

PLAB 2 consultation and safety net

Take a focused history of infections, bleeding, fatigue, drugs, alcohol, nutritional intake, systemic symptoms and family history. Explain why urgent tests or referral are needed without prematurely declaring cancer.

PLAB application: Advise immediate care for fever, uncontrolled bleeding, fainting or severe breathlessness.

PLAB 2 — Explaining pancytopenia

Introduce yourself and assess concerns. Explain: “Your blood test shows that several types of blood cells are lower than expected. This can happen for different reasons, including vitamin deficiencies, medications or conditions affecting the bone marrow. We need more tests and a specialist assessment to find the cause.” Ask about infection, bleeding, fatigue, medications and systemic symptoms. Check understanding and safety-net for fever, bleeding and severe breathlessness.

Deep Dive

Aplastic anaemia versus MDS

Aplastic anaemia typically features hypocellular marrow and immune-mediated stem-cell injury. MDS features clonal ineffective haematopoiesis and dysplasia; hypocellular MDS can overlap clinically, so specialist marrow and molecular assessment matters.

Reticulocytes and marrow response

Reticulocytes measure marrow red-cell output. Interpret with anaemia severity and recent transfusion; a low response in severe anaemia suggests production failure.

MDS risk assessment

Blast percentage, cytopenias, cytogenetic and molecular changes inform modern prognostic models and treatment decisions. Do not rely on a single cell count.

Inherited marrow failure

Younger patients, congenital anomalies, unusual family histories or longstanding cytopenias may require evaluation for inherited marrow-failure syndromes.

Transfusion and infection support

Blood products are given for clinical indications under local guidance. Transfusion requirements, alloimmunisation, infection risks and iron overload may affect long-term care.

Communication in PLAB 2

Explain that pancytopenia has several possible causes, including treatable deficiencies and bone-marrow disorders. Discuss urgent tests, patient questions and clear emergency advice.
