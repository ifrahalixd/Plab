Acute Coronary Syndrome, Cardiac Arrest & Life-Threatening Arrhythmias

Emergency rule: Chest pain with suspected STEMI needs urgent reperfusion assessment. Unresponsive and not breathing normally? Start CPR and attach a defibrillator.

Immediate chest-pain assessment

Start ABCDE, vital signs, monitoring, IV access and a 12-lead ECG as soon as possible, ideally within 10 minutes of first medical contact. Ask onset, radiation, associated symptoms, risk factors and contraindications to treatment.

PLAB application: Do not dismiss ACS because pain is atypical or the initial ECG is normal.

ACS classification

Acute coronary syndrome includes STEMI, NSTEMI and unstable angina. STEMI generally reflects acute coronary occlusion requiring urgent reperfusion; NSTEMI has evidence of myocardial injury; unstable angina has ischaemic symptoms without diagnostic troponin rise.

PLAB application: Use symptoms, serial ECGs and troponin interpreted with timing and local assay pathways.

Recognising STEMI

ST elevation in anatomically contiguous leads, or other occlusion patterns, with compatible symptoms prompts immediate senior/cardiology assessment. Posterior and right-sided ECG leads can help when indicated.

PLAB application: Never delay urgent reperfusion referral while waiting for troponin in clear STEMI.

NSTEMI and unstable angina

These may show ST depression, T-wave changes or a non-diagnostic ECG. High-sensitivity troponin requires an assay-specific serial pathway; rising or falling values and clinical context distinguish acute injury.

PLAB application: Risk stratify and arrange urgent specialist management; not all raised troponins indicate type 1 MI.

Initial ACS medicines

Give aspirin loading when ACS is suspected unless contraindicated, using current local protocols. Further antiplatelet and anticoagulant choices depend on STEMI/NSTEMI strategy, bleeding risk and cardiology advice. Treat pain and nausea appropriately.

PLAB application: Check allergy, active bleeding and medication history; do not give routine oxygen if saturation is normal.

Reperfusion and escalation

Primary PCI is preferred for eligible STEMI when available within recommended timeframes; fibrinolysis may be considered where timely PCI is unavailable and no contraindications exist. Activate the regional pathway early.

PLAB application: Time-critical reperfusion decisions require cardiology and local network guidance.

ACS complications

Watch for cardiogenic shock, acute pulmonary oedema, ventricular tachyarrhythmia, bradyarrhythmia and mechanical complications. Recheck observations and ECG with deterioration.

PLAB application: A hypotensive patient with chest pain or new arrhythmia needs immediate senior resuscitation support.

Cardiac arrest recognition

An unresponsive adult not breathing normally is presumed in cardiac arrest. Call the emergency team, begin high-quality CPR and attach an AED/defibrillator promptly. Agonal gasps are not normal breathing.

PLAB application: Do not spend excessive time checking for a pulse; follow current resuscitation training.

Adult basic life support

Compress the centre of the chest at 100–120/min, depth 5–6 cm, allowing full recoil and minimising interruptions. Provide ventilation according to training and available equipment; use AED prompts.

PLAB application: Swap compressors when possible to maintain quality and avoid long pauses.

Shockable cardiac arrest: VF/pulseless VT

Deliver defibrillation as soon as available, immediately resume CPR for two minutes and follow ALS rhythm checks and drug timings. Treat reversible causes in parallel.

PLAB application: Defibrillation and CPR take priority; do not delay shocks for IV access or drugs.

Non-shockable arrest: PEA/asystole

Continue high-quality CPR, give adrenaline as directed by ALS protocol, and search for reversible causes. Do not shock true asystole or PEA.

PLAB application: Check leads and rhythm carefully; identify and treat hypoxia, hypovolaemia and other reversible causes.

Reversible causes: 4 Hs and 4 Ts

Hypoxia, hypovolaemia, hypo-/hyperkalaemia or other metabolic disturbance, hypothermia; thrombosis (coronary/pulmonary), tamponade, toxins and tension pneumothorax.

PLAB application: Use targeted history, blood gas, ultrasound and examination without interrupting CPR.

Unstable tachyarrhythmias

Adverse features include shock, syncope, myocardial ischaemia and severe heart failure. Tachycardia with a pulse and serious adverse features generally requires urgent synchronised cardioversion by trained staff.

PLAB application: Assess pulse and stability first; never confuse pulseless VT with VT with a pulse.

Stable tachyarrhythmias

Obtain a 12-lead ECG and distinguish narrow/wide and regular/irregular rhythms. Vagal manoeuvres or adenosine may be appropriate for selected regular narrow-complex SVT under protocol; avoid unsafe empiric treatment of undifferentiated broad-complex tachycardia.

PLAB application: Seek expert help for broad-complex or pre-excited atrial fibrillation.

Bradycardia with adverse features

Look for shock, syncope, ischaemia or heart failure. Follow adult bradycardia algorithm: atropine when indicated, early pacing/chronotropic support if ineffective, and treat reversible causes.

PLAB application: A high-grade AV block may require urgent pacing and specialist involvement.

Post-ROSC care and PLAB 2 handover

After return of spontaneous circulation, reassess ABCDE, oxygenation, ventilation, haemodynamics, glucose, temperature and 12-lead ECG. Investigate the cause and arrange critical care/cardiology input. Communicate using SBAR and document timings.

PLAB application: In OSCEs, state what you would do immediately, call for help and reassess after interventions.

PLAB 2 — emergency station opening

“I am concerned about an acute cardiac emergency. I will call for senior and resuscitation help, assess ABCDE, attach monitoring, obtain an ECG, treat immediate threats and reassess continuously. If the patient becomes unresponsive and is not breathing normally, I will begin CPR and use the defibrillator.”

Deep Dive — Acute cardiac emergencies

ECG reasoning

A normal first ECG does not rule out ACS. Repeat during ongoing pain, review prior tracings and consider posterior/right-sided leads when appropriate. Interpret in clinical context.

Troponin pitfalls

High-sensitivity troponin measures myocardial injury, not necessarily plaque rupture. Timing, serial change, symptoms, ECG and alternative causes such as sepsis, renal dysfunction or myocarditis matter.

Reperfusion decision

Urgent STEMI pathways coordinate primary PCI and, in selected circumstances, fibrinolysis. Do not invent a universal delay threshold; follow current UK regional pathways and cardiology direction.

ALS rhythm distinction

VF and pulseless VT are shockable; asystole and PEA are non-shockable. Pulse status changes the algorithm. Keep interruptions to compressions minimal.

Cardioversion versus defibrillation

Synchronised cardioversion treats selected unstable tachyarrhythmias with a pulse. Unsynchronised defibrillation treats VF/pulseless VT. Follow trained team protocols.

Safe communication

Explain urgency without speculation; document symptom onset, ECG time, medicines, shocks, CPR, rhythm changes and response. Handover using SBAR.
