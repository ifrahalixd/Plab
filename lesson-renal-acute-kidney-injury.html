Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

PLAB safety: AKI with severe hyperkalaemia, pulmonary oedema, shock or uraemic complications requires immediate escalation.

01. Definition and KDIGO staging

AKI is a sudden decline in kidney function, identified by serum creatinine rise ≥26.5 micromol/L within 48 hours, ≥1.5 times baseline within 7 days, or urine output <0.5 mL/kg/hour for 6 hours. Creatinine can lag behind injury.

PLAB focus: Compare with baseline and monitor urine output.

02. AKI stage 1

Creatinine 1.5–1.9 times baseline or increase ≥26.5 micromol/L; or urine output <0.5 mL/kg/hour for 6–12 hours.

PLAB focus: Even stage 1 warrants prompt assessment.

03. AKI stages 2 and 3

Stage 2: creatinine 2–2.9 times baseline or urine output <0.5 mL/kg/hour for ≥12 hours. Stage 3: creatinine ≥3 times baseline, ≥353.6 micromol/L with acute rise, renal replacement therapy, urine output <0.3 mL/kg/hour ≥24 hours, or anuria ≥12 hours.

PLAB focus: Urgently escalate severe AKI.

04. Causes: pre-renal

Hypovolaemia (vomiting, diarrhoea, bleeding), sepsis, heart failure and reduced effective circulating volume can impair renal perfusion.

PLAB focus: Assess perfusion; avoid reflex fluids in pulmonary oedema.

05. Causes: intrinsic renal

Acute tubular injury from ischaemia or nephrotoxins; glomerulonephritis; acute interstitial nephritis; vascular causes.

PLAB focus: Blood/protein in urine may suggest intrinsic disease.

06. Causes: post-renal

Obstruction from prostatic enlargement, stones, malignancy, catheter blockage or bilateral ureteric obstruction.

PLAB focus: Check catheter and bladder; arrange imaging when indicated.

07. Risk factors

Older age, chronic kidney disease, diabetes, heart failure, liver disease, sepsis, dehydration, recent surgery, contrast exposure and nephrotoxic medicines.

PLAB focus: Recognise AKI in high-risk patients early.

08. Symptoms and examination

May be asymptomatic. Ask about reduced urine, thirst, vomiting, infection, pain, haematuria and medications. Assess ABCDE, blood pressure, volume status, JVP, oedema and bladder distension.

PLAB focus: Identify shock, fluid overload and urinary retention.

09. Immediate investigations

Repeat U&E/creatinine, potassium and bicarbonate; FBC, CRP if indicated, glucose, ECG when hyperkalaemia suspected, urine dipstick and urine output chart. Check prior results.

PLAB focus: Do not delay emergency treatment for investigations.

10. Urinalysis and ultrasound

Dipstick for blood, protein, leucocytes, nitrites and glucose. Haematuria plus proteinuria without UTI/trauma may suggest nephritis. Urgent ultrasound if obstruction suspected or cause unclear; immediate imaging if pyonephrosis suspected.

PLAB focus: Discuss possible glomerulonephritis with nephrology.

11. Initial management

Treat underlying cause, optimise perfusion and oxygenation, give carefully reassessed isotonic crystalloid if hypovolaemic, manage sepsis and obstruction, monitor fluid balance, daily weight, U&E and urine output.

PLAB focus: Fluid decisions depend on volume status.

12. Medication review

Review NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors/ARBs, diuretics, metformin and other renally cleared or nephrotoxic medicines. Temporarily withholding some drugs may be appropriate during dehydrating illness; individualise and plan restart.

PLAB focus: Do not permanently stop beneficial medicines without review.

13. Hyperkalaemia

Dangerous hyperkalaemia may cause arrhythmia. Obtain ECG and urgently treat according to local emergency protocol: cardiac membrane stabilisation with IV calcium when indicated, insulin/glucose to shift potassium, and potassium removal strategies.

PLAB focus: Escalate immediately for ECG changes or severe potassium elevation.

14. Other complications

Pulmonary oedema, metabolic acidosis, uraemic pericarditis/encephalopathy, electrolyte disturbances and drug accumulation require prompt recognition.

PLAB focus: Check for life-threatening complications repeatedly.

15. When to refer and dialysis

Urgent nephrology/critical care input for refractory hyperkalaemia, acidosis, fluid overload, uraemic complications, stage 3 AKI, unclear cause or suspected vasculitis/glomerulonephritis. Dialysis depends on clinical indications, not creatinine alone.

PLAB focus: Use AEIOU as a memory aid, not a standalone rule.

16. Recovery, discharge and prevention

Document AKI stage and cause; arrange renal function, potassium and medication follow-up, counsel on hydration and avoiding NSAIDs, and assess for persistent CKD. AKI increases future CKD risk.

PLAB focus: Ensure a clear follow-up and medication restart plan.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Renal autoregulation

Afferent arteriolar prostaglandins and efferent angiotensin II help maintain filtration during low perfusion. NSAIDs and RAAS blockers can alter this balance.

Creatinine kinetics

Creatinine reflects a changing balance of production, distribution and clearance; an early normal value does not exclude evolving injury.

Tubular injury

Ischaemia and toxins can injure tubular cells, impair concentrating ability and cause tubular obstruction or back-leak.

Urinary sediment

Red cell casts and significant proteinuria suggest glomerular pathology; muddy brown casts can occur with tubular injury.

Potassium physiology

Reduced renal excretion plus acidosis and tissue breakdown may cause dangerous potassium elevation. ECG can be normal despite severe hyperkalaemia.

RRT reasoning

Renal replacement therapy is considered for life-threatening complications that cannot be managed medically, alongside trajectory and overall prognosis.
