Psychiatric Emergencies: Suicide Risk, Acute Agitation, Psychosis & Mental Health Act Assessments

Emergency red flags: Immediate suicidal intent, severe agitation with danger, delirium, overdose, medical instability or inability to maintain safety require urgent multidisciplinary assessment.

Emergency priorities

Ensure immediate physical safety, call for support and use ABCDE. Assess overdose, injury, hypoglycaemia, hypoxia, intoxication, infection and delirium before attributing disturbance to psychiatric illness. Maintain a calm environment and a clear exit route.

PLAB application: Do not miss a reversible medical emergency.

Suicide and self-harm assessment

Ask directly and compassionately about current suicidal thoughts, intent, plans, access to means, recent attempts, preparatory behaviour and protective factors. Assess previous self-harm, psychiatric symptoms, substance use, social stressors and safeguarding concerns.

PLAB application: A screening score cannot replace a personalised psychosocial assessment.

After self-harm

Provide immediate treatment for injuries and poisoning. Offer psychosocial assessment by an appropriate mental health professional at the earliest opportunity, alongside physical care. Do not use risk scales to predict suicide or decide discharge.

PLAB application: NICE NG225 recommends needs-based assessment and safety planning.

Immediate suicide risk and observation

If there is immediate danger, do not leave the patient alone; arrange proportionate observation, remove hazards where safe, seek senior mental health help and plan a safe environment. Discuss collaborative safety measures and document rationale.

PLAB application: Observation does not substitute for therapeutic engagement.

Acute agitation: de-escalation

Introduce yourself, use one calm speaker, give space, listen, acknowledge distress, offer choices and reduce noise and crowds. Ask about pain, fear, trauma and communication needs. Avoid threats, confrontation or unnecessary touching.

PLAB application: Verbal de-escalation and environmental changes come first whenever safe.

Violence and restrictive interventions

Follow local policy and NICE NG10. Physical restraint is a last resort, proportionate, time-limited and carried out by trained staff. Protect airway and breathing, avoid prone restraint where possible, and continuously monitor physical wellbeing.

PLAB application: Use the least restrictive effective intervention.

Rapid tranquillisation

If de-escalation fails and there is immediate danger, senior clinicians may consider medicines under local protocols after evaluating physical health, intoxication, ECG/QT risk, prior medication and capacity. Monitor pulse, blood pressure, respiration, consciousness and oxygenation.

PLAB application: Do not memorise a single drug choice for every agitated patient.

Acute psychosis

Assess hallucinations, delusions, thought disorder, disorganisation, mood symptoms, insight and functional decline. Ask about command hallucinations, threats, self-neglect and ability to meet basic needs. Consider first-episode psychosis and arrange urgent specialist evaluation.

PLAB application: Psychosis can be substance-induced or due to a medical disorder.

Mania and severe mood disorder

Look for decreased need for sleep, elevated or irritable mood, increased activity, pressured speech, grandiosity and risky behaviour. Assess suicidal risk even in mania and evaluate psychotic features. Seek specialist care for severe illness.

PLAB application: A person may appear cheerful while being at substantial risk.

Delirium versus psychiatric illness

Acute fluctuating attention, disorientation and altered arousal suggest delirium. Check observations, glucose, medications, infection, withdrawal and metabolic causes. Treat underlying illness urgently.

PLAB application: New confusion in an older adult is delirium until assessed otherwise.

Alcohol and drug emergencies

Assess intoxication and withdrawal, respiratory compromise, trauma, glucose, temperature and toxidromes. Severe alcohol withdrawal may cause seizures or delirium tremens; sedative withdrawal can be dangerous. Use toxicology and local emergency protocols.

PLAB application: Do not assume intoxication excludes concurrent mental illness or injury.

Capacity and consent

Under the Mental Capacity Act 2005 in England and Wales, presume capacity unless established otherwise. Assess capacity for the specific decision at the relevant time: understand, retain, use/weigh information and communicate a choice. Provide decision support and document findings.

PLAB application: An unwise decision alone does not prove lack of capacity.

Mental Health Act: key principles

In England and Wales, the Mental Health Act 1983 (as amended) allows assessment or treatment without consent when statutory criteria and procedures are met. Section 2 concerns assessment, generally up to 28 days; Section 3 concerns treatment, initially up to six months. Approved professionals and doctors have defined roles.

PLAB application: Do not confuse detention under the MHA with lack of capacity under the MCA.

Emergency holding powers and geography

Sections 5(2) and 5(4) apply only in specified inpatient contexts and roles; they are not general emergency-department detention powers. Police powers under sections 135 and 136 have separate criteria. Scotland and Northern Ireland use different mental health legislation.

PLAB application: Seek local senior/legal advice rather than improvising a holding power.

Safeguarding, confidentiality and communication

Explore domestic abuse, exploitation, dependants, children at risk and vulnerability. Explain confidentiality and lawful disclosure when serious harm is threatened. Use interpreters and trauma-informed, non-stigmatising language. Involve carers with consent where possible.

PLAB application: Document exactly what was said and why information was shared.

Safe disposition and PLAB 2 handover

Before discharge, confirm physical stability, psychosocial assessment, a collaborative safety plan, crisis contacts, follow-up and safe support. Avoid discharge based solely on a low-risk label. Use SBAR to communicate medical findings, mental state, immediate risks and legal status.

PLAB application: A safety plan is individualised and is not a no-suicide contract.

PLAB 2 — Emergency psychiatric consultation

Introduce yourself, establish a safe setting, assess immediate medical problems, ask directly about suicide and violence risk, perform a mental state examination, consider capacity, and escalate to senior mental health clinicians. Explain next steps empathetically and agree safety arrangements.
