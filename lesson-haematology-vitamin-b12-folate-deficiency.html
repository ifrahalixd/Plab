Vitamin B12 & Folate Deficiency Anaemia

PLAB red flag: B12 deficiency may cause progressive neurological injury without anaemia. Do not delay appropriate treatment when neurological signs strongly suggest deficiency.

Overview

Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) and folate are needed for DNA synthesis. Deficiency can cause megaloblastic anaemia with macrocytosis. B12 deficiency may also damage the nervous system.

PLAB application: Neurological symptoms can occur without anaemia or macrocytosis.

Symptoms

Fatigue, pallor, breathlessness and glossitis may occur. B12 deficiency can cause paraesthesia, gait imbalance, impaired vibration/proprioception, cognitive or mood symptoms.

PLAB application: Ask about sensory symptoms and walking difficulties in every suspected B12 deficiency.

Blood count and film

FBC may show high MCV, low haemoglobin and sometimes leukopenia or thrombocytopenia. Film may show macro-ovalocytes and hypersegmented neutrophils.

PLAB application: Normal MCV does not exclude B12 deficiency, especially with coexisting iron deficiency.

B12 absorption

Dietary B12 binds intrinsic factor from gastric parietal cells and is absorbed in the terminal ileum. Body stores can last years.

PLAB application: Gastric disease, ileal disease or surgery can impair absorption.

B12 deficiency causes

Autoimmune gastritis (formerly pernicious anaemia), gastrectomy, ileal disease/resection, low dietary intake, and medicines such as metformin or acid suppression can contribute.

PLAB application: Ask about diet, surgery, gastrointestinal disease and medicines.

Folate deficiency causes

Low intake, alcohol use, malabsorption, increased requirements (including pregnancy), and drugs such as methotrexate or certain antiepileptics can contribute.

PLAB application: Folate stores are smaller than B12 stores, so deficiency can develop sooner.

Initial investigations

Request FBC and film, B12 and folate testing guided by NICE NG239 and local laboratory pathways. Consider reticulocytes, ferritin, thyroid and liver tests if appropriate.

PLAB application: If neurological signs strongly suggest B12 deficiency, do not delay treatment for test results.

Interpreting B12 tests

Total B12 or active B12 may be used; borderline results sometimes need methylmalonic acid (MMA) testing depending on context and assay. MMA can rise with renal impairment.

PLAB application: Interpret biochemical results alongside symptoms and risk factors.

Autoimmune gastritis

Autoimmune destruction of gastric parietal cells can reduce intrinsic factor and B12 absorption. Anti-intrinsic factor antibodies can support diagnosis but a negative result does not exclude it.

PLAB application: Long-term replacement is often needed for irreversible malabsorption.

Neurological complications

Subacute combined degeneration can affect posterior columns and corticospinal tracts, causing impaired vibration sense, proprioception, ataxia and sometimes spasticity.

PLAB application: Urgent treatment may prevent progression; delayed treatment risks incomplete recovery.

Treating B12 deficiency

Use oral or intramuscular B12 according to cause, severity, neurological involvement, patient preferences and NICE NG239/local BNF. Intramuscular replacement is appropriate for selected malabsorption and neurological cases.

PLAB application: Do not give folic acid alone before addressing possible B12 deficiency.

B12 with neurological symptoms

Suspected B12-related neurological symptoms warrant prompt assessment and treatment. UK hydroxocobalamin regimens differ when neurological involvement is present; use current BNF.

PLAB application: Refer or seek specialist advice for severe or progressive neurological signs.

Treating folate deficiency

After assessing and treating coexisting B12 deficiency, folic acid is used for folate deficiency, with duration and dosing according to cause and BNF.

PLAB application: Folate can improve anaemia while untreated B12 neurological injury progresses.

Monitoring response

Reticulocytes and haemoglobin generally improve after effective replacement. Reassess symptoms, adherence, diagnosis and the underlying cause if response is poor.

PLAB application: Neurological recovery may be slower and sometimes incomplete.

Pregnancy and special situations

Folate requirements rise in pregnancy. Standard preconception folic acid supplementation is distinct from treatment of established folate deficiency; higher-risk groups may need higher prescribed doses.

PLAB application: Use current NHS/NICE pregnancy recommendations rather than a universal high dose.

PLAB 2 counselling

Explain the cause, treatment route, likely duration and follow-up. Ask about diet, medicines, numbness and walking; discuss safety-netting for worsening weakness, imbalance or severe anaemia symptoms.

PLAB application: Patients with irreversible malabsorption may need lifelong B12 replacement.

PLAB 2 — B12 / folate deficiency consultation

Introduce yourself, confirm identity and consent. Ask about fatigue, diet, alcohol, medicines, GI surgery, numbness and walking difficulties. Explain testing, B12 replacement route, folate treatment only after considering B12 deficiency, follow-up and neurological safety-netting.

Deep Dive

Megaloblastosis

Defective DNA synthesis delays nuclear maturation while cytoplasmic development continues, producing macro-ovalocytes and hypersegmented neutrophils.

Neurological injury

B12 deficiency can cause peripheral neuropathy and subacute combined degeneration affecting posterior and lateral spinal columns.

Why folate alone is risky

Folic acid can improve blood counts without treating B12-related neurological dysfunction.

Intrinsic factor

Autoimmune gastritis impairs intrinsic factor-mediated absorption and may require lifelong replacement.

MMA testing

MMA may support diagnosis when B12 results are indeterminate but renal impairment limits specificity.

Monitoring

Blood counts may respond before neurological symptoms; failure to improve should trigger reassessment.
