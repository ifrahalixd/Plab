Neutropenia, Agranulocytosis & Neutropenic Sepsis

Emergency rule: An unwell patient receiving anticancer treatment may have neutropenic sepsis even without fever. Escalate and give empiric antibiotics immediately under local protocol.

Definitions

Neutropenia is a reduced absolute neutrophil count (ANC). Common severity bands: mild 1.0–1.5, moderate 0.5–1.0 and severe below 0.5 × 10⁹/L. Agranulocytosis generally refers to profound neutropenia, often ANC below 0.2 × 10⁹/L.

PLAB application: Use the absolute count, not just the total white cell count.

Why neutrophils matter

Neutrophils are crucial for defence against bacterial and fungal infection. Risk rises as the ANC falls, particularly with prolonged profound neutropenia.

PLAB application: Severe infection may have few local inflammatory signs.

Causes: reduced production

Chemotherapy, marrow infiltration, aplastic anaemia, myelodysplasia, B12 or folate deficiency, viral infections and some medicines suppress marrow production.

PLAB application: Review the medication chart and prior blood counts.

Causes: increased destruction or redistribution

Autoimmune neutropenia, some infections, hypersplenism and immune drug reactions can lower circulating neutrophils.

PLAB application: Interpret the count with the clinical picture and blood film.

Drug-induced agranulocytosis

Clozapine, antithyroid drugs such as carbimazole, and some antibiotics and other medicines can cause severe neutropenia. New fever or sore throat on a high-risk medicine needs urgent FBC assessment.

PLAB application: Follow medicine-specific monitoring and stopping rules; do not restart a suspected culprit without specialist advice.

Benign low neutrophil counts

Duffy-null associated neutrophil count is a common constitutional pattern in some populations and does not by itself imply increased infection risk.

PLAB application: Avoid diagnosing a disorder from a single isolated low ANC without context.

Symptoms and signs

Fever, rigors, sore throat, oral ulcers, cough, dysuria, perianal pain or new confusion can signal infection. In severe neutropenia, redness or pus may be absent.

PLAB application: Ask specifically about chemotherapy and central venous lines.

Neutropenic sepsis: recognition

Suspect neutropenic sepsis in a patient receiving anticancer treatment who becomes unwell, with or without fever. A commonly used diagnostic criterion is neutrophils ≤0.5 × 10⁹/L plus temperature >38°C or other features of clinically significant sepsis.

PLAB application: Do not wait for a measured fever if the patient is clinically deteriorating.

Immediate ABCDE response

Assess airway, breathing, circulation, disability and exposure; record observations and NEWS2, obtain IV access and escalate immediately. Give oxygen and fluids when indicated, monitoring for overload.

PLAB application: Neutropenic sepsis is an acute medical emergency.

Investigations

Send FBC with differential, renal and liver function, CRP, lactate when indicated, and blood cultures including line cultures if appropriate. Urine and other samples depend on symptoms; obtain a chest X-ray when clinically indicated.

PLAB application: Collect cultures promptly but never delay antibiotics to obtain them.

Antibiotic timing and selection

NICE recommends immediate empiric antibiotic therapy for suspected neutropenic sepsis. Piperacillin–tazobactam is a recommended initial IV monotherapy option in NICE CG151 unless contraindicated; adapt to allergy, local resistance and protocols.

PLAB application: Do not delay treatment while awaiting neutrophil results or cultures.

Risk assessment and disposition

Patients require urgent hospital assessment and specialist involvement. After initial stabilisation, validated risk assessment may identify selected low-risk patients suitable for supervised outpatient treatment.

PLAB application: Never send an unstable patient home on oral antibiotics.

G-CSF and prophylaxis

Granulocyte colony-stimulating factor may be used for selected chemotherapy regimens or specific severe neutropenia syndromes. Routine therapeutic use in established neutropenic sepsis is not universally recommended.

PLAB application: Seek oncology or haematology advice rather than prescribing reflexively.

Persistent fever and fungal infection

Persistent fever despite broad-spectrum antibiotics may indicate resistant infection, an occult source or invasive fungal disease, especially with prolonged severe neutropenia.

PLAB application: Reassess clinically and discuss antimicrobial and antifungal strategies with specialists.

Chronic neutropenia work-up

Repeat FBC, review old results, blood film, medications, infection history and nutritional status; consider viral, autoimmune and marrow investigations guided by clinical findings.

PLAB application: Urgent referral if severe neutropenia, recurrent serious infections or other cytopenias.

Discharge advice and prevention

Patients receiving chemotherapy need a clear 24-hour contact number and instructions to seek urgent help for fever, chills or feeling suddenly unwell. Reinforce hand hygiene, food safety per local policy and prescribed prophylaxis.

PLAB application: Safety-net even when the patient is afebrile at consultation.

PLAB 2 — Fever after chemotherapy

Introduce yourself, confirm identity, assess ABCDE and observations, ask about chemotherapy timing, temperature, rigors, line symptoms, respiratory or urinary symptoms, medication allergies and previous infections. Explain that reduced infection-fighting cells can make infection dangerous and that urgent admission, blood tests, cultures and IV antibiotics are needed. Escalate promptly; do not wait for results.

Deep Dive

Fever may be absent

Patients with severe neutropenia may lack a typical inflammatory response. Hypothermia, confusion, tachypnoea, hypotension or simply feeling unwell can be the only clues.

Drug safety

Clozapine and antithyroid medicines have medicine-specific ANC monitoring and action thresholds. Patients taking carbimazole who develop fever or sore throat should stop it and seek urgent blood testing according to UK safety advice.

Chemotherapy nadir

Neutrophil counts often reach a nadir days after cytotoxic chemotherapy, but timing varies widely by regimen. Never rely on the expected nadir to exclude infection.

Antibiotic strategy

Initial empirical IV treatment should follow NICE and local antimicrobial policy, accounting for allergies, resistance, renal function and prior cultures. Modify treatment when results and clinical response are available.

Duffy-null associated counts

A stable isolated lower ANC can be physiological in individuals with Duffy-null phenotype; it should be distinguished from marrow failure and infection-prone neutropenia without using ethnicity alone as a diagnostic test.

Escalation and reassessment

Repeat observations, review lactate and organ function, seek senior critical-care support for shock or respiratory failure, and reassess possible sources including central lines.
