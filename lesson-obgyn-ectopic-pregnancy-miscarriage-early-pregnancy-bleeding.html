Ectopic Pregnancy, Miscarriage & Early Pregnancy Bleeding

Emergency: Collapse, hypotension, shoulder-tip pain, peritonism or severe bleeding in early pregnancy requires immediate resuscitation and senior gynaecology assessment.

Clinical priorities

Bleeding or pain in the first trimester may represent a viable intrauterine pregnancy, miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy or other pathology. Assess haemodynamic stability before interpreting tests.

PLAB application: A positive pregnancy test plus abdominal pain or bleeding requires ectopic pregnancy to be considered.

ABCDE and immediate triage

Check airway, breathing, circulation, vital signs, mental state and pain. Look for pallor, peritonism, shoulder-tip pain, syncope and shock. Obtain IV access, bloods and urgent senior gynaecology input if unstable.

PLAB application: Do not delay resuscitation for ultrasound in a collapsed patient.

Focused history

Ask gestational age and last menstrual period, amount of bleeding, clots or tissue, unilateral pain, shoulder-tip pain, dizziness, prior scans, fertility treatment, previous ectopic pregnancy, pelvic infection, surgery and contraception.

PLAB application: A low-risk history does not exclude ectopic pregnancy.

Examination and consent

Record observations, abdominal tenderness, guarding and peritonism. Pelvic examination is based on clinical need, patient consent, privacy, chaperone and local pathway; do not delay emergency care.

PLAB application: Respect dignity and offer a chaperone for intimate examination.

Ectopic pregnancy: mechanism and sites

Ectopic pregnancy implants outside the uterine cavity, most often in a fallopian tube. Rupture can cause life-threatening internal haemorrhage. Less common sites include cervical, interstitial and caesarean-scar pregnancy.

PLAB application: Shoulder-tip pain, collapse or peritonism can indicate intraperitoneal bleeding.

Ectopic pregnancy: risk factors

Prior ectopic pregnancy, tubal surgery, pelvic inflammatory disease, assisted conception and smoking increase risk. Pregnancy with an intrauterine device in place merits ectopic assessment, although IUDs lower overall pregnancy risk.

PLAB application: Ectopic pregnancy also occurs without risk factors.

Investigations

Perform a pregnancy test, transvaginal ultrasound when appropriate, full blood count and blood group/Rh status; crossmatch if significant bleeding. Serial serum hCG may help assess pregnancy location or progression but cannot alone locate a pregnancy.

PLAB application: Never use one hCG result to rule out ectopic pregnancy.

Pregnancy of unknown location

A positive pregnancy test with no intrauterine or extrauterine pregnancy seen on ultrasound is a pregnancy of unknown location (PUL), not a final diagnosis. Arrange early pregnancy assessment, follow-up hCG approximately 48 hours apart as indicated and explicit emergency return advice.

PLAB application: Symptoms override apparently reassuring hCG trends.

Ectopic pregnancy: management

Options include expectant monitoring, methotrexate or surgery depending on clinical stability, pain, ultrasound, hCG, eligibility and patient preference. NICE NG126 gives detailed selection criteria; surgery is urgent if rupture or instability is suspected.

PLAB application: Methotrexate needs strict follow-up and pregnancy avoidance advice per specialist protocol.

Miscarriage terminology

Miscarriage is pregnancy loss before 24 completed weeks in UK practice. Types include threatened miscarriage (bleeding with ongoing pregnancy), missed miscarriage, incomplete miscarriage and complete miscarriage. Diagnosis depends on symptoms and ultrasound criteria.

PLAB application: Do not diagnose miscarriage from bleeding alone.

Ultrasound and uncertain viability

Transvaginal ultrasound assesses location, gestational sac, embryo and heartbeat. Strict ultrasound criteria and repeat-scan intervals are essential before confirming non-viability. Avoid assumptions from last menstrual period alone.

PLAB application: When viability is uncertain, follow the recommended repeat ultrasound pathway.

Miscarriage management choices

For confirmed early miscarriage, options may include expectant, medical and surgical management based on symptoms, bleeding, infection, clinical needs and informed choice. Explain expected pain and bleeding, follow-up, complications and emergency return advice.

PLAB application: Heavy bleeding, haemodynamic instability or infection changes management urgency.

Septic miscarriage and retained products

Suspect infection with fever, uterine tenderness, offensive discharge or systemic illness. Escalate urgently for sepsis assessment, antibiotics and gynaecology review; evacuation may be required. Retained products may cause ongoing bleeding or infection.

PLAB application: Do not manage suspected septic miscarriage as routine outpatient follow-up.

Anti-D prophylaxis in early pregnancy

Anti-D immunoglobulin eligibility depends on RhD-negative status and the type of event or intervention. NICE NG126 recommends anti-D for RhD-negative patients having a surgical procedure to manage ectopic pregnancy or miscarriage; it does not recommend it for solely medical management, threatened miscarriage, complete miscarriage or PUL. Check current local policy.

PLAB application: Avoid the incorrect rule that all first-trimester bleeding requires anti-D.

Recurrent miscarriage and emotional care

Acknowledge loss without blame, avoid unsupported causal claims, assess psychological impact and offer follow-up. Recurrent miscarriage merits specialist evaluation according to local criteria and individual circumstances. Discuss future pregnancy plans and early scan access.

PLAB application: Use sensitive, non-judgemental language and ask about support.

Safety-netting and PLAB 2 synthesis

Explain diagnosis and uncertainty in plain language, use shared decision-making and document follow-up. Return immediately for worsening unilateral pain, shoulder-tip pain, fainting, severe bleeding, fever or feeling very unwell. Provide written advice and a contact route.

PLAB application: Always communicate that ectopic pregnancy remains possible until pregnancy location is established.

PLAB 2 — Early pregnancy bleeding consultation

Begin with empathy and privacy. Ask about bleeding, pain, collapse and prior pregnancy history; assess observations and red flags. Explain that symptoms alone cannot distinguish a miscarriage from an ectopic pregnancy. Discuss ultrasound, blood tests, follow-up and options using shared decisions. Offer written safety-net advice and emotional support.

Deep Dive

Discriminatory hCG caution

Do not assume that a specific hCG threshold reliably proves or excludes ectopic pregnancy. NICE advises using hCG in PUL to assess trophoblastic proliferation and follow-up rather than determine location.

Avoiding false miscarriage diagnosis

Early ultrasound may be indeterminate. Use strict criteria for embryo size, sac size and heartbeat; obtain repeat ultrasound at the correct interval and second opinion when required.

Methotrexate follow-up

Medical treatment is only suitable for selected stable patients and requires serial hCG monitoring, access to emergency services, counselling on adverse effects and pregnancy avoidance.

Anti-D nuance

NICE NG126 distinguishes surgical from solely medical management in early pregnancy; verify RhD status, local policy and updates.

Rh and other differentials

Bleeding may also be due to cervical causes, infection or non-gynaecological abdominal pathology. Do not anchor on miscarriage or ectopic without assessment.

Trauma-informed care

Pregnancy loss can be emotionally significant. Explain options at a suitable pace, allow support people if desired and avoid blame or unsupported reassurances.
