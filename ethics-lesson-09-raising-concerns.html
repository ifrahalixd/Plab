Raising Concerns, Whistleblowing & Patient Safety

Objectives: Recognise unsafe practice; protect patients promptly; escalate concerns appropriately; document objectively; distinguish incident reporting, speaking up, and duty of candour.

1 · GMC professional responsibility

Under Good medical practice (2024), doctors must act promptly if patient safety or dignity is, or may be, seriously compromised. Raise concerns through appropriate channels, do not ignore or conceal safety risks, and support colleagues who raise concerns. Patient safety takes priority over loyalty to colleagues or fear of embarrassment.

2 · First response to unsafe care

  1. Protect the patient: address immediate danger, seek urgent senior help, arrange safe cover and treatment.
  2. State the concern factually to the person able to act, where safe and appropriate.
  3. Escalate to supervising consultant, clinical lead, safeguarding lead or organisational speaking-up route as appropriate.
  4. Keep a factual, timely record and follow incident-reporting procedures.
  5. If no action is taken and risk persists, escalate further; use external routes when justified.

3 · Recognise red flags

4 · Incident report versus clinical record

Document the patient's assessment, treatment, communication and relevant facts in the clinical record. Use the organisation's incident system for learning and safety investigation. Neither replaces the other. Avoid speculation, blame, alteration of records or identifying patient information in public channels.

5 · Speaking up and whistleblowing

Start with the most appropriate effective route, unless that route is implicated or unsafe. In England, a Freedom to Speak Up Guardian may provide an additional route. Employment-law protections and external disclosures have specific conditions; the uploaded GMC PDFs establish professional duties but do not set out all statutory whistleblowing tests.

6 · Duty of candour is related but distinct

When something goes wrong and causes or may cause harm or distress, be open and honest with patients, apologise, explain what is known, offer support and follow up. An apology is not, by itself, an admission of legal liability.

PLAB trap: Do not wait for conclusive proof or investigate a potentially dangerous colleague yourself while patients remain at risk. Act on reasonable concerns and escalate proportionately.

SAFE escalation pathway

  1. S — Secure immediate safety: stabilise patients, stop unsafe action where within your role, obtain urgent help.
  2. A — Alert the right person: senior clinician, clinical governance lead or emergency management according to urgency.
  3. F — Facts, not accusations: describe what you observed, when, who may be at risk, and what has been done.
  4. E — Escalate and evaluate: use incident/speaking-up routes, check that risk is addressed, escalate again if unresolved.

Choose the route

Immediate threat: emergency clinical response and senior escalation now. Persistent clinical safety issue: supervisor, medical director/clinical governance, incident reporting. Concern involving your line manager: alternative senior route or speaking-up guardian. Safeguarding: safeguarding lead and urgent statutory routes as appropriate. Unresolved serious concern: consider regulator or other appropriate external authority with advice and proportionate confidentiality safeguards.

Four PLAB clinical cases

Case 1 · Colleague appears intoxicated

A doctor smells strongly of alcohol and is about to prescribe for acutely ill patients. Best action: prevent immediate risk through appropriate senior help, ensure safe clinical cover and report concerns promptly; do not confront alone if unsafe.

Case 2 · Repeated medication errors

You discover several near misses from confusing packaging. Best action: address current risks, use incident reporting, escalate the system hazard and propose safer processes.

Case 3 · Supervisor dismisses a concern

You have documented repeated missed observations on a ward, but your supervisor refuses to act. Best action: escalate via an alternative appropriate route, ensuring patients are safe.

Case 4 · Wrong dose given

A patient receives an incorrect anticoagulant dose. Best action: assess and treat promptly, notify seniors, document objectively, explain and apologise as appropriate, and report the incident.

PLAB 2 OSCE · Raising a safety concern

Station: You are an FY2 who has observed repeated missed sepsis observations. Speak to the senior doctor who is initially defensive.

Suggested opening

“I'm concerned that some patients with suspected sepsis are missing timely observations. I want to make sure they're safe and agree what we can do immediately.”

Station approach

  1. Introduce yourself and state the specific patient safety concern.
  2. Describe observable facts, not personal accusations.
  3. Explain current risk and request immediate action or review.
  4. Remain respectful if challenged; refocus on patient safety.
  5. Agree who will assess patients and what safety measures start now.
  6. Escalate further if the risk remains unresolved.
  7. Record relevant clinical facts and report incidents appropriately.

Examiner checklist

Avoid promising secrecy, arguing about blame or delaying action until a formal investigation is complete.

Examination traps

  • Do not ignore safety risks to protect a colleague.
  • Do not delay immediate care for an incident form.
  • Do not make unsupported accusations or public disclosures.
  • Do not assume an internal report alone satisfies candour duties.
  • Do not stop escalating if a serious unresolved risk remains.
