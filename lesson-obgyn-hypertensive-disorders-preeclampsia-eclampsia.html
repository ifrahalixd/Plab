Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy: Pre-eclampsia & Eclampsia

Emergency: Seizure, BP ≥160 systolic or ≥110 diastolic, severe headache, visual symptoms, pulmonary oedema or HELLP features require immediate obstetric assessment.

Overview

Hypertensive disorders in pregnancy include chronic hypertension, gestational hypertension, pre-eclampsia and eclampsia. These disorders can endanger both mother and fetus.

PLAB application: Classify by timing, features and end-organ involvement.

Blood pressure thresholds

Hypertension in pregnancy is blood pressure at least 140/90 mmHg. Severe hypertension is systolic at least 160 or diastolic at least 110 mmHg. Confirm readings with appropriate technique.

PLAB application: Severe readings need urgent assessment and treatment.

Classification

Chronic hypertension is present before pregnancy or before 20 weeks. Gestational hypertension develops after 20 weeks without significant proteinuria or organ dysfunction. Pre-eclampsia is new hypertension after 20 weeks plus proteinuria, maternal organ dysfunction or uteroplacental dysfunction.

PLAB application: Proteinuria is not required when other diagnostic criteria are present.

Pathophysiology

Abnormal placentation and maternal endothelial dysfunction contribute to vasoconstriction, capillary leak, coagulation changes and placental insufficiency.

PLAB application: Understand the link to renal, hepatic, neurological and fetal complications.

Risk factors

Risk rises with prior pre-eclampsia, chronic hypertension, chronic kidney disease, diabetes, autoimmune disease, first pregnancy, multiple pregnancy, higher BMI, older maternal age and family history.

PLAB application: Assess risk at booking and revisit when clinical status changes.

Prevention

NICE recommends low-dose aspirin 75–150 mg daily from 12 weeks until birth for women at high risk or with more than one moderate risk factor; selection follows NG133 criteria.

PLAB application: Do not start or stop medicines without obstetric review.

Clinical symptoms

Ask about severe headache, visual disturbances, epigastric or right upper quadrant pain, vomiting, sudden facial or hand swelling, breathlessness, reduced fetal movements and seizures. Some patients have no symptoms.

PLAB application: Symptoms can signal organ involvement even before results return.

Assessment and examination

Use ABCDE if unwell. Check repeated blood pressure, pulse, oxygen saturation, mental state, reflexes where indicated, urine output and signs of pulmonary oedema; assess fetal wellbeing.

PLAB application: Escalate immediately for severe hypertension, neurological symptoms or seizures.

Investigations

Test urine protein using validated methods; quantify significant proteinuria with protein:creatinine or albumin:creatinine ratio as indicated. Order FBC/platelets, renal function and liver enzymes. Assess fetus with growth ultrasound, amniotic fluid and Doppler when indicated.

PLAB application: Do not use proteinuria quantity alone to determine severity or timing of birth.

Severe pre-eclampsia

Concerning features include severe hypertension, severe or persistent headache, visual symptoms, epigastric pain, worsening platelets, liver or kidney function, pulmonary oedema or fetal compromise.

PLAB application: Urgent obstetric and anaesthetic senior input is needed.

HELLP syndrome

HELLP stands for haemolysis, elevated liver enzymes and low platelets. It may cause upper abdominal pain, nausea and malaise and can occur with variable blood pressure findings.

PLAB application: Recognise HELLP as a potentially life-threatening obstetric emergency.

Eclampsia

Eclampsia is a seizure associated with pre-eclampsia not attributable to another cause. Protect from injury, summon emergency help, manage airway and breathing, place in left lateral position and give magnesium sulfate under emergency protocol.

PLAB application: Do not delay stabilisation while awaiting investigations.

Antihypertensive management

NICE NG133 generally recommends treatment for sustained blood pressure at least 140/90 mmHg, aiming for about 135/85 mmHg on treatment. Labetalol is commonly first choice; nifedipine or methyldopa are alternatives based on clinical circumstances. Severe hypertension requires urgent hospital treatment.

PLAB application: ACE inhibitors and ARBs are generally avoided in pregnancy.

Magnesium sulfate

Intravenous magnesium sulfate is used for eclampsia and selected severe pre-eclampsia. Monitor respiration, reflexes and urine output; toxicity requires immediate action, including calcium gluconate under protocol.

PLAB application: Magnesium sulfate prevents or treats seizures; it is not an antihypertensive.

Timing of birth and postpartum care

Delivery of the placenta is definitive treatment, but timing balances maternal and fetal risks, gestational age and clinical condition. Monitor closely after birth because pre-eclampsia and eclampsia may first present postpartum. Arrange blood pressure follow-up and review long-term cardiovascular risk.

PLAB application: Never assume the risk ends at delivery.

PLAB 2 consultation and safety-net

Explain diagnosis in plain language, assess symptoms and fetal movements, discuss monitoring, medicines, admission or delivery plans, obtain consent and provide clear emergency return precautions.

PLAB application: Use empathetic communication and shared decisions while prioritising safety.

PLAB 2 — Antenatal hypertension consultation

Introduce yourself and confirm identity and gestation. Ask about headache, vision, epigastric pain, breathlessness, fetal movements, prior pregnancies and medical history. Explain blood pressure and urine/blood testing; assess maternal and fetal risks. Escalate severe features promptly, explain treatment and monitoring, invite questions and safety-net.

Deep Dive

Pre-eclampsia without proteinuria

New-onset hypertension after 20 weeks can be accompanied by renal, liver, neurological, haematological or uteroplacental dysfunction without significant proteinuria.

Severe hypertension and emergency thresholds

A systolic pressure of 160 mmHg or higher OR diastolic pressure of 110 mmHg or higher is severe and requires urgent hospital evaluation.

Aspirin prevention

NICE NG133 recommends 75–150 mg aspirin daily from 12 weeks until birth for eligible high-risk women or women with more than one moderate risk factor.

HELLP and diagnostic overlap

HELLP may coexist with pre-eclampsia; investigate haemolysis, platelet count and liver enzymes. Differential diagnoses include acute fatty liver of pregnancy and thrombotic microangiopathies.

Seizure treatment

Magnesium sulfate is first-line for eclampsia and is considered for selected severe pre-eclampsia. Follow local IV dosing and monitoring protocols; assess reflexes, respiratory rate and urine output.

Delivery and aftercare

Birth is the definitive placental treatment, but the timing and route are individualised. Continue postpartum surveillance, medication review and counselling about future cardiovascular risk.
