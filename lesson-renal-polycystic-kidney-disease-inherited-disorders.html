Polycystic Kidney Disease & Inherited Renal Disorders

UKMLA / PLAB-focused renal medicine, safe escalation and clinical reasoning.

01. Overview

Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD) is an inherited disorder with progressive bilateral renal cyst formation. It can cause hypertension, haematuria, pain and chronic kidney disease. Autosomal recessive PKD (ARPKD) is rarer and often presents earlier in life.

PLAB pearl: Ask about family history and assess blood pressure and renal function.

02. ADPKD genetics

Most ADPKD results from pathogenic variants in PKD1 or PKD2. Inheritance is autosomal dominant: each child of an affected parent has a 50% chance of inheriting the variant. Severity varies, even within families. De novo cases occur.

PLAB pearl: Do not equate a family history with a definitive diagnosis; genetic counselling is important.

03. Pathophysiology

Polycystin dysfunction affects tubular signalling and promotes cyst growth. Enlarging cysts compress functioning kidney tissue, contributing to hypertension, inflammation and progressive loss of kidney function.

PLAB pearl: Cyst burden and eGFR decline are not identical at every stage.

04. Typical presentation

Hypertension, flank or abdominal discomfort, visible or microscopic haematuria, recurrent urinary infections, stones or incidentally discovered enlarged cystic kidneys. Some patients remain asymptomatic for decades.

PLAB pearl: Take a three-generation family history and ask about sudden severe headaches.

05. Initial assessment

Record BP, hydration, abdominal masses/tenderness and signs of infection. Request creatinine/eGFR, electrolytes, urinalysis and urine ACR. Consider pregnancy status and medications when choosing imaging and treatment.

PLAB pearl: Fever with flank pain may indicate cyst infection or pyelonephritis and needs prompt assessment.

06. Diagnosis and imaging

Renal ultrasound is commonly first-line in adults at risk. Age-dependent ultrasound criteria and family history matter. MRI or CT can clarify equivocal findings and assess kidney volume in selected specialist cases. Simple cysts alone do not establish ADPKD.

PLAB pearl: A negative ultrasound in a young at-risk person may not exclude ADPKD.

07. Genetic testing

Testing may be considered for uncertain diagnosis, absent family history, living-related kidney donation assessment and reproductive planning. Specialist genetics teams interpret variants and provide counselling.

PLAB pearl: Discuss implications for relatives, privacy and reproductive choices.

08. Hypertension management

BP control is central to preserving kidney function. ACE inhibitors or ARBs are commonly used, particularly with albuminuria, with monitoring of potassium and creatinine. Individual targets depend on age, CKD stage and guidance.

PLAB pearl: Avoid ACE inhibitors and ARBs in pregnancy; discuss pregnancy planning.

09. Disease-modifying therapy

Tolvaptan, a vasopressin V2-receptor antagonist, may slow progression in selected adults with ADPKD and evidence of rapid progression, subject to specialist eligibility and local commissioning. It causes marked thirst/polyuria and requires liver safety monitoring.

PLAB pearl: Tolvaptan is not appropriate for every person with ADPKD.

10. Haematuria and pain

Cyst bleeding can cause sudden flank pain and haematuria, but stones, infection and malignancy must be considered. Assess haemodynamic status, infection and obstruction. Persistent or severe symptoms require imaging and specialist review.

PLAB pearl: Do not assume all haematuria in a person with ADPKD is benign cyst bleeding.

11. Infections and stones

UTIs, cyst infections and nephrolithiasis are recognised complications. Cyst infection may require prolonged specialist-directed antibiotics; evaluate sepsis and obstruction. Drug penetration into cysts can influence antibiotic selection.

PLAB pearl: Sepsis or an infected obstructed kidney needs urgent escalation.

12. Extrarenal manifestations

Hepatic cysts are common. Other associations include intracranial aneurysms, cardiac valve abnormalities and abdominal wall hernias. Intracranial aneurysm screening is individualised, especially with personal or family history of aneurysm or subarachnoid haemorrhage.

PLAB pearl: Sudden thunderclap headache is an emergency, not a routine outpatient symptom.

13. Progression and CKD complications

Monitor eGFR, ACR, BP and symptoms over time. Manage anaemia, bone-mineral disease, acidosis and cardiovascular risk according to CKD guidance. Advanced disease may require dialysis or transplantation.

PLAB pearl: Trend kidney function rather than relying on one creatinine result.

14. ARPKD and other cystic diseases

ARPKD is associated with PKHD1 and can present in infancy with enlarged echogenic kidneys and congenital hepatic fibrosis. Differential diagnoses include acquired cystic kidney disease, tuberous sclerosis complex and other inherited cystic disorders.

PLAB pearl: Paediatric presentations require specialist assessment.

15. Alport syndrome and inherited nephropathies

Alport syndrome involves type IV collagen genes (COL4A3, COL4A4, COL4A5) and can cause persistent haematuria, progressive CKD, sensorineural hearing loss and ocular abnormalities. Family history, audiology and genetics help evaluation.

PLAB pearl: Haematuria plus hearing impairment suggests Alport syndrome.

16. PLAB 2 consultation and safety-netting

Explain the inherited condition clearly; ask about family and reproductive concerns, offer referral for genetic counselling, discuss BP checks and renal monitoring. Urgent review is needed for fever/flank pain, reduced urine, severe haematuria or sudden severe headache.

PLAB pearl: Use shared decision-making and avoid deterministic predictions about progression.

Deep Dive

Cyst biology

Polycystin signalling disruption promotes abnormal tubular epithelial growth and fluid secretion; expanding cysts impair surrounding nephrons.

Imaging nuances

Ultrasound diagnostic and exclusion criteria depend on age, genotype likelihood and family history. MRI can refine equivocal cases and measure total kidney volume.

Tolvaptan mechanism

V2 receptor antagonism reduces vasopressin-mediated cyclic AMP signalling and cyst growth; specialist prescribing requires counselling about aquaresis and liver toxicity.

Aneurysm screening

Routine screening for everyone is not automatic. Individual decisions consider family/personal aneurysm history and other risk factors; MRA is commonly used when indicated.

Inherited differential

Alport syndrome involves COL4 genes and glomerular basement membrane abnormalities; ARPKD affects renal collecting ducts and is associated with congenital hepatic fibrosis.

Clinical communication

A 50% transmission probability does not predict age of onset or disease severity. Avoid assumptions; offer genetic and reproductive counselling.
