Normal Pregnancy, Antenatal Care & Screening

Urgent safety net: Severe abdominal pain, heavy bleeding, collapse, severe headache or visual changes, chest symptoms, or reduced fetal movements need prompt maternity assessment.

Scope and objectives

Describe normal pregnancy physiology, calculate gestational age, plan UK antenatal care, explain screening choices, recognise urgent symptoms and practise an empathetic first booking consultation.

PLAB application: Screening is an informed choice, not a compulsory test.

Confirming pregnancy

A positive urine pregnancy test detects hCG. Ask about last menstrual period, cycle regularity, contraception, fertility treatment, symptoms and previous pregnancy. Ultrasound confirms location and dates when indicated.

PLAB application: Pain or bleeding in early pregnancy requires assessment for ectopic pregnancy.

Dating and estimated due date

Gestational age is counted from the first day of the last menstrual period; an estimated due date is approximately 40 weeks later. A first-trimester dating scan, usually at 11–14 weeks in UK pathways, provides more reliable dating than an uncertain LMP.

PLAB application: Use the documented ultrasound-based EDD once established.

Physiological changes

Normal pregnancy increases plasma volume, cardiac output and renal filtration; physiological haemodilution may lower haemoglobin. Nausea, urinary frequency, breast tenderness and mild dependent oedema may occur.

PLAB application: Do not label severe headache, visual disturbance or sudden swelling as normal.

Booking appointment

Arrange the first midwife booking appointment ideally by 10 weeks. Review medical, surgical, obstetric, mental health and family history; medicines, allergies, smoking, alcohol, safeguarding, domestic abuse and social support. Measure BP, BMI and assess individual risks.

PLAB application: Ask sensitive questions privately and without judgement.

Antenatal appointment schedule

NICE NG201 recommends a personalised schedule; routine appointments are more frequent in a first pregnancy than after previous uncomplicated births. Review wellbeing, blood pressure, urine protein when appropriate, fetal growth later in pregnancy and concerns at every contact.

PLAB application: Risk factors or complications require additional surveillance.

Routine booking blood tests

Offer full blood count, blood group and rhesus D status, red-cell antibody screening and infectious disease screening for HIV, hepatitis B and syphilis. Assess haemoglobinopathy risk and offer screening according to the NHS programme.

PLAB application: Explain consent, confidentiality and what positive results mean.

Ultrasound screening

The dating scan is offered around 11–14 weeks; the fetal anomaly scan is usually offered at 18–20+6 weeks. Explain that ultrasound cannot detect every condition and may prompt further tests.

PLAB application: Clarify whether a scan is for dating, screening or diagnostic evaluation.

Chromosomal screening

Offer NHS screening for Down syndrome (trisomy 21), Edwards syndrome (trisomy 18) and Patau syndrome (trisomy 13). Combined screening uses ultrasound and maternal blood markers when feasible; alternative screening pathways depend on gestation and local programme. Higher-chance results may lead to NIPT or diagnostic testing.

PLAB application: Screening estimates chance; CVS or amniocentesis can establish a diagnosis but have procedural risks.

Fetal growth and movements

From 24 weeks, assess symphysis–fundal height at appropriate visits in uncomplicated singleton pregnancy. Explain the baby’s usual movement pattern and seek immediate maternity assessment for reduced or changed movements; do not wait until the next day.

PLAB application: Do not reassure solely with a home Doppler.

Blood pressure and pre-eclampsia prevention

Check BP at antenatal visits and assess for proteinuria where recommended. Identify high-risk patients for low-dose aspirin from 12 weeks according to NICE NG133 criteria and clinician assessment. Explain symptoms of pre-eclampsia.

PLAB application: Severe headache, visual changes, epigastric pain or sudden swelling warrant urgent review.

Nutrition, supplements and lifestyle

Recommend folic acid 400 micrograms daily before conception through 12 weeks for most; higher-dose 5 mg is indicated for selected higher-risk patients after clinical advice. Recommend vitamin D 10 micrograms daily. Discuss balanced diet, food safety, exercise, smoking cessation and avoiding alcohol.

PLAB application: Review all prescribed, OTC and herbal medicines for pregnancy safety.

Vaccination and infection prevention

Offer vaccines according to the current UK pregnancy programme, including pertussis, influenza and RSV where eligible; COVID-19 advice depends on current eligibility. Discuss hand hygiene, food-borne infection prevention and when to seek care for fever.

PLAB application: Check current NHS/UKHSA seasonal timing and eligibility rather than memorising outdated schedules.

Rhesus D and anti-D

Determine maternal RhD status and antibody screen. RhD-negative, non-sensitised patients may need routine antenatal anti-D and anti-D after sensitising events according to local protocol; fetal RhD testing may guide care.

PLAB application: Bleeding, trauma and invasive procedures may require prompt assessment for anti-D.

Common symptoms and red flags

Mild nausea and reflux are common, but severe vomiting with dehydration needs evaluation. Urgent symptoms include heavy vaginal bleeding, severe abdominal pain, collapse, severe headache or visual symptoms, chest pain, breathlessness, fever, fluid leakage and reduced fetal movements.

PLAB application: Escalate based on gestational age and local early-pregnancy/maternity pathway.

Birth planning and shared decisions

Discuss place of birth, preferences, pain relief, feeding plans, support people and postnatal care without presuming choices. Document personalised risk assessment, interpreters if needed and a clear plan for follow-up.

PLAB application: Use teach-back and respect informed refusal of screening.

PLAB 2 — First antenatal booking consultation

Scenario: A patient attends at approximately 8 weeks after a positive home pregnancy test. Ask about symptoms, previous pregnancies, medical history and medicines. Explain antenatal visits, screening options and ultrasound. Ask about mental health and safeguarding privately. Explore ideas, concerns and expectations, confirm understanding, respect consent and provide clear safety-net advice.

Deep Dive

Interpreting screening

Explain the difference between a higher-chance screening result and a confirmed diagnosis. Discuss choices and referral to specialist fetal medicine counselling.

Dating and viability

The ultrasound-based estimated due date generally takes precedence when LMP is uncertain. Early pregnancy pain or bleeding may need urgent assessment and transvaginal ultrasound.

Pre-eclampsia prevention

NICE NG133 identifies high-risk and multiple moderate-risk groups for aspirin prophylaxis from 12 weeks. Risk assessment must be individualised.

RhD prophylaxis

Non-sensitised RhD-negative pregnant patients may be offered anti-D according to local policy and fetal RhD status; sensitising events require timely assessment.

Communication and equity

Use interpreters when needed, explain uncertainty, avoid assumptions about family structure, support informed choice and discuss sensitive topics privately.

UK practice reminders

NHS screening and vaccine schedules can change. Use current NICE, NHS and UKHSA guidance for exact eligibility, appointment timing and local pathways.
