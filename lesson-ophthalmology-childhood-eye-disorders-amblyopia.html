Childhood Eye Disorders & Amblyopia

Urgent red flags: Leukocoria, abnormal red reflex, acute squint with neurological signs or rapidly worsening vision need urgent specialist assessment.

Overview

Childhood eye disorders may threaten visual development even when a child does not complain. Amblyopia is reduced best-corrected vision from abnormal visual experience during development.

PLAB application: Assess each eye separately and refer promptly.

Visual development

The visual system is particularly adaptable in early childhood. Persistent blur, misalignment or deprivation can disrupt cortical visual development.

PLAB application: Earlier detection improves treatment opportunity.

Amblyopia causes

Strabismus, unequal refractive errors (anisometropia), high bilateral refractive error and visual deprivation such as congenital cataract are important causes.

PLAB application: Search for an underlying cause rather than labelling all reduced vision amblyopia.

Amblyopia symptoms

Amblyopia is often asymptomatic. Clues include squint, poor depth perception or a failed vision screening test.

PLAB application: Children may compensate with their stronger eye.

Vision screening

UK childhood vision screening is generally offered around age 4–5 years. Test monocular acuity with age-appropriate methods.

PLAB application: A failed screen needs orthoptic/eye-service assessment.

Strabismus

Strabismus is ocular misalignment; it can be constant or intermittent. Persistent squint after early infancy needs assessment.

PLAB application: A new acute squint with neurological symptoms requires urgent assessment.

Cover test

The cover–uncover test detects manifest deviation; the alternate cover test can reveal latent deviation. Observe fixation and eye movements.

PLAB application: Explain the manoeuvre to the child and caregiver.

Refractive errors

Hyperopia, myopia and astigmatism may reduce acuity. Anisometropia can cause unilateral amblyopia.

PLAB application: Cycloplegic refraction is often part of specialist assessment.

Amblyopia management

Correct refractive error with glasses. Orthoptists may prescribe patching or atropine penalisation of the stronger eye in selected cases.

PLAB application: Treatment must be supervised to avoid harming the better eye.

Patching counselling

Patching encourages use of the weaker eye. Explain prescribed duration, adherence, school impact and follow-up; never invent a universal patching schedule.

PLAB application: Work with caregivers on practical adherence.

Congenital cataract

A congenital cataract can obstruct the visual axis and cause deprivation amblyopia. An abnormal red reflex is a red flag.

PLAB application: Urgent ophthalmology referral for leukocoria or absent red reflex.

Retinoblastoma

Retinoblastoma can present with leukocoria, strabismus or visual concerns. It is an important sight- and life-threatening differential.

PLAB application: An unexplained white pupil needs urgent specialist assessment.

Nasolacrimal duct obstruction

Congenital nasolacrimal duct obstruction often causes watering and sticky discharge without a painful red eye. Most cases improve during infancy.

PLAB application: Fever, painful medial canthal swelling or systemic illness suggests dacryocystitis.

Childhood conjunctivitis

Conjunctivitis may cause redness and discharge; assess pain, photophobia and vision for more serious pathology.

PLAB application: A painful photophobic eye or corneal opacity warrants urgent evaluation.

Red flags

Leukocoria, abnormal red reflex, acute-onset squint with neurological symptoms, proptosis, painful eye movements and sudden visual deterioration require urgent escalation.

PLAB application: Never reassure based solely on a normal-looking external eye.

PLAB 2 consultation

Take pregnancy/birth history, family history, visual behaviour, onset of squint and screening results. Examine red reflex, acuity, alignment and ocular movements as appropriate.

PLAB application: Explain referral, glasses, patching and follow-up in accessible language.

PLAB 2 — Child with reduced vision or squint

Introduce yourself to child and caregiver; obtain appropriate consent. Ask about visual behaviour, screening, onset, family history and development. Check each eye separately, red reflex and ocular alignment when possible. Explain glasses, orthoptic assessment, supervised patching or atropine, and safety-net urgent signs.

Deep Dive

Critical period

Visual pathways develop through childhood. Early persistent blur or misalignment can cause amblyopia; treatment responsiveness generally decreases with age.

Why patching works

Covering the stronger eye encourages use of the weaker eye; dosing is individualised by specialists.

Refraction

Cycloplegic refraction can reveal significant hyperopia or anisometropia that children may accommodate through.

Red reflex

An absent, asymmetrical or white reflex can signal cataract or intraocular disease including retinoblastoma.

Strabismus and binocular vision

Misalignment may impair stereopsis and produce suppression; both acuity and alignment matter.

Family-centred care

Adherence is often challenging; involve caregivers and the child, explain goals and avoid blame.
