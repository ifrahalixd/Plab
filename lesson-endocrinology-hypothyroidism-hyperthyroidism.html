Hypothyroidism & Hyperthyroidism

Emergency: Thyroid storm and myxoedema coma require immediate hospital assessment and specialist escalation.

Thyroid physiology

The hypothalamus releases TRH, pituitary releases TSH and thyroid produces T4 and T3. Free T4 and T3 suppress TSH by negative feedback.

PLAB application: Interpret TSH together with free T4; T3 may help assess hyperthyroidism.

Hypothyroidism presentation

Fatigue, cold intolerance, constipation, weight gain, dry skin, hair thinning, bradycardia, heavy periods and slowed reflex relaxation may occur.

PLAB application: Symptoms are nonspecific; confirm biochemically.

Hypothyroidism causes

Hashimoto autoimmune thyroiditis is common; other causes include thyroidectomy, radioiodine, iodine imbalance, drugs such as amiodarone or lithium and pituitary disease.

PLAB application: Check relevant medicines and prior neck treatments.

Hypothyroidism blood tests

Primary hypothyroidism: raised TSH and low free T4. Subclinical primary hypothyroidism: raised TSH with normal free T4. Central hypothyroidism: low free T4 with inappropriately low or normal TSH.

PLAB application: A normal TSH does not exclude central hypothyroidism.

Hypothyroidism treatment

Levothyroxine is first-line for primary hypothyroidism. Adjust using TSH and clinical response, considering age, cardiac disease, pregnancy and interactions.

PLAB application: Start cautiously in older people or those with coronary disease; follow current NICE dosing guidance.

Levothyroxine counselling

Take consistently, usually before breakfast, and separate from iron or calcium supplements. Improvement takes weeks; repeat TSH after starting or changing dose per guidance.

PLAB application: Do not change or stop doses without medical review.

Subclinical hypothyroidism

Management depends on persistent TSH elevation, symptoms, age, thyroid autoantibodies and pregnancy context. Repeat testing and shared decisions are important.

PLAB application: NICE considers levothyroxine when TSH is persistently ≥10 mIU/L on two occasions 3 months apart; individualise other cases.

Hyperthyroidism presentation

Weight loss despite appetite, heat intolerance, sweating, tremor, palpitations, diarrhoea, anxiety, proximal weakness and menstrual changes are typical.

PLAB application: Examine pulse, thyroid, tremor, eyes and signs of heart failure.

Hyperthyroidism causes

Graves disease, toxic multinodular goitre, toxic adenoma and thyroiditis are key causes; excess thyroid hormone ingestion is another possibility.

PLAB application: Graves may cause eye disease; thyroiditis may release preformed hormone.

Hyperthyroidism blood tests

Overt primary hyperthyroidism usually shows suppressed TSH and raised free T4 and/or T3. TSH receptor antibodies support Graves disease.

PLAB application: Low TSH alone does not establish overt hyperthyroidism; interpret alongside thyroid hormones.

Hyperthyroidism investigations

Check ECG if tachycardic or palpitations; consider TRAb testing, thyroid imaging or specialist assessment according to cause. Pregnancy changes investigation choices.

PLAB application: Do not use radionuclide scans during pregnancy.

Hyperthyroidism management

Beta-blockers may control adrenergic symptoms if suitable. Antithyroid drugs, radioactive iodine or surgery are options depending on cause and patient factors.

PLAB application: Antithyroid drugs require counselling about rare agranulocytosis and liver injury.

Carbimazole safety

Before antithyroid medication, obtain baseline full blood count and liver tests as advised. Fever, sore throat or mouth ulcers require stopping the drug and urgent blood count assessment.

PLAB application: Do not dismiss possible agranulocytosis.

Thyroid eye disease

Graves orbitopathy may cause gritty eyes, proptosis, diplopia and sight-threatening optic neuropathy. Smoking increases risk.

PLAB application: New visual loss, colour desaturation or severe eye pain warrants urgent specialist assessment.

Thyroid storm

Life-threatening thyrotoxicosis with fever, marked tachycardia, agitation or delirium, gastrointestinal symptoms and organ dysfunction.

PLAB application: ABCDE, urgent senior/endocrine/critical-care help and protocol-directed treatment; do not delay escalation.

Myxoedema coma and PLAB 2

Severe decompensated hypothyroidism can cause hypothermia, reduced consciousness, bradycardia, hypotension and hypoventilation. For counselling, explain diagnosis, treatment, follow-up and red flags.

PLAB application: Suspected myxoedema coma is a medical emergency requiring urgent admission and specialist treatment.

PLAB 2: Thyroid consultation OSCE

  1. Introduce yourself, confirm identity and consent; explore concerns.
  2. Ask about weight, temperature tolerance, bowel habits, tremor, palpitations, fatigue and menstrual symptoms.
  3. Check medication history, pregnancy possibility, neck symptoms and family history.
  4. Explain TSH and FT4 results in plain language and discuss management choices.
  5. Safety-net chest pain, severe palpitations, confusion, fever, severe drowsiness and visual deterioration.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Negative feedback

TSH rises when circulating thyroid hormone is insufficient in primary gland failure, but pituitary failure may prevent this rise.

T3 thyrotoxicosis

Some patients have suppressed TSH and elevated T3 despite normal FT4, making T3 measurement useful.

Why palpitations occur

Excess thyroid hormone increases beta-adrenergic sensitivity and predisposes to tachyarrhythmia.

Thyroiditis versus Graves

Destructive thyroiditis releases stored hormone and often differs from sustained hormone overproduction in treatment approach.

Pregnancy and thyroid disease

Pregnancy affects thyroid physiology and medication choices; specialist management is important, especially for antithyroid drugs.

Emergency distinction

Thyroid storm is hypermetabolic with fever and tachycardia; myxoedema coma is hypometabolic with hypothermia and depressed consciousness.
