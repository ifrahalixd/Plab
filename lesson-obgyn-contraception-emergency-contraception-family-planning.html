Contraception, Emergency Contraception & Family Planning

Red flags: Severe lower abdominal pain, collapse, positive pregnancy test with pain or bleeding, or suspected sexual assault requires urgent assessment and appropriate support.

Overview

Contraception is chosen through informed, non-judgemental shared decision-making. Discuss effectiveness, safety, preferences, bleeding effects, STI protection, reversibility and reproductive goals.

PLAB application: Ask what matters most to the patient before recommending a method.

Pregnancy risk and sexual history

Ask about last menstrual period, intercourse dates, contraception and missed doses, pregnancy symptoms, postpartum or breastfeeding status, STI risk and safeguarding concerns.

PLAB application: Offer a pregnancy test when indicated; a very early negative test cannot exclude pregnancy.

Effectiveness and typical use

Long-acting reversible contraception (LARC), including implants and intrauterine methods, is highly effective without daily adherence. Pills, patches, rings and condoms depend on correct use.

PLAB application: Explain typical-use failure rather than promising zero risk.

Combined hormonal contraception

Combined pill, patch and vaginal ring contain oestrogen plus progestogen. Benefits include cycle control; risks include venous thromboembolism and selected cardiovascular complications.

PLAB application: Check blood pressure, migraine with aura, smoking age ≥35, VTE history and UKMEC eligibility.

Progestogen-only pill

Progestogen-only pills avoid oestrogen. Missed-pill rules differ by formulation (traditional, desogestrel and drospirenone). Irregular bleeding is common.

PLAB application: Use the specific product guidance for late or missed pills and backup contraception.

Implant and injection

The etonogestrel implant is a highly effective LARC method with irregular bleeding as a common reason for review. Depot medroxyprogesterone injections can affect bleeding and bone density.

PLAB application: Discuss return to fertility, injection timing and patient preference.

Copper IUD

The copper intrauterine device provides long-acting, hormone-free contraception and can be used for emergency contraception. It may worsen heavy or painful periods.

PLAB application: Check pregnancy exclusion and discuss insertion, expulsion and infection warning signs.

Levonorgestrel intrauterine system

The LNG-IUS is highly effective and often reduces menstrual bleeding; irregular spotting can occur initially. Duration varies by device and indication.

PLAB application: Avoid assuming all LNG-IUS products have identical licensed durations.

Barrier methods and STI prevention

External and internal condoms reduce STI transmission and provide contraception. Diaphragms and fertility-awareness methods require correct use and counselling.

PLAB application: Offer STI screening and condoms even when a LARC method is used.

Emergency contraception options

Copper IUD is the most effective emergency contraception. Oral options include ulipristal acetate 30 mg within 120 hours and levonorgestrel 1.5 mg generally within 72 hours, with specialist guidance for later use.

PLAB application: Offer the copper IUD whenever eligible; act quickly after unprotected sex.

Emergency copper IUD timing

A copper IUD can generally be fitted within 5 days of the first unprotected intercourse in a cycle or within 5 days of the earliest estimated ovulation, whichever allows later fitting, under specialist assessment.

PLAB application: Assess multiple intercourse episodes and cycle uncertainty; refer urgently for fitting.

Ulipristal and hormonal contraception

After ulipristal emergency contraception, wait 5 days before starting or restarting hormonal contraception; use condoms or abstain during this period and until the chosen method becomes effective.

PLAB application: Explain why immediate progestogen may reduce ulipristal effectiveness.

Levonorgestrel and ongoing contraception

Hormonal contraception can generally start immediately after levonorgestrel emergency contraception; advise appropriate backup protection based on the chosen method.

PLAB application: Give clear written quick-start and backup instructions.

Drug interactions, weight and vomiting

Enzyme-inducing medicines can reduce effectiveness of oral emergency contraception and some regular methods. Higher weight/BMI may affect oral EC effectiveness. Vomiting soon after oral EC may require repeat dosing.

PLAB application: Check BNF/FSRH advice for enzyme inducers, timing and dosing; prioritise copper IUD where suitable.

Postpartum and breastfeeding contraception

Fertility can return before the first period. Method suitability depends on time after birth, breastfeeding and VTE risk; some methods can be started immediately postpartum.

PLAB application: Do not assume breastfeeding alone reliably prevents pregnancy unless strict LAM criteria are met.

Follow-up, consent and safeguarding

Confirm informed consent, confidentiality and capacity. Offer accessible information, follow-up for adverse effects, pregnancy testing at 21 days after the last unprotected sex when indicated, and urgent review for red flags.

PLAB application: Safeguard sensitively and explain that emergency contraception does not treat existing pregnancy or protect against STIs.

PLAB 2 — Contraception consultation

Introduce yourself; confirm confidentiality and consent. Explore goals, pregnancy risk, menstrual and sexual history, migraine with aura, VTE, smoking, medicines and STI risks. Explain suitable choices with balanced effectiveness and side-effect information. Agree a plan, backup contraception, pregnancy test timing and safety-netting. Offer condoms and STI screening as appropriate.

Deep Dive

UKMEC eligibility

UK Medical Eligibility Criteria (UKMEC) categories guide safety: 1 no restriction; 2 benefits generally outweigh risks; 3 risks usually outweigh benefits; 4 unacceptable health risk. Use the current FSRH UKMEC for the exact method and condition.

Emergency contraception comparison

Copper IUD is most effective and offers ongoing contraception. Ulipristal is licensed to 120 hours; levonorgestrel is usually offered within 72 hours. The choice depends on timing, ovulation, medicines, eligibility and preference.

Quick start and follow-up

Quick-start methods may be possible when pregnancy can reasonably be excluded; otherwise discuss uncertainty and repeat pregnancy testing. Following oral EC, test at least 21 days after the last unprotected sex if no normal period or pregnancy remains possible.

Post-EC counselling

Ulipristal: delay hormonal methods 5 days. Levonorgestrel: hormonal methods may begin immediately. Provide method-specific backup instructions and counsel that subsequent unprotected sex can cause pregnancy.

Safeguarding and autonomy

Provide confidential, trauma-informed care. Explore coercion or sexual violence sensitively, assess capacity, offer appropriate referrals and respect patient choice without pressure.

Common exam pitfalls

Do not prescribe combined hormonal contraception without checking migraine with aura or thrombotic risk. Do not assume an oral EC pill protects against later sex. Do not omit copper IUD discussion or STI prevention.
