Polymyalgia Rheumatica & Inflammatory Myopathies

PLAB priority: Differentiate painful PMR stiffness from true myopathic weakness; screen for GCA and urgent bulbar or respiratory complications.

01. Overview

Polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR) is an inflammatory syndrome of older adults with shoulder and hip girdle pain and stiffness. Inflammatory myopathies cause true proximal muscle weakness.

PLAB application: Distinguish pain-limited movement from objective weakness.

02. PMR presentation

PMR typically affects adults over 50, with bilateral shoulder aching, hip girdle symptoms, and morning stiffness often longer than 45 minutes.

PLAB application: Ask about dressing, rising from a chair and morning function.

03. PMR examination

Shoulder and hip movements may be painful, but primary muscle strength is generally preserved when pain is accounted for.

PLAB application: Document power carefully; objective weakness suggests another diagnosis.

04. PMR investigations

ESR and CRP are usually elevated but can occasionally be normal. Check FBC, renal and liver function, thyroid function and other tests to exclude mimics; CK is typically normal.

PLAB application: Do not diagnose PMR from inflammatory markers alone.

05. PMR differential diagnosis

Consider elderly-onset rheumatoid arthritis, rotator cuff disease, osteoarthritis, hypothyroidism, inflammatory myopathy, infection, malignancy and medication-related muscle symptoms.

PLAB application: Review systemic symptoms and drug history.

06. PMR and giant cell arteritis

PMR is associated with giant cell arteritis (GCA). Ask specifically about new headache, scalp tenderness, jaw claudication and visual disturbance at diagnosis and follow-up.

PLAB application: Suspected GCA requires urgent assessment and immediate indicated treatment to protect vision.

07. PMR treatment

Specialist-guided oral glucocorticoids usually produce marked improvement; gradual tapering and relapse monitoring are important. Consider bone protection and adverse-effect prevention.

PLAB application: A poor response or atypical course should prompt diagnostic review.

08. Inflammatory myopathy overview

Idiopathic inflammatory myopathies include dermatomyositis, immune-mediated necrotising myopathy, antisynthetase syndrome and selected polymyositis-like presentations.

PLAB application: Progressive proximal weakness needs neuromuscular or rheumatology assessment.

09. Dermatomyositis

Symmetrical proximal weakness may accompany a heliotrope rash around the eyelids, Gottron papules over knuckles and photosensitive skin changes.

PLAB application: Inspect skin and assess swallowing and respiratory symptoms.

10. Muscle weakness pattern

Patients may struggle to climb stairs, rise from a low chair, lift arms or wash hair. Dysphagia, neck flexor weakness and respiratory weakness are red flags.

PLAB application: Test shoulder abduction, hip flexion and functional sit-to-stand.

11. Myositis investigations

CK is often elevated but can be normal in some disease phenotypes. Investigations may include aldolase, autoantibodies, MRI muscle, EMG and targeted muscle or skin biopsy.

PLAB application: Interpret tests in clinical context; do not exclude myositis solely on normal CK.

12. Antisynthetase syndrome

Features may include inflammatory myopathy, interstitial lung disease, arthritis, Raynaud phenomenon and rough cracked mechanic’s hands.

PLAB application: New breathlessness warrants prompt pulmonary assessment.

13. Inclusion body myositis

Typically develops in later adulthood with slowly progressive weakness involving finger flexors and quadriceps, often asymmetric, and may cause falls or dysphagia.

PLAB application: Poor response to standard immunosuppression and characteristic pattern suggest IBM.

14. Cancer and medication associations

Adult-onset dermatomyositis can be associated with malignancy; assess according to individual risk and specialist guidance. Statins and other medicines can cause toxic or immune-mediated myopathy.

PLAB application: Ask about constitutional symptoms and medication exposure.

15. Management and complications

Inflammatory myopathies require specialist-directed immunosuppression, rehabilitation, swallowing assessment and monitoring for lung or cardiac involvement when indicated.

PLAB application: Severe dysphagia or respiratory compromise requires urgent hospital care.

16. PLAB 2 approach

Elicit symptom chronology, morning stiffness, functional weakness, rash, dysphagia, breathing symptoms, medication exposure and GCA red flags. Examine proximal power, joints and skin.

PLAB application: Explain why PMR and myositis differ, and safety-net for vision loss and swallowing or breathing difficulty.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Why PMR feels stiff

Inflammation around shoulders and hips causes pain and stiffness rather than primary destruction of muscle fibres.

Why CK helps

CK reflects muscle-fibre injury, so is typically normal in PMR and frequently elevated in active myositis, but neither finding is absolute.

Dermatomyositis skin clues

Heliotrope rash and Gottron papules can help identify disease even when muscle involvement is mild.

Antisynthetase and lungs

Autoimmune disease may affect the lung interstitium, making cough, dyspnoea and oxygenation important.

Inclusion body myositis pattern

Selective finger flexor and quadriceps involvement, often asymmetric, distinguishes IBM from many other inflammatory myopathies.

PMR–GCA relationship

Both are inflammatory conditions of older adults; GCA may threaten vision and needs a different urgency and treatment approach.
