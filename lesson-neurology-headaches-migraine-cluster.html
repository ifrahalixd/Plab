Headaches, Migraine & Cluster Headache

PLAB priority: First identify secondary headache red flags; thunderclap headache needs emergency assessment.

01. Approach to headache

Characterise onset, time to peak, duration, frequency, location, severity, associated features, triggers, medications and functional impact. Check vitals and neurological examination.

PLAB pearl: A sudden maximal-at-onset headache is a red flag even if examination is normal.

02. Emergency red flags

Thunderclap onset; fever with meningism; focal neurological deficit; altered consciousness; papilloedema; new headache with cancer or immunosuppression; pregnancy/postpartum; head injury; new headache after age 50 with jaw claudication or visual symptoms.

PLAB pearl: Do not label a new thunderclap headache as migraine.

03. Primary versus secondary

Primary headaches include migraine, tension-type and trigeminal autonomic cephalalgias. Secondary causes include subarachnoid haemorrhage, meningitis, mass lesions, giant cell arteritis and cerebral venous sinus thrombosis.

PLAB pearl: Clinical context determines imaging and urgency.

04. Migraine phenotype

Recurrent attacks typically last 4–72 hours in adults; often unilateral, pulsating, moderate to severe and worsened by activity, with nausea or light/sound sensitivity. Some attacks are bilateral.

PLAB pearl: Ask about disability and frequency, not just pain intensity.

05. Migraine aura

Typical aura consists of gradually spreading, fully reversible visual, sensory or speech symptoms, often lasting 5–60 minutes per symptom. Sudden-onset or persistent focal symptoms need urgent alternative assessment.

PLAB pearl: Do not assume abrupt weakness is benign aura.

06. Acute migraine treatment

NICE CG150 supports an oral triptan plus NSAID or paracetamol, considering contraindications and patient preference; antiemetic may help even without vomiting. Avoid opioids and ergots for acute migraine.

PLAB pearl: Check vascular contraindications to triptans and pregnancy considerations.

07. Migraine prevention

Consider prophylaxis when attacks are frequent or disabling. NICE options include propranolol, topiramate or amitriptyline after individual discussion. Topiramate has important pregnancy-prevention restrictions; check current MHRA advice.

PLAB pearl: Discuss medication review, adherence and response over time.

08. Migraine in pregnancy

Use non-drug measures first where possible; paracetamol is generally preferred for acute pain. Seek specialist advice about triptans, NSAIDs and preventive medicines; topiramate carries major fetal risks.

PLAB pearl: Never prescribe preventive therapy without reproductive-safety review.

09. Tension-type headache

Typically bilateral pressing or tightening pain, mild to moderate, not aggravated by routine activity and without prominent nausea. Consider paracetamol or NSAID if appropriate, avoiding frequent use.

PLAB pearl: Do not use opioids; consider stress, sleep and posture.

10. Cluster headache

Excruciating strictly unilateral orbital, supraorbital or temporal pain lasting 15–180 minutes, occurring in bouts, with ipsilateral lacrimation, red eye, nasal congestion or ptosis and marked restlessness.

PLAB pearl: Autonomic signs and agitation distinguish it from many migraines.

11. Cluster treatment

Acute treatment is high-flow 100% oxygen through a non-rebreather mask (at least 12 L/min) and/or subcutaneous or nasal triptan, as clinically appropriate. Verapamil prevention requires ECG monitoring and specialist input.

PLAB pearl: Do not offer oral triptans for acute cluster attacks.

12. Medication-overuse headache

Suspect when headache develops or worsens with regular acute medication use for at least 3 months: triptans/opioids/combination analgesics on ≥10 days/month or simple analgesics on ≥15 days/month.

PLAB pearl: Explain withdrawal may briefly worsen headaches; offer support and follow-up.

13. Subarachnoid haemorrhage

Thunderclap headache reaching maximal intensity within minutes may signal SAH. Urgent emergency assessment and non-contrast CT brain; subsequent testing depends on timing and specialist guidance.

PLAB pearl: A normal neurological exam does not rule out SAH.

14. Giant cell arteritis

Usually in adults over 50: new temporal headache, scalp tenderness, jaw claudication, systemic symptoms or visual disturbance. Urgent same-day assessment and corticosteroids when suspected, especially with visual symptoms.

PLAB pearl: Do not delay treatment for biopsy or ultrasound if vision is threatened.

15. Other secondary headache syndromes

Consider meningitis, raised intracranial pressure, idiopathic intracranial hypertension, cerebral venous sinus thrombosis, acute glaucoma, pre-eclampsia and carbon monoxide poisoning when history fits.

PLAB pearl: Pregnancy/postpartum and visual changes alter the differential.

16. PLAB 2 headache consultation

Introduce yourself, confirm identity and consent. Explore ICE, headache diary, medication use, red flags, impact and preferences. Explain likely diagnosis, shared plan, safety-net and follow-up.

PLAB pearl: Urgent return for sudden worst headache, new neurological deficit, fever with neck stiffness or vision loss.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Trigeminovascular pathway

Migraine involves trigeminovascular activation, sensory processing and neuropeptides including CGRP; mechanisms are multifactorial.

Cortical spreading depolarisation

Migraine aura is associated with a wave of altered cortical neuronal activity that may produce spreading neurological symptoms.

Cluster autonomic activation

Trigeminal pain pathways and cranial parasympathetic activation help explain tearing, rhinorrhoea and red eye.

Why oxygen for cluster?

High-concentration oxygen can abort attacks in many patients; deliver promptly using an appropriate non-rebreather system.

Medication overuse mechanism

Repeated acute drug exposure can perpetuate headache susceptibility. Withdrawal and preventive support are central to management.

Secondary headache reasoning

Onset and systemic or neurological signs determine urgency. SAH, GCA, infection and venous thrombosis must not be missed.
