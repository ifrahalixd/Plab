Hyperkalaemia, Hypokalaemia & Electrolyte Disorders

PLAB focus: Recognise ECG emergencies, safely correct potassium disorders and prevent overly rapid sodium correction.

01. Clinical approach

Electrolyte disorders can cause arrhythmias, seizures, weakness and altered consciousness. Assess ABCDE, observations, ECG, fluid status, renal function and medications.

PLAB application: Treat the patient and ECG, not the laboratory value alone.

02. Potassium physiology

Most potassium is intracellular. Kidneys excrete excess potassium under aldosterone influence; insulin and beta-2 stimulation shift potassium into cells.

PLAB application: Rapid shifts can change serum potassium without changing total-body stores.

03. Hyperkalaemia: causes

AKI, CKD, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, potassium-sparing diuretics, potassium supplements, acidosis and tissue breakdown can raise potassium. Haemolysis can produce pseudohyperkalaemia.

PLAB application: Check for a haemolysed sample but do not delay emergency treatment if unstable.

04. Hyperkalaemia: recognition

May cause weakness or palpitations, but can be asymptomatic. ECG changes include peaked T waves, PR prolongation, broad QRS and sine-wave pattern; a normal ECG does not exclude danger.

PLAB application: Obtain urgent ECG and monitoring in significant hyperkalaemia.

05. Hyperkalaemia: severity

UKKA commonly categorises mild 5.5–5.9, moderate 6.0–6.4 and severe ≥6.5 mmol/L; urgency also depends on symptoms, ECG and rate of rise.

PLAB application: Severe hyperkalaemia requires emergency assessment even with a normal ECG.

06. Hyperkalaemia: protect the heart

If ECG changes attributable to hyperkalaemia, give IV calcium according to local resuscitation protocol. Calcium stabilises myocardium but does not lower serum potassium.

PLAB application: Repeat ECG and assess need for repeat calcium per protocol.

07. Hyperkalaemia: shift potassium

IV insulin with glucose shifts potassium intracellularly; nebulised salbutamol is an adjunct. Monitor glucose closely for delayed hypoglycaemia.

PLAB application: Do not rely on salbutamol alone for severe hyperkalaemia.

08. Hyperkalaemia: remove potassium

Stop potassium sources, review causative drugs, consider potassium binders as locally recommended, and arrange urgent dialysis for refractory severe hyperkalaemia or other dialysis indications.

PLAB application: Temporising therapies wear off; arrange definitive potassium removal.

09. Hypokalaemia: causes

Loop/thiazide diuretics, vomiting, diarrhoea, poor intake, hyperaldosteronism and intracellular shifts cause hypokalaemia. Hypomagnesaemia can make it refractory.

PLAB application: Review diuretics, GI losses and magnesium.

10. Hypokalaemia: presentation

Weakness, cramps, constipation, paralysis and arrhythmias may occur. ECG may show flattened T waves, ST depression and prominent U waves.

PLAB application: Assess ECG and severity before selecting replacement route.

11. Hypokalaemia: management

Use oral potassium when safe and appropriate; severe, symptomatic or ECG-associated cases may require carefully controlled IV replacement with monitoring. Correct magnesium and the cause.

PLAB application: Never give undiluted IV potassium boluses.

12. Hyponatraemia: assessment

Check serum osmolality, glucose, volume status, urine osmolality and urine sodium where appropriate. Causes include hypovolaemia, SIADH, heart failure and endocrine disorders.

PLAB application: Exclude hyperglycaemia-related translocational hyponatraemia.

13. Severe symptomatic hyponatraemia

Seizures, coma or severe neurological symptoms need emergency senior-led management with hypertonic saline under protocol. Avoid excessive correction to prevent osmotic demyelination.

PLAB application: Monitor sodium frequently during active correction.

14. Hypernatraemia

Usually reflects water deficit or impaired access to water; causes include dehydration, osmotic diuresis and diabetes insipidus. Assess haemodynamic stability and replace water carefully.

PLAB application: Rapid changes can be harmful; individualise correction and monitoring.

15. Magnesium and phosphate

Low magnesium may provoke arrhythmias and refractory hypokalaemia; low phosphate can cause respiratory or muscle weakness. Severe abnormalities need supervised replacement and monitoring.

PLAB application: Check magnesium in persistent low potassium.

16. PLAB 2 consultation and handover

Explain the abnormality, possible causes, ECG and blood-test monitoring, treatment risks and recurrence prevention. Escalate red flags using SBAR.

PLAB application: For unstable patients prioritise ABCDE, immediate help and treatment.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Potassium distribution

Na/K ATPase maintains intracellular potassium. Insulin and beta-2 stimulation temporarily redistribute potassium.

Cardiac effects

Altered extracellular potassium changes membrane excitability and cardiac conduction, potentially causing fatal dysrhythmias.

Why calcium comes first with ECG changes

IV calcium rapidly reduces cardiac excitability risk while potassium-shifting and elimination therapies are arranged.

Renal potassium excretion

Distal nephron sodium delivery and aldosterone promote potassium secretion; AKI and RAAS-inhibiting drugs impair excretion.

Hyponatraemia and brain adaptation

Chronic hypotonicity leads to brain osmolyte adaptation; rapid correction risks osmotic demyelination.
