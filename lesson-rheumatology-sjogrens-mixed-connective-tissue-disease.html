Sjögren's Syndrome & Mixed Connective Tissue Disease

PLAB priority: Identify sicca symptoms and systemic complications, distinguish Sjögren from MCTD, and recognise ocular and cardiopulmonary red flags.

01. Overview

Sjögren disease is an autoimmune disorder causing lymphocytic inflammation of exocrine glands, often producing dry eyes and dry mouth. It can occur alone or alongside rheumatoid arthritis or lupus. Mixed connective tissue disease (MCTD) combines features of systemic sclerosis, lupus and inflammatory myositis, usually with anti-U1-RNP antibodies.

PLAB application: Ask about gland symptoms and systemic disease rather than treating dryness as trivial.

02. Primary and associated Sjögren

Sjögren disease may occur without another defined connective tissue disorder or in association with conditions such as RA and SLE. It affects women more commonly, but any sex can be affected.

PLAB application: Avoid assuming every dry-mouth patient has autoimmune disease.

03. Sicca symptoms

Dry gritty burning eyes, frequent use of artificial tears, difficulty swallowing dry food, needing water at night, altered taste and recurrent dental caries are typical. Parotid enlargement can occur.

PLAB application: Ask about dental disease, oral candidiasis, eye discomfort and impact on daily life.

04. Extraglandular manifestations

Fatigue, arthralgia, inflammatory arthritis, Raynaud phenomenon, purpura, peripheral neuropathy, renal tubular acidosis and interstitial lung disease may occur. Persistent salivary gland swelling and lymphadenopathy warrant evaluation for lymphoma.

PLAB application: Recognise that Sjögren disease can be multisystem.

05. Clinical examination

Inspect eyes for redness and reduced tear film, oral mucosa for dryness and candidiasis, teeth for caries, and salivary glands for swelling. Examine joints, skin, peripheral nerves, lymph nodes and lungs if systemic symptoms are present.

PLAB application: Assess for corneal injury when eye pain, photophobia or visual change occurs.

06. Differential diagnosis of dryness

Common alternatives include dehydration, anticholinergic drugs, antidepressants, antihistamines, diabetes, mouth breathing, menopause-related symptoms and previous head/neck radiation. Dryness is not diagnostic by itself.

PLAB application: Review medications and common causes before labelling autoimmune disease.

07. Blood tests and antibodies

Initial workup may include FBC, ESR/CRP, renal profile, liver tests, urinalysis, immunoglobulins, ANA and ENA including anti-Ro/SSA and anti-La/SSB. Anti-Ro supports diagnosis but negative antibodies do not exclude Sjögren disease.

PLAB application: Interpret antibodies with symptoms and objective tests; no single blood test confirms every case.

08. Objective gland tests

Schirmer testing assesses tear production; ocular staining evaluates surface damage; unstimulated salivary flow may be measured. Minor labial salivary gland biopsy is useful in selected uncertain cases after specialist review.

PLAB application: Refer for objective eye and salivary testing rather than diagnosing on dryness alone.

09. Sjögren classification and diagnosis

The 2016 ACR/EULAR classification criteria use weighted anti-SSA/Ro, focal lymphocytic sialadenitis on lip biopsy, ocular staining, Schirmer and salivary flow measures. Classification criteria support research and specialist assessment; they are not a substitute for clinical judgement.

PLAB application: Distinguish clinical diagnosis from classification criteria.

10. Management of dry eyes

Regular preservative-free lubricating drops or gels are common first steps; environmental adjustments and ophthalmology input may help. Severe inflammatory eye disease may need specialist prescription treatment.

PLAB application: Painful red eye, photophobia or visual loss requires urgent eye assessment.

11. Management of dry mouth

Frequent water sips, sugar-free gum where suitable, saliva substitutes, fluoride toothpaste and regular dental reviews reduce symptoms and dental complications. Pilocarpine may be considered by specialists in selected patients with residual gland function.

PLAB application: Discuss oral hygiene and prevention of caries and candidiasis.

12. Systemic Sjögren treatment

Systemic therapy depends on organ involvement and specialist assessment. Hydroxychloroquine is sometimes considered for inflammatory musculoskeletal features, while significant organ disease may need immunosuppression. Do not promise systemic medicines will restore tear or saliva production.

PLAB application: Escalate neuropathy, kidney, lung or vasculitic features.

13. MCTD presentation

MCTD may present with Raynaud phenomenon, puffy fingers, inflammatory arthritis, myositis, oesophageal dysmotility and sclerodactyly. Overlap features can evolve over time and the diagnosis may be revised.

PLAB application: Look for a combination of clinical features, not antibody positivity alone.

14. MCTD investigations and complications

High-titre anti-U1-RNP is characteristic but not sufficient by itself. Investigate according to symptoms: CK for myositis, urine and renal function, lung function tests, echocardiography and high-resolution CT when indicated. Pulmonary arterial hypertension and interstitial lung disease are major concerns.

PLAB application: Breathlessness, syncope or falling exercise tolerance warrants prompt cardiopulmonary assessment.

15. Pregnancy and reproductive counselling

Anti-Ro/SSA or anti-La/SSB antibodies can be relevant in pregnancy because of fetal/neonatal risks, including congenital heart block. Pre-pregnancy rheumatology and obstetric review is appropriate; medication safety and maternal organ disease need individual assessment.

PLAB application: Do not provide blanket reassurance; coordinate specialist pregnancy care.

16. PLAB 2 counselling and red flags

Explain that autoimmune inflammation can affect moisture glands and sometimes other organs. Ask about medicines, fatigue, dryness, joint pain and breathlessness; discuss symptom relief and dental care. Urgent flags include visual loss, corneal symptoms, persistent unilateral gland swelling, systemic B symptoms, significant weakness, syncope or respiratory compromise.

PLAB application: Safety-net clearly and arrange appropriate rheumatology, ophthalmology and dental follow-up.

Deep Dive

Autoimmune gland injury

Lymphocytic infiltration and immune dysregulation damage salivary and lacrimal gland function; symptom severity and systemic activity do not always match.

Anti-Ro and classification

Anti-SSA/Ro and salivary gland histology carry substantial weight in ACR/EULAR classification, but clinical assessment remains essential.

Why the eyes matter

Tear deficiency destabilises the ocular surface, risking keratitis and corneal injury; severe pain or vision loss should never be dismissed as routine dryness.

B-cell activation and lymphoma

Sjögren is associated with B-cell hyperactivity and an increased risk of non-Hodgkin lymphoma; persistent gland swelling and lymphadenopathy merit assessment.

Overlap and U1-RNP

MCTD describes a clinical overlap phenotype with anti-U1-RNP; antibodies alone cannot establish the diagnosis, and manifestations can evolve.

Pulmonary vascular disease

Pulmonary arterial hypertension may cause progressive breathlessness, fatigue, chest discomfort or syncope. Timely investigation and specialist care are critical.
