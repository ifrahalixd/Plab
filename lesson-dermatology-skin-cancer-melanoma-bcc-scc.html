Skin Cancer: Melanoma, Basal Cell & Squamous Cell Carcinoma

PLAB priority: Recognise suspicious changing skin lesions, distinguish melanoma from BCC and SCC, and use urgent UK suspected-cancer referral pathways appropriately.

01. Why skin cancer matters

Skin cancers include melanoma, basal cell carcinoma (BCC) and cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cSCC). Melanoma can metastasise early; BCC is usually locally invasive; cSCC can metastasise, especially in high-risk cases.

PLAB application: A changing lesion needs appropriate assessment, not reassurance based on painlessness.

02. Risk factors

Ultraviolet exposure, sunburn, fair skin, increasing age, previous skin cancer, immunosuppression and family history increase risk. Melanoma can occur in any skin tone and at acral sites.

PLAB application: Ask about sun exposure, previous lesions, immune suppression and family history.

03. Melanoma ABCDE

Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Colour variation, Diameter (often >6 mm, but smaller lesions occur), Evolution. An ugly-duckling lesion or a new changing mole is concerning.

PLAB application: Evolution is particularly important; ABCDE is a prompt, not a diagnostic rule.

04. Other melanoma presentations

Nodular melanoma may be rapidly growing, elevated and firm, sometimes with uniform pigmentation. Amelanotic melanoma may be pink or red. Acral lentiginous melanoma affects palms, soles and nail units.

PLAB application: Do not dismiss a rapidly growing pink nodule or changing nail pigmentation.

05. Nail melanoma

Longitudinal melanonychia with widening, irregular pigment, nail dystrophy or pigment extending onto surrounding skin (Hutchinson sign) raises concern. Benign causes also exist.

PLAB application: Refer suspicious changing nail lesions for specialist assessment.

06. Basal cell carcinoma

BCC commonly appears as a pearly translucent papule with telangiectasia, rolled border and possible central ulceration; superficial BCC may be a scaly pink patch. It rarely metastasises but can damage local tissue.

PLAB application: Look for a persistent non-healing or bleeding lesion on sun-exposed skin.

07. Squamous cell carcinoma

cSCC often presents as a firm enlarging keratotic nodule, scaly plaque or ulcer. It can arise in actinic keratosis or chronic wounds. High-risk features include size, depth, poor differentiation and immunosuppression.

PLAB application: A growing keratinising lesion needs urgent assessment; check regional nodes.

08. Actinic keratosis and Bowen disease

Actinic keratoses are rough sun-damaged lesions with malignant potential; Bowen disease is squamous cell carcinoma in situ, typically a persistent well-demarcated scaly plaque.

PLAB application: Differentiate precancerous lesions from invasive SCC.

09. Focused skin history

Ask onset, evolution, bleeding, itching, ulceration, colour or shape change, previous treatments, personal/family cancer history, sunbeds, immunosuppression and systemic symptoms.

PLAB application: Ask about change even if the lesion is asymptomatic.

10. Skin examination

Obtain consent and appropriate exposure; inspect lesion size, site, shape, border, pigmentation, surface, ulceration and surrounding skin. Examine other relevant skin areas and regional lymph nodes when indicated.

PLAB application: Describe objective morphology and document measurements or clinical photographs with consent.

11. Dermoscopy and biopsy

Dermoscopy by trained clinicians supports assessment. Histopathology establishes diagnosis. Suspicious melanoma usually requires complete excision biopsy with a narrow clinical margin when feasible; specialist pathways apply to difficult sites.

PLAB application: Do not attempt destructive treatment of a suspicious pigmented lesion.

12. UK referral pathways

NICE NG12 uses a weighted 7-point checklist for pigmented lesions: major features change in size, irregular shape, irregular colour (2 points each); minor inflammation, oozing, altered sensation, diameter ≥7 mm (1 each). Score ≥3 supports suspected-cancer referral; dermoscopic concern also warrants referral.

PLAB application: Use urgent suspected-cancer pathways for suspicious melanoma or SCC; BCC urgency varies with site and clinical concern.

13. Melanoma staging and management

After diagnosis, definitive wide local excision margin depends on Breslow thickness and specialist guidance. Sentinel lymph node biopsy may be discussed for eligible cases. Advanced disease may require immunotherapy or targeted therapy.

PLAB application: Avoid promising prognosis without pathology and staging.

14. BCC and SCC management

Options include surgical excision, Mohs surgery for selected high-risk sites, and other treatments for selected low-risk superficial BCC. SCC usually requires definitive excision and risk-adapted follow-up.

PLAB application: Facial, recurrent, aggressive or immunosuppression-associated lesions need specialist planning.

15. Prevention and safety-netting

Recommend shade, protective clothing, broad-spectrum sunscreen as an adjunct, avoidance of sunbeds and checking for changing lesions. Explain that darker skin does not eliminate skin cancer risk.

PLAB application: Advise reassessment for new, changing, bleeding or non-healing lesions.

16. PLAB 2 communication

Explain uncertainty without alarm, obtain consent for examination, discuss referral and possible biopsy, acknowledge cosmetic and cancer concerns, and check understanding. Use inclusive descriptions for different skin tones.

PLAB application: State the urgency clearly and arrange a documented follow-up plan.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

UV mutagenesis and cancer

Ultraviolet radiation can damage DNA and impair normal control of keratinocyte or melanocyte proliferation. Immune surveillance and inherited susceptibility influence risk.

Melanoma biology

Melanoma arises from melanocytes; growth pattern and tumour depth affect spread. Early recognition is important because small lesions can still be dangerous.

Why BCC looks pearly

Basaloid tumour growth and surface vessels create a translucent pearly appearance; ulceration and a rolled border may develop.

Why cSCC may metastasise

Invasive atypical keratinocytes can spread through lymphatics. Tumour size, depth, differentiation, location and host immune status modify risk.

Histology and margins

Excision biopsy is intended to establish diagnosis and Breslow thickness. Definitive melanoma surgery often requires a subsequent wider excision, planned by the specialist team.

Inclusive recognition

Melanoma in darker skin may occur on palms, soles or nails, and erythema may be less visually obvious. Do not assume low risk based solely on skin colour.
