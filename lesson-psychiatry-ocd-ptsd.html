Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD) & Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)

PLAB priority: Recognise intrusive obsessions and rituals, trauma-related symptoms, risk and NICE-recommended therapies.

01. Clinical overview

OCD involves intrusive obsessions and repetitive compulsions; PTSD develops after exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury or sexual violence. Both can cause marked distress and avoidance.

PLAB application: Identify the specific syndrome before choosing treatment.

02. OCD: obsessions

Obsessions are recurrent unwanted thoughts, images or urges that provoke anxiety or disgust. Examples include contamination, harm, symmetry, religious or sexual intrusive thoughts. Patients often recognise these as their own unwanted mental events.

PLAB application: Ask sensitively about taboo thoughts without assuming intent.

03. OCD: compulsions

Compulsions are repetitive behaviours or mental acts aimed at reducing distress or preventing feared outcomes: washing, checking, ordering, counting, praying or seeking reassurance. Relief is temporary and reinforces the cycle.

PLAB application: Mental rituals may be invisible to observers.

04. Distinguishing OCD from psychosis

OCD usually features intrusive thoughts with at least some insight, whereas delusions are held with greater conviction; insight in OCD can be poor, so assess carefully. Intrusive harm thoughts do not by themselves mean someone intends harm.

PLAB application: Explore insight, intent, risk and context without judgement.

05. OCD assessment and severity

Ask about time spent on rituals, distress, avoidance, functioning, insight, family accommodation and comorbid depression. Ask about suicide and self-harm risk, especially with severe symptoms. Consider the Yale–Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale in specialist settings.

PLAB application: Quantify impact on study, work, relationships and daily living.

06. OCD treatment: CBT with ERP

Cognitive behavioural therapy incorporating exposure and response prevention (ERP) is a core evidence-based treatment. Patients gradually face feared situations while resisting rituals, supported by a collaborative hierarchy.

PLAB application: Explain that ERP is planned and supported, not forced exposure.

07. OCD pharmacological treatment

SSRIs are used when indicated, often alongside CBT depending on severity and preference. Improvement may take longer than for depression; review adherence, adverse effects and response before altering treatment. Specialist input is appropriate for complex or refractory cases.

PLAB application: Do not stop an SSRI prematurely because OCD symptoms persist early in treatment.

08. OCD in children and young people

Symptoms may be hidden due to shame. Involve family appropriately, assess school and social impact, and offer developmentally adapted CBT with ERP. Medication decisions should follow age-specific specialist guidance.

PLAB application: Explore parental participation in reassurance rituals without blaming families.

09. PTSD: qualifying trauma and presentation

PTSD may follow traumatic exposure. Core clusters include re-experiencing (flashbacks or nightmares), avoidance, persistent sense of threat or hyperarousal, and negative changes in mood or cognition. Symptoms cause functional impairment.

PLAB application: Ask about trauma sensitively; do not demand a detailed recounting.

10. Acute stress reactions versus PTSD

Distress shortly after trauma may be a normal acute response; persistent or clinically significant symptoms need assessment. NICE advises active monitoring for subthreshold symptoms within one month, with follow-up. Avoid routine psychological debriefing.

PLAB application: A single mandatory debriefing session is not recommended.

11. PTSD assessment and safety

Assess nightmares, dissociation, sleep, substance use, depression, suicidality, ongoing abuse and safeguarding. Consider domestic violence and current safety. Physical illness and traumatic brain injury may coexist.

PLAB application: Prioritise immediate safety and stabilisation when needed.

12. PTSD treatment: trauma-focused therapy

NICE recommends individual trauma-focused CBT or EMDR for many adults with PTSD, chosen according to presentation and timing. Treatment should be delivered by trained clinicians with appropriate supervision.

PLAB application: Generic supportive counselling alone is not a substitute for evidence-based trauma therapy.

13. PTSD medicines

Medication is not routinely preferred over trauma-focused therapy. For adults who prefer medication, NICE identifies venlafaxine or an SSRI such as sertraline as options, with monitoring. Do not offer benzodiazepines to prevent PTSD after trauma.

PLAB application: Discuss shared decision-making and adverse effects.

14. Complex PTSD and comorbidity

Prolonged or repeated interpersonal trauma may lead to PTSD symptoms with difficulties in emotion regulation, self-concept and relationships. Treat coexisting depression, substance misuse or anxiety while planning trauma-focused care.

PLAB application: Do not withhold PTSD treatment solely because of comorbidity; individualise sequencing.

15. Differentials and red flags

Differentiate OCD from generalised anxiety, obsessive-compulsive personality traits, psychosis and autism-related repetitive behaviours. Differentiate PTSD from adjustment disorder, depression, panic disorder and acute stress reactions. Red flags include active suicidality, psychosis, severe self-neglect and ongoing danger.

PLAB application: Escalate urgently for immediate safety concerns.

16. PLAB 2 consultation and follow-up

Introduce yourself, obtain consent, use open questions, validate distress, assess risk and impact, explain treatment choices and arrange follow-up. For trauma, offer choice and control, ask permission before sensitive questions and avoid re-traumatisation.

PLAB application: End with a collaborative safety plan and clear referral/follow-up route.

Clinical reasoning · 6 topics

Deep Dive

The OCD negative reinforcement loop

An intrusive thought produces distress; ritualising briefly reduces that distress. This immediate relief reinforces future rituals, even though the feared event was never shown to be likely. ERP breaks the cycle by enabling new learning without ritualising.

Intrusive thoughts versus intention

Unwanted taboo thoughts are common in OCD and can be profoundly distressing. Assessment should distinguish intrusive ego-dystonic thoughts from genuine intent, psychosis or safeguarding concerns without making assumptions.

Why SSRIs may take time in OCD

OCD often requires a longer therapeutic trial than patients expect. Adherence, tolerability, adequate dosing and specialist review should be considered before deciding treatment has failed.

PTSD fear memory and avoidance

Traumatic reminders may trigger autonomic threat responses and vivid re-experiencing. Avoidance gives short-term relief but can prevent processing and recovery. Trauma-focused therapies work through structured, supported engagement.

Why mandatory debriefing is discouraged

A forced early recounting of trauma has not been shown to prevent PTSD and may be unhelpful. Initial support should emphasise safety, practical needs, informed choice and monitoring.

Trauma-informed PLAB 2 practice

Explain confidentiality and its limits, seek permission before sensitive topics, offer pauses, avoid graphic questioning, validate distress and assess current safety. Shared decisions and follow-up are essential.
