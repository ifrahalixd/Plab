Haemolytic Anaemia & Sickle Cell Disease

PLAB safety: Fever, hypoxia, chest pain or neurological deficits in sickle cell disease require urgent assessment; suspected TTP is a haematological emergency.

01. Recognising haemolysis

Premature red-cell destruction causes anaemia, jaundice, dark urine and sometimes splenomegaly. Assess breathlessness, chest pain, syncope and haemodynamic stability.

PLAB focus: Severe symptomatic anaemia or rapidly progressive haemolysis requires urgent assessment.

02. Core haemolysis blood tests

Typical pattern: raised reticulocytes, raised unconjugated bilirubin, raised LDH and reduced haptoglobin. Request FBC, blood film, renal function and group-and-save as indicated.

PLAB focus: Reticulocytes can be inappropriately low with marrow suppression or aplastic crisis.

03. Intravascular versus extravascular

Intravascular destruction may cause haemoglobinuria and marked haptoglobin depletion; extravascular destruction predominantly occurs in spleen and liver, often causing jaundice and splenomegaly.

PLAB focus: Both processes can coexist; laboratory patterns are supportive, not definitive.

04. Immune haemolysis and DAT

Direct antiglobulin test (DAT) supports autoimmune haemolytic anaemia (AIHA), though DAT-negative cases exist. Warm AIHA is usually IgG mediated; cold agglutinin disease commonly involves complement.

PLAB focus: Assess triggers such as autoimmune disease, lymphoproliferative disease and drugs.

05. Warm autoimmune haemolytic anaemia

Features include anaemia, jaundice and spherocytes; DAT often positive for IgG. Specialist-directed corticosteroids are commonly first-line for warm AIHA, with other therapies if refractory.

PLAB focus: Unstable patients need urgent haematology and transfusion service input.

06. Cold agglutinin disease

Cold-sensitive antibodies can cause acrocyanosis and haemolysis, often with complement-positive DAT. Avoid cold exposure and seek specialist evaluation; treatment differs from warm AIHA.

PLAB focus: Do not assume corticosteroids work equally well for cold agglutinin disease.

07. Hereditary spherocytosis

Inherited membrane defects cause extravascular haemolysis, jaundice, splenomegaly and pigment gallstones. Blood film shows spherocytes; confirm with specialist tests such as eosin-5-maleimide binding.

PLAB focus: Spherocytes also occur in warm AIHA: DAT helps differentiate.

08. G6PD deficiency

X-linked enzyme deficiency predisposes to oxidant-triggered haemolysis from infections, certain drugs or fava beans. Film may show bite cells; enzyme testing during acute haemolysis can be falsely normal.

PLAB focus: Remove trigger, manage complications and repeat testing later if suspicion remains.

09. Microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia

Schistocytes suggest mechanical fragmentation, including TTP, HUS or DIC. TTP can cause thrombocytopenia, neurological symptoms and renal injury.

PLAB focus: Suspected TTP is a haematological emergency: urgent specialist treatment; do not await full classical pentad.

10. Sickle cell genetics and pathophysiology

Sickle cell disease results from haemoglobin S polymerisation during deoxygenation, causing haemolysis and vaso-occlusion. HbSS and some compound heterozygous states cause significant disease.

PLAB focus: Sickle cell trait is not equivalent to sickle cell disease.

11. Sickle cell clinical presentation

Painful vaso-occlusive episodes, chronic anaemia, jaundice, infection risk and organ injury are common. Functional asplenia increases susceptibility to encapsulated bacteria.

PLAB focus: Fever in sickle cell disease needs urgent assessment for serious infection.

12. Vaso-occlusive pain episode

Assess pain, vitals, oxygen saturation, hydration, fever and red flags. Give prompt analgesia tailored to severity, maintain appropriate hydration and treat precipitants; oxygen only if hypoxic.

PLAB focus: Avoid excessive IV fluids; reassess pain and monitor respiratory status.

13. Acute chest syndrome

New pulmonary infiltrate with fever, chest pain, cough or respiratory symptoms in sickle cell disease suggests acute chest syndrome. Arrange urgent hospital care, antibiotics, oxygen if hypoxic and specialist assessment for transfusion.

PLAB focus: Can deteriorate rapidly; obtain chest imaging and monitor closely.

14. Stroke, sequestration and aplastic crisis

New focal neurology is a stroke emergency. Acute splenic sequestration causes sudden splenic enlargement and falling haemoglobin; parvovirus B19 may cause aplastic crisis with low reticulocytes.

PLAB focus: Urgent haematology assessment and transfusion planning may be lifesaving.

15. Long-term sickle cell care

Specialist care includes vaccinations, infection prevention, hydroxycarbamide where indicated, screening for organ complications, individualised transfusion strategies and psychosocial support.

PLAB focus: Provide an individualised emergency plan and discuss pregnancy and genetic counselling.

16. PLAB 2 consultation and safety-netting

Ask about pain, fever, breathing difficulty, neurological symptoms, triggers, medication, transfusion history and patient-specific care plan. Explain investigations and shared treatment decisions empathetically.

PLAB focus: Escalate fever, chest symptoms, hypoxia, severe anaemia or focal neurological deficits immediately.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Why haptoglobin falls

Free plasma haemoglobin binds haptoglobin; intravascular haemolysis consumes circulating haptoglobin.

DAT nuance

A positive DAT must be interpreted alongside clinical haemolysis; a minority of AIHA cases are DAT negative.

Membrane versus enzyme disorders

Hereditary spherocytosis changes membrane stability; G6PD deficiency impairs protection against oxidative stress.

HbS polymerisation

Low oxygen tension promotes HbS polymerisation, endothelial adhesion and microvascular obstruction.

Acute chest syndrome

Infection, fat embolism and pulmonary vaso-occlusion can contribute; rapid deterioration makes early recognition critical.

Transfusion complexity

Alloimmunisation and iron overload complicate repeated transfusions; specialist matching and indication assessment are important.
