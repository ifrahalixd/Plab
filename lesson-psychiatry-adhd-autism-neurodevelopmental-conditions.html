ADHD, Autism Spectrum Disorder & Neurodevelopmental Conditions

PLAB priority: Specialist diagnosis, developmental history, functional impact, medication safety and reasonable adjustments.

01. Neurodevelopmental overview

ADHD and autism are neurodevelopmental conditions with onset in childhood, often continuing into adult life. They can coexist and are not caused by poor parenting.

PLAB application: Use respectful, person-centred language and ask about strengths, support needs and functional impact.

02. ADHD core features

ADHD involves persistent inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity that is developmentally inappropriate, occurs across settings and causes impairment. Presentation may be predominantly inattentive, hyperactive-impulsive or combined.

PLAB application: Explore school, work, relationships, organisation and driving; symptoms alone do not establish a diagnosis.

03. ADHD developmental history

Symptoms must have begun during childhood; obtain school reports or collateral history where possible. Adult ADHD may present as disorganisation, procrastination, restlessness and emotional dysregulation rather than obvious hyperactivity.

PLAB application: Ask about childhood patterns and current impairment in at least two settings.

04. ADHD differential diagnosis

Anxiety, depression, sleep deprivation, trauma, substance use, bipolar disorder, thyroid disease and learning difficulties can resemble or coexist with ADHD.

PLAB application: Assess alternative explanations and comorbidities before attributing all symptoms to ADHD.

05. ADHD diagnosis and referral

NICE NG87 recommends diagnosis by a specialist with training and expertise in ADHD following full clinical and psychosocial assessment, developmental and psychiatric history, observer reports and mental state assessment. Rating scales alone are insufficient.

PLAB application: In primary care, recognise and refer; do not diagnose solely from an online questionnaire.

06. ADHD non-drug support

Offer psychoeducation, environmental modifications and tailored support in education or work. Psychological interventions may help adults with persistent impairment. For children, parent-training support and school-based adjustments are important.

PLAB application: Discuss routines, reminders, reduced distractions and realistic shared goals.

07. ADHD medication

Medication should be initiated by a clinician with ADHD expertise after baseline assessment. NICE lists methylphenidate as first-line pharmacological treatment for children aged 5 and over and for adults, with age-specific alternatives such as lisdexamfetamine, atomoxetine and guanfacine according to guidance.

PLAB application: Know that drug choice, age, response and contraindications matter; do not prescribe without appropriate specialist pathway.

08. Medication safety and monitoring

Before ADHD medication, review cardiovascular history, pulse, blood pressure, height/weight where relevant, psychiatric risks and current medicines. Routine ECG is not required without specific cardiac risk features. Monitor pulse, blood pressure, growth/weight and adverse effects.

PLAB application: Ask about palpitations, syncope, appetite suppression, insomnia and misuse/diversion risk.

09. Autism core characteristics

Autism involves persistent differences in social communication and interaction alongside restricted or repetitive patterns, sensory differences, routines or focused interests. Manifestations vary widely and may be masked, particularly in some adults.

PLAB application: Avoid assuming all autistic people have intellectual disability or lack empathy.

10. Autism assessment

Assessment uses developmental history, observations, functional needs and information from the individual and others where appropriate. In adults, consider NICE CG142; in under-19s NICE CG128 covers recognition, referral and diagnosis.

PLAB application: Ask about early development, friendships, communication, sensory needs and everyday functioning.

11. Autism support and reasonable adjustments

Offer clear language, predictable appointments, reduced sensory overload, accessible written information and extra processing time. Support should be tailored to individual preferences. Autism itself is not an indication for medication.

PLAB application: For PLAB 2, ask how the patient prefers to communicate and adapt the consultation.

12. Autism comorbidities

ADHD, anxiety, depression, epilepsy, sleep difficulties, gastrointestinal problems and intellectual disability may coexist. New behavioural change may signal pain, distress or another health problem.

PLAB application: Do not attribute acute deterioration to autism without evaluating physical and mental health.

13. Learning disability versus specific learning disorder

Intellectual disability involves significant limitations in intellectual and adaptive functioning beginning during development. Dyslexia is a specific learning difficulty and does not imply low intelligence.

PLAB application: Check capacity for the particular decision, not on the basis of diagnosis alone.

14. Tics and Tourette syndrome

Tics are sudden, repetitive movements or vocalisations. Tourette syndrome involves multiple motor tics and at least one vocal tic over time, beginning before adulthood. Tics can wax and wane; assess impact and associated ADHD/OCD.

PLAB application: Avoid drawing attention to harmless tics; refer if severe impairment or diagnostic uncertainty.

15. Safeguarding, transition and inclusion

Assess bullying, exploitation, self-harm, school exclusion and caregiver strain. Transition from child to adult services requires planning. Respect autonomy and obtain consent for collateral information where appropriate.

PLAB application: Use accessible safety planning and consider reasonable adjustments in healthcare.

16. PLAB 2 communication and OSCE approach

Start with an open question, clarify the main concern, explore developmental history, settings, impairment, comorbidities and risk, then summarise and agree a plan. Avoid stigmatising terms and explain that specialist assessment may be needed.

PLAB application: Show empathy, ask about accommodations and provide safety-netting and follow-up.

Mechanisms and clinical reasoning

Deep Dive

Executive function in ADHD

Executive functions include planning, inhibition, working memory and task switching. Impairment can create substantial functional difficulties despite intact intelligence.

Why childhood history matters

Neurodevelopmental symptoms begin during development. Retrospective adult assessment needs careful reconstruction and corroboration where feasible, rather than assuming recent concentration problems equal ADHD.

Stimulants and monitoring

Stimulants modify catecholaminergic signalling and can improve attention and impulse control. They may increase pulse or blood pressure and reduce appetite, so baseline assessment and ongoing monitoring are essential.

Autistic sensory processing

Noise, light, touch and unexpected changes can increase distress. Healthcare adjustments may improve access and reduce misunderstandings without changing the person’s identity.

Diagnostic overlap and masking

ADHD, autism, anxiety and mood disorders can coexist. Some individuals consciously mask differences, which can delay recognition; assess real-life effort and exhaustion as well as observed behaviour.

Capacity and neurodiversity-affirming care

Neither autism nor intellectual disability automatically removes capacity. Offer accessible information, supported decision-making and reasonable adjustments; avoid infantilising language.
