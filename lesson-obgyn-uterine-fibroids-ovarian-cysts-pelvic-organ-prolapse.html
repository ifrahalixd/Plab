Uterine Fibroids, Ovarian Cysts & Pelvic Organ Prolapse

Red flags: sudden severe pelvic pain with vomiting, collapse, heavy bleeding, pregnancy-associated pain, urinary retention or a suspicious adnexal mass need urgent assessment.

Clinical priorities

Fibroids, ovarian cysts and pelvic organ prolapse are common but require distinct assessment. First exclude pregnancy when relevant, assess haemodynamic stability and consider acute ovarian torsion, ruptured cyst, ectopic pregnancy or malignancy.

PLAB application: Sudden severe unilateral pelvic pain with vomiting warrants emergency gynaecology assessment.

Focused history

Ask about heavy or intermenstrual bleeding, dysmenorrhoea, pelvic pressure, urinary frequency or retention, constipation, dyspareunia, fertility goals, pregnancy possibility, menopausal status, bloating, early satiety and family history. Ask how symptoms affect daily life.

PLAB application: Clarify whether symptoms are chronic, cyclical, progressive or suddenly severe.

Examination and consent

Record observations; perform abdominal examination. Offer pelvic examination when appropriate with informed consent, privacy and chaperone. Examine for pelvic mass, uterine enlargement and prolapse during straining; assess bladder symptoms.

PLAB application: Never assume an adnexal mass is benign solely because the patient is young.

Fibroids: definition and types

Uterine leiomyomas are benign smooth-muscle tumours. Submucosal fibroids project toward the uterine cavity and commonly cause bleeding; intramural fibroids occur within the myometrium; subserosal fibroids project outward and may cause pressure symptoms.

PLAB application: Submucosal fibroids can disproportionately affect bleeding and fertility.

Fibroid symptoms and complications

Many fibroids are asymptomatic. Others cause heavy menstrual bleeding, iron-deficiency anaemia, pelvic pressure, urinary symptoms, constipation, pain and subfertility. Rapidly changing symptoms or postmenopausal bleeding need assessment rather than reassurance.

PLAB application: Check a full blood count in heavy menstrual bleeding.

Fibroid investigations

Transvaginal or transabdominal ultrasound assesses size, number and location. Consider hysteroscopy when history suggests submucosal fibroids or endometrial pathology. Investigate abnormal bleeding according to risk factors; do not rely on ultrasound alone to exclude cancer.

PLAB application: Select imaging according to symptoms and examination, not merely fibroid presence.

Fibroid management

Management depends on symptoms, size, location and reproductive goals. Options include observation, tranexamic acid, NSAIDs, LNG-IUS when appropriate, hormonal treatments, specialist medical therapies, myomectomy, uterine artery embolisation or hysterectomy. Discuss benefits, fertility and recurrence.

PLAB application: Myomectomy preserves the uterus but fibroids can recur; hysterectomy is definitive.

Ovarian cyst classification

Functional follicular and corpus luteum cysts are common in premenopausal patients. Other masses include endometriomas, dermoid cysts, benign cystadenomas and malignant tumours. Ultrasound morphology, symptoms, age and menopausal status determine risk assessment.

PLAB application: Simple and complex cysts follow different pathways.

Ovarian cyst presentation

Cysts may be incidental or cause unilateral pelvic discomfort, bloating, pressure or dyspareunia. Acute severe pain with nausea/vomiting suggests torsion; rupture may cause sudden pain and, rarely, haemoperitoneum.

PLAB application: Do a pregnancy test where relevant to exclude ectopic pregnancy.

Ovarian cyst assessment

Pelvic ultrasound, preferably transvaginal where suitable, describes cyst size, septations, solid areas and vascularity. In postmenopausal patients, CA125 and ultrasound support malignancy risk assessment. CA125 is nonspecific and a normal result does not exclude cancer.

PLAB application: Escalate persistent bloating, early satiety or suspicious complex masses.

Ovarian cyst management

Many simple premenopausal cysts resolve without intervention. Follow-up depends on size, appearance, symptoms and local protocol. Persistent, enlarging, complex or symptomatic cysts may require specialist review or surgery; suspected malignancy follows an urgent pathway.

PLAB application: Combined oral contraceptives do not hasten resolution of existing functional cysts.

Ovarian torsion: emergency

Torsion twists the ovary/adnexa, compromising blood flow. Presentation is usually sudden unilateral pain with nausea/vomiting, sometimes intermittent. Normal Doppler flow does not reliably exclude torsion. Urgent gynaecology and operative assessment are needed.

PLAB application: Do not delay referral waiting for definitive ultrasound proof.

Pelvic organ prolapse: types

Pelvic organ prolapse is descent of pelvic structures into the vagina: anterior wall (often bladder/cystocele), posterior wall (often rectocele), uterine or vault prolapse. Risk factors include vaginal childbirth, ageing, obesity and chronic raised abdominal pressure.

PLAB application: Symptoms matter more than examination grade alone.

Prolapse symptoms and assessment

Ask about vaginal bulge or dragging, urinary incontinence, incomplete bladder emptying, bowel symptoms, sexual discomfort and quality of life. Examine with consent, sometimes during Valsalva or standing; document compartment and severity.

PLAB application: Check for retention, ulceration, bleeding and significant bowel symptoms.

Prolapse management

Offer lifestyle advice and supervised pelvic floor muscle training for suitable symptomatic prolapse. Vaginal pessaries are a non-surgical option; topical vaginal oestrogen may help menopausal vaginal symptoms. Consider surgery when symptoms persist and preferences support it.

PLAB application: Discuss pessary follow-up, discharge, bleeding and potential erosions.

PLAB 2 communication and safety net

Explore ideas, concerns, fertility wishes and effect on relationships. Explain that fibroids are usually benign and many cysts resolve, but assessment is needed for concerning symptoms. Shared decision-making should address fertility, bleeding control and recovery.

PLAB application: Seek urgent help for sudden severe pain, fainting, heavy bleeding, fever or urinary retention.

PLAB 2 — Pelvic pressure or bleeding consultation

Introduce yourself, confirm identity and consent; establish pregnancy possibility and red flags. Ask about bleeding, pain, pressure, bladder and bowel symptoms, fertility wishes, menopause and cancer risk factors. Offer appropriate examination with chaperone, explain ultrasound and blood tests, discuss options without pressuring the patient, check understanding and safety-net clearly.

Deep Dive

Fibroid location and treatment choice

Submucosal fibroids commonly cause heavy bleeding and may be suitable for hysteroscopic removal. Intramural or subserosal fibroids may require other approaches depending on size, symptoms and fertility goals.

Endometrial safety

Persistent intermenstrual bleeding, postmenopausal bleeding or risk factors for endometrial pathology warrant directed investigation; the presence of fibroids does not exclude malignancy.

Adnexal mass risk

Ultrasound features such as solid components, papillary projections, ascites and bilateral complex masses increase concern. Menopausal status and symptoms inform referral. CA125 is not a stand-alone exclusion test.

Torsion and Doppler

Ovarian blood supply is dual, and torsion can be intermittent. Preserved Doppler flow cannot reliably rule out torsion in a patient with typical symptoms.

Prolapse conservative care

Supervised pelvic floor muscle training, weight management when appropriate, management of constipation and pessary fitting can improve symptoms; surgical choices must consider future pregnancies and patient preferences.

Fertility and shared decisions

Fibroid interventions and ovarian surgery can affect fertility or ovarian reserve. Discuss options with specialist input and document patient goals, especially before irreversible treatment.
