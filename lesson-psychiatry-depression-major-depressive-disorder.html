Depression & Major Depressive Disorder

PLAB priority: Assess suicide risk directly, screen for bipolar disorder and choose treatment through shared decision-making.

01. Definition and diagnostic framework

Depression is a syndrome of persistent low mood and/or loss of interest or pleasure, with associated cognitive, physical and functional changes. UK NICE NG222 uses a clinical assessment rather than a questionnaire score alone.

PLAB application: Ask about both mood and anhedonia, duration, distress and impairment.

02. Symptoms and duration

Symptoms often include low energy, impaired concentration, guilt or worthlessness, altered sleep or appetite, psychomotor change, hopelessness and suicidal thoughts. A depressive episode usually involves symptoms most days for at least 2 weeks.

PLAB application: Assess whether symptoms are persistent and represent a change from baseline.

03. History of presenting illness

Establish onset, triggers, prior episodes, severity, work/study function, relationships, self-care, sleep, appetite, substance use, medical illness and treatments tried. Ask what matters to the patient.

PLAB application: A focused psychosocial history helps choose acceptable treatment.

04. Suicide and self-harm risk

Ask directly and compassionately about passive death wishes, suicidal thoughts, intent, plans, access to means, past attempts, self-harm, protective factors and immediate safety. Risk scales cannot reliably predict suicide or replace clinical assessment.

PLAB application: Current intent or inability to stay safe requires urgent same-day mental health assessment and safety measures.

05. Mental state examination

Document appearance, behaviour, speech, mood (subjective), affect (observed), thought form and content, perception, cognition, insight and judgement. Look for psychotic symptoms and marked psychomotor slowing.

PLAB application: Describe findings rather than writing “MSE normal”.

06. Severity and functioning

NICE distinguishes less severe from more severe depression based on symptom burden and functional impairment. PHQ-9 may support monitoring but does not replace clinical judgement.

PLAB application: Ask about work, eating, washing, childcare and ability to leave home.

07. Differential diagnoses

Consider bipolar depression, adjustment disorder, grief, anxiety disorders, PTSD, substance-related disorders, hypothyroidism, anaemia, medication effects and neurological illness. Grief can coexist with major depression.

PLAB application: Screen for mania or hypomania before prescribing an antidepressant.

08. Bipolar screening

Ask about previous periods of abnormally elevated or irritable mood, reduced need for sleep, increased energy, impulsive spending or risk-taking. Antidepressant monotherapy may be inappropriate in bipolar disorder.

PLAB application: Refer for specialist assessment when bipolar disorder is suspected.

09. Investigations

Depression is a clinical diagnosis. Perform targeted physical examination and tests when indicated by symptoms or history, such as FBC, TSH, B12 or metabolic assessment; routine broad testing is not required for everyone.

PLAB application: Investigate plausible medical contributors without delaying safety assessment.

10. Less severe depression treatment

NICE NG222 supports shared decision-making and generally recommends not routinely offering antidepressants first-line unless that is the informed preference. Options include guided self-help, CBT, behavioural activation and exercise-based approaches.

PLAB application: Explore preference, availability, previous response and barriers to engagement.

11. More severe depression treatment

Options include individual CBT combined with an antidepressant, individual CBT, behavioural activation and antidepressant monotherapy, selected through shared decision-making. Specialist care may be needed for complex or high-risk cases.

PLAB application: Discuss benefits, adverse effects and expected time course; arrange follow-up.

12. SSRIs and counselling

SSRIs such as sertraline are common first-line choices when medication is indicated. Explain possible nausea, sleep change, sexual dysfunction, initial agitation, interactions and discontinuation symptoms. Effects develop over weeks.

PLAB application: Review early, particularly in younger people or when suicide risk is present.

13. Monitoring and follow-up

NICE advises reviewing treatment usually within 2 weeks, or within 1 week for ages 18–25 or when suicide risk is a particular concern. Check adherence, side effects, suicidality and function.

PLAB application: Provide a clear safety-net and a specific follow-up plan.

14. Continuation and stopping

Continue antidepressants for at least 6 months after remission in many cases, reviewing relapse risk. Taper gradually with individualised steps; abrupt cessation can cause withdrawal symptoms.

PLAB application: Distinguish discontinuation symptoms from recurrence and plan taper collaboratively.

15. Psychotic depression and emergencies

Delusions or hallucinations accompanying severe depression, catatonia, refusal of food/fluids, or immediate suicide risk warrant urgent specialist assessment. ECT can be considered in selected severe or life-threatening depression under specialist care.

PLAB application: Escalate urgently when psychosis or inability to maintain basic safety is present.

16. PLAB 2 communication and safeguarding

Use open questions, validation without assumptions, confidentiality explanation, direct suicide questions, shared decisions and a safety plan. Consider perinatal context, safeguarding, domestic abuse and capacity when relevant.

PLAB application: End the station by summarising, checking understanding and arranging support.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Monoamines and neural circuits

Monoamine signalling contributes to depression, but no single neurotransmitter deficiency explains the disorder. Stress, learning, neuroplasticity and social factors interact.

Cognitive and behavioural loops

Low mood can reduce activity and reinforcement; avoidance can sustain depression. Behavioural activation targets this cycle through meaningful planned activity.

Why screen for bipolar disorder

A depressive episode may be part of bipolar illness. Prior hypomania or mania changes management and warrants specialist assessment.

Understanding suicide risk

Suicidal thinking is dynamic and contextual. Collaborative safety planning, immediate risk assessment and continuity of care matter more than a numeric risk category.

Antidepressant adaptation

SSRIs alter serotonin reuptake rapidly, while clinical benefits generally emerge over weeks. Side effects may appear earlier; monitor activation and suicidal thinking.

Relapse and recovery

Residual symptoms, prior episodes and psychosocial stressors influence recurrence. Continuation treatment, psychotherapy, sleep and follow-up help maintain recovery.
