Hodgkin & Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma

UKMLA / PLAB focus: persistent lymphadenopathy, B symptoms, biopsy, staging, lymphoma emergencies and counselling.

01. What is lymphoma?

Lymphoma is a malignant proliferation of lymphocytes, usually presenting in lymph nodes or extranodal tissue. The two broad groups are Hodgkin lymphoma (HL) and non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL). Subtypes vary greatly in tempo and treatment.

PLAB application: Do not assume every persistent lymph node is reactive.

02. Hodgkin lymphoma

Classical HL has characteristic Hodgkin/Reed–Sternberg cells in an inflammatory background, typically CD30-positive and often CD15-positive. Nodular lymphocyte-predominant B-cell lymphoma is classified separately in modern systems.

PLAB application: A young adult with painless cervical lymphadenopathy and B symptoms suggests HL.

03. Non-Hodgkin lymphoma

NHL includes diverse B-, T- and NK-cell malignancies. Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is aggressive but often treatable with curative intent; follicular lymphoma is commonly indolent.

PLAB application: A rapidly enlarging mass needs urgent assessment.

04. Symptoms and B symptoms

Painless persistent lymphadenopathy is typical. B symptoms are unexplained fever, drenching night sweats and >10% unintentional weight loss over six months. Itching, fatigue and alcohol-related node pain can occur in HL, but are not diagnostic.

PLAB application: Ask specifically about weight change, fever and night sweats.

05. Examination

Check cervical, supraclavicular, axillary and inguinal nodes; describe size, consistency, tenderness, fixation and distribution. Examine liver and spleen, assess performance status and look for compressive symptoms.

PLAB application: Supraclavicular lymphadenopathy warrants careful assessment.

06. Red flags and referral

Persistent unexplained lymphadenopathy or splenomegaly, especially with systemic symptoms, needs assessment under NICE NG12. Airway compromise, superior vena cava obstruction, spinal cord compression and severe cytopenias require emergency escalation.

PLAB application: Never delay urgent referral for a trial of antibiotics without evidence of bacterial infection.

07. Initial investigations

FBC and film, renal/liver profile, LDH, inflammatory markers and infection testing as clinically indicated. HIV, hepatitis B/C status matter before some treatments. Blood tests alone cannot exclude lymphoma.

PLAB application: Normal FBC does not rule out lymphoma.

08. Definitive diagnosis

Tissue biopsy is essential. Excision biopsy often preserves architecture best; image-guided core biopsy may be appropriate for deep lesions. Fine-needle aspiration alone is generally insufficient for primary lymphoma classification.

PLAB application: Histology, immunophenotyping and molecular studies determine subtype.

09. Staging

PET-CT is widely used for FDG-avid lymphomas such as HL and DLBCL; other imaging depends on subtype. Lugano/Ann Arbor stages I–IV describe distribution; stage is not the same as prognosis.

PLAB application: Ask about disease above and below the diaphragm and extranodal involvement.

10. Hodgkin lymphoma treatment

Specialist therapy commonly uses combination chemotherapy, with radiotherapy in selected cases. Regimens depend on stage, risk and patient factors; fertility preservation and late toxicity should be discussed before treatment.

PLAB application: Do not memorise one regimen as suitable for all patients.

11. Aggressive NHL treatment

DLBCL usually needs prompt specialist treatment, often rituximab-containing combination chemoimmunotherapy for CD20-positive disease. Assess tumour lysis risk, infection risk and cardiac function where relevant.

PLAB application: Aggressive lymphoma can be curable, but delays can be dangerous.

12. Indolent NHL treatment

Asymptomatic low-burden follicular lymphoma may be observed under specialist care; symptomatic or bulky disease may need immunotherapy or systemic treatment. Transformation can cause rapid growth and rising LDH.

PLAB application: Watch-and-wait is active monitoring, not abandonment.

13. Emergencies

Tumour lysis syndrome causes hyperkalaemia, hyperphosphataemia, hypocalcaemia, hyperuricaemia and AKI. Neutropenic sepsis, spinal cord compression and airway/SVC obstruction are time-critical.

PLAB application: Suspected neutropenic sepsis requires immediate emergency management.

14. Treatment complications

Chemotherapy may cause cytopenias, infection, infertility, neuropathy and organ toxicity. Anti-CD20 treatment can reactivate hepatitis B; check specialist screening and prophylaxis protocols. Long-term HL survivors need surveillance for late effects.

PLAB application: Ask about fever after chemotherapy and assess urgently.

15. Differential diagnoses

Reactive viral or bacterial nodes, tuberculosis, HIV, metastatic solid cancer, leukaemia and inflammatory disorders can resemble lymphoma. Node features and systemic signs guide evaluation, but biopsy confirms malignancy.

PLAB application: Avoid assuming TB or lymphoma solely from neck swelling.

16. PLAB 2 communication

Take a structured history, examine sensitively, explain that enlarged nodes have several possible causes, arrange appropriate urgent tests and referral, discuss uncertainty honestly and safety-net for breathing difficulty, fever, neurological symptoms or rapid growth.

PLAB application: Avoid telling a patient they have cancer before tissue diagnosis.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Reed–Sternberg cell biology

Classical Hodgkin lymphoma derives from abnormal germinal-centre B cells. A sparse population of malignant cells interacts with a large inflammatory microenvironment.

Indolent versus aggressive

Growth rate shapes urgency and intent. Indolent lymphomas may be observed for years; aggressive subtypes may be rapidly fatal untreated but responsive to intensive therapy.

Immunophenotyping

Surface markers such as CD20 and CD30 help classify lymphoma and identify targeted therapies; results must be interpreted with morphology and genetics.

Staging and prognosis

Anatomic stage, bulk, symptoms, performance status, LDH and subtype contribute to risk. Stage alone does not determine management.

Tumour lysis physiology

Rapid tumour-cell destruction releases potassium and phosphate and increases urate, causing arrhythmia, hypocalcaemia and kidney injury. Prevention and monitoring depend on risk.

PLAB 2 communication

Explain why biopsy is needed, avoid premature diagnosis, ask about fertility and family support when relevant, and give explicit emergency safety-netting.
