Leukaemia (AML, ALL, CML & CLL)

UKMLA / PLAB focus: recognise acute leukaemia emergencies, distinguish AML, ALL, CML and CLL, and refer safely.

01. Overview

Leukaemia is a malignancy of blood-forming cells. Acute leukaemias progress rapidly and feature immature blasts; chronic leukaemias usually have more mature abnormal cells. AML and ALL are acute; CML and CLL are chronic.

PLAB application: Unexplained cytopenias, blasts or systemic illness require prompt haematology assessment.

02. Clinical warning signs

Fatigue, pallor, infections, fever, bruising, epistaxis, gum bleeding, weight loss, night sweats, lymphadenopathy and splenomegaly may occur. Bone pain is more characteristic in ALL.

PLAB application: Neutropenic sepsis and major bleeding are emergencies.

03. Initial investigations

Request FBC with differential and blood film, renal function, liver tests, LDH, urate and clotting screen. Assess for infection and tumour lysis risk. Diagnosis and classification use marrow studies, flow cytometry, cytogenetics and molecular testing.

PLAB application: A normal or modest total white count does not exclude acute leukaemia.

04. Acute myeloid leukaemia

AML is a clonal myeloid blast disorder, often presenting with anaemia, thrombocytopenia and neutropenia. Auer rods support myeloid lineage. Risk classification depends on genetics and other factors.

PLAB application: Suspected AML needs urgent specialist referral, not routine outpatient follow-up.

05. Acute promyelocytic leukaemia

APL is a distinct AML subtype commonly involving PML::RARA. It can cause catastrophic disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC). Suspected APL requires immediate specialist treatment, often starting all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA) before molecular confirmation.

PLAB application: Bleeding plus abnormal promyelocytes is a time-critical haematology emergency.

06. Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia

ALL is a lymphoid precursor malignancy, common in children but also seen in adults. It may cause bone pain, lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly and marrow failure. Immunophenotyping distinguishes B-ALL and T-ALL.

PLAB application: ALL treatment includes specialist multi-agent regimens and CNS-directed therapy.

07. Chronic myeloid leukaemia

CML is associated with the BCR::ABL1 fusion (Philadelphia chromosome), producing a constitutively active tyrosine kinase. Often presents with neutrophilic leucocytosis, left shift, basophilia and splenomegaly.

PLAB application: Confirm with BCR::ABL1 testing; tyrosine kinase inhibitors are the mainstay.

08. Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia

CLL is a mature B-cell neoplasm, typically in older adults, often discovered through persistent lymphocytosis. Blood film may show smudge cells. Flow cytometry confirms a characteristic clonal B-cell phenotype.

PLAB application: Asymptomatic early CLL is commonly observed rather than immediately treated.

09. Distinguishing the four

AML: myeloid blasts and sometimes Auer rods. ALL: lymphoblasts and bone pain. CML: BCR::ABL1 and myeloid proliferation. CLL: persistent clonal mature B lymphocytosis. These are patterns, not definitive diagnostic tests.

PLAB application: Classification requires laboratory confirmation, not a single blood-film clue.

10. Neutropenic sepsis

Fever or deterioration during chemotherapy may signal neutropenic sepsis, even with subtle symptoms. Assess ABCDE, obtain cultures without delaying therapy, and give immediate empirical IV antibiotics per local policy.

PLAB application: NICE CG151: suspected neutropenic sepsis is an acute medical emergency.

11. Tumour lysis syndrome

Rapid cell breakdown releases potassium, phosphate and urate, causing hyperkalaemia, hypocalcaemia and AKI. It can occur spontaneously or after therapy. Monitor biochemistry, hydrate appropriately and use specialist urate-lowering strategies.

PLAB application: Recognise and escalate dangerous electrolyte disturbances urgently.

12. Bleeding and DIC

Thrombocytopenia causes petechiae and mucosal bleeding. APL can trigger severe coagulopathy with low fibrinogen, prolonged clotting times and elevated D-dimer. Resuscitate and coordinate blood-product support with haematology.

PLAB application: Do not delay APL escalation while waiting for all confirmatory tests.

13. Treatment principles

AML/ALL often need intensive or risk-adapted specialist therapy. CML typically receives a BCR::ABL1 inhibitor. CLL treatment depends on symptoms, progression, cytopenias and molecular risk; targeted agents may be used. Stem-cell transplantation is considered selectively.

PLAB application: Avoid assuming every leukaemia requires immediate chemotherapy.

14. Supportive care

Supportive measures include infection prevention and treatment, transfusions when indicated, antiemetics, tumour lysis prevention, fertility discussion and psychosocial support. Blood products may need special specifications.

PLAB application: Seek specialist guidance on transfusion and immunisation decisions.

15. Referral and communication

Explain that abnormal blood counts require further tests, avoid premature diagnosis, arrange urgent assessment when indicated, and use clear safety-netting for fever, bleeding or breathlessness. Discuss consent and confidentiality.

PLAB application: PLAB 2 rewards empathetic explanation and a clear urgent plan.

16. High-yield comparison

Acute disease: blasts, rapidly evolving marrow failure. Chronic disease: often incidental leucocytosis/lymphocytosis, but can progress. CML has BCR::ABL1; APL has PML::RARA; CLL may be monitored.

PLAB application: Always prioritise sepsis, haemorrhage, leukostasis and tumour lysis.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Myeloid versus lymphoid

Myeloid progenitors form granulocytes, erythrocytes, monocytes and megakaryocytes; lymphoid precursors produce B, T and NK lineages. Leukaemia classification uses immunophenotype and genetics.

Acute versus chronic

Acute leukaemias are characterised by immature blasts and can progress over days to weeks. Chronic forms often have mature-appearing abnormal cells, but aggressive transformation is possible.

CML mechanism

The Philadelphia chromosome creates BCR::ABL1, a continuously active tyrosine kinase. Targeted inhibitors transformed chronic-phase outcomes; molecular response is monitored.

APL coagulopathy

Abnormal promyelocytes activate coagulation and fibrinolysis, causing major bleeding risk. Suspected APL requires immediate specialist intervention.

Tumour lysis

Cell lysis releases intracellular potassium and phosphate; nucleic acids metabolise to uric acid. This can produce arrhythmias, seizures and renal injury.

PLAB 2 counselling

Explain uncertainty without falsely reassuring, ask about patient concerns, outline urgent tests and safety-net for fever, new bleeding, breathlessness and confusion.
