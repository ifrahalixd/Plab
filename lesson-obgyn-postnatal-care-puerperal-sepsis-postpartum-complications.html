Postnatal Care, Puerperal Sepsis & Postpartum Complications

Emergency priority: Postpartum sepsis, major bleeding, pulmonary embolism, severe hypertension and postpartum psychosis require urgent assessment.

Postnatal period

The postnatal or puerperal period covers approximately six weeks after birth. Assess physical recovery, emotional wellbeing, infant care and family support.

PLAB application: Ask about bleeding, pain, fever, mood, feeding and safety.

Immediate maternal observations

After birth assess pulse, blood pressure, temperature when indicated, uterine tone, vaginal loss, bladder function and perineal or operative wounds. Escalate abnormal observations.

PLAB application: Use ABCDE for deterioration and quantify bleeding.

Normal lochia and involution

Lochia changes from red to pink/brown then pale over weeks. The uterus gradually involutes. Increasing heavy bleeding, offensive discharge, severe pain or fever are abnormal.

PLAB application: Differentiate expected lochia from secondary postpartum haemorrhage.

Puerperal sepsis recognition

Postpartum uterine, wound, urinary or other infection may cause fever, rigors, pelvic pain, offensive lochia, tachycardia or systemic illness. Fever may be absent in severe sepsis.

PLAB application: Do not delay escalation in a deteriorating patient.

Maternal sepsis first response

Assess ABCDE, call senior obstetric and acute care teams, take observations and lactate, obtain cultures when feasible without delaying treatment, and give prompt IV antibiotics per local sepsis protocol. Identify source and provide fluids judiciously.

PLAB application: Suspected septic shock requires emergency resuscitation and senior involvement.

Postpartum endometritis

Endometritis presents with uterine tenderness, fever, pelvic pain and sometimes offensive lochia, often after caesarean birth or prolonged rupture of membranes. Diagnosis is largely clinical.

PLAB application: Arrange urgent assessment and broad-spectrum antibiotics according to local guidance.

Perineal and caesarean wound infection

Look for increasing pain, erythema, discharge, wound separation and fever. Deep infection or necrotising infection may have disproportionate pain and systemic toxicity.

PLAB application: Examine the wound and escalate rapidly if severe.

Secondary postpartum haemorrhage

Abnormal or excessive bleeding from 24 hours to 12 weeks after birth can result from retained products, endometritis or subinvolution. Heavy bleeding or haemodynamic instability is an emergency.

PLAB application: Resuscitate, seek senior review and investigate the cause.

Venous thromboembolism

The postpartum period increases VTE risk. DVT may cause unilateral leg swelling and pain; PE may cause breathlessness, pleuritic pain, syncope or collapse.

PLAB application: Urgently assess suspected PE; use pregnancy/postnatal-specific pathways.

Postpartum hypertension and pre-eclampsia

Pre-eclampsia can first present after birth. Severe headache, visual symptoms, right upper quadrant pain, dyspnoea or severe hypertension require urgent evaluation.

PLAB application: Check BP and urine and escalate severe features.

Breastfeeding and mastitis

Support feeding choice and assess latch, breast pain and engorgement. Mastitis causes localised tender erythema and systemic symptoms; consider antibiotics when bacterial infection is likely or symptoms persist/worsen.

PLAB application: Offer practical feeding support and review for abscess if a focal mass persists.

Bladder, bowel and pelvic floor

Ask about urinary retention, incontinence, constipation, faecal incontinence and pelvic-floor symptoms. Recognise obstetric anal sphincter injury and refer appropriately.

PLAB application: Offer pelvic-floor rehabilitation and targeted examination.

Postpartum mental health

Baby blues are transient; persistent depression, marked anxiety or inability to function warrant assessment. Postpartum psychosis may involve mania, delusions, confusion or hallucinations and requires same-day emergency specialist assessment.

PLAB application: Ask sensitively about self-harm, harm to baby, sleep and support.

Postnatal follow-up and contraception

NICE postnatal care emphasises personalised assessment and support. Discuss contraception early because ovulation can precede the first period; consider breastfeeding and VTE risk when choosing methods.

PLAB application: Explain options and arrange follow-up tailored to risk.

RhD and immunisation considerations

For an RhD-negative unsensitised mother with an RhD-positive baby, postpartum anti-D immunoglobulin is generally offered within 72 hours, with dose guided by fetomaternal haemorrhage testing and local protocol.

PLAB application: Check maternal antibody status and newborn blood group.

PLAB 2 consultation and safety net

Use open questions, assess bleeding, fever, pain, chest symptoms, mood, feeding and social support. Explain findings without jargon, check understanding and provide clear escalation instructions.

PLAB application: Emergency signs: heavy bleeding, collapse, severe headache, chest pain, breathlessness, fever with deterioration or psychotic symptoms.

PLAB 2 — Postnatal fever consultation

Introduce yourself, confirm identity and consent, assess ABCDE if unwell, ask about timing of birth, delivery method, temperature, rigors, lochia, abdominal pain, urinary symptoms, breast and wound symptoms, medicines and allergies. Explain possible infection, urgent examination, blood tests and antibiotics. Use SBAR to escalate and provide safety-net advice.

Deep Dive

Maternal sepsis

Maternal infection may progress rapidly to organ dysfunction. Assess physiology and lactate, initiate resuscitation and prompt antimicrobials under local guidance; seek source control when indicated.

Endometritis versus retained products

Both can cause postpartum bleeding, pain and fever. Pelvic examination, ultrasound when indicated and specialist review guide management; ultrasound findings alone require clinical interpretation.

Secondary PPH

Consider infection, retained tissue and less common vascular causes. Stabilise before investigations in unstable patients; manage according to obstetric haemorrhage pathways.

VTE after delivery

Risk is highest around delivery and early postpartum. Assess symptoms and risk factors; investigate urgently rather than relying on D-dimer alone in the postpartum setting.

Postpartum psychosis

Usually begins in the first weeks after birth and can fluctuate quickly. It is a psychiatric emergency requiring urgent specialist assessment and a safe care plan for mother and baby.

Communication and safeguarding

Ask about domestic abuse privately when appropriate, support needs, infant safety, health literacy and access to urgent care. Use interpreters when needed.
