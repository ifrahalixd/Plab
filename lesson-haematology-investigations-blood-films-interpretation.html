Haematology Investigations, Blood Films & Interpretation

Red flags: Blasts, schistocytes with thrombocytopenia, febrile neutropenia or major bleeding require urgent assessment.

A systematic approach to the FBC

Interpret haemoglobin, MCV, white-cell differential and platelets together; compare with previous results, symptoms and clinical context. A single out-of-range value is not a diagnosis.

PLAB application: Check trends and urgency before ordering broad panels.

Haemoglobin and haematocrit

Low haemoglobin indicates anaemia; raised haemoglobin/haematocrit may reflect dehydration, hypoxia or myeloproliferative disease. Reference intervals vary by laboratory and patient context.

PLAB application: Assess symptoms, bleeding and physiological context.

MCV: microcytic, normocytic, macrocytic

Microcytosis suggests iron deficiency or thalassaemia; normocytosis occurs in acute bleeding, inflammation or renal disease; macrocytosis occurs with B12/folate deficiency, alcohol, liver disease, hypothyroidism, reticulocytosis or marrow disease.

PLAB application: Do not diagnose iron deficiency from MCV alone.

Reticulocyte count

Reticulocytes reflect marrow response. A raised count may indicate haemolysis or recovery after bleeding; an inappropriately low count in anaemia suggests reduced production. Interpret corrected counts when anaemia is significant.

PLAB application: Separate increased destruction/loss from underproduction.

Blood film: why it matters

A peripheral film assesses cell size, shape, inclusions, immature cells and platelet appearance. It complements but does not replace FBC, history and confirmatory testing.

PLAB application: Request urgent film review for suspected blasts or microangiopathy.

Microcytic film patterns

Hypochromia and anisopoikilocytosis favour iron deficiency; target cells may appear in thalassaemia or liver disease. Basophilic stippling can occur in several conditions including lead exposure.

PLAB application: Use ferritin and iron studies, then haemoglobinopathy testing as indicated.

Macrocytic film patterns

Macro-ovalocytes and hypersegmented neutrophils suggest megaloblastic anaemia. Round macrocytes can accompany alcohol or liver disease. Dysplastic cells may raise concern for MDS.

PLAB application: Check B12/folate and relevant clinical causes.

Haemolysis markers

Haemolysis commonly raises LDH, indirect bilirubin and reticulocytes while lowering haptoglobin. DAT distinguishes some immune causes; interpretation requires clinical correlation.

PLAB application: Check renal function and urine when intravascular haemolysis is suspected.

Recognising key red-cell shapes

Schistocytes suggest fragmentation and may signal TTP/HUS/DIC; spherocytes occur in immune haemolysis or hereditary spherocytosis; sickle cells suggest HbS disorders; tear-drop cells suggest marrow infiltration/fibrosis.

PLAB application: Schistocytes plus thrombocytopenia require urgent escalation.

White-cell differential

Neutrophilia often accompanies inflammation, infection or corticosteroid use; lymphocytosis can be reactive or clonal; eosinophilia has allergic, parasitic, drug-related and other causes. Marked abnormalities need contextual review.

PLAB application: Fever with neutropenia is an emergency.

Blasts and leukaemia clues

Circulating blasts, unexplained cytopenias, Auer rods or concerning constitutional symptoms require urgent specialist assessment. Normal or modest white-cell counts do not exclude acute leukaemia.

PLAB application: Escalate suspected acute leukaemia promptly.

Platelets and pseudothrombocytopenia

Low platelets may reflect destruction, consumption, sequestration or marrow underproduction. EDTA-dependent clumping can cause a spuriously low automated count; confirm with film/repeat sample when appropriate.

PLAB application: Do not dismiss bleeding or critical results as artefact.

Coagulation tests

PT/INR assesses extrinsic/common pathways and is used for warfarin monitoring; APTT assesses intrinsic/common pathways. Fibrinogen and D-dimer aid selected evaluations. DOACs may affect tests unpredictably.

PLAB application: Normal PT/APTT does not exclude all bleeding disorders.

Iron, B12, folate and haemoglobin studies

Ferritin helps assess iron stores but rises with inflammation; transferrin saturation may assist. B12/folate testing follows clinical context. Haemoglobin electrophoresis/HPLC helps identify haemoglobin variants and thalassaemia.

PLAB application: Interpret iron studies with inflammatory markers and history.

Bone marrow and specialised tests

Persistent unexplained cytopenias, blasts or suspected marrow neoplasms may need marrow aspirate/trephine, flow cytometry, cytogenetics and molecular tests guided by haematology.

PLAB application: Avoid implying a single blood test confirms all malignancies.

Clinical prioritisation and safety-netting

Urgent action is required for suspected TTP, acute leukaemia, major bleeding, severe symptomatic anaemia or neutropenic sepsis. Confirm identity and sample quality, review trends, and communicate critical results.

PLAB application: Use ABCDE, local escalation policies and early senior input.

PLAB 2 — Explaining an abnormal blood count

Confirm identity, explore symptoms and ICE, explain the affected cell line without prematurely diagnosing malignancy, discuss a repeat FBC/film and targeted tests, agree follow-up, and safety-net for bleeding, fever, chest pain or severe breathlessness.

Deep Dive

MCV and reticulocyte matrix

Microcytosis with low ferritin suggests iron deficiency; macrocytosis with hypersegmented neutrophils suggests megaloblastosis; normocytic anaemia with high reticulocytes suggests haemolysis or bleeding. Exceptions are common.

Schistocytes and emergency pathways

Fragmented red cells in the setting of thrombocytopenia and haemolysis suggest thrombotic microangiopathy. Suspected TTP needs immediate specialist input; do not delay urgent management for ADAMTS13 results.

Blood film limitations

Morphology is supportive, not pathognomonic. Film quality, sample age, recent transfusion and observer variation can alter findings. Always reconcile with counts, history and confirmatory testing.

Ferritin caveat

Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant and may be normal or raised despite iron deficiency when inflammation coexists. Interpret with CRP, transferrin saturation and clinical context.

Pseudothrombocytopenia

Automated platelet counts can be spuriously low because of EDTA-dependent clumping. A film and appropriate repeat anticoagulant sample may clarify; urgent symptoms still take priority.

Critical results and communication

Verify identity, assess symptoms and vital signs, review previous counts and medications, document actions and communicate urgent findings promptly to the appropriate team.
