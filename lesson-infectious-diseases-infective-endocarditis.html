Infective Endocarditis

PLAB priority: Recognise IE early, obtain blood cultures, arrange echocardiography, treat sepsis and identify embolic or cardiac complications.

01. Definition and importance

Infective endocarditis (IE) is infection of the endocardial surface, usually a heart valve, producing infected vegetations. It can destroy valves, embolise and cause sepsis, heart failure or stroke. Native, prosthetic-valve and cardiac-device infection have different risks.

PLAB application: Suspect IE in unexplained fever with a murmur, embolic signs, prosthetic valve or relevant bacteraemia.

02. Risk factors

Risk rises with previous IE, prosthetic valves or valve repair material, congenital heart disease, certain cardiac devices, haemodialysis, intravenous drug use and structural valve disease. Staphylococcus aureus bacteraemia is an important trigger for investigation.

PLAB application: Ask about previous valve surgery, dental procedures, injection drug use and recent bloodstream infections.

03. Microbiology

Staphylococcus aureus can cause acute destructive disease, including right-sided IE in people who inject drugs. Viridans-group streptococci may follow oral-source bacteraemia; enterococci may relate to gastrointestinal or urinary sources. Coagulase-negative staphylococci are important with prosthetic material. HACEK organisms are uncommon causes.

PLAB application: Obtain blood cultures before antibiotics when clinically safe; do not delay life-saving treatment in sepsis.

04. History

Ask about persistent fever, rigors, night sweats, weight loss, fatigue, dyspnoea, chest pain and neurological symptoms. Explore risk factors, recent procedures, antibiotics, dental problems and possible infection sources.

PLAB application: Ask specifically about sudden focal neurological deficit and breathlessness: embolism and heart failure are emergencies.

05. Examination

Record observations and assess ABCDE. Look for new or changing murmur, heart failure, splenomegaly, petechiae, splinter haemorrhages, painless Janeway lesions, tender Osler nodes and retinal Roth spots. Classical peripheral signs are uncommon and absence does not exclude IE.

PLAB application: Do not wait for classical stigmata before investigating suspected IE.

06. Initial investigations

Take three sets of blood cultures from separate venepunctures before antibiotics if stable, following local microbiology advice; do not wait between sets unnecessarily. Request FBC, CRP, renal function, liver tests, urinalysis and ECG. Evaluate sepsis and organ dysfunction.

PLAB application: Blood cultures are central; communicate recent antibiotic exposure to microbiology.

07. Echocardiography

Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) is usually the initial scan. Transoesophageal echocardiography (TOE) is more sensitive for vegetations, abscesses and prosthetic-valve infection and is indicated when suspicion remains despite nondiagnostic TTE or for selected high-risk cases.

PLAB application: A negative initial TTE does not rule out IE; arrange further imaging with the specialist team.

08. Diagnosis and Duke-ISCVID criteria

Diagnosis combines microbiology, cardiac imaging, clinical features and sometimes pathology using current Duke-ISCVID criteria. Typical positive cultures and imaging evidence are major elements. Fever, predisposition, vascular and immunological findings may support diagnosis.

PLAB application: Do not diagnose or exclude IE using a single feature or one negative scan.

09. Empirical and targeted antibiotics

Treatment is prolonged, usually intravenous initially, and tailored to organism, susceptibility, valve type, renal function and complications. Infectious diseases/microbiology and cardiology should direct regimens. Take cultures first when feasible, but treat promptly if unstable or septic.

PLAB application: Avoid memorising one universal antibiotic combination: local resistance and valve type change the regimen.

10. Surgical indications

Early cardiac surgery may be required for heart failure from severe valve dysfunction, uncontrolled infection (such as abscess or persistent bacteraemia) or prevention of embolism in selected high-risk vegetations. Decisions involve an endocarditis multidisciplinary team.

PLAB application: New pulmonary oedema, severe regurgitation or conduction abnormalities warrant urgent specialist escalation.

11. Embolic and neurological complications

Left-sided IE may cause ischaemic stroke, systemic emboli, splenic or renal infarcts. Right-sided IE may cause septic pulmonary emboli. Intracranial haemorrhage or infective aneurysm can occur. Acute neurological symptoms require emergency assessment and brain imaging.

PLAB application: Thrombolysis is generally contraindicated in IE-associated ischaemic stroke; urgent stroke and endocarditis teams must assess.

12. Heart failure and conduction disease

Valve destruction causes acute regurgitation and pulmonary oedema. Perivalvular abscess can produce new PR prolongation or heart block, particularly in aortic-valve IE. Monitor ECG, haemodynamics and signs of shock.

PLAB application: A new heart block in suspected IE suggests possible abscess and requires urgent assessment.

13. Prosthetic-valve and device infection

Prosthetic-valve IE can be difficult to visualise and may require TOE, CT or nuclear imaging in specialist pathways. Cardiac implantable electronic device infection may require complete device and lead extraction with antibiotics.

PLAB application: Do not dismiss persistent fever in a patient with a prosthetic valve or pacemaker.

14. Culture-negative endocarditis

Prior antibiotic exposure is a common cause of negative cultures. Fastidious organisms such as Coxiella burnetii or Bartonella may need targeted serology or molecular tests. Seek specialist microbiology advice rather than simply repeating empirical courses.

PLAB application: Ask whether antibiotics were taken before cultures.

15. Prevention and dental advice

Good oral hygiene and prompt dental care are important. NICE CG64 does not recommend antibiotic prophylaxis routinely for dental or other procedures; individual complex decisions should involve relevant specialists and current guidance. Do not overlook the differences between UK and international recommendations.

PLAB application: For PLAB, state the NICE position accurately: not routinely offered, rather than never.

16. Follow-up, counselling and OSCE

Explain that IE is a serious infection of the heart lining or valves requiring hospital treatment and repeated blood tests, often prolonged antibiotics and sometimes surgery. Discuss source management, dental health, medication adherence, recurrence symptoms and follow-up echocardiography when indicated.

PLAB application: In PLAB 2, use clear language, check understanding and safety-net fever, breathlessness, chest pain and neurological deficits.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Vegetations and embolism

Microbial colonies embedded in fibrin and platelets form vegetations that can damage valves and shed septic emboli. Left-sided lesions embolise systemically; right-sided lesions embolise to the lungs.

Blood culture strategy

Multiple blood culture sets help distinguish true bacteraemia from contamination and identify susceptibility. In unstable patients, cultures must not delay emergency antibiotics.

Why TOE matters

TOE brings the ultrasound probe closer to the heart, improving detection of small vegetations, perivalvular abscesses and prosthetic valve complications.

Duke-ISCVID reasoning

Current criteria combine major microbiological and imaging evidence with clinical and other supporting findings. They support classification but do not replace expert clinical judgement.

Antibiotic versus surgical treatment

Antimicrobials sterilise infection, but severe valve destruction, abscess or uncontrolled infection may need surgery. Embolic risk and neurological complications affect surgical timing.

Prevention in UK practice

NICE CG64 recommends against routine antibiotic prophylaxis for IE for dental and other procedures. Good oral health and tailored specialist advice are central; international guidance may differ.
