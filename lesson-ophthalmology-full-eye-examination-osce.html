Full Eye Examination & PLAB 2 OSCE

Emergency priority: Immediate irrigation for chemical injury, avoid pressure in open-globe injury, urgent assessment for sudden vision loss or severe painful red eye.

Preparation and consent

Wash hands, introduce yourself, confirm identity, explain examination and obtain consent. Ask about pain, visual impairment, glasses and contact lenses.

PLAB application: Offer a chaperone where appropriate; check both eyes.

Focused history

Ask onset, unilateral/bilateral symptoms, pain, redness, photophobia, flashes, floaters, field loss, diplopia, trauma, headache and systemic disease.

PLAB application: Sudden vision loss, chemical injury and painful red eye are urgent.

Visual acuity

Use a Snellen or logMAR chart at the specified distance, test each eye separately with habitual correction and pinhole if appropriate. Record exact results.

PLAB application: Visual acuity is the vital sign of the eye.

Near vision and colour

Assess near acuity when relevant and colour vision with Ishihara plates if optic nerve pathology is suspected.

PLAB application: Red desaturation can accompany optic neuropathy.

Visual fields

Confrontation fields: compare each quadrant while the patient fixes on your nose. Test each eye separately.

PLAB application: A new field defect may indicate retinal, optic nerve or cerebral disease.

Pupils

Inspect pupil size, symmetry and shape. Test direct and consensual light reflexes, and swinging flashlight test for RAPD.

PLAB application: A RAPD suggests asymmetric afferent pathway dysfunction.

Eye movements

Ask the patient to follow a target in an H pattern without moving the head. Ask about diplopia and inspect for nystagmus or limitation.

PLAB application: New third nerve palsy needs emergency assessment.

External eye

Inspect eyelids, lashes, conjunctiva, sclera, cornea, alignment and swelling. Check for proptosis and ptosis.

PLAB application: Painful eye movements, proptosis or reduced vision with swollen lids suggest orbital cellulitis.

Corneal assessment

Use a pen torch to assess clarity; fluorescein under blue light can reveal epithelial defects when indicated.

PLAB application: Do not press on the eye if open globe injury is suspected.

Red reflex

Use a direct ophthalmoscope to assess the red reflex. In children, a white or absent reflex needs urgent assessment.

PLAB application: Leukocoria can signal retinoblastoma or cataract.

Fundoscopy

Dim lights, explain proximity, use ophthalmoscope and inspect optic disc, vessels, retina and macula where visible.

PLAB application: Describe disc colour, margins, cup-to-disc ratio, haemorrhages and exudates.

Intraocular pressure

Tonometry is generally performed with appropriate equipment by trained staff. Do not rely on palpation for diagnosis.

PLAB application: Painful red eye with haloes, nausea and reduced vision suggests acute angle closure.

Cover test and alignment

Use cover–uncover and alternate cover tests when squint is suspected; observe corrective eye movements.

PLAB application: Test ocular alignment especially in children and diplopia.

Systemic examination

Assess blood pressure, glucose history, neurological signs and temporal artery symptoms when clinically relevant.

PLAB application: Visual loss with jaw claudication or scalp tenderness suggests giant cell arteritis.

Emergency escalation

Chemical injury: irrigate immediately. Suspected open globe: rigid shield and no pressure. Sudden loss of vision, retinal detachment, orbital cellulitis or acute glaucoma need urgent care.

PLAB application: Do not delay emergency treatment for a complete routine examination.

PLAB 2 completion

Thank the patient, help them replace glasses, wash hands, summarise findings and propose investigations/referral. Explain warning symptoms and document visual acuity.

PLAB application: Use clear communication, consent and structured handover.

PLAB 2 — Full eye examination script

Introduce yourself, obtain consent and wash hands. Ask about symptoms and examine visual acuity in each eye. Assess visual fields, pupils and RAPD, extraocular movements, external eye, red reflex and fundus as appropriate. Thank the patient, summarise, explain any urgent concerns and document findings.

Deep Dive

Visual acuity and pinhole

Visual acuity is recorded per eye with appropriate correction. Pinhole can help distinguish refractive blur from pathology, but normal acuity does not exclude serious disease.

Afferent pupillary pathway

Retinal and optic nerve signals reach pretectal nuclei and Edinger–Westphal pathways. RAPD is detected by alternating illumination.

Fundoscopy technique

Use an ophthalmoscope with suitable dioptre setting, approach from the side and follow a vessel to the optic disc. Small pupils limit examination.

Eye movement localisation

CN III supplies most extraocular muscles; IV supplies superior oblique; VI supplies lateral rectus. Diplopia patterns help localise deficits.

Red eye danger signs

Photophobia, severe pain, reduced vision, corneal opacity, abnormal pupil or contact lens-related symptoms warrant urgent evaluation.

Clinical prioritisation

ABCDE and immediate management supersede routine OSCE sequence in chemical injury, penetrating trauma and severe acute visual loss.
