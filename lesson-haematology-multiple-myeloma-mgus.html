Multiple Myeloma & MGUS

Emergency red flags: new neurological deficit with back pain, severe hypercalcaemia, acute kidney injury or sepsis.

Overview

Multiple myeloma is a clonal plasma-cell malignancy producing monoclonal immunoglobulin or free light chains. MGUS is a premalignant plasma-cell condition without myeloma-defining organ damage.

PLAB application: Think myeloma in older adults with unexplained bone pain, anaemia, kidney dysfunction or hypercalcaemia.

CRAB features

CRAB stands for hyperCalcaemia, Renal impairment, Anaemia and Bone lesions. These may indicate myeloma-related end-organ damage.

PLAB application: Identify emergencies while arranging diagnostic assessment.

Bone symptoms

Persistent back or rib pain, pathological fractures and vertebral compression are common. Lytic lesions arise from increased osteoclast activity.

PLAB application: New weakness, sensory loss or bladder dysfunction suggests cord compression.

Renal impairment

Filtered monoclonal light chains may cause cast nephropathy; dehydration, hypercalcaemia and nephrotoxic drugs can worsen kidney injury.

PLAB application: Assess renal function and hydration promptly.

Anaemia and infection

Marrow infiltration suppresses normal haematopoiesis. Impaired normal immunoglobulin production increases infection susceptibility.

PLAB application: Fever in an immunocompromised patient requires urgent assessment.

Initial blood tests

Request FBC, renal profile, calcium, albumin and appropriate inflammatory markers. Check serum protein electrophoresis, immunofixation and serum free light chains according to pathway.

PLAB application: Normal total protein does not exclude light-chain myeloma.

Urine investigations

Urine protein assessment and tests for monoclonal light chains may complement serum testing; use local specialist diagnostic protocol.

PLAB application: Bence Jones protein refers to monoclonal free light chains in urine.

Bone marrow examination

Bone marrow aspirate and trephine assess clonal plasma cells, with flow cytometry and cytogenetic studies for classification and risk.

PLAB application: A paraprotein alone does not confirm symptomatic myeloma.

Imaging

Whole-body low-dose CT, whole-body MRI or PET-CT may be used according to NICE guidance and clinical circumstances. Plain skeletal surveys are less sensitive.

PLAB application: Urgent MRI is required for suspected spinal cord compression.

SLiM-CRAB

Myeloma-defining events include CRAB damage attributable to plasma-cell disease or selected biomarkers: ≥60% clonal marrow plasma cells, involved/uninvolved free-light-chain ratio ≥100 with involved chain ≥100 mg/L, or >1 focal MRI lesion ≥5 mm.

PLAB application: Specialists apply formal diagnostic criteria and attribution.

MGUS

MGUS generally involves low-level monoclonal protein, fewer than 10% clonal marrow plasma cells and no myeloma-defining events. It carries a risk of progression and requires risk-adapted monitoring.

PLAB application: Do not label MGUS as cancer requiring chemotherapy.

Smouldering myeloma

Smouldering myeloma lies between MGUS and active myeloma: higher clonal burden without myeloma-defining events. Specialist risk stratification and monitoring are essential.

PLAB application: Differentiate observation from active treatment.

Treatment overview

Specialist therapy may combine immunomodulatory drugs, proteasome inhibitors, monoclonal antibodies and corticosteroids; transplant eligibility and frailty guide regimens.

PLAB application: Do not promise one universal chemotherapy regimen.

Supportive management

Treat pain, infections, renal dysfunction and hypercalcaemia. Bone-modifying agents and thromboprophylaxis may be used when indicated by treatment and risk.

PLAB application: Consider medication toxicity, vaccination and infection prevention.

Emergency complications

Spinal cord compression, severe hypercalcaemia, acute kidney injury, sepsis and hyperviscosity are urgent complications.

PLAB application: Use ABCDE and immediate senior/haematology escalation.

PLAB 2 counselling

Explain abnormal plasma cells and paraprotein, discuss investigations, treatment versus monitoring, safety-netting and psychosocial support.

PLAB application: Ask about pain, fatigue, infections, function and patient priorities.

PLAB 2 — Myeloma consultation

Introduce yourself, confirm identity, obtain consent, assess pain, fatigue, infection and neurological symptoms. Explain paraprotein testing, marrow and imaging investigations, possible treatment versus MGUS monitoring, and urgent safety-netting.

Deep Dive

Plasma-cell biology

A clone of terminally differentiated B cells produces monoclonal immunoglobulin or free light chains.

Bone destruction

Osteoclast activation and reduced osteoblast activity cause lytic lesions and fractures.

Renal pathophysiology

Filtered light chains can precipitate in tubules and cause cast nephropathy; calcium and dehydration worsen injury.

Diagnostic criteria

SLiM-CRAB criteria combine attributable organ damage with biomarkers of imminent progression.

MGUS and smouldering myeloma

These precursor states differ in clonal burden and progression risk; both require individualised monitoring.

Modern therapy

Anti-CD38 antibodies, proteasome inhibitors, immunomodulators and transplant are selected by specialist teams.
