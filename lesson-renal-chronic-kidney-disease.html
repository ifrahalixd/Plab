Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

PLAB safety: Do not label a single abnormal eGFR as chronic kidney disease. Exclude AKI and confirm persistence for at least 3 months.

01. Definition

CKD means abnormalities of kidney structure or function present for at least 3 months with health implications: eGFR below 60 mL/min/1.73 m² or markers such as albuminuria, abnormal sediment or imaging.

PLAB focus: Do not diagnose CKD from one isolated eGFR.

02. GFR classification

G1 ≥90; G2 60–89; G3a 45–59; G3b 30–44; G4 15–29; G5 <15 mL/min/1.73 m². G1/G2 require another marker of kidney damage.

PLAB focus: Record G and A categories together.

03. Albuminuria classification

Urine ACR: A1 <3 mg/mmol; A2 3–30; A3 >30. Albuminuria predicts renal and cardiovascular risk.

PLAB focus: Prefer urine ACR to dipstick alone.

04. Common causes

Diabetes, hypertension, glomerulonephritis, polycystic kidney disease, obstructive uropathy and chronic tubulointerstitial disease.

PLAB focus: Assess for reversible and inherited causes.

05. Risk factors

Diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, previous AKI, family history, structural urinary disease and nephrotoxic exposure.

PLAB focus: Target testing to at-risk people.

06. Clinical presentation

Often asymptomatic early. Later: fatigue, pruritus, nocturia, oedema, breathlessness, anorexia and uraemic symptoms.

PLAB focus: Symptoms alone cannot stage CKD.

07. Initial investigations

Check creatinine/eGFR, urine ACR, urine dip for blood, blood pressure, HbA1c if indicated, electrolytes and full blood count. Compare previous results.

PLAB focus: Repeat unexpected new eGFR below 60 promptly to exclude AKI.

08. Confirming chronicity

Establish persistence for ≥3 months from prior bloods, urine results or imaging; assess acute illness and medication effects.

PLAB focus: Distinguish CKD from AKI and AKI on CKD.

09. Kidney imaging

Renal ultrasound if obstruction, haematuria, rapid decline, advanced CKD, family history of polycystic disease or other guideline indications.

PLAB focus: Imaging is not mandatory for every mild CKD case.

10. Risk assessment

Use eGFR trend, ACR, blood pressure, diabetes and kidney failure risk equation where appropriate to guide referral and monitoring.

PLAB focus: A higher ACR increases risk even with preserved eGFR.

11. Blood pressure and RAAS blockade

Optimise BP; ACE inhibitor or ARB is indicated for selected albuminuric CKD groups. Monitor creatinine and potassium after initiation or dose increase; avoid ACE inhibitor plus ARB combination.

PLAB focus: Individualise BP targets and medication selection.

12. Diabetes and kidney protection

Optimise glycaemic and cardiovascular risk management; SGLT2 inhibitors are kidney protective for eligible CKD patients according to current NICE indications and eGFR thresholds.

PLAB focus: Check current prescribing guidance and sick-day advice.

13. Lifestyle and medicine safety

Smoking cessation, exercise, dietary support, salt reduction, vaccination, avoid NSAIDs and adjust renally cleared medicines.

PLAB focus: Do not advise blanket fluid loading.

14. Complications

Anaemia, CKD-mineral bone disorder, hyperkalaemia, acidosis, fluid overload and cardiovascular disease become more common as kidney function declines.

PLAB focus: Investigate and treat the cause of anaemia rather than assuming CKD.

15. Referral and urgent warning signs

Refer for significant decline, heavy albuminuria, haematuria with albuminuria, resistant hypertension, suspected genetic disease or high kidney failure risk. Urgent review for severe hyperkalaemia, pulmonary oedema or uraemic symptoms.

PLAB focus: Apply NICE NG203 referral thresholds and local pathways.

16. Advanced CKD and planning

Shared decision-making about dialysis, transplantation, conservative kidney management and symptom control; prepare early when progression risk is high.

PLAB focus: Dialysis is based on clinical indications and patient preference, not eGFR alone.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

GFR and albuminuria

eGFR estimates filtration while urine ACR reflects kidney damage. Both predict outcomes and inform CKD risk stratification.

Hyperfiltration and nephron loss

Remaining nephrons may hyperfilter after nephron loss, contributing to progressive glomerular injury.

RAAS blockade

ACE inhibitors and ARBs lower intraglomerular pressure and albuminuria but require monitoring for hyperkalaemia and changes in kidney function.

SGLT2 kidney protection

SGLT2 inhibition changes proximal tubular sodium-glucose handling and tubuloglomerular feedback, reducing intraglomerular pressure in eligible patients.

Anaemia and bone disease

Reduced erythropoietin contributes to anaemia; phosphate retention and altered vitamin D/PTH contribute to CKD-mineral bone disorder.

Referral reasoning

Trajectory, albuminuria, haematuria, resistant hypertension and kidney failure risk matter more than a single creatinine value.
