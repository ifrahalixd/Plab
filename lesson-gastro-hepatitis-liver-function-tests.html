Hepatitis & Liver Function Tests

Emergency: New confusion, prolonged INR, bleeding or hypoglycaemia in acute liver disease needs urgent assessment.

Why liver blood tests matter

A liver panel typically includes ALT, AST, ALP, bilirubin and albumin; INR assesses synthetic function. These tests indicate injury patterns but are not a standalone diagnosis.

PLAB application: An isolated abnormality should be interpreted with symptoms, medicines, alcohol, risk factors and previous results.

Hepatocellular versus cholestatic patterns

ALT and AST predominate in hepatocellular injury; ALP and GGT predominate in cholestasis. Bilirubin may rise in either pattern.

PLAB application: ALT is more liver-specific than AST; ALP may also originate from bone, so GGT helps establish hepatic origin.

Synthetic function and severity

Albumin may be low in chronic disease or other illnesses; prolonged INR can indicate impaired hepatic synthesis. Acute liver failure includes coagulopathy with encephalopathy in a person without pre-existing cirrhosis.

PLAB application: Urgently assess confusion, bleeding, hypoglycaemia and raised INR; do not equate a high ALT with severity.

Clinical presentation

Acute hepatitis may cause fatigue, nausea, anorexia, fever, right upper quadrant discomfort, dark urine, pale stools and jaundice; chronic hepatitis may be asymptomatic.

PLAB application: Examine for jaundice, hepatomegaly, chronic liver disease stigmata, ascites and encephalopathy.

Viral hepatitis A and E

Hepatitis A and E are commonly transmitted faecal–orally. Hepatitis A is usually acute and self-limited; hepatitis E can be particularly severe in pregnancy and immunocompromised people.

PLAB application: Ask about travel, food/water exposure and contacts; public health notification may apply.

Hepatitis B

HBV spreads through blood and body fluids, including sexual and perinatal transmission. Acute infection may resolve; chronic infection can cause cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma.

PLAB application: HBsAg suggests current infection; anti-HBs indicates immunity; anti-HBc indicates prior or current natural infection. Specialist interpretation is needed.

Hepatitis C

HCV is primarily blood-borne, including injecting equipment and historical unscreened blood exposure. Chronic infection is common without treatment.

PLAB application: Anti-HCV screens for exposure; HCV RNA confirms current infection. Direct-acting antiviral treatment is highly effective.

Hepatitis D

HDV requires hepatitis B surface antigen for infection and can worsen HBV-associated liver disease.

PLAB application: Consider HDV testing in HBsAg-positive patients according to specialist protocols.

Other causes of hepatitis

Alcohol-associated liver injury, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, autoimmune hepatitis, medicines (especially paracetamol overdose), ischaemia and Wilson disease can cause hepatocellular injury.

PLAB application: Medication and supplement history, alcohol assessment, metabolic risk and autoimmune features matter.

Initial investigations

Repeat and characterise abnormal tests when appropriate; take a focused history, assess severity, request FBC, renal function, INR, viral serology, autoimmune/iron tests as indicated and liver ultrasound.

PLAB application: Urgent referral is needed for jaundice with systemic illness, suspected obstruction, encephalopathy or significant coagulopathy.

Interpreting hepatitis B serology

HBsAg indicates current HBV infection; anti-HBs without anti-HBc suggests vaccine immunity; anti-HBc with anti-HBs suggests past natural infection. IgM anti-HBc often indicates recent acute infection but can occur in flares.

PLAB application: Do not interpret one marker in isolation; HBV DNA and specialist review guide staging and treatment.

Management of acute viral hepatitis

Most uncomplicated hepatitis A requires supportive care, hydration, avoidance of hepatotoxic exposures and appropriate infection-control advice. Severe disease needs inpatient assessment.

PLAB application: Do not prescribe antivirals for all hepatitis types; treatment is virus- and severity-specific.

Chronic HBV management

Specialist care considers HBV DNA, ALT, fibrosis, comorbidities and pregnancy. Some patients need nucleos(t)ide analogue treatment and HCC surveillance.

PLAB application: HBV vaccination and contact testing help prevent transmission.

Chronic HCV management

Refer for HCV RNA confirmation, fibrosis assessment and direct-acting antiviral therapy. Check HBV status and drug interactions before treatment.

PLAB application: Successful HCV cure does not eliminate HCC surveillance need in established cirrhosis.

Prevention and public health

Vaccination prevents hepatitis A and B in indicated groups. Use safe sharps, screened blood, safer sex and antenatal HBV screening; manage exposures promptly.

PLAB application: Hepatitis B post-exposure prophylaxis depends on exposure and vaccination/immune status.

PLAB 2 explanation and safety-netting

Explain the pattern and possible causes without labelling every abnormal LFT as hepatitis. Ask about stigma and confidentiality, explain tests and follow-up.

PLAB application: Seek urgent care for confusion, bleeding, severe vomiting, worsening jaundice or severe abdominal pain.

PLAB 2: explaining abnormal liver blood tests

  1. Explore symptoms, medicines, supplements, alcohol, exposures and concerns.
  2. Explain that raised enzymes can show liver irritation but not necessarily liver failure.
  3. Discuss serology, ultrasound, repeat blood tests and specialist referral.
  4. Explain transmission prevention and confidentiality sensitively.
  5. Safety-net confusion, bleeding, worsening jaundice or severe abdominal pain.

Deep Dive

Why ALT is not function

Damaged hepatocytes release aminotransferases; a very high ALT may coexist with preserved synthesis early, while severe cirrhosis may show modest ALT.

Bilirubin pathways

Unconjugated bilirubin arises from haem breakdown; hepatocytes conjugate it for biliary excretion. Obstruction tends to raise conjugated bilirubin.

HBV serology nuance

HBsAg, anti-HBs, total/IgM anti-HBc, HBeAg and HBV DNA answer different questions; acute flares can complicate interpretation.

HCV antibody versus RNA

Antibody persists after clearance or cure, whereas RNA indicates active infection.

Fibrosis and HCC

Persistent inflammation promotes fibrosis, cirrhosis and malignancy risk; HBV may cause HCC even without cirrhosis.

Acute liver failure

Urgent management includes recognising hypoglycaemia, cerebral dysfunction and coagulopathy and early transplant-centre discussion.
