Osteoarthritis

PLAB priority: Diagnose typical osteoarthritis clinically, offer therapeutic exercise and self-management, use topical NSAIDs for knee OA, and recognise hot swollen joints as urgent red flags.

01. Definition and burden

Osteoarthritis (OA) is a whole-joint disorder involving cartilage loss, subchondral bone remodelling, osteophytes and variable synovial inflammation. It causes pain, stiffness and functional limitation, commonly in knees, hips, hands and spine.

PLAB application: Distinguish OA from inflammatory arthritis and identify opportunities for exercise-based treatment.

02. Risk factors

Age, obesity, previous joint injury, occupational loading, family history, malalignment and muscle weakness increase risk. OA can occur in younger adults after injury.

PLAB application: Ask about previous injuries, weight, occupation and activity limitations.

03. Typical symptoms

Pain is often activity-related and relieved by rest, although advanced OA can cause rest or night pain. Morning stiffness is usually absent or lasts 30 minutes or less. Patients report reduced movement and difficulty with stairs or walking.

PLAB application: Persistent prolonged morning stiffness or marked systemic illness suggests another diagnosis.

04. Knee osteoarthritis

Joint-line tenderness, bony enlargement, crepitus, reduced flexion and possible varus deformity are typical. Small effusions can occur. Assess gait, quadriceps strength and function.

PLAB application: A hot, acutely swollen knee needs urgent evaluation for septic or crystal arthritis.

05. Hip osteoarthritis

Groin pain may radiate to the thigh or knee; internal rotation is often restricted first. Observe gait and assess passive hip movement.

PLAB application: Referred knee pain may originate in the hip.

06. Hand osteoarthritis

Distal interphalangeal joint osteophytes (Heberden nodes), proximal interphalangeal nodes (Bouchard nodes), and thumb-base pain are characteristic. Erosive hand OA may be inflammatory.

PLAB application: DIP predominance contrasts with the usual MCP/wrist pattern of rheumatoid arthritis.

07. Clinical diagnosis

NICE NG226 supports clinical diagnosis in people aged 45 or older with activity-related joint pain and no morning stiffness or stiffness lasting 30 minutes or less. Imaging is not routinely required for typical presentations.

PLAB application: Do not order routine X-rays to confirm straightforward OA.

08. When to investigate

Consider X-ray or other testing when symptoms are atypical, diagnosis uncertain, alternative pathology suspected or surgery is being planned. Radiographic changes include joint-space narrowing, osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis and cysts.

PLAB application: X-ray severity correlates imperfectly with pain and function.

09. Differential diagnoses

Consider rheumatoid arthritis, gout, pseudogout/CPPD, septic arthritis, inflammatory spondyloarthritis, avascular necrosis, fracture and malignancy. Fever, rapid swelling, severe rest pain or unexplained weight loss are warning features.

PLAB application: An acutely hot swollen joint is not routine OA: urgent aspiration and assessment may be needed.

10. Core treatment: therapeutic exercise

Offer tailored therapeutic exercise to all people with OA, including local muscle strengthening and general aerobic fitness. Initial discomfort may occur but regular exercise improves pain, function and quality of life.

PLAB application: Explain that safe movement is treatment, not evidence of joint damage.

11. Weight management and education

For overweight or obese patients, weight loss can improve pain and function; greater loss generally gives greater benefit. Use shared decisions, pacing, self-management and activity adaptation.

PLAB application: Discuss achievable goals without blaming patients.

12. Topical and oral NSAIDs

Offer a topical NSAID for knee OA; consider it for other affected joints. If insufficient or unsuitable, consider an oral NSAID at the lowest effective dose for the shortest time, assessing GI, renal and cardiovascular risks and offering gastroprotection.

PLAB application: Do not routinely offer paracetamol or weak opioids; NICE advises against strong opioids.

13. Injections and other treatments

Consider intra-articular corticosteroid injection for short-term relief when other medicines are ineffective or unsuitable, or to support therapeutic exercise. Do not offer hyaluronan injections. Supports or walking aids may help selected patients.

PLAB application: Injections do not replace exercise and other core management.

14. Surgical referral

Consider referral for joint replacement if pain, stiffness, deformity or reduced function substantially affects quality of life and non-surgical management is ineffective or unsuitable. Use clinical assessment rather than numerical scoring alone.

PLAB application: Do not exclude referral solely because of age, obesity, smoking or comorbidity.

15. Complications and follow-up

OA can contribute to reduced mobility, falls, sleep disruption, low mood and deconditioning. Review pain, function, adherence, drug adverse effects and evolving red flags.

PLAB application: Reassess if the clinical picture changes rather than assuming every new symptom is OA.

16. PLAB 2 consultation and counselling

Explore impact on work, mobility and goals; examine relevant joints and gait; explain OA in plain language; agree exercise, weight support and appropriate analgesia. Safety-net for a hot swollen joint, fever, severe sudden pain or inability to bear weight.

PLAB application: Use shared decision-making and avoid promising that imaging or surgery is always necessary.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Whole-joint pathology

OA affects cartilage, subchondral bone, synovium, ligaments and surrounding muscles. Cartilage thinning and bone remodelling contribute to pain and stiffness.

Pain versus X-ray severity

Some patients with marked radiographic change have modest symptoms and vice versa. Treatment should target pain, function and goals rather than X-ray appearance alone.

OA versus RA

OA often affects DIP and first carpometacarpal joints with brief stiffness; RA typically causes persistent inflammatory MCP/PIP/wrist synovitis and prolonged morning stiffness. Overlap exists.

Why exercise helps

Muscle strengthening improves joint support; aerobic activity improves fitness and function. Tailor type and intensity, and expect some initial discomfort.

NSAID risk balancing

Oral NSAIDs may cause gastrointestinal bleeding, kidney injury and cardiovascular events. Review comorbidities, other medicines and gastroprotection before prescribing.

When to suspect another cause

Rapidly progressive symptoms, hot swollen joints, systemic illness, severe unremitting night pain or a major change in function require reassessment for infection, crystal arthritis, fracture or malignancy.
