Paediatric PLAB 2 OSCE: Clinical Examination, Communication, Prescribing & Emergency Stations

OSCE priorities: Child safety, consent, ABCDE for emergencies, accurate weight-based prescribing, clear parent communication and escalation.

OSCE station structure

Read the task outside the station; introduce yourself, confirm the child and accompanying adult, obtain consent, assess immediate safety and use age-appropriate language. Structure the encounter around the specific task and leave time to summarise, agree a plan and safety-net.

PLAB application: Ask the examiner for observations when assessing an acutely ill child; do not spend the whole station taking a routine history.

Paediatric history-taking

Clarify presenting concern, onset, progression, feeding, hydration, urine and stool output, activity, sleep, fever, pain, exposure, travel, immunisations, medication and allergies. Include birth, neonatal, developmental, growth, past medical and family history when relevant.

PLAB application: Ask caregivers what worries them most and establish what is normal for this particular child.

Age-appropriate examination

Observe before touching: interaction, work of breathing, colour, posture and behaviour. Examine gently with a caregiver present, saving distressing steps for last. Record growth, vital signs and relevant systems.

PLAB application: Seek consent and explain what you are doing to both the caregiver and child at a developmentally suitable level.

ABCDE for a deteriorating child

Assess airway patency and noises; breathing rate, effort and oxygenation; circulation, heart rate, capillary refill and pulses; disability including AVPU, pupils and glucose; exposure for rash, temperature and injuries. Call for senior help immediately, intervene and reassess.

PLAB application: Recognise that hypotension is late in paediatric shock; tachycardia and poor perfusion may be earlier.

Respiratory examination station

Look for nasal flaring, recessions, grunting, stridor, wheeze and cyanosis. Count respiratory rate and assess oxygen saturation, chest expansion and auscultation; distinguish upper from lower airway noise.

PLAB application: A silent chest in severe asthma and stridor with exhaustion are emergency signs.

Cardiovascular examination station

Assess appearance, central cyanosis, pulses including femorals, capillary refill, precordium, heart sounds and murmur timing. Ask about feeding sweats, failure to thrive, exercise intolerance and syncope.

PLAB application: Weak femoral pulses suggest coarctation; a murmur with syncope needs urgent cardiac assessment.

Abdominal examination station

Observe comfort and distension, inspect scars and hernias, gently palpate for tenderness, guarding, masses and organomegaly, and examine groins/testes when clinically indicated with consent and chaperone.

PLAB application: Green bilious vomiting, peritonitis or an acute scrotum warrants urgent surgical review.

Neurological and developmental station

Assess consciousness, cranial nerves when indicated, tone, power, reflexes, gait and coordination in age-appropriate ways. Ask about milestones and regression. Look for asymmetry and abnormal movements.

PLAB application: Developmental regression and persistent focal deficits require prompt assessment.

Explaining a diagnosis to parents

Ask what the parent already understands, use a short plain-language explanation, check understanding and avoid unqualified reassurance. Describe what is known, what remains uncertain, tests, treatments and warning signs.

PLAB application: Use teach-back: “Just so I know I explained it clearly, what will you look out for at home?”

Confidentiality and safeguarding

Explain confidentiality and its limits; assess whether the young person can understand the decision. Document concerns objectively, quote disclosures verbatim and escalate to the safeguarding lead when indicated.

PLAB application: Do not promise secrecy or wait for proof of maltreatment before raising a concern.

Consent and assent

Parents with parental responsibility usually consent for younger children; competent under-16s may consent under Gillick principles, and 16–17-year-olds are generally presumed able to consent. Involve children in decisions and assess capacity and best interests where relevant.

PLAB application: Do not equate a parent accompanying a child with automatically holding parental responsibility.

Prescribing and dose calculations

Confirm age, current weight in kilograms, allergies, indication, formulation, route, renal/hepatic concerns and local formulary guidance. Calculate mg/kg dose and maximum dose; check concentration before converting mg to mL.

PLAB application: Write the full prescription with units and frequency; independently check high-risk drugs and never guess a dose.

Fluids and dehydration station

Assess degree of dehydration and shock using clinical signs and urine output. Use oral rehydration for suitable children and paediatric protocols for IV resuscitation. Consider ongoing losses, glucose, electrolytes and frequent reassessment.

PLAB application: Children with shock or altered consciousness require emergency escalation rather than routine oral rehydration.

Febrile child and sepsis station

Apply the NICE fever traffic-light framework for under-5s, assess for focal infection and meningitis, check hydration and perfusion and consider age. Infants under 3 months with temperature 38°C or above are high risk.

PLAB application: A child with suspected sepsis needs urgent senior review and timely antibiotics according to local protocols.

Anaphylaxis and seizure emergency stations

Anaphylaxis with airway, breathing or circulation compromise requires immediate IM adrenaline according to age-appropriate resuscitation guidance. For a convulsive seizure lasting five minutes or longer, summon help and follow emergency seizure protocol.

PLAB application: Never delay IM adrenaline for antihistamines; never restrain or place anything in the mouth during a seizure.

Closing an OSCE and SBAR handover

Summarise findings and differential, communicate urgency, agree immediate actions, explain follow-up and give specific safety-net advice. For escalation use SBAR: Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation. Document timing and key decisions.

PLAB application: State clearly who is responsible for the next step and when reassessment will occur.

PLAB 2 mini-script — febrile infant

“I can see your baby is unwell. I am going to check their breathing, circulation and blood sugar now, and ask the senior paediatric team to join us. We will explain each step and keep you updated.”

Reassess, escalate, document and give a structured handover.

Deep Dive — OSCE clinical reasoning

Clinical reasoning under time pressure

The examiner rewards prioritisation and safe decision-making. In a deteriorating child, verbalise immediate ABCDE assessment, call for help, use appropriate monitoring and reassess after each intervention. Avoid giving a definitive diagnosis when key data are missing.

Dose-calculation worked example

For a hypothetical prescription of 10 mg/kg to a 15 kg child, the arithmetic is 150 mg. If a hypothetical liquid contains 50 mg in 5 mL, its concentration is 10 mg/mL and the calculated volume is 15 mL. This is arithmetic practice only, not a recommendation for any medication. Verify actual doses, maximums and formulations against BNFC/local protocols.

Communicating uncertainty

Say what is most likely, what dangerous alternatives must be excluded, what the immediate plan is and which changes should trigger urgent reassessment. Do not promise an outcome.

Safety-netting with specific triggers

Tailor warnings to the scenario: worsening work of breathing, blue lips, inability to wake, persistent seizures, reduced urine, non-blanching rash, severe abdominal pain or green vomit require immediate help. Confirm the family understands access to emergency care.

Ethical practice in adolescents

Speak with the young person directly, offer private discussion when safe, assess decision-specific competence and explain confidentiality boundaries. If serious risk or abuse is disclosed, share necessary information proportionately and involve safeguarding services.

Examiner checklist

Introduction and consent; structured focused assessment; recognition of red flags; sensible differential; immediate management and escalation; accurate prescribing checks; empathy, shared understanding, safety-netting and clear closure.
