Paediatric Emergencies: The Critically Ill Child, Febrile Seizures, Dehydration & Paediatric Resuscitation

Emergency red flags: Apnoea, central cyanosis, reduced consciousness, prolonged seizure, shock, severe dehydration or non-blanching rash with illness demand urgent paediatric assessment.

Recognising the critically ill child

Use the paediatric assessment triangle: appearance, work of breathing and circulation to skin. An abnormal triangle prompts immediate ABCDE and early senior help. Children compensate before sudden deterioration.

PLAB application: A normal blood pressure does not exclude shock.

Paediatric ABCDE and escalation

Assess airway patency, breathing effort and oxygenation, perfusion and pulse, consciousness and glucose, temperature and full exposure. Treat threats as found and reassess. Use age-appropriate vital signs and paediatric early warning systems.

PLAB application: Call the paediatric emergency or resuscitation team early.

Airway and respiratory failure

Look for stridor, grunting, recession, apnoea, cyanosis and exhaustion. Position airway, use appropriately sized equipment and provide oxygen/ventilation according to resuscitation guidance. Avoid upsetting a child with suspected epiglottitis.

PLAB application: A silent or tiring child can be more dangerous than a noisy child.

Shock recognition and causes

Tachycardia, prolonged capillary refill, cool peripheries, weak pulses, altered mental state and reduced urine suggest impaired perfusion. Consider sepsis, dehydration, bleeding, anaphylaxis and cardiogenic causes. Hypotension is late.

PLAB application: Do not rely on blood pressure alone.

Sepsis and meningococcal disease

Sepsis can present with poor feeding, lethargy, mottling, temperature instability, respiratory distress or altered behaviour. Initiate ABCDE, senior review, timely cultures if feasible without delaying treatment, antibiotics and source control per NICE/local pathway.

PLAB application: Meningococcal sepsis can occur without a rash.

Fever under five and risk stratification

Use NICE NG143 traffic-light features and age-specific thresholds. Temperature 38°C or above in an infant under 3 months is high risk; assess urgently. Look for hydration, breathing, responsiveness and non-blanching rash.

PLAB application: Antipyretic response does not rule out serious illness.

Febrile seizures: definitions

A simple febrile seizure is generalised, lasts under 15 minutes and does not recur within 24 hours; complex features include focality, duration 15 minutes or longer, or recurrence. Assess infection, meningitis features and recovery.

PLAB application: Most simple febrile seizures do not imply epilepsy.

Ongoing seizure and status epilepticus

Protect from injury, time seizure, support airway, oxygenation and check glucose. A convulsive seizure lasting 5 minutes or longer needs emergency treatment according to age-specific protocol, typically a benzodiazepine first line. Escalate refractory seizures.

PLAB application: Never restrain movements or place anything in the mouth.

Gastroenteritis and dehydration

Assess fluid intake, urine output, weight change, capillary refill, mucous membranes, eyes, consciousness and shock. Mild-to-moderate dehydration is usually managed with oral rehydration; shock requires immediate IV or intraosseous resuscitation and reassessment.

PLAB application: Reassess frequently for fluid overload or ongoing losses.

Oral rehydration under NICE CG84

For clinical dehydration in under-fives, NICE recommends 50 mL/kg low-osmolarity ORS over 4 hours plus maintenance needs, with ongoing losses addressed. Use small frequent amounts; consider nasogastric ORS if oral intake fails.

PLAB application: Do not routinely give anti-diarrhoeal drugs to young children.

Paediatric fluids and glucose

Prescribe fluids by weight, clinical state, sodium and local paediatric protocol. Check glucose early in reduced consciousness or seizures. Use cautious, reassessed boluses and specialist advice for DKA, cardiac disease and complex electrolyte problems.

PLAB application: Adult fluid algorithms are not interchangeable with paediatric ones.

Anaphylaxis in children

Rapid airway, breathing or circulation compromise after likely exposure requires immediate intramuscular adrenaline into anterolateral thigh using age-appropriate dose per Resuscitation Council UK. Position appropriately, summon help and repeat if symptoms persist per guideline.

PLAB application: Skin signs may be absent; antihistamines are not first-line for ABC compromise.

Bronchiolitis, asthma and croup

Bronchiolitis is managed mainly with oxygen/hydration support when indicated; do not routinely use salbutamol or steroids. Severe asthma needs inhaled bronchodilators and systemic steroids; croup usually needs dexamethasone, with nebulised adrenaline for severe cases under supervision.

PLAB application: Differentiate upper airway stridor from lower airway wheeze.

Paediatric basic life support

Check responsiveness and normal breathing, call emergency help, open airway and give five initial rescue breaths per UK paediatric BLS guidance; then provide compressions and ventilations with appropriate ratio based on training and rescuer status. Attach AED as soon as available.

PLAB application: Follow the current Resuscitation Council UK paediatric algorithm and dispatcher instructions.

Paediatric advanced life support

Use team-based ABCDE, high-quality CPR, rhythm recognition, appropriate defibrillation and drug dosing per current UK PALS/EPALS algorithm. Search for reversible causes, including hypoxia, hypovolaemia and metabolic problems.

PLAB application: Use weight-based calculations and double-check drug doses.

PLAB 2 communication, safeguarding and handover

Explain findings to parents in clear language while prioritising treatment; obtain consent where appropriate, document observations and treatment times, assess safeguarding concerns and provide explicit return precautions. Handover using SBAR and state weight, vital trends and interventions.

PLAB application: Never discharge a child who remains clinically unstable or without a safe plan.

PLAB 2 — The deteriorating child

Introduce yourself to child and caregiver, call for help, assess ABCDE, use age-appropriate observations and weight-based interventions, communicate calmly, reassess and give a structured SBAR handover. Explain the emergency plan and safety-net.
