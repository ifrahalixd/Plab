Neck Lumps & Cervical Lymphadenopathy

Urgent red flags: stridor, drooling, rapidly enlarging swelling, sepsis, facial weakness or persistent unexplained adult neck mass.

Definition and approach

A neck lump may arise from lymph nodes, salivary glands, thyroid, skin or congenital structures. Establish the site, duration, growth, tenderness and systemic symptoms.

PLAB application: Do not assume a persistent adult neck mass is reactive.

Neck anatomy

Cervical lymph nodes are grouped into levels I–VI. Submandibular nodes drain parts of the oral cavity; jugular chain nodes drain pharyngeal structures; posterior triangle nodes can be associated with scalp, infection or malignancy.

PLAB application: Describe the anatomical level and laterality.

Focused history

Ask about onset, duration, recent URTI, dental symptoms, sore throat, weight loss, night sweats, fever, dysphagia, odynophagia, hoarseness, otalgia and tobacco/alcohol exposure.

PLAB application: Ask about tuberculosis exposure and travel when relevant.

Examination technique

Inspect from the front and behind; palpate size, location, consistency, mobility, tenderness, warmth and overlying skin. Examine mouth, teeth, tonsils, scalp, ears, thyroid and salivary glands.

PLAB application: Check for supraclavicular nodes and perform a relevant cranial nerve examination.

Reactive lymphadenopathy

Tender, mobile nodes commonly accompany viral URTIs, tonsillitis or dental infection and often resolve as the underlying illness improves.

PLAB application: Persistent, enlarging or unexplained lumps need reassessment rather than repeated empirical antibiotics.

Bacterial cervical lymphadenitis

May cause painful, warm, erythematous swelling with fever; an abscess may be fluctuant. Assess for deep neck space infection.

PLAB application: Systemic toxicity, trismus, drooling or airway symptoms need emergency assessment.

Tuberculous lymphadenitis

Often presents with chronic, relatively painless cervical lymphadenopathy; nodes may become matted or develop sinuses. Investigate according to TB and local infectious disease pathways.

PLAB application: Consider TB in persistent nodes, especially with relevant epidemiological risk.

Lymphoma

Persistent rubbery, usually non-tender lymphadenopathy may occur with fever, drenching night sweats, weight loss or pruritus.

PLAB application: NICE NG12 recommends considering suspected cancer pathway referral for unexplained lymphadenopathy in adults, taking associated symptoms into account.

Metastatic head and neck cancer

A persistent firm or fixed adult neck node can represent metastatic squamous cell carcinoma, sometimes from an occult oropharyngeal primary.

PLAB application: Ask about persistent unilateral throat symptoms, dysphagia, otalgia and voice changes.

Supraclavicular lymphadenopathy

An unexplained supraclavicular node is concerning for malignancy, although infection and other causes exist.

PLAB application: Prompt investigation is warranted; do not reassure based on lack of pain.

Salivary gland swelling

Parotid or submandibular swelling may reflect sialolithiasis, sialadenitis, autoimmune disease or neoplasm. Pain associated with meals suggests obstruction.

PLAB application: Facial weakness with a parotid mass is a red flag.

Thyroid and midline masses

Thyroid nodules typically move on swallowing; a thyroglossal duct cyst is usually midline and may move with swallowing and tongue protrusion.

PLAB application: Thyroid swelling and unexplained hoarseness warrant appropriate assessment.

Congenital cysts

Branchial cleft cysts often present as lateral neck swellings; thyroglossal duct cysts are midline. Adult lateral cystic masses require assessment for malignancy before being labelled congenital.

PLAB application: Do not assume a new lateral cystic neck mass in an adult is benign.

Investigations

Depending on clinical suspicion: full blood count, inflammatory markers, infection testing, ultrasound and ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration or core biopsy; ENT may arrange nasendoscopy and cross-sectional imaging.

PLAB application: Biopsy method should be selected by the specialist; suspected lymphoma often requires tissue architecture.

UK referral and emergencies

Apply NICE NG12 suspected cancer referral criteria for unexplained neck lumps or lymphadenopathy. Airway compromise, rapidly expanding swelling, sepsis and deep neck infection require emergency assessment.

PLAB application: A suspected cancer pathway generally targets specialist assessment within 2 weeks, not definitive diagnosis within 2 weeks.

PLAB 2 OSCE and safety-netting

Explain the differential without prematurely diagnosing cancer; obtain consent for examination, check patient concerns, outline investigation and referral, and give clear return precautions.

PLAB application: Urgently return for breathing difficulty, swallowing difficulty, rapidly growing swelling or new neurological symptoms.

PLAB 2 OSCE: neck lump

  1. Introduce yourself, confirm identity and obtain consent.
  2. Ask about duration, growth, pain, infection, swallowing, voice, ear pain, weight loss and night sweats.
  3. Inspect and palpate the neck; examine oral cavity, thyroid, salivary glands and relevant cranial nerves.
  4. Explain possible causes and why further investigation may be needed.
  5. Agree a referral and safety-net plan; assess the patient concerns.

Deep Dive

Why location matters

Drainage patterns help identify infection or an occult primary. Supraclavicular nodes warrant particular attention.

Reactive versus malignant nodes

Tender mobile nodes after infection are often reactive, while persistent hard fixed nodes are more concerning. Neither pattern is diagnostic alone.

Why adult lateral cysts need investigation

Cystic metastatic oropharyngeal nodes can mimic benign branchial cleft cysts.

Fine needle aspiration versus core biopsy

FNA can help assess metastatic carcinoma, but suspected lymphoma may need tissue architecture from core or excision biopsy.

Referral urgency

NICE NG12 criteria guide suspected cancer referral. Airway or sepsis concerns require emergency care rather than routine cancer pathways.
