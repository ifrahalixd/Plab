Childhood Immunisations, Vaccination Schedule & Vaccine-Preventable Diseases

2026 UK update: MMRV and an 18-month appointment are now part of the programme. Vaccine eligibility depends on birth cohort. Always verify the latest UKHSA schedule.

1. Why immunise?

Vaccines prime adaptive immunity and reduce serious disease, complications and transmission. Explain individual protection and community benefit without implying perfect protection.

PLAB application: Ask about previous vaccines and offer a clear recommendation.

2. UK schedule in 2026

The UK schedule changed in 2025–2026, including routine MMRV and an 18-month visit. Follow UKHSA schedule by birth cohort; older children may need different catch-up plans.

PLAB application: Do not rely on an outdated memorised MMR timetable.

3. Infant primary series

The 6-in-1 vaccine protects against diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, polio, Hib and hepatitis B. Infant visits also include age-specific MenB, rotavirus and pneumococcal vaccination.

PLAB application: Verify vaccine, age, dose, route and previous records before administration.

4. One-year and 18-month visits

One-year vaccines include meningococcal B and pneumococcal boosters plus an MMR-containing dose according to birth cohort. From 2026, eligible children receive MMRV and a further 6-in-1 dose at the 18-month visit.

PLAB application: Check date of birth: MMRV doses vary by cohort.

5. Preschool and adolescent vaccination

Preschool diphtheria-tetanus-pertussis-polio booster and cohort-dependent MMR-containing vaccines are followed later by adolescent HPV, MenACWY and tetanus-diphtheria-polio programmes. Seasonal influenza eligibility also applies.

PLAB application: Use the live UKHSA schedule rather than assuming one pattern fits all ages.

6. Consent and communication

Explain the disease, expected benefit, common adverse effects, rare serious risks and alternatives. Seek valid consent from a person with parental responsibility or a competent young person, as applicable.

PLAB application: Ask what specifically worries the parent; answer without judgement.

7. Contraindications and precautions

Previous anaphylaxis to a vaccine or component is an important contraindication. Live vaccines may be contraindicated with significant immunosuppression; mild afebrile illness alone usually is not a reason to defer.

PLAB application: Distinguish a true contraindication from a common misconception.

8. Adverse effects and anaphylaxis

Mild fever, local pain and irritability are common. Recognise anaphylaxis by rapid airway, breathing or circulation compromise and manage immediately using emergency protocols and intramuscular adrenaline.

PLAB application: Observe, document and report suspected serious adverse reactions appropriately.

9. Missed vaccines and catch-up

Never automatically restart a completed vaccine series. Reconstruct the record, check minimum intervals and age limits, and use UKHSA catch-up guidance; rotavirus has strict upper age limits.

PLAB application: Arrange an individualised catch-up plan and follow-up.

10. Measles

Measles causes fever, cough, coryza, conjunctivitis and a spreading rash; Koplik spots may occur. Complications include pneumonia and encephalitis. It is highly infectious.

PLAB application: Suspected measles: isolate promptly, notify public health and assess vulnerable contacts.

11. Pertussis

Whooping cough causes prolonged paroxysmal cough, post-tussive vomiting or apnoea in young infants. Infants can deteriorate without a classic whoop.

PLAB application: Assess respiratory compromise and follow notification and treatment guidance.

12. Meningococcal disease

Meningococcal sepsis may present with fever, rapidly progressive illness, limb pain, cold extremities and a non-blanching rash, but rash can be absent early.

PLAB application: Urgent ABCDE, emergency transfer and prompt antibiotics for suspected disease.

13. Hib and pneumococcal infection

Hib may cause epiglottitis or meningitis; pneumococcus can cause pneumonia, sepsis or meningitis. Vaccination reduces risk but does not exclude disease.

PLAB application: Do not delay emergency treatment because a child is vaccinated.

14. Rotavirus and polio

Rotavirus causes severe gastroenteritis and dehydration; oral live rotavirus vaccination is age-limited. Polio can cause acute flaccid paralysis; routine immunisation remains important.

PLAB application: Assess hydration and neurological red flags independently of vaccine history.

15. Varicella, rubella and mumps

Varicella can cause skin infection and severe disease in vulnerable people; congenital rubella can cause major fetal harm; mumps can cause parotitis and complications. MMRV covers all four viruses.

PLAB application: Check pregnancy or immunosuppression precautions for live vaccines.

16. Clinical practice and safety-netting

Check the Red Book or electronic immunisation record, administer safely, document batch/site/date, advise on expected symptoms and provide clear return precautions. Consult the Green Book and UKHSA updates.

PLAB application: Give clear advice for breathing difficulty, collapse, persistent lethargy or a rapidly worsening child.

PLAB 2 — Vaccine-hesitancy consultation

Introduce yourself, confirm the child’s age and immunisation record, explore concerns and prior reactions, explain the relevant vaccine’s benefits and adverse effects, discuss true contraindications, obtain valid consent and agree a plan. Avoid coercion. Explain what to do if the child becomes unwell and document the discussion.

Deep Dive

Birth-cohort MMRV transitions

UKHSA guidance states children born on or after 1 January 2025 are offered MMRV at 12 and 18 months. Those born 1 July–31 December 2024 are offered MMRV at 18 months and 3 years 4 months. Older cohorts follow different rules. Check the live schedule.

Live vaccine precautions

MMRV and rotavirus are live vaccines. Severe immunosuppression can be a contraindication; assess specific immunological conditions, previous anaphylaxis, vaccine components and guidance rather than making blanket assumptions.

Vaccine myths and consent

Vaccines do not cause autism. Explore the exact concern, discuss expected mild reactions and rare severe reactions, and offer trusted UKHSA/NHS information. Respect informed decisions and document.

Public health duties

Suspected measles and meningococcal disease require prompt isolation or infection-control action, clinical management and notification under applicable UK public health procedures.

Catch-up complexities

Vaccine age limits, birth-cohort eligibility and previous doses matter. For incomplete histories, consult UKHSA catch-up algorithms and seek immunisation team advice.

Risk-based immunisation

Some children require additional vaccines because of asplenia, immunosuppression, chronic disease, travel or specific exposure. Use specialist and UKHSA recommendations.
