Polycythaemia, Essential Thrombocythaemia & Myelofibrosis

Red flags: Acute stroke symptoms, chest pain, severe breathlessness, limb ischaemia or major bleeding require emergency assessment.

The myeloproliferative neoplasms

Philadelphia-negative classical myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPNs) include polycythaemia vera (PV), essential thrombocythaemia (ET) and primary myelofibrosis (PMF). These clonal marrow disorders can cause thrombosis, bleeding, splenomegaly and constitutional symptoms.

PLAB application: Recognise persistent abnormal counts and refer to haematology.

Polycythaemia: definition

Erythrocytosis means raised red-cell mass or concentration. Relative erythrocytosis results from reduced plasma volume; absolute erythrocytosis may be primary (PV) or secondary (hypoxia or excess erythropoietin).

PLAB application: A high haematocrit is a finding, not automatically PV.

PV presentation

Headache, dizziness, facial plethora, aquagenic pruritus, erythromelalgia, thrombosis and splenomegaly may occur. Some patients are discovered incidentally.

PLAB application: Ask about itching after a hot shower and previous thrombosis.

PV investigations

Repeat FBC, review haematocrit, Hb, WBC and platelets, test JAK2 V617F/exon 12 as indicated, assess serum erythropoietin, oxygen saturation and secondary causes. Bone marrow examination may be required under diagnostic criteria.

PLAB application: PV often has JAK2 mutation and subnormal EPO; use specialist criteria.

Secondary erythrocytosis

Causes include smoking/carboxyhaemoglobin, obstructive sleep apnoea, chronic lung or cyanotic cardiac disease, high altitude, testosterone use and EPO-producing tumours.

PLAB application: Investigate oxygenation and exposures before attributing erythrocytosis to PV.

PV management

Specialist management aims to reduce thrombosis: venesection to a haematocrit target below 45%, low-dose aspirin if appropriate, and cytoreduction for selected higher-risk patients. Address cardiovascular risk.

PLAB application: Check bleeding contraindications before aspirin; management is individualised.

Essential thrombocythaemia

ET is a clonal platelet-producing MPN with persistent thrombocytosis, often due to JAK2, CALR or MPL variants. It may cause arterial or venous thrombosis, microvascular symptoms and bleeding.

PLAB application: Do not diagnose ET from one raised platelet count.

Reactive thrombocytosis

Common alternatives include infection, inflammation, iron deficiency, recent surgery, bleeding and malignancy. Confirm persistence with repeat FBC and investigate iron studies and inflammatory markers.

PLAB application: Rule out reactive causes before molecular work-up.

ET investigations

Haematology may arrange blood film, JAK2/CALR/MPL testing and marrow biopsy to distinguish ET from prefibrotic myelofibrosis and other myeloid neoplasms.

PLAB application: Molecular results alone do not establish the full diagnosis.

ET treatment

Thrombosis risk stratification considers age, previous thrombosis and JAK2 status. Aspirin and cytoreductive therapy may be used selectively; observation can be suitable for lower-risk patients.

PLAB application: Avoid automatic aspirin in extreme thrombocytosis until acquired von Willebrand syndrome is considered.

Myelofibrosis

Primary myelofibrosis features abnormal megakaryocytes, marrow fibrosis and sometimes extramedullary haematopoiesis. Secondary myelofibrosis can evolve from PV or ET.

PLAB application: Think of myelofibrosis with splenomegaly, anaemia and constitutional symptoms.

Myelofibrosis clinical features

Fatigue, weight loss, night sweats, early satiety from enlarged spleen, bone pain, anaemia and cytopenias can develop. Advanced disease can cause transfusion dependence.

PLAB application: Ask about early satiety and left upper quadrant fullness.

Blood film and marrow clues

A leucoerythroblastic film may show nucleated red cells, immature myeloid cells and tear-drop erythrocytes (dacrocytes). Bone marrow biopsy and molecular tests help establish diagnosis.

PLAB application: Tear-drop cells plus splenomegaly are important clues, not proof.

Myelofibrosis management

Specialist options include symptom-directed JAK inhibitors, transfusion support, management of cytopenias and allogeneic stem-cell transplantation for selected eligible patients.

PLAB application: Transplant is potentially curative but not appropriate for everyone.

MPN complications and emergencies

All three can cause thrombosis, including unusual-site venous thrombosis; bleeding can occur despite high platelets. Progressive disease may transform to myelofibrosis or acute myeloid leukaemia.

PLAB application: New focal neurological deficit, chest pain or acute limb ischaemia requires emergency assessment.

PLAB 2 consultation and safety net

Explore headaches, itch, visual changes, bleeding, clotting history, smoking, OSA symptoms, medications, family history and constitutional symptoms. Explain blood tests and specialist referral without prematurely labelling cancer.

PLAB application: Safety-net for stroke symptoms, severe chest pain, breathlessness and major bleeding.

PLAB 2 — Raised blood counts consultation

Introduce yourself, confirm identity and consent. Ask about headache, hot-shower itch, thrombosis, bleeding, constitutional symptoms, smoking, hypoxia and medicines. Explain that an elevated count has several possible causes; outline repeat FBC, blood film and specialist tests. Discuss urgent symptoms and agree follow-up.

Deep Dive

Why thrombosis occurs

MPNs create an inflammatory, prothrombotic milieu with abnormal blood cells. In PV, increased red-cell mass contributes to viscosity; in ET, platelet function and other risk factors matter.

JAK–STAT signalling

JAK2 V617F activates signalling in many cases of PV and some ET/PMF. CALR and MPL variants are also associated with ET and PMF; mutation-negative disease exists.

Haematocrit target in PV

Evidence supports maintaining haematocrit below 45% to reduce vascular events in PV; venesection and other therapy are specialist-supervised.

Very high platelets can bleed

Extreme thrombocytosis may lead to acquired von Willebrand syndrome; aspirin may worsen bleeding. Platelet count alone is not a complete thrombosis-risk measure.

Prefibrotic PMF vs ET

Distinguishing prefibrotic myelofibrosis from ET often requires marrow morphology, clinical features and molecular context.

Transformation and prognosis

PV and ET can evolve into secondary myelofibrosis; PMF carries variable risk of progression to acute leukaemia. Prognostic scoring guides transplant discussions.
