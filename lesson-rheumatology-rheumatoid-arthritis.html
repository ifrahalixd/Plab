Rheumatoid Arthritis

PLAB priority: Recognise persistent inflammatory synovitis and refer urgently, even if rheumatoid factor or inflammatory markers are normal. Early DMARD treatment protects joints.

01. Definition and clinical importance

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic systemic autoimmune inflammatory synovitis that can cause irreversible erosive joint damage, disability and extra-articular disease. Early diagnosis and disease-modifying treatment improve outcomes.

PLAB application: Persistent inflammatory small-joint synovitis needs urgent rheumatology referral.

02. Epidemiology and risk factors

RA occurs more often in women and commonly begins in middle adulthood, although any adult can be affected. Smoking, family history and genetic susceptibility including HLA-DRB1 shared epitope alleles increase risk.

PLAB application: Ask about smoking and family history, but absence of risk factors does not rule out RA.

03. Pathophysiology

Immune activation drives synovial inflammation, pannus formation, cartilage injury and bone erosion. Cytokines including TNF and IL-6 sustain inflammation; autoantibodies such as rheumatoid factor (RF) and anti-CCP are associated with subsets of RA.

PLAB application: Explain that DMARDs modify the disease rather than merely relieve pain.

04. Typical presenting symptoms

Symmetrical pain, swelling and stiffness of MCP, PIP, wrist and MTP joints are characteristic. Morning stiffness often lasts more than 30–60 minutes and improves with movement. Fatigue and functional difficulty are common. DIP predominance suggests an alternative diagnosis.

PLAB application: Ask whether patients struggle to make a fist, open jars or walk in the morning.

05. Joint examination

Look for warm boggy synovial swelling, tenderness, restricted movement and reduced grip. Examine MCPs, PIPs, wrists, elbows, shoulders, knees, ankles and MTP squeeze tenderness. Chronic disease may cause ulnar deviation, swan-neck and boutonnière deformities.

PLAB application: Document a joint count and assess functional impact, not only pain.

06. Extra-articular manifestations

RA may cause rheumatoid nodules, anaemia of inflammation, dry eyes, interstitial lung disease, pleuritis, pericarditis, vasculitis and increased cardiovascular risk. Cervical spine instability is an uncommon but important complication of established disease.

PLAB application: New breathlessness, chest symptoms or neurological signs warrant assessment beyond the joints.

07. Investigations: blood tests

Order FBC, CRP or ESR, RF and anti-CCP in the appropriate clinical context. RF and anti-CCP support but do not independently confirm diagnosis; seronegative RA exists. Normal inflammatory markers do not exclude RA. Baseline renal and liver tests support treatment planning.

PLAB application: Do not delay referral while awaiting blood tests.

08. Imaging and diagnosis

X-rays of hands and feet can show periarticular osteopenia, joint-space narrowing and erosions; early X-rays may be normal. Ultrasound can demonstrate synovitis and power Doppler activity. Diagnosis is clinical, supported by investigations and specialist assessment.

PLAB application: Normal radiographs or negative antibodies do not exclude early inflammatory arthritis.

09. Urgent specialist referral

NICE NG100 recommends urgent referral for suspected persistent synovitis of undetermined cause, even with normal acute-phase response or negative RF/anti-CCP, particularly if small joints of hands or feet are affected, more than one joint is affected, or symptoms have lasted three months or longer.

PLAB application: Refer urgently rather than trialling analgesics for months.

10. Initial disease-modifying therapy

A rheumatology team should start a conventional synthetic DMARD as soon as possible, ideally within three months of persistent symptom onset. Methotrexate, leflunomide or sulfasalazine are typical first-line options; hydroxychloroquine may suit mild or palindromic disease. Individual selection depends on comorbidities and pregnancy plans.

PLAB application: Methotrexate is usually taken once weekly, never daily for RA.

11. Symptom relief and glucocorticoids

Short-term glucocorticoids may be used as bridging therapy for rapid symptom control when starting or changing a DMARD, with an appropriate taper. NSAIDs may ease symptoms but do not prevent joint destruction; use the lowest effective dose for the shortest time with gastroprotection when indicated.

PLAB application: Do not confuse NSAID symptom control with disease modification.

12. Monitoring and treat-to-target

NICE supports treat-to-target care aiming for remission or low disease activity, with regular assessments such as DAS28, CRP and functional measures. DMARD therapy needs laboratory monitoring for cytopenias, liver toxicity and other adverse effects according to the drug and local protocols.

PLAB application: Escalate uncontrolled disease through rheumatology rather than repeatedly prescribing steroids.

13. Biologics and targeted therapies

If conventional DMARDs fail, specialists may consider biologic DMARDs such as TNF inhibitors or targeted synthetic DMARDs such as JAK inhibitors under current NICE technology appraisals. Screen for infections including tuberculosis and hepatitis where appropriate and assess cardiovascular, malignancy and thromboembolic risk for JAK inhibitors.

PLAB application: Ask about fever or infection and review vaccination plans before immunosuppression.

14. Differential diagnosis

Osteoarthritis causes more mechanical pain, brief stiffness and often DIP or first CMC involvement. Psoriatic arthritis may involve dactylitis, nail pitting and DIP joints. Gout, CPPD, viral arthritis, SLE and septic arthritis can mimic inflammatory arthritis.

PLAB application: A hot, acutely swollen single joint requires urgent exclusion of septic arthritis.

15. Pregnancy, prevention and multidisciplinary care

Preconception medication review is essential: methotrexate and leflunomide are contraindicated in pregnancy; specialist advice is needed for stopping, washout and pregnancy-compatible options. Encourage smoking cessation, exercise, hand therapy, occupational therapy, vaccination review and cardiovascular risk reduction.

PLAB application: Do not advise abrupt medication changes without specialist coordination.

16. PLAB 2 consultation and safety-netting

Explore symptom pattern, morning stiffness, impact on work and self-care, extra-articular symptoms, medication use and pregnancy intentions. Explain chronic inflammation and the value of early DMARD treatment in plain language. Red flags include fever with a hot joint, severe infection while immunosuppressed, new breathlessness or neurological deficits.

PLAB application: Summarise findings, arrange urgent referral and give clear safety-net advice.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Synovial pannus and erosions

Autoimmune synovitis stimulates proliferating inflammatory tissue (pannus), which invades cartilage and bone. Osteoclast activation contributes to erosions; early control reduces structural damage.

Seropositive and seronegative disease

RF is not specific to RA; anti-CCP is more specific but neither is mandatory. Persistent clinical synovitis takes priority over negative tests when deciding referral.

Why early DMARDs matter

Analgesics and NSAIDs may improve symptoms without stopping structural damage. Conventional DMARDs suppress immune-driven disease and are adjusted to achieve remission or low activity.

Methotrexate medication safety

Low-dose methotrexate for RA is typically weekly. Dosing errors, marrow suppression, hepatotoxicity and pneumonitis can be serious. Prescribing includes folic acid and laboratory monitoring per specialist protocol.

Distinguishing RA from mimics

RA typically causes symmetric small-joint inflammatory synovitis. Osteoarthritis is often mechanical with bony enlargement; psoriatic arthritis may have nail pitting and dactylitis; crystal arthritis or infection may cause abrupt monoarthritis.

Systemic risk and shared care

RA increases cardiovascular and infection risks and may affect lungs, eyes and other organs. Good care combines inflammation control, comorbidity management, vaccinations, rehabilitation and pregnancy-safe planning.
