Ankylosing Spondylitis & Axial Spondyloarthritis

PLAB priority: Chronic inflammatory back pain in a young adult can be axial spondyloarthritis despite normal X-ray, CRP or negative HLA-B27. Painful photophobic red eye needs same-day eye assessment.

01. Definitions

Axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) is chronic inflammatory disease primarily affecting the sacroiliac joints and spine. Radiographic axSpA (historically ankylosing spondylitis) has definite structural sacroiliitis on plain X-ray; non-radiographic axSpA may have no such X-ray changes.

PLAB application: Do not exclude axSpA because the X-ray is normal.

02. Who develops it?

Symptoms often begin before 45 years. It affects all sexes; non-radiographic disease may be under-recognised. HLA-B27 is associated but neither necessary nor sufficient for diagnosis.

PLAB application: Ask about age at onset and symptom duration.

03. Inflammatory back pain

Typically insidious back or buttock pain for over three months, morning stiffness, improvement with movement and little relief with rest; night pain may improve on getting up. Alternating buttock pain suggests sacroiliitis.

PLAB application: Distinguish inflammatory from mechanical pain.

04. Associated conditions

Ask about acute anterior uveitis, psoriasis, inflammatory bowel disease, peripheral arthritis, enthesitis and dactylitis. A family history of spondyloarthritis supports suspicion.

PLAB application: Red painful photophobic eye needs same-day ophthalmology assessment.

05. History and red flags

Explore pain, stiffness, sleep, function, work, response to NSAIDs, infection history and extra-articular symptoms. Screen for cancer, infection, trauma, neurological deficit, saddle anaesthesia and bladder/bowel change.

PLAB application: Emergency spinal red flags override routine rheumatology assessment.

06. Examination

Assess posture, spinal flexion and extension, modified Schober measurement, lateral lumbar flexion, chest expansion, cervical movement, sacroiliac provocation where appropriate and hip range of motion. Examine joints, entheses and skin.

PLAB application: A normal examination early in disease does not rule it out.

07. Laboratory tests

CRP and ESR may be raised but are often normal. HLA-B27 supports probability in the right clinical setting, but a negative result does not exclude disease. No single blood test confirms axSpA.

PLAB application: Interpret investigations with clinical features.

08. Imaging

Sacroiliac joint X-ray can show established sacroiliitis; MRI using an inflammatory back pain protocol can identify active inflammation when X-ray is non-diagnostic. MRI findings need expert interpretation because mechanical changes can mimic inflammation.

PLAB application: Use specialist imaging pathways rather than relying on a normal X-ray.

09. NICE referral approach

NICE NG65 advises referral for back pain starting before 45 and lasting over 3 months when at least 4 additional clinical criteria are met; with exactly 3 criteria, HLA-B27 testing may guide referral. Suspected axSpA can still warrant specialist review despite unmet criteria.

PLAB application: Know that clinical suspicion can justify referral.

10. Differential diagnoses

Mechanical back pain, degenerative disc disease, fibromyalgia, diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis, osteitis condensans ilii, infection, malignancy and other inflammatory arthritides may mimic axSpA.

PLAB application: Investigate red flags and avoid anchoring.

11. Non-drug treatment

Individualised physiotherapy and regular exercise, including stretching, strengthening, posture and aerobic activity, are core treatments. Encourage smoking cessation and cardiovascular risk management.

PLAB application: Exercise is beneficial even when medications are needed.

12. NSAIDs and analgesia

NSAIDs are a common first pharmacological option, using the lowest effective dose for the shortest necessary duration with gastrointestinal, renal and cardiovascular risk assessment. A different NSAID may be tried if response is inadequate.

PLAB application: Review contraindications and treatment response.

13. Biologic and targeted therapy

Persistent active disease despite appropriate conventional management may qualify for specialist-directed biologic or targeted treatment, such as TNF or IL-17 inhibition, under current NICE criteria. Screen for infection including TB and hepatitis before relevant immunosuppression.

PLAB application: Do not start biologics in primary care.

14. Peripheral disease and complications

Peripheral arthritis, enthesitis, hip involvement and reduced spinal mobility can occur. Long-standing disease may cause osteoporosis, vertebral fracture and restricted chest expansion; assess new severe spinal pain carefully.

PLAB application: A fracture may occur after relatively minor trauma in an ankylosed spine.

15. Uveitis and other emergencies

Acute anterior uveitis causes a painful red photophobic eye and blurred vision: arrange same-day ophthalmology assessment. New neurological deficit, suspected spinal fracture, or cauda equina symptoms require emergency care.

PLAB application: Do not label a painful photophobic eye as simple conjunctivitis.

16. PLAB 2 counselling and follow-up

Explain the chronic inflammatory nature, variable course, exercise, medication benefits/risks, adherence and monitoring. Ask about eye symptoms, bowel symptoms and psoriasis; discuss smoking cessation, vaccination and safety-netting.

PLAB application: Offer shared decisions and a clear referral/follow-up plan.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Enthesitis and sacroiliitis

Inflammation at entheses and sacroiliac joints contributes to pain and stiffness. Chronic inflammation may lead to structural new bone formation and ankylosis.

Radiographic versus non-radiographic disease

Both can cause substantial symptoms. Classification depends partly on imaging findings; a normal plain film does not rule out active axial inflammation.

MRI interpretation

Bone marrow oedema on MRI can support active sacroiliitis, but mechanical loading, pregnancy-related changes and other conditions can cause similar appearances; interpretation requires clinical context.

HLA-B27 and referral reasoning

HLA-B27 increases probability but has limited positive predictive value alone. NICE NG65 uses combinations of clinical features and sometimes HLA-B27 to guide referral.

Therapy mechanisms

NSAIDs reduce prostaglandin-mediated inflammation; TNF and IL-17 inhibitors target immune pathways in selected patients. Choice depends on disease, comorbidities and infection risk.

Extra-articular disease

Uveitis, psoriasis and IBD reflect systemic inflammatory associations. Treatment choice may be influenced by these conditions, especially coexisting IBD.
