Pituitary Disorders: Prolactinoma, Acromegaly & Hypopituitarism

Emergency: Sudden severe headache with visual loss or collapse may be pituitary apoplexy. Resuscitate, give hydrocortisone when indicated and escalate urgently.

Pituitary overview

The anterior pituitary secretes ACTH, TSH, LH, FSH, GH and prolactin. The posterior pituitary releases ADH and oxytocin. Lesions cause hormone excess, deficiency or mass effects.

PLAB application: Always assess vision, headache and pituitary hormone function.

Pituitary mass history

Ask about headaches, visual field loss, galactorrhoea, menstrual change, infertility, libido, erectile dysfunction, growth of hands or feet, fatigue and steroid exposure.

PLAB application: Sudden severe headache with visual symptoms or collapse suggests apoplexy.

Pituitary mass examination

Check visual acuity and fields, eye movements, blood pressure, signs of acromegaly, Cushingoid features, hypogonadism and thyroid/adrenal insufficiency.

PLAB application: Bitemporal hemianopia suggests optic chiasm compression.

Prolactin physiology

Dopamine inhibits prolactin secretion. Raised prolactin may suppress GnRH, lowering LH/FSH and causing hypogonadism.

PLAB application: Interpret prolactin with pregnancy, medications and thyroid tests.

Hyperprolactinaemia causes

Causes include prolactinoma, pregnancy, lactation, dopamine-antagonist medicines (such as antipsychotics), primary hypothyroidism, renal impairment and stalk compression.

PLAB application: Do not diagnose prolactinoma from one elevated result without considering alternatives.

Prolactinoma presentation

Women may have oligomenorrhoea, amenorrhoea, infertility and galactorrhoea; men may present with low libido, erectile dysfunction or headache and visual changes.

PLAB application: Ask sensitively about fertility, pregnancy and sexual symptoms.

Prolactinoma assessment

Confirm and contextualise prolactin elevation, review medication and pregnancy possibility, test thyroid and renal function and arrange pituitary MRI when indicated.

PLAB application: Markedly raised prolactin supports prolactinoma, but laboratory and imaging context matter.

Prolactinoma management

Cabergoline, a dopamine agonist, is commonly first-line under specialist care; monitor prolactin, tumour response and adverse effects. Surgery is selected for intolerance, resistance or compression.

PLAB application: Discuss pregnancy plans and follow-up with endocrinology.

Acromegaly pathophysiology

Usually a GH-secreting pituitary adenoma causes increased hepatic IGF-1 and progressive soft tissue and bone changes after epiphyseal closure.

PLAB application: Screen with age-adjusted IGF-1, not a random GH alone.

Acromegaly clinical features

Enlarging hands or feet, altered facial features, prognathism, sweating, headaches, carpal tunnel syndrome, hypertension, diabetes and obstructive sleep apnoea.

PLAB application: Ask about changes in shoe or ring size and old photographs.

Acromegaly diagnosis

Raised IGF-1 prompts specialist confirmation, commonly oral glucose GH suppression testing and pituitary MRI. GH normally suppresses with glucose.

PLAB application: Failure of GH suppression supports acromegaly in the right context.

Acromegaly treatment

Transsphenoidal surgery is often first-line for resectable adenoma. Somatostatin analogues, pegvisomant, dopamine agonists or radiotherapy may be considered by specialists.

PLAB application: Address cardiovascular, metabolic and sleep-related comorbidities.

Hypopituitarism

Deficiency of one or more pituitary hormones can cause fatigue, hypotension, hyponatraemia, amenorrhoea, low libido, infertility or growth issues. Causes include masses, surgery, radiotherapy and apoplexy.

PLAB application: Secondary adrenal insufficiency can be life-threatening.

Assessing hormone axes

Specialist evaluation may include 9 am cortisol/ACTH, TSH and free T4, prolactin, LH/FSH with sex hormones, IGF-1 and dynamic tests where needed.

PLAB application: Central hypothyroidism can have low FT4 with non-elevated TSH.

Replacement and safety

Treat cortisol deficiency before starting levothyroxine when both are present to avoid precipitating adrenal crisis. Provide sick-day steroid advice and emergency plans.

PLAB application: Never start thyroid replacement ahead of needed glucocorticoid replacement.

Pituitary apoplexy

Sudden severe headache, visual loss, ophthalmoplegia, vomiting, reduced consciousness or hypotension can reflect haemorrhage/infarction in a pituitary lesion.

PLAB application: Emergency ABCDE, urgent senior/endocrine/neurosurgical input and immediate hydrocortisone when suspected.

PLAB 2: Pituitary consultation OSCE

  1. Introduce yourself, confirm identity, obtain consent and ask about concerns.
  2. Explore headache, visual change, menstrual and sexual symptoms, fertility, galactorrhoea, changes in appearance and medicines.
  3. Examine visual fields and relevant endocrine signs, explaining each step.
  4. Explain hormone blood tests and MRI; discuss specialist treatment and follow-up.
  5. Safety-net sudden headache, visual loss, collapse or severe vomiting as urgent emergencies.

Mechanisms and exam reasoning

Deep Dive

Hypothalamic-pituitary feedback

Peripheral hormones generally suppress pituitary tropic hormones; prolactin differs because dopamine provides tonic inhibition.

Stalk effect versus prolactinoma

A non-functioning mass can interrupt dopamine delivery and moderately raise prolactin; interpret concentrations with imaging and assay limitations.

GH and IGF-1

GH secretion is pulsatile; IGF-1 is more stable. Glucose normally suppresses GH, which fails in acromegaly.

Mass effect

An enlarging sellar lesion can compress the optic chiasm and disrupt multiple anterior pituitary axes.

Secondary adrenal insufficiency

ACTH deficiency reduces cortisol; aldosterone is usually relatively preserved because renin-angiotensin largely regulates it.

Why steroid replacement precedes thyroxine

Thyroxine can increase cortisol clearance and metabolic demand, exposing untreated adrenal insufficiency.
