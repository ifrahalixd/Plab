PCOS, Endometriosis & Adenomyosis

Red flags: Acute severe pelvic pain, suspected ectopic pregnancy, haemodynamic instability, fever with pelvic pain or rapidly progressive virilisation need urgent evaluation.

Overview

PCOS is a heterogeneous endocrine disorder associated with ovulatory dysfunction and hyperandrogenism; endometriosis is endometrial-like tissue outside the uterus; adenomyosis is endometrial tissue within the myometrium.

PLAB application: Distinguish cycle irregularity from cyclical pelvic pain and heavy bleeding.

Focused history

Ask about cycles, pain timing, deep dyspareunia, dyschezia, urinary symptoms, infertility, acne, hirsutism, weight changes, heavy bleeding, family history, sexual health and pregnancy possibility. Assess impact on work, school and wellbeing.

PLAB application: Explore fertility goals and ideas, concerns and expectations.

Red flags and initial assessment

Urgent assessment for haemodynamic instability, pregnancy with acute pain or bleeding, peritonism, fever with pelvic pain, severe unilateral pain, or suspected ovarian torsion.

PLAB application: Perform pregnancy test when relevant; do not assume chronic pelvic pain is benign.

PCOS diagnosis in adults

After excluding mimics, adult diagnosis generally requires two of: ovulatory dysfunction, clinical/biochemical hyperandrogenism, or polycystic ovarian morphology on ultrasound; validated AMH may substitute for ultrasound in adults under appropriate guidance. Ultrasound or AMH is unnecessary when irregular cycles and hyperandrogenism coexist.

PLAB application: Polycystic-looking ovaries alone do not diagnose PCOS.

PCOS in adolescents

Adolescents require both persistent irregular cycles defined by years since menarche and hyperandrogenism, after excluding alternatives. Do not use ovarian ultrasound or AMH for adolescent diagnosis.

PLAB application: Avoid overdiagnosing normal pubertal menstrual variability.

PCOS differential and investigations

Exclude pregnancy, thyroid disease, hyperprolactinaemia and non-classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia as appropriate. Rapid virilisation or markedly elevated androgens prompts evaluation for androgen-secreting tumours. Check metabolic risk including blood pressure, lipids and glycaemia.

PLAB application: Assess diabetes risk and sleep, mood and cardiovascular risk factors.

PCOS management

Shared decisions about nutrition, activity, weight-related health without stigma, cycle regulation, acne/hirsutism treatments, and fertility goals. Combined hormonal contraception may manage irregular cycles and hyperandrogenic symptoms if suitable; consider progestogen-based endometrial protection when needed.

PLAB application: Metformin may be useful for metabolic indications; individualise and check contraindications.

PCOS and fertility

Anovulation may impair fertility, but pregnancy remains possible. For anovulatory infertility with PCOS and no other infertility factors, letrozole is recommended first-line pharmacological ovulation induction in international guidance, under specialist pathways and local prescribing rules.

PLAB application: Discuss preconception folate and metabolic optimisation.

Endometriosis presentation

Suspect with period-related pain affecting daily life, chronic pelvic pain, deep dyspareunia, cyclical bowel or bladder symptoms and infertility. Symptoms may be substantial even with limited visible disease.

PLAB application: A normal examination or ultrasound does not rule out endometriosis.

Endometriosis examination and tests

With consent offer appropriate abdominal and pelvic examination, and transvaginal ultrasound if suitable; consider transabdominal scan if not. Imaging can identify endometriomas and deep disease, but superficial disease may not be visible.

PLAB application: Refer based on symptoms and impact, not imaging alone.

Endometriosis treatment

Offer suitable analgesia and hormonal treatment such as combined hormonal contraception or progestogens, discussing contraindications and fertility plans. Consider specialist referral for persistent symptoms, endometrioma, suspected deep disease or infertility. Surgery is individualised.

PLAB application: Hormonal suppression treats pain but does not improve fertility while actively preventing ovulation.

Adenomyosis presentation

Endometrial tissue in the uterine muscle can cause heavy menstrual bleeding, dysmenorrhoea, chronic pelvic pain and a bulky tender uterus. It often coexists with fibroids or endometriosis.

PLAB application: Think adenomyosis with heavy bleeding plus significant period pain.

Adenomyosis diagnosis

Transvaginal ultrasound is usually first-line when suspected, depending on acceptability; MRI may help clarify uncertain cases. Histology after hysterectomy is definitive but is not routinely needed for clinical management.

PLAB application: Choose investigations with patient preferences and fertility goals in mind.

Adenomyosis treatment

Options include levonorgestrel intrauterine system where suitable, other hormonal suppression, NSAIDs and tranexamic acid for bleeding when appropriate. Refer for refractory symptoms; hysterectomy is definitive for those who have completed childbearing and choose it.

PLAB application: Avoid presenting hysterectomy as first-line or mandatory.

Distinguishing conditions

PCOS: irregular ovulation and hyperandrogenism; endometriosis: cyclical pelvic pain, dyspareunia or bowel symptoms; adenomyosis: heavy painful periods and sometimes enlarged tender uterus. Conditions may overlap.

PLAB application: Treat the person and symptom pattern, not only the label.

Follow-up and safety net

Review response to treatment, bleeding and anaemia, metabolic health in PCOS, psychological impact and fertility plans. Escalate new severe pain, fainting, heavy bleeding, fever or pregnancy-related pain promptly.

PLAB application: Use shared decision-making and offer continuity of care.

PLAB 2 — Chronic pelvic pain and irregular periods

Introduce yourself, confirm identity and consent. Explore pain pattern, bleeding, pregnancy possibility, sexual and bowel symptoms, fertility goals and psychosocial impact. Offer appropriate examination and pregnancy testing. Explain that PCOS, endometriosis and adenomyosis are different conditions and may coexist. Discuss suitable tests, options and referral, check understanding, and safety-net urgent symptoms.

Deep Dive

PCOS diagnostic nuance

Adult criteria differ from adolescent criteria. Ultrasound morphology and AMH should not be used to diagnose PCOS in adolescents; do not use AMH as a standalone diagnostic test.

Endometrial protection

Prolonged anovulation can increase risk of endometrial hyperplasia. Discuss regular progestogen exposure or other cycle management where indicated.

Endometriosis without imaging findings

Normal pelvic ultrasound does not exclude superficial endometriosis. A clinical diagnosis and empirical treatment can be appropriate; persistent symptoms merit review and referral.

Endometriosis and fertility

Suppressive hormonal treatment may help pain but should not be offered to improve spontaneous pregnancy rates when actively trying to conceive. Individualise fertility referral.

Adenomyosis versus fibroids

Both can cause heavy bleeding. Adenomyosis is diffuse myometrial disease, whereas fibroids are discrete smooth-muscle tumours; imaging and examination help distinguish them.

Communication and consent

Ask permission before sensitive questions and pelvic examination; offer chaperone, explain uncertainty, and avoid dismissing symptoms or weight stigma.
