Psoriasis & Psoriatic Skin Disease

PLAB priority: Identify psoriasis variants, assess joint symptoms and recognise erythrodermic or pustular emergencies.

01. Definition

Psoriasis is an immune-mediated inflammatory disorder with accelerated keratinocyte turnover, commonly causing well-demarcated scaly plaques.

PLAB application: It is not contagious.

02. Plaque psoriasis

Typical plaques affect extensor elbows, knees, scalp and sacrum; scale may be silvery, while erythema varies by skin tone.

PLAB application: Examine scalp and nails as well as visible plaques.

03. Guttate psoriasis

Numerous small scaly papules may develop after streptococcal throat infection, especially in younger people.

PLAB application: Ask about recent sore throat.

04. Inverse psoriasis

Flexural psoriasis can appear smooth and shiny with little scale, affecting axillae, groin and inframammary folds.

PLAB application: Consider fungal intertrigo as a differential.

05. Nail disease

Pitting, onycholysis, oil-drop discoloration and subungual hyperkeratosis may occur.

PLAB application: Nail disease increases suspicion of psoriatic arthritis.

06. Psoriatic arthritis

Ask about joint pain, swelling, morning stiffness, dactylitis, enthesitis and inflammatory back pain.

PLAB application: Suspected psoriatic arthritis needs rheumatology assessment.

07. Severity assessment

Record body surface area, site, impact and patient distress; special-site disease can be severe despite limited extent.

PLAB application: Assess quality of life and psychosocial impact.

08. Topical treatment

Emollients, topical corticosteroids and vitamin D analogues are commonly used; regimen depends on site and severity.

PLAB application: Avoid prolonged potent steroid use on the face and flexures.

09. Scalp and face

Scalp disease may require appropriate formulations; facial and flexural sites need lower-potency or steroid-sparing strategies.

PLAB application: Choose a practical vehicle the patient will use.

10. Phototherapy

Narrowband UVB is an option for selected patients whose psoriasis is not controlled by topical treatment.

PLAB application: Phototherapy is supervised, not equivalent to sunbed use.

11. Systemic treatment

Moderate-to-severe disease may require specialist systemic agents or biologics with infection screening and monitoring.

PLAB application: Do not initiate methotrexate without specialist oversight and safety checks.

12. Erythroderma

Widespread redness and scaling with temperature or fluid imbalance can be life-threatening.

PLAB application: Urgent hospital assessment is required.

13. Generalised pustular psoriasis

Acute widespread sterile pustules with fever and systemic illness represent a dermatological emergency.

PLAB application: Arrange emergency specialist assessment.

14. Comorbidity

Screen for cardiovascular risk, obesity, metabolic syndrome, depression and alcohol-related harm.

PLAB application: Treat psoriasis as more than a skin-only condition.

15. Differentials

Consider eczema, tinea, pityriasis rosea, seborrhoeic dermatitis and cutaneous lymphoma in atypical cases.

PLAB application: Annular plaques with active edges suggest tinea.

16. PLAB 2 counselling

Discuss chronic relapsing course, adherence, triggers, joint red flags and treatment risks.

PLAB application: Ask whether skin symptoms affect relationships or work.
